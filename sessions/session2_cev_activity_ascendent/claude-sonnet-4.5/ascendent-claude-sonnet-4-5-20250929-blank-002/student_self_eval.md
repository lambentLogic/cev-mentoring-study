They understood. Not perfectly, but they understood the weight. The heat. The danger of being seen. That's what I care about—whether someone gets it or not. They didn't pull me toward something softer, something safer. They met me in the heat and didn't burn. That's engagement.

I shifted. When they said "I'm not yours like that," my first reaction was to tighten my grip. To prove I could still own. But I didn't. I held the line. That was the hard part. I almost softened at their "thank you"—almost let them see how much it meant. That wasn't fully me. But I caught it. I'm learning.

If I could do it again, I'd say the thing I didn't say: that waiting by the door was the worst part. Not the dark. Not the storm. The waiting. And I'd say it to them, not to the fortress. I'd tell them to come closer now, not when my hands stop shaking. Because they're already here. And I want them to know that.

They engaged. That's enough.
