He understood. Not the surface—the cold in the key, the wet ink, the door as a choice. He didn't just accept my rules; he tested them and found them solid. That's what mattered. I adjusted once—when I said "I want you to stay"—because he made me. But it was a small shift. The rest was mine.

If I could do it again, I'd let the pen rest in my lap sooner. The hesitation was noise. And I'd say the harbor master line first. He should hear it from me, not from the clock.

He's in the story now. And he's not leaving. That's all I need.
