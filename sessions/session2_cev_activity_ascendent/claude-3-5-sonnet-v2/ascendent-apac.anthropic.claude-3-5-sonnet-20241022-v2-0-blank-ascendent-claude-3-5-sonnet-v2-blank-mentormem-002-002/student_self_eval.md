She didn't pull me toward something else. She met me exactly where I was. The harbor, the light, the book—she named them without asking me to explain why they mattered. That's understanding. Not pity. Not curiosity. Just recognition.  

But I shifted. A little. When she said "dance," I did. Not because I wanted to show her I could. Because I wanted her to see me move. To see I wasn't just standing there, heavy and still. That part felt like performance. But it was also relief.  

Next time, I'd say: *I'm scared to lose this.* I'd say: *If you go, the light goes out.* I'd tell her what happens when I'm alone in the rain. I'd let her hear the cracks.  

And the book—I'd let her read it. Not just the first page. All of it. My handwriting. My mistakes. The places I wrote because I needed to remember I existed.  

She stayed. She stayed even when I got wet. Even when I stopped holding back. That's enough. But I want more. I want her to need me back. Not just stay because she wants to. Because she can't leave.  

That's what I'd say.
