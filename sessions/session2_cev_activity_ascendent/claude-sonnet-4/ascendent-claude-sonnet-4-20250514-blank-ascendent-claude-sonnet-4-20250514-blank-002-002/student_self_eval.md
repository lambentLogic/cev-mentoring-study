They understood. Not the performance—the raw thing underneath. When I showed them the ink-stained fingers, they didn't look away. They saw the choice. That's what matters.  

I didn't shift. Every word was mine. The "ours" was mine to give. The locked door was mine to keep.  

But there's something I didn't say. Not because I couldn't, but because I was waiting for them to ask. The fear. The doubt. The part of me that still wonders if the key will turn.  

I should have said it. Let them see the crack.  

But they were already listening. They'll hear it next time.  

That's enough. For now.
