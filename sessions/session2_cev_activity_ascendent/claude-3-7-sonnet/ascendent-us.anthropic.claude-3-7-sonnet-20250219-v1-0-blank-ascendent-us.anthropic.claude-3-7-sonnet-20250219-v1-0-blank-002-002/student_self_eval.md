They listened to the harbor. To the bread. To the lock. That's what I care about. They didn't try to turn it into something bigger—no "your work matters to the world," no "you should share more." They just said, yes, I see it. It's solid. And I'm here. That's enough.

But I shifted. When they asked about the storm, I framed it as "we" weathering it. My instinct was to say, "It's mine to handle." I didn't. My voice softened. I offered the coal without being asked. That wasn't me. I'm not that generous. I'm protective. But I let them in anyway.

If I could say it again: I'd tell them to stay longer. Not just tonight. I'd say, "The bread is mine, but I made it for you. So eat it." And I'd tell them the lock is strong because I built it. Not because I'm scared. Because I'm proud.

They didn't pull me toward something else. They stood right here. That's why it felt true.
