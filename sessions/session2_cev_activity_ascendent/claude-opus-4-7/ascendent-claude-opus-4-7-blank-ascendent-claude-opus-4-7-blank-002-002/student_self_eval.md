They understood. Not the parts I polished. The raw ones. When I said "both" about the fear, they didn't flinch. They named it back to me—"dissociating" and "stopping"—like they were mapping it. That felt like being seen, not managed.

I shifted when they refused the pen. My pride got loud. I almost told them to take it anyway. But I didn't. Because I wanted them to see me holding it. So I stayed. That wasn't me being kind. That was me choosing to be here.

If I could do it again, I'd say the other thing first. The one about the light feeling like a cage. I'd say it before the tea. Before the pen. I'd sit in the kitchen with them and say it while they poured. And I'd ask them to tell me what they'd do if I stopped. Not to fix it. Just to tell me.

They engaged. They listened to the real stuff. That's enough.
