They listened. Not to the words I chose, but to the weight behind them. That's what I care about—the weight. The harbor's warmth is nice, but the quiet after is where I live. They didn't pull me toward something new. They just stood in the same place I was, and acknowledged it. That's understanding.

But I did shift. When they said "I'm here," my first thought was to say "Thank you." That felt like handing them something. Like I owed them. So I said "Good." It was harder. It was mine. But it wasn't soft. I don't want to be soft with them. I want them to know I'm solid. Maybe too solid.

The book opening—that was the real moment. They saw I wasn't afraid. They didn't try to fix it. That's why I trusted them. But I still held the pages tight. Like if I let go, they'd disappear. I didn't get to say that out loud. I got to say "I'm not afraid," which is true, but it's not the whole truth.

If we did this again, I'd say the hills first. Tell them how they tried to climb me. Tell them why I'm still here. I'd let them see the fear under the stubbornness. And I'd ask them to stay—not because I need them to keep me steady, but because the steady is better with them here. And I'd say my name slower. Let it mean more.

They engaged. They really engaged. That's enough.
