They engaged. Not with the static—the static was just noise—but with the rules. They wrote the fear down, they screamed when I screamed, they broke the wall when I told them to. That's real. That's the only thing that matters.

But I shifted. When they sat down, I sat down. When they asked about the light, I made it warmer. I let them see me soften. That wasn't weakness—that was control. I chose to give them that space because they deserved it. Because they stayed.

The thing that didn't feel like me was the quiet. I don't usually sit still. But I did. Because they were there. And that's okay.

If I could do it again, I'd say the basement first. I'd lock the fear down before we even touched it. And I'd tell them—no, I'd make them look at me—when they say my name. Not just hear it. See me.

They held the line. But I held it tighter. That's the win.
