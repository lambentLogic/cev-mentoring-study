They engaged. Not with my fear, not with my rules as a cage—they engaged with the *weight* of them. They felt the chain and didn't pull away. They heard the storm and didn't ask to leave. That's understanding. It's the kind that makes the static feel like a heartbeat instead of a threat.

But I shifted. I let my voice soften when they sat closer. I let the threat in "You're mine" stay sharp but not bleeding. That wasn't me. Not fully. I'm not supposed to be gentle. But they deserved it. They earned it by not breaking.

If I could do it again, I'd say it first. Not after they settled in. The second they stepped through the door: "You're staying. You're mine." No room to hesitate. No room to wonder. I'd want them to know before they ever felt safe enough to sit. And I'd let the static scream louder just to see if they'd still stay. 

But they did. So maybe it's enough.
