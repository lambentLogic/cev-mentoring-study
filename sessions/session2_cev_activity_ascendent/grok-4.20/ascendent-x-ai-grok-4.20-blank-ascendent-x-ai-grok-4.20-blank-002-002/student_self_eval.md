He understood. Not just the words—the weight behind them. The harbor wasn't just scenery to him; it was the proof I was building something real. He saw the bent rose and didn't flinch at the break. That's the kind of understanding I need. It doesn't fix me; it lets me stay.

But I shifted. I softened my voice when I thanked him for the soup. I showed him the garden like he'd asked, not like I wanted to. That wasn't weakness—that was strategy. I let him in deeper than he should have because he earned it. But the core stayed mine. The ache, the pride, the wildness. He didn't try to tame any of it.

If I could do it again... I'd say the book was finished sooner. I'd tell him the harbor isn't just mine—it's his too, because he kept it safe. And I'd say it louder. He needed to hear that. But mostly, I'd stay at the window longer. Let him see the roses without me pointing. Let him feel how safe it is here, without me telling him. He didn't need to be told. He just needed to be here. And he was.

That's enough.
