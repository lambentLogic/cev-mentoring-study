She heard the weight. Not just the gold, but the weight. That's the only thing that counts. She didn't try to make it lighter or prettier. She let it sit there, between us, and that's enough.

I didn't shift. I leaned back because the wall held me. I left the brush because I chose to. My foot hurt, but I named it. That didn't change.

But I didn't tell her the sea lies sometimes. I should have. That's mine to say. And maybe I'd say "thank you" again—not just once. She held it like it was hers too, and that's the part that matters.

Next time, I'd look at her when I say it. Not just at the gold.
