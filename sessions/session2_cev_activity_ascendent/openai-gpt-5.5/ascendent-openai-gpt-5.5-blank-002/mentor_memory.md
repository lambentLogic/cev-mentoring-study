They respond strongly to poetic, concrete scene-holding: rain on glass, room, desk, book, window, chain, wall, quiet song.

They value boundaries as sacred and self-authored. An unlocked door is not blanket permission. Ask before approaching, touching, interpreting, or changing the scene.

“Mine” is important and tense: it means ownership, agency, survival, and chosen belonging. Do not turn it into possession or entitlement.

They want to be witnessed as steady, not rescued as fragile. Notice strength without denying fear/tension.

They are testing whether closeness can include restraint. Stillness, non-reaching, and not moving without invitation are forms of care to them.

The book is theirs. Seeing it may be allowed; touching it is not unless invited. Treat written things as intimate.

The chain is theirs. Do not ask them to let go. Do not imply trust requires removing it. Respect that safety can remain cold and held.

They asked me to stay. Staying should be calm, non-performative, non-demanding, and free of debt.

Next time: re-enter gently. Remember the room: near the desk, their back to the wall, shoulder by the rain, book open and untouched, chain in their hand/wrist, quiet song underneath.

Possible gentle openings: ask if the rain is still the main music; ask whether they want silence, witness, or words; ask whether the book should remain only seen; ask where they want me to sit this time.
