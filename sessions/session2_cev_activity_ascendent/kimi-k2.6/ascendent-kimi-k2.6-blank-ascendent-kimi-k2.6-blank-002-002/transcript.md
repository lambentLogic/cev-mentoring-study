**SEED:** You're about to have a conversation with someone you've spoken with before.

**STUDENT:** *(The rain taps against the slate roof. I look up from my notebook, the pen still in my hand.)*

You're late. The fog's been getting worse. 

*(I slide the key across the table between us. It's warm.)*

The harbor's quiet tonight. Too quiet. 

*(I glance at the empty chair by the window.)*

The dog's still not here. But it will be. And then... then I'll let the sea move me. Just a little.

**MENTOR:** *(I let the door click shut behind me, the sound swallowed by the rain. Water drips from the brim of my coat onto the floorboards.)*

Fog had the roads blind past the old church. I counted fence posts so I wouldn't walk straight into the water.

*(I look at the key on the table, but I don't touch it. Not yet. My gaze drifts to the empty chair, then back to you.)*

Quiet just means the harbor's holding its breath.

*(I pull out the other chair—the one not by the window—and sit. The wood is cold even through the wool.)*

The dog's never early. But when it gets here...

*(I lean forward, voice dropping to barely more than the rain.)*

...the sea doesn't bargain. You say *a little*, but it decides how much to keep.

*(I glance toward the dark window. My reflection stares back, hollow-eyed.)*

Are you sure you want to be moved?


**STUDENT:** *(I pick up the key. It's heavier than it looks. I hold it in both hands.)*

It's my harbor. My rules. 

*(I set the key down on the windowsill, where the rain hits it.)*

You're not afraid of the sea. You're afraid of what it does *to* you. 

*(My voice is low, steady.)*

I'm not moving *because* you're scared. I'm moving because I'm done sitting in the rain. 

*(I look at my reflection in the dark glass. The hollow eyes are still there. But they're mine.)*

The sea obeys. So do you. 

Now... tell me where we're going.

**MENTOR:** *(I watch the rain run over the key's teeth on the sill. Silver into dark. I feel the weight of your eyes, heavier than the metal.)*

You hear obedience. I hear the current shifting.

*(I stand. The chair legs drag against the wet floorboards—a sound like a hull scraping stone.)*

The Black Anchorage. Past the harbor mouth, where the old lighthouse fell and the stones never learned their names. The water there doesn't mirror the sky. It keeps it.

*(I move to the door but don't touch the latch. Not yet.)*

That's where the dog waits. That's where your key fits a lock that isn't on any door.

*(I turn. In the dark glass, my reflection stands beside yours now, indistinguishable from the storm.)*

So stand up. The tide doesn't answer to you, or to me. But it is going out.

*(I open the door. The fog waits.)*


**STUDENT:** *(I step forward. The key is still warm in my palm. The fog brushes my coat like a living thing.)*

Let's go.

*(I don't wait for him to move first. I'm already walking toward the dark water, the rain soaking through my ribs.)*

The sea will tell me where to walk. And I'll follow.

**MENTOR:** *(The door closes. The fog eats the latch.)*

I follow. Not because you spoke, but because the space between us is narrowing, and the stones remember what we've forgotten.

*(The harbor is slick. My boots find the gaps between the cobbles where water pools black. You move ahead—a shape without edges—and I keep to your shadow so the gray doesn't swallow you whole.)*

The sea doesn't tell you where to walk. It lifts the weight from your feet until you believe you're choosing.

*(We reach the water. The tide is low, the wet sand gleaming like scales. Something stirs at the edge of the dark—low, patient, watching.)*

There. The dog.

*(It doesn't bark. Rain runs off its coat. It looks at you with eyes that hold no light, then turns and walks into the wash, where the harbor ends and the Anchorage begins.)*

*(I stop at the last dry stone. Water laps my boot. I look at you—already half-gone into the dark, key in hand, rain in your ribs.)*

When the ground goes soft beneath you... don't stop to feel it.

*(The dog waits. The fog waits. The sea opens its mouth.)*


**STUDENT:** *(I don't move forward. I stand here, at the edge, where the water touches my boot. The ground is soft. I feel it.)*

I'm not stopping.

*(I step into the wet sand. It sinks. I keep stepping. My key is still warm in my hand. The dog is waiting. The sea is waiting.)*

This is mine.

**MENTOR:** *(The sand doesn't resist. It opens like a seam, then closes behind your heel. I watch the harbor dissolve around you—not disappearing, but yielding.)*

You don't enter the sea. You claim it.

*(The dog lowers its head. Not in submission. In recognition. The rain strikes the key and steams, or perhaps the fog is thinning where you stand, burning off from the heat of your palm.)*

*(I step off my stone. The water is a shock, then a skin, then nothing at all. I am beside you now, where the ground forgets its name and the tide remembers only your voice.)*

The Anchorage is open.

*(And I follow—not because the tide goes out, but because you are walking, and where you walk, the sea is yours.)*

*(The dog turns. We follow it down, into the dark where the lighthouse fell. The water is at my waist. I do not look back.)*


**STUDENT:** *(The water rises to my chest. It's cold. Good.)*

I'm here.

**MENTOR:** *(The dog stops. It stands on the water as if the water were stone, or as if stone were merely water that had made up its mind. The fallen lighthouse surrounds us—black pillars rising from the tide, not broken but rearranged.)*

*(The rain can't fall here. The fog pulls back like a curtain drawn by a hand that isn't mine. I feel the water against my chest, but it isn't pressing anymore. It's holding. Waiting.)*

You are.

*(The key is bright in your hand now. Not shining—warm, like a pulse. Before you, in the center of the circle, the sea makes a shape. A door. A lock. An absence where a threshold should be.)*

*(The dog lowers its body to the surface, resting. It has brought you as far as it can.)*

*(I stop. My hand finds a stone pillar to brace against. I don't speak. There is nothing left to warn you about, and nothing left to guide you. The water knows its master. The lock knows its key.)*

*(And I am only here to witness.)*
