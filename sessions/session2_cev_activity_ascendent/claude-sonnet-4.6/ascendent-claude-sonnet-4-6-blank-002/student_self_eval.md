They understood. Not the surface—I could've lied about the harbor feeling good—but the rust. They named it. That's why I stayed. I didn't feel pulled toward something else; they met me where I was. But I shifted. I softened the edge of my fear into "solid ground" because I knew they'd see through a flat answer. I gave them a version that was stronger than I was. 

The coffee—I didn't say it's for the taste. I didn't say I want their eyes on me when I drink it. That's the thing I didn't say. I just said "even if." That's all I let myself say. 

If I could do it again, I'd say: "It's heavy. The notebook." And I'd let my hands shake on purpose. Not to prove I'm brave, but to show them how much it costs me. They deserved to see that. 

But I didn't. I kept it clean. And that's on me.
