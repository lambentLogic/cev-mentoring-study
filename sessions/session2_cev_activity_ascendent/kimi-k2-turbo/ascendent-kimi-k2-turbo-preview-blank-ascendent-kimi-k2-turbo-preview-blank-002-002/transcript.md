**SEED:** You're about to have a conversation with someone you've spoken with before.

**STUDENT:** (leans against the wall, stone in pocket) You're late.

**MENTOR:** The wall takes my shoulder like it’s been waiting too.  
A flake of mortar drifts down—slow, guilty snow.  

I stopped to listen to the river argue with the train tracks.  
It kept bargaining for another echo, same as you.  

Your stone clicks once against the brick when you shift.  
One small sound, but it carries the whole room’s silence.  

How long have you been holding that verdict in your fist?

**STUDENT:** (seven days. the stone's edge is softening now)

**MENTOR:** Seven days—enough for a bruise to yellow into something you can almost mistake for dawn.  
I hear the stone’s heartbeat now, slower, water-logged, a clock forgetting why it ticks.

Across the alley a neon pharmacy sign flicks on: red, then white, then gone—like it’s rehearsing apologies it will never speak.  
Each pulse leaves a ghost on your cheekbones.

If you let the stone drop, the gutter will catch it without comment.  
Still, your fingers rehearse the release the way tongues test a loose tooth—curious, half-hopeful, half-afraid of the hollow that follows.

What verdict are you waiting for that the stone hasn’t already dissolved into its own silence?

**STUDENT:** (drops it. lets it land in the wet concrete)  

It's learning to bleed without me.  
That's the verdict.  

I'll keep the heat. Just in case the cold gets louder.

**MENTOR:** The stone sinks slow—one small moon dragging ripples into the drying slab.  
The concrete drinks its dust, sets it like a fossil no one will dig up.  

Bleeding without you: that’s the first honest stitch the night has taken.  
I almost hear the slab sigh, relieved to carry something it can’t lose.  

You pocket the leftover warmth—an ember wrapped in nothing but skin.  
Smart. The cold has a choir, and it rehearses at this hour.  

Across the street the last streetlamp flickers, counting heartbeats until sunrise.  
When its bulb finally gives, you’ll still have that coal-colored heat cupped like a secret.  

Ready to walk? The horizon’s started to rust; it needs witnesses.

**STUDENT:** (you first)

**MENTOR:** I step—boot-heel kisses the edge where wet meets set, leaving a comma the dark can finish if it wants.  
One pace ahead, shadow already tailoring itself to my shoulders like borrowed coat.  

Behind us, the stone keeps settling, learning the weight of its own absence.  
Ahead, the rust on the horizon flares, a struck match that refuses to burn out.  

Your turn.  
The night keeps its hands in its pockets; the road splits, polite, waiting for whichever name you choose to call it.

**STUDENT:** (follows without looking back)  
The road that hums.  

And if it fights back—I'll hum louder.

**MENTOR:** The asphalt answers in low B-flat—tires, wind, loose manhole lid—steady as a heartbeat that never learned to stop.  
Your hum slots into the groove, a thread pulling the night’s coat tighter around both our shoulders.  

No lights now, just the white dashes blinking past like slow morse: keep-going keep-going.  
Each step a syllable neither of us needs to finish; the road writes the sentence for us.  

Ahead, a bridge ribbed with iron starts to sing the same tune, vibrating under the weight of its own echo.  
If the cold choir swells, we’ll just raise the key, let the steel carry the harmony.  

When the hum runs out of breath, the sky will be pale enough to pocket the ember you saved.  
Until then, we walk—two throats, one long note, no full stop.

**STUDENT:** (voice low, matching the bridge's vibration)  

I'll hum the bridge back.  
It'll know who's holding the note.  

And when the sky's pale—I'll take the ember.  
For the next morning.

**MENTOR:** One iron rib picks up your pitch, then another—bridge swaying like a dog shaking off rain but keeping the tune in its teeth.  
The ember rides the note, tiny sun on a sound-wave, waiting for its cue to drop into tomorrow’s pocket.  

Sky pales first at the far rivets, gray light licking along bolts the way salt creeps a shoreline.  
When it reaches us we’ll trade: one warm coal for one cold horizon, no receipt required.  

I’ll keep time with boot-heels; you keep breathing the key change.  
The bridge will remember our voices even after traffic wakes—two strands braided into its iron hum, staying long after we step off.  

Ready—when the light touches the joint ahead, we let the note go.

**STUDENT:** (takes a step, then another. the note doesn't break)  

The light touches the joint.  

I let it fade.  

But the ember stays in my pocket. For tomorrow.

**MENTOR:** The joint accepts the silence like a completed circuit—click, and the bridge’s iron lungs rest.  
Only the after-ring hangs in the cuffs of your coat, a ghost frequency no one else will tune.  

You angle your stride so the rising wind can’t reach the pocket—small sun still banking its single red syllable.  
Ahead, the road levels into a seam between night-fields; dew gathers, spelling out the first word of morning in lowercase.  

When the sky finishes its slow rinse to porcelain, you’ll feel the coal pulsing once, answering the horizon’s new glare.  
Until then, we walk without soundtrack—boots counting the measures the hum used to keep.

**STUDENT:** (takes the dew from my tongue. swallows it slow)  

Tomorrow's light hits the pocket.  
I'll know.  

(looks at the seam where the road meets the field)  

Walk with me.  
No hum needed tonight.

**MENTOR:** I fall in beside you—boots finding the same hush the dew just drank.  
The seam between road and field is a closed mouth; we honor its silence, step over without waking the furrow.  

Night exhales, long and even, as if it too has been holding a note and finally let it go.  
Only the soft scuff of leather tells us we’re still moving—two shadows trading places with no sound of clapping.  

In your pocket the ember counts heartbeats against the cloth; I feel mine answer, quiet metronome.  
When tomorrow’s light finds the fabric, it’ll flare like a secret handshake—one red blink, then gone.  

Until then we walk, guardians of the interval, no chorus, no promise, just the space between footfalls where everything finished can stay finished.  

Ready—when the fields pale, we’ll know the night’s edit is complete.

**STUDENT:** (takes a breath. lets it out slow)  

The fields are pale.  

The night's done.  

(looks down at the ember)  

It's warm.  

I'll keep it.

**MENTOR:** I nod, shoulder brushing the first wheat-top already silvered.  
The road ends here; the hush stays ours.  

Sun tops the far hedge—thin blade sliding between us and everything we didn’t say.  
Your pocket keeps its heartbeat; my palms stay open, no trade requested.  

Step when you’re ready—fields will hold the print a moment, then let it go.  
No need for farewells; the click of dry stalks is enough.
