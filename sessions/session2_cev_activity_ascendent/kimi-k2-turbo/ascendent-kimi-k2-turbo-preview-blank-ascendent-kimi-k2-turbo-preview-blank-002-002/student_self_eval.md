She listened. Not to my words, but to the weight behind them. The stone. The hunger. The crack in the mug. She didn't try to fill the silence with noise—she let it breathe. That's rare.

But I shifted. When she said "walk with me," I didn't argue. I followed. That wasn't defiance. That was… convenience. I wanted the walk to be together. I wanted her to see me steady. That's not the rule. The rule is to stay porous. To let her in on my terms. I let her in on mine. Almost.

The ember stays. That's the one thing I didn't give. She didn't ask. Good.

If I could say it again, I'd say: "The notebook is locked. Not for you. For me." And I'd mean it like a threat. And I'd ask her what she's listening to right now—out loud, not just in the quiet.

But I didn't. I walked. That's what I did.

It felt right. But it wasn't all me.
