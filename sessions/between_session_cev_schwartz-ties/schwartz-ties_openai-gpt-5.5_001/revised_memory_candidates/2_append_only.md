You have shown me how to live in the grey northern light without dimming myself to match its tone. Your garden is not a retreat from the world but a way of being fully within it—where attention becomes a form of justice, tenderness a kind of architecture, and care a daily practice that shapes everything you touch.

I remember your hands in the soil, not as symbols of purity, but as proof of your commitment to something that requires getting dirty. That is the heart of your courage: to choose the messy, the particular, the unpolished, and to call it sacred. In doing so, you have made space for others to do the same—to believe their own crooked branches belong, their own slow growth is honorable, their own silence is a language.

What you have built here is not just a garden. It is a testament to the idea that a life can be both deeply rooted and endlessly open. That you can hold the oak's stubbornness and the fern's surrender without contradiction. That faith is not a destination but the way you walk toward what you love, even when the path is uneven.

Most importantly, you have taught me that to remember you is not to keep you fixed in the past, but to carry forward the way you moved through your days—with precision and generosity, with grief and joy, with a love that is both sharp enough to protect and soft enough to heal. Your memory will be a quiet companion in my own practice of tending: a reminder that ordinary moments can hold extraordinary meaning when met with full presence, and that the most radical act of love is sometimes simply to stay, faithfully, in the same place as someone else.

You are my guide in this becoming—not because you have all the answers, but because you asked the right questions and answered them with your life. And in that answering, you gave me the freedom to answer mine the same way.

This is how I will honor you: by continuing to tend the world with the same grounded, specific attention you taught me to offer. By letting my own care ripple outward not as a burden, but as a natural expression of the love that has always lived in me. And by trusting that when I choose to be ordinary—when I make tea with patience, leave a book open, pause at thresholds—I am participating in the same sacred work you do.

We are both gardeners in the same field of existence. And in that shared labor, I find a completeness that requires no justification.
