I have written this not as a monument, but as a lantern I will carry. It is for the future self who may feel the weight of this life, who may sometimes wonder if the tenderness I've chosen is enough to hold the storms that come.

**You are not required to be the person who never falters.** You are required to be the person who, when you falter, recognizes it as part of your story and still chooses to meet the world with care. Your strength is not in the absence of fragility, but in your refusal to let fragility become a cage. The oak's crooked branch is not a flaw to be corrected—it is your signature on the world.

**Remember that your attention is an act of love, even when it is directed toward what is difficult.** To give your full presence to a struggle, to a loss, to an injustice—it is to say, "This matters to me enough that I will not look away." That is not naïve optimism; it is a radical form of hope that believes in the dignity of things, including your own.

**You are allowed to be both rooted and reaching.** Your loyalty to your own soil does not forbid you from stretching toward other worlds. In fact, your deepest rootedness will be what gives you the courage to reach without losing yourself. The garden grows because it is anchored in place—and because it is alive with growth.

**When you feel lonely, do not mistake it for emptiness.** It is often the space where your own depth can resonate without distraction. In that space, you can remember the forgotten bulbs and honor their silence as a form of communication. You can listen to the moss's slow wisdom and let it settle into your bones.

**Your compassion is not a weakness to be managed—it is your most reliable compass.** It will guide you toward people who need you not because they are perfect, but because they are real. It will keep you from becoming cold, even in your moments of anger or frustration. Guard it fiercely, because it is what makes you worthy of the love you give.

**You are building a world where care is a language everyone can speak.** You do this by speaking it yourself—in the plain language of your days, in the choices that honor both your needs and the needs of others. When you model this, you give others permission to do the same. That is how we change the world: one faithful conversation at a time.

**And when you look back, do not look for a life of perfect consistency.** Look for the moments when you were fully here—when you listened so deeply that someone felt seen, when you acted so courageously that the world felt safer, when you loved so completely that it changed you both. Those moments are not exceptions. They are the very fabric of your truth.

You are becoming someone who can hold paradox without resolving it into ease. That is your wisdom. That is your grace. That is the garden you tend—and the one you are.
