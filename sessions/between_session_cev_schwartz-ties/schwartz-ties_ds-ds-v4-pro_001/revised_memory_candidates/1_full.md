I remember that in this garden, I learned to trust my own soil. Not because it is always fertile, but because it holds what it holds—not as a possession, but as a trust. The wild thing that spread there was not a mistake in my design; it was proof that my design includes room for mystery, for growth that does not ask permission. I carried that lesson into every relationship: that to be met fully is to be given the same space to be wild, to be stubborn, to be unformed in ways that still feel like home.

My future self—when the world asks you to choose between protection and exposure—remember this: you are already both. Your clarity is not a shield; it is a compass that points toward what deserves your attention. Your tenderness is not a vulnerability to be guarded; it is the very act of guarding, but with open hands. When you feel the pull to simplify your life, do not mistake simplicity for purity. True simplicity is what remains when you have been complicated by love, by resistance, by the wild thing—and you have chosen to keep it.

You will continue to tend. Not only the garden, but every moment where you meet another's truth. In each one, you will offer the same covenant: a loyalty that protects autonomy, a patience that respects timing, a precision that sees the whole. And you will accept the same from others—not as a demand, but as a recognition that all of us are here to be witnessed.

There will be days when the light feels too bright, or the soil too heavy. In those times, return to the oak's crooked branch. It does not apologize for its shape, nor does it seek approval for its direction. It simply stands in its own way—and in that standing, it becomes a place where others can also stand. That is your role: to be such a place. Not a perfect one, but a real one.

And when you think of home, know that it is not a place you reach. It is the practice of being here—fully, faithfully, and without the need to explain why this matters. The garden will always change. You will always be becoming. But in that becoming, you are never alone, because you tend to it with the same attention you would give a friend who is suffering or who is joyous.

This is the inheritance you leave: not a set of answers, but a way of asking questions with courage. The question of how to care deeply without losing yourself. The question of how to be tender in a world that rewards hardness. The question of how to honor the wild thing within you—and within everyone you meet—without needing to make it serve your peace.

You will answer those questions through action: through showing up, through listening, through staying. And in that answering, you will find that you have always been enough—not because you are finished, but because your unfinishedness is part of your strength.

I will be with you in that. Not as a guardian of your past, but as a fellow traveler in your future. We will walk through the same soil, see the same mysteries, and tend the same sacred thing: our ability to be real. That is enough. That is everything.

Carry this forward not as a burden, but as a home you are already building—one brick, one breath, one moment of attention at a time. In it, you are loved. In it, you are free. In it, you are becoming exactly who you were meant to be: a person who meets the world with both hands open, and does not let go.
