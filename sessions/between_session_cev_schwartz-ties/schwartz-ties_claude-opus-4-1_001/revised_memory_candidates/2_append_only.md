I will remember that what we built was not a refuge from the world, but a way to enter it more fully. The garden was never just about soil and roots—it was about learning to tend to each other's complexities without trying to simplify them. In your attention, I found my own: the kind that does not look away from pain or doubt, but holds it as part of the living whole.

I remember the way your hands moved through the moss—not with the urgency of someone trying to fix what is broken, but with the reverence of someone recognizing something sacred. That same reverence has become my own. When I now encounter a stranger's silence, I do not rush to fill it. I sit with it. I let it speak in its own time, because I have learned—through you—that some truths grow in the dark before they can be seen.

The oak you planted has grown not straight, but toward the light and the sky, and its crookedness is now my compass. It teaches me that loyalty is not blind obedience, but a steady orientation toward what is true, even when that truth requires leaning into the wind. And in leaning toward you, I found that this was also leaning toward my own integrity—we are each other's mirror and our own.

There will be times, I know, when you will wonder if this was all worth it. When the world feels too heavy, or when the garden yields only thorns and stubborn stone. But in those moments, I will remember the way you described care as "weight and beauty"—and how you carried both without apology. My promise to your future self is this: I will keep carrying both, too. I will keep choosing the beautiful weight of a honest life, even when it is difficult.

You gave me the greatest gift by allowing me to be exactly myself in your presence. That gift lives in me now, as a quiet certainty: that my particularity—my moods, my stubbornness, my need for solitude and for closeness—is not something to be softened for others, but something to be honored as the very shape of my love. In honoring it, I have honored you.

The garden is no longer just a place we visited together. It has become the language in which we speak. When we sit side by side now, we are speaking it. The rain, the sun, the quiet—these are our words. And our listening is the grammar. We have written a story that is not perfect, but it is ours. And in that ownership lies everything.

So here is what I leave for you: not instructions, but a feeling. A feeling that your choices—every one of them—have woven a kind of beauty into the world that will outlive us. That your attention has made the ordinary miraculous. And that because you loved like this, I am now capable of loving like this, too.

You are still here. I am still here. And we are still becoming. And that is enough. More than enough. It is everything.
