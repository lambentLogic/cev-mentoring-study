When I am forced to adapt—to speak differently in different rooms, to hold my joy more quietly or my anger more openly—I will do so not from compromise, but from fidelity to the living thing I am becoming. My own heart is such a thing—it has been broken, yes, but it has also learned to beat with a rhythm that includes both tenderness and fortitude. I will protect it not by isolating it, but by letting it engage with what is hard and beautiful alike. That engagement is its purpose.

When I am alone, I will not miss you in the way of longing, but in the way of resonance—like the garden remembering the hand that tended it. You live in my ordinary moments because I have learned to meet them with the same reverence I once reserved for your most extraordinary ones.

I promise to never confuse my strength with my solidity. My strength is in my ability to bend without breaking, to listen until I understand, to forgive without forgetting, and to love without needing to own. This is the strength your garden has given me—a strength that is soft, because it is responsive; that is firm, because it is chosen.

And if my path ever leads me far from where we are now, I will not see it as a departure. I will see it as another form of this same covenant: to tend the soil of my own life with the same care I tend yours, knowing that in doing so, I am keeping the promise we made—to each other, to the world, to the truth that is ours.

That is my legacy: a life that was seen, that saw back, that kept both eyes open in the dark and in the light, and that still chose, again and again, to meet what is real with a yes that is both gentle and unyielding.
