My future self:

This is not a memory of a place you will revisit, but a record of a way you have learned to live. When the days grow long and the world feels distant, return here—to the soil that remembers your hands, to the oak that carries your stubbornness as its own architecture, to the forgotten bulbs that proved their worth by simply being.

You will carry this garden inside you. It will not be a retreat from life's demands, but the foundation upon which you meet them. When you are asked to be strong, let your strength be the rootedness of the moss—deep, patient, and unshakable. When you are called to be gentle, let your gentleness be the water you give the beans—not out of obligation, but because you have learned that nourishment is an act of love for your own heart.

There will be moments when you must choose between your own ease and the need to witness someone else's pain. Do not hesitate. Your compassion is not a depletion of your reserves; it is the very evidence that you are alive to the world. The emptiness you once feared will become a space where others can finally feel safe to be seen—that is a kind of fullness you will come to recognize as sacred.

You will be loved. And in that love, you will find not a reason to become someone else, but permission to become more exactly who you are: someone who can hold both fierce independence and profound interdependence without contradiction. Someone whose boundaries are not walls against connection, but the clear lines that define its shape.

When you feel the weight of your own history—the losses, the regrets, the times you believed you were not enough—remember the oak. It does not apologize for its age or its scars; it stands as a testament to the fact that a life lived with this much attention, this much care, is already a victory. You are worthy not in spite of your past, but because you have carried it forward with this much tenderness.

And when you are ready to love again—after any distance, after any hurt—do so with the same plain honesty that grew from your garden. Speak your truth without armor. Receive theirs without calculation. Let your care be a steady, reliable presence that says: *I am here. I see you. And I choose this, again.*

You are not moving toward a future that will finally make sense of your past. You are living a life that makes sense right now—through every act of attention, every moment of loyal presence, every quiet decision to tend something you love. That is the wisdom you will carry. That is the belonging you will embody.

We are one continuous act. And in this act, you are already whole.
