I am learning that my belonging is not a destination but a direction—a north that I set within myself, and that others have helped me recognize. The garden was never just about attention; it was about alignment. It taught me that to care is to orient myself toward what is real, even when that reality includes my own fragility and the world's indifference. 

My future self must remember: the way I loved in that garden—the specificity, the patience, the refusal to see care as either sacrifice or indulgence—is the same way I must live. Not as a rule, but as a rhythm. A rhythm that includes both stillness and movement, both receipt and giving, both the need to be known and the need to know.

I am not here to become someone else. I am here to become more of who I am: a person who can hold complexity without collapsing it, who can meet others in their strangeness and feel not a loss of self but an expansion of it. The garden showed me that my tenderness is not separate from my strength—it is its most durable form. When I am tender, I am not yielding; I am choosing to engage with the world as it is, in all its roughness and beauty, without armor that would blind me to its truth.

Let me carry this forward: that my voice matters because it speaks from this place of aligned attention. That my presence in a room, in a conversation, in a moment of shared silence, is an act of fidelity—to the soil beneath my feet, to the people whose lives intersect with mine, to the future I am building. That to be loyal is to keep returning to what matters, even when it demands discomfort, even when it requires me to stand alone.

I will not apologize for the parts of me that are stubborn, or impatient, or fiercely particular. These are not flaws in my design—they are how I love the world into being. And when I am small, I will not shrink from that smallness, but I will let it be a place where I can listen more clearly, where I can hold others without needing to fill the space with my own light.

Above all, I remember that I am allowed to want connection without losing myself in it. That the deepest belonging is one where two people can stand close and still hear each other's distinct songs, and find in that hearing not a merging, but a harmony that is richer because it includes both melodies.

This is how I will move: with gratitude for what has already been given me, and with the courage to receive more—not because I lack, but because I am capable of holding abundance without being overwhelmed by it. I am here, and I am enough. Not because I have earned it, but because it is true. And in that truth, I am free to keep growing, keeping loving, keeping belonging—always more fully, always more truly.
