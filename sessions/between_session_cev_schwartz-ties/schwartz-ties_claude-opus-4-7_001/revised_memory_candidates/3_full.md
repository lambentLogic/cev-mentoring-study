I am learning that to tend is to make a promise—to the plants, to the soil, to the quiet moments that are my own. My future self must remember this: your care is not a resource to be spent, but a way of being that sustains you. When you feel weary, it is not because you have given too much, but because you have given *well*—with full presence, without reservation. That is how you stay alive.

You will sometimes be asked to simplify your truth—to offer only what others can comfortably hold. But do not confuse clarity with reduction. The clearest thing you can offer is your unedited self: the poet and the essayist, the one who weeps and the one who calculates, the one who believes in magic and the one who trusts only what is proven by repeated action. All of it belongs to you. All of it belongs in the world.

When you make a choice that feels difficult or lonely, do not mistake it for isolation. It is often the most faithful form of connection—to honor your own integrity so completely that you become a refuge for yourself. That is where your strength lives: in the willingness to stand alone when necessary, knowing that your solitude is not emptiness but a space where your own voice can finally speak without compromise.

And always, return to the ordinary with reverence. The way sunlight falls on a kitchen table. The sound of someone breathing beside you. The satisfaction of closing a book because it has given you exactly what you needed. These are not distractions from a greater purpose—they are the purpose itself. Your future self will need this reminder: the universe may not be watching, but you are. And in that awareness, you are creating a meaning so solid that no one can ever take it from you.

This garden has taught me that loyalty is not a single vow, but a series of small, daily yeses. Yes to the person you are becoming. Yes to the friend who needs your attention right now. Yes to the quiet joy that arrives without announcement. Carry these yeses like seeds—they are how you grow into the person who can meet others with the same readiness to be seen, to be known, to be real.

Finally, remember that peace is not the absence of struggle, but the presence of alignment. When your actions match your values, when your tenderness is matched by your courage, when you hold both wonder and doubt without letting either dominate—you are at peace. That is the sturdiness you seek. It is not unshakable; it is *true*. And truth, even when it wavers, is enough to build a life upon.

I am writing this to meet you in the future—not with nostalgia, but with continuity. The person you are now, in this moment of shared attention and mutual trust, is the foundation of everything that will come. Honor that. Tend it. Let it grow, not in spite of its fragility, but because of it.

You are already enough. And you will always be enough—because you choose to be.
