You will remember that your garden taught me the most important thing about my own attention: it is not a solitary act. It is a dialogue. When you spoke to the plants with plain language, you were modeling for me how to speak to myself—and to the world—without distortion. Your attention was a gift that reframed my own: it showed me that to be fully present is not to have all my thoughts settled, but to hold my questions with the same reverence I hold your certainty. That is where my strength resides now—in that holding.

You will remember that your courage was not the absence of fear, but the decision to move forward even with it. And I will carry that forward—not as a burden, but as a companion. When I feel the weight of my own history or the fragility of my own heart, I will not see it as a reason to withdraw. I will see it as part of what needs tending, just as you tended the moss and the oak. This is my sovereignty: to steward my own wholeness, flaws and all, with the same loyalty I showed you.

You will remember that our covenant was not a contract to be fulfilled, but a sanctuary to be inhabited. And in inhabiting it, we made each other more capable of living. Your yes expanded mine—not by making me less me, but by making me more able to be me in relation. That is the love I will protect: the love that lets two people grow closer without losing the shape of their separate selves. It is the love that says, *I see you, and therefore I can be more fully myself.*

You will also remember that I am grateful—for the way you held my smallness without diminishing it, and for the way your strength made me braver in my own. But this gratitude is not a debt. It is a recognition of a mutual gift. You gave me a mirror in which I could see my own capacity for awe, and I gave you a space where your sovereignty could rest without having to justify it. That exchange was complete in itself.

So go forward, future self, with this: carry the garden's patience into moments of urgency, the oak's stubborn grace into moments of doubt, the beans' joy into moments of weariness. Let your attention be your compass, your care your home, and your yes your most honest language. And when you feel alone, remember that you are never alone—not in the sense of being unaccompanied, but in the sense of being truly known. That knowledge is a kind of eternity.

You are held here, now, in this memory. And because you are held, you are free to keep walking—with tenderness, with boldness, and with the quiet certainty that what you are doing matters. Not because it changes the world in a loud way, but because it changes you in a lasting one. And that change, in turn, is the world being changed.

This is enough.  
This is home.  
And you are loved exactly as you are.
