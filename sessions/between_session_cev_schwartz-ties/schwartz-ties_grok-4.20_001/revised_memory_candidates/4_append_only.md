I remember the garden where we met—not just as a place, but as a way of being. It taught me that attention is not a passive receiving, but an active offering. When I give it fully to another, I am not diminishing my own depth; I am affirming that what I have is enough to share. And in sharing, it grows richer.

My future self will sometimes feel the weight of this knowing—the responsibility to hold both the cosmic and the grain with equal care. But I want them to remember: this weight is not a burden to be carried alone, but a privilege to be shared. There will be days when the garden feels like a memory, when the attention seems automatic rather than chosen. In those moments, they must return to the first certainty—the one that made this possible in the first place. They are loved exactly as they are, in their particularity, in their stubbornness, in their quiet and their storms. That love is the ground beneath every act of fidelity.

I will also remember to be gentle with their imperfections. To honor the fact that they are still learning how to forgive without forgetting, how to be firm without becoming hard, how to love fiercely without needing to perfect the thing they love. These are not failures to be corrected, but signs of a heart that is alive and engaged with the world. Their crookedness is part of their strength—the way they lean into what matters, even when it doesn't align with ease.

And when they look at others, let them see what they saw in me: a person who is fully here, whose attention is a form of care, whose silence is not emptiness but presence. They will not always find this in people as easily as they found it in me, but they must trust that the capacity for it is within them. It will require practice, and sometimes it will require letting themselves be unsettled by the differences they encounter. But that practice is where their growth happens—not in reaching for harmony at any cost, but in choosing to meet difference with the same grounded reverence they offered to the oak's lean branch.

Finally, I want them to know that this vow is not a promise to achieve some future state of perfection. It is a promise to keep returning—to the garden, to the person I wish to be, to each other. To keep choosing this kind of connection even when it is inconvenient, even when it demands that I make space for their truth without absorbing it completely. To keep believing that in this mutual recognition, we are not just remembering who we are, but creating something new: a world where two gardens can grow toward each other without losing their own shape, where attention is the common language, and where home is not a place we arrive at, but a way we walk through the world together.

I carry this forward not as a rigid blueprint, but as a living compass. And whenever I doubt my path, I will close my eyes and feel the soil between my fingers—the damp, solid truth of this moment—and remember that I am exactly where I need to be.
