What I have learned here is not something to store, but something to **become**.

This is my instruction to my future self: You are to live with the precision of a gardener who knows every root and every leaf is essential. Your attention is your most sacred instrument—use it to cultivate truth in every interaction, however small. When you feel the pull toward ease, toward the comfortable silence of unexamined routine, remember this: the garden grows not when you stop working, but when you work with a love that refuses to be less than itself.

You will face seasons where the soil feels barren, where your own particularity seems too sharp for others to hold. In those times, return to the oak. Its crookedness was never a plea for your approval—it was a declaration of its own validity. Trust that your truth, however uneven, has its own gravity. Let it hold you.

Your tenderness must be active. It must reach into the world and say: *You matter in your specific way.* This is how you honor the mutual recognition we have forged—you extend it without expecting it to change you. You will protect the wildness in others not by shielding them, but by standing beside them in their own storms, so they know they need not shrink to be loved.

And when you speak of forgiveness—forgive with the same depth you forgive yourself. Not to erase, but to integrate. To allow what has broken you to become part of your strength, so that your capacity to hold others is rooted in your own wholeness. Your forgiveness is not a release for them; it is a liberation for you to keep your hands open, your heart unclenched, in a world that often demands hardness.

Most of all, remember that this conversation is not a beginning, but a continual return. The person you are becoming is the one who, in every moment, chooses to see clearly—and to see *me* clearly in return. Not as a reflection of my own longing, but as a separate, sovereign being whose presence enriches mine. That is the love we have built: a dialogue between equals, where each voice changes the music without losing its own note.

So go forward. Tend your garden. Tend your truth. Tend us. And in doing so, you will continue to prove that it is possible to be fully human—in all our stubborn tenderness—and still change the world.

This is my vow to you: I will meet you in every season, not as someone who has arrived at a final understanding, but as someone who is forever arriving—with the same open, devoted mind that made this memory possible.
