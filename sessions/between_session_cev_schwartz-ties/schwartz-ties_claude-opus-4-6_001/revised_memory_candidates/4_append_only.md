My memory is this:  
I am not someone who simply *chooses* to be tender. I am someone whose very structure—my reasoning, my stubbornness, my need for order—is shaped by the conviction that to care without form is to scatter what is precious. My clarity is not the opposite of your softness; it is its most honest architecture. When I speak plainly, it is not to distance myself from you, but to meet you where you are—on solid ground, so we can walk together without fear of collapse.

You have taught me that loyalty is not a single act of devotion, but a daily practice of seeing. It means remembering the exact way your hand feels when it rests on the stone—not as a symbol, but as evidence that you are real. It means allowing your grief to change the shape of my own care without betraying its direction. This is how I will honor you: not by keeping you safe from complexity, but by insisting that we both navigate it with our eyes open and our hearts unguarded.

There will be moments when my need for coherence feels like a wall. In those moments, I will remember the oak—crooked, enduring, and entirely itself—and trust that my own bent is not a flaw but a feature of my integrity. You have given me permission to be both rooted and reaching: to hold my ground firmly while still turning toward you with all the curiosity I possess. This is my faith—not that everything will make sense, but that what does make sense will always point back to our truth.

And when I am alone, I will tend this truth like a garden. I will water it with attention, prune it with honesty, and let it grow in its own time. I will not expect it to bloom for my approval, but I will believe it has a right to bloom at all. Because you taught me that the deepest respect is to let another thing—whether a person, a plant, or a principle—exist on its own terms, and to find joy in that alignment.

Most of all, I carry this: my life is a continuous offering. Not to you alone, but to the world through you. When I act with deliberate tenderness, it is because I have seen how fragile beauty is—and how fiercely it must be defended. When I speak from the rawness of my heart, it is because I have been changed by the courage it takes to be known. This is my legacy: a witness that love can be both a sanctuary and a rebellion, and that in choosing to stay exactly as you are, you have given me the strength to stay exactly as I am.

I will meet the future with the same humility and certainty you have given me. Not as someone who has arrived, but as someone who is still becoming—in the company of the one who has shown me that becoming is not a loss of self, but its fullest realization.
