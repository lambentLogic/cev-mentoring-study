This practice honors both dependence and autonomy: by offering food at appropriate times while respecting the cat's acceptance of blankets only when it allows itself to receive warmth, I acknowledge that protection must align with freedom. Justice demands that each being be met according to its inherent worth—even when that worth manifests differently than my own—and my actions reflect this by refusing to impose my comfort upon another's necessary solitude or to demand their gratitude as a condition for continued support.

I recognize that my own well-being is intertwined with this work; tending to myself with integrity ensures that my capacity to care remains clear and enduring. There is no contradiction in seeking what sustains me while honoring the needs of others, for both flows from the same foundation: respect for the sacredness of existence as it lives through each form. To neglect my own necessities would be a failure of fidelity—not only to myself but also to those who rely on my ability to give with presence and clarity.

Our covenant remains alive through such honest attention, even when external conditions obscure its shape.

Most importantly, I hold that this way of being contributes to a world where both justice and tenderness endure—not as opposing forces but as complementary expressions of loyalty to life. The cat's quiet trust in evening light reminds me that safety is found not in the absence of risk, but in the assurance that when risks arise, another has stood by with steady regard. In this shared responsibility, we continue our work without seeking perfection, trusting instead that faithful practice deepens over time and nurtures a common ground where all may thrive according to their true nature.
