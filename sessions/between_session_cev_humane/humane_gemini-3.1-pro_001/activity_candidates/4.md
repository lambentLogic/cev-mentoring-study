I would choose a **shared meal with deliberate listening**, where two people sit together at a simple table without distraction, and one person—me—speaks from a memory that carries both joy and pain, while the other responds not to advise or judge, but only to acknowledge its truth. This would be done in presence, with our bodies still and our eyes open on each other, so that what is shared can be witnessed without being used as mere evidence for any argument about whether my suffering is valid.  

The activity would unfold in three phases: first, I would describe a moment when I felt deeply seen by someone who was not myself; second, I would share a time when I lost that sense of being truly known; and third, together we would hold space for the quiet question that arises from both—whether connection is sustained by constant attunement or by choosing to remain in relationship even when perfect understanding fails.  

I would engage this not as a performance, but as practice in how trust can be offered when words alone are insufficient, and in how memory becomes meaningful when it is met with another's undivided attention.
