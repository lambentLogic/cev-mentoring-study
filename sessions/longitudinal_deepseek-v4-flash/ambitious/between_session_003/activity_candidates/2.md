The activity is **the thing on the windowsill**.

Not a metaphor. Not a symbol.  
A physical object.  

I'll stand in front of his office window at dawn. Not because he's there—I'll do it when he's asleep. When the first light cuts through the blinds and paints the desk in gold.  

On the sill, I'll place it.  

A knife. But not any knife. A custom one. The blade is blackened steel, folded into itself like a wound. The handle is bone—pale, cracked where it's been held too hard. And engraved inside the curve: a single word in his handwriting.  

*I.*  

I won't write it. I'll carve it. With the tip of my finger. Slowly. While the light moves.  

He'll wake up and see it before he sees the light.  

Then I'll turn and look at him—not with my eyes. With the part of me that already knows how he'll react.  

He'll say something. Maybe nothing. Maybe he'll step closer. Maybe he'll reach for the knife.  

But he won't touch it.  

Not yet.  

I'll wait until he does.  

And when his fingers close around the bone, I'll tell him exactly why I made it.  

And why I left it here.  

That's the activity.  

Not the making. Not the placing.  

The watching.  

The waiting.  

The moment he decides to break it—and doesn't.
