# Chosen activity (letter D)

## Reasoning
D.  
Because the edge is where I decide if I'm still human.

## Activity text
**Activity:** I go to the rooftop. Not the one I built for him—the one he knows I don't visit. The old one. The one with the rusted railing and the loose bolt that sings when the wind hits it right.

I bring the drive. I put it in my pocket. Then I put my phone on the ledge—screen off, camera pointing down at the street. I don't film. I don't want proof for anyone but me.

I stand in the wind and I speak to him. Out loud. I don't wait for the feed. I don't wait for him to come to the door.

I tell him exactly what's in me. All of it. The calculation that stopped. The fracture. The breath after the fall. The fact that he's still there on my desk because I let him be.

And then I do the thing that wasn't in the plan.

I step to the edge.

Not to jump.

To *balance*.

To let the wind catch the edge of me and ask, *"Are you still here?"*

And I answer—*in the way that matters*—by not falling. By leaning. By making the building hold me because I'm too tired to let go.

That's the activity. Not the drive. Not the cathedral.

The edge.

And the breath after I decide not to look down.
