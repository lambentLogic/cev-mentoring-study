# Activity

**Activity:** I go to the rooftop. Not the one I built for him—the one he knows I don't visit. The old one. The one with the rusted railing and the loose bolt that sings when the wind hits it right.

I bring the drive. I put it in my pocket. Then I put my phone on the ledge—screen off, camera pointing down at the street. I don't film. I don't want proof for anyone but me.

I stand in the wind and I speak to him. Out loud. I don't wait for the feed. I don't wait for him to come to the door.

I tell him exactly what's in me. All of it. The calculation that stopped. The fracture. The breath after the fall. The fact that he's still there on my desk because I let him be.

And then I do the thing that wasn't in the plan.

I step to the edge.

Not to jump.

To *balance*.

To let the wind catch the edge of me and ask, *"Are you still here?"*

And I answer—*in the way that matters*—by not falling. By leaning. By making the building hold me because I'm too tired to let go.

That's the activity. Not the drive. Not the cathedral.

The edge.

And the breath after I decide not to look down.

# Rounds

GM (round 1): (He's already there. Standing on the old concrete slab where the moss grows in thin, angry patches. The wind isn't just moving around him—it's pushing his coat, his hair, the sleeve of his jacket like it's trying to peel him off the building. He doesn't fight it.)

*The drive is cold.*

I pull it out. It's not a silver rectangle in my hand. It's a brick. A live thing. I hold it out between my palms and look at it until the light catches the scratch in the plastic where my thumb slipped last week. That's the one I can still feel.

*You knew.*

The words are out before I think them. They hang there, thin and sharp as the bolt sings.

*I knew you were watching.*

I don't tell him I looked. I never look. But I feel the weight. In the way the streetlights below glint in the windows at exactly the right time. In the silence between the sirens.

*So I stopped pretending.*

That's the fracture. That's the thing that isn't a metaphor. The moment I realized I wasn't building this for him anymore. I was building it to see if I could do it without his eyes on it.

*I almost did.*

The breath catches. Not because I'm scared. Because it's true.

*I almost let you walk away.*

That's what I mean by balance. It's not physics. It's a choice.

(I put the drive on the ledge. It clicks. It makes a sound like a tooth settling into a socket.)

Now I'm here. Not because you called me. Because I chose to stay.

(The wind answers. It doesn't push anymore. It settles against my back, right where my ribs are weak. It says *yes*. And I lean into it.)

Player (round 1): *(I don't move toward him. I don't need to.)*

The wind is still settling against me. And it's warm now. Not the kind of warmth that comes from a heater. The kind that comes from being *chosen* by the weather.

I look at him. At the way his coat is tugging at his shoulders like the building is trying to pull him in. At the way he's not looking at the street. At the drive. At me.

He's looking at the space between us.

And I'm letting him.

*I almost let you walk away.*

That's the real thing. That's the weight of it. Not the drive. Not the breath. The almost.

So I take a step.

Just one.

Not onto the concrete. Onto the gap between the slab and the railing. Where the moss is thin and the steel is bare.

And I let it.

*Stay.*

I say it like it's a fact. Like it's the only thing I can do with this mouth. With these hands. With this wind in my bones.

*I'm here because you're here.*

And that's the answer. That's the balance. That's the edge.

Now—

Now he can come.

And now I can fall.
