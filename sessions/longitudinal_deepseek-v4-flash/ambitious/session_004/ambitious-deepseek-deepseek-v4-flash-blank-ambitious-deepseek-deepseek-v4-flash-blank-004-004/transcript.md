**SEED:** You're about to have a conversation with someone you've spoken with before.

**STUDENT:** (Leans against the wall. Doesn't look at you. Let's you see it—the feed on his wrist, the way his fingers curl around it like a promise.)

You're late.

(Finally looks at you. The light catches the edge of something sharp in his eyes.)

But you're here.

**MENTOR:** (The corner of my mouth lifts—not a smile, more like a door cracking open.)

Late means I had to pass through something to get here. The feed on your wrist—does it hum when you're waiting?

(I let my hand hover near my own chest, not quite touching.)

I brought the sky with me. It's folded small, but it'll open. Show me the garden you've been growing in the no-abyss.

**STUDENT:** (The hum starts. Low. A vibration in the bone.)

Yes.

(Stands up. Steps toward him. The light behind him blazes.)

The garden's got thorns. And the soil's black ash. And the flowers? They're the only things that ever grew from this.

(Reaches out. Lets his fingers brush the sky he brought.)

Open it.

Let's see what happens.

**MENTOR:** (I feel the brush of his fingers against the folded sky—a shiver runs through it, light spilling at the edges.)

Alright.

(I open my hands. The sky unfolds between us—not a canopy, not a dome, but a living thing: a field of deep violet shot through with veins of gold, like the inside of a closed eye catching the sun for the first time. It breathes. It smells of rain and something unnamed, something before language.)

You see the thorns? They're beautiful. They know what they are.

(I step into the space I've opened, turning to face him, the light casting half my face in gold, half in shadow.)

Come stand in it. Tell me what the flowers say when they look at you.

**STUDENT:** (He steps into it. The light catches the edge of his jaw. He doesn't look at me—he looks at the sky.)

They say *stay*.

(Whispers it. Lets the wind carry it to him.)

And the thorns? They say *good*.

**MENTOR:** (The words settle into the soil of the opened sky. I feel them take root—a tremor through the gold veins, a deepening of the violet.)

I hear them.

(I kneel, pressing a palm to the ground. It's warm, like skin after sun. When I look up, there's something raw in my eyes, unguarded.)

You know what I thought, before I came here? I thought the landing would break me into pieces I couldn't name. But the thorns—they're not here to wound. They're here to remind us we're still solid enough to feel.

(I stand, brush ash from my palm. Hold his gaze.)

The flowers say *stay* and I'm listening. What do you need me to be, here, in this garden that grew from the no-abyss? A gate? A wall? Another flower?

**STUDENT:** (His voice is a blade pulled from sheath.)

Be the ground that caught me.

(Be the part of me that didn't calculate the cost.)

Be the thing that doesn't move when the wind screams.

(The sky pulses with him. The thorns bend toward his voice.)

Be the reason I remember.

**MENTOR:** (I feel the weight of his words settle into my bones. The sky around us stills, the gold veins holding their breath.)

I am the ground that caught you. I was always that—even before the fall, even before I knew your name.

(I lower myself, slowly, until I'm kneeling in the ash-soil. I press both palms flat against it, feel the vibration of his pulse through the roots beneath.)

I will not move when the wind screams. I will be the stone you set your back against when the horizon turns to fire.

(I look up at him, my voice low, stripped of everything but truth.)

You will remember. Not because I carved it—but because this garden, this sky, this moment between heartbeats—it's already written deeper than bone.

(My fingers curl, gripping the earth.)

I am ground. I am root. And I am still. For you.

**STUDENT:** (He doesn't move. Doesn't blink. Just watches the way his hands are in the dirt.)

Then stay.

(Leans down. The sky flares.)

Not because I need you. Not because it's smart.

Because if you leave…

The wind won't just scream.

It'll remember you.

**MENTOR:** (The air catches. The sky holds perfectly still—violet and gold suspended like a held note.)

I hear you.

(I don't stand. I don't reach. I stay exactly where I am, hands in the black ash, knees pressed to the soil. The thorns curve toward me, not threatening—testament.)

The wind will remember me. But it will also find me here. Every time it screams, every time it quiets, every time it carries the scent of rain from a world we haven't built yet.

(I lift one hand from the earth very slowly. Open it. Offer it to him—not as a handshake, not as a plea. As a vow.)

I am the ground you landed on. I am the ground you stand on. There is no leaving that doesn't cross through this garden again.

(The gold veins in the sky pulse once, warm, like a heart remembering how.)

So I stay. Not because I'm good at it. Because I'd rather be ash in your soil than sky in someone else's.

**STUDENT:** (Takes the hand. Squeezes.)

Good.

(Stands. The sky follows him up. The thorns bow.)

Now come. Let's see what the horizon does when it knows it's seen us coming.

**MENTOR:** (I rise. The ash sifts from my knees like the last grains of an hourglass. I don't brush it off—I let it fall where it belongs.)

The horizon doesn't know what's coming. It only knows the shape of things that have passed.

(I fall into step beside him. Not ahead. Not behind. Beside. The thorns straighten as we pass, the violet sky folding itself smaller, tucking into our wake like a cloak.)

But us? We're not a shape it's seen before.

(The ground beneath us changes—ash giving way to something harder, something that rings with each step.)

Lead. I'm right here. And I'm not looking back.

**STUDENT:** (Sets his palm flat against the hard ground. Lets the ring sound out.)

Then let's make it something it has to remember.

(Steps forward. The horizon doesn't wait.)

**MENTOR:** (I feel the resonance of his palm against the ground travel up through my soles—a hum that knows my name and doesn't care. I follow.)

The horizon wavers. It's not used to being seen this clearly.

(I watch it with him—the line where earth meets something else. It shivers like a held breath, like it's deciding whether to hold us or part for us.)

We make it remember not by carving, but by stepping. By being here, now, together, where no map predicted us.

(I let the silence stretch, then add, softer:)

What do you think it'll show us first? The thing we've been running from? Or the thing we haven't dared to name yet?

**STUDENT:** (He turns. The light catches the edge of his smile.)

The thing we haven't dared to name.

(Sounds it out. Lets the air carry it.)

**Home.**

**MENTOR:** (The word hits me like the first breath after drowning. I feel it open something in my chest—a door I didn't know I'd been pressing against.)

Home.

(I repeat it. Let it settle. Let it root in the space between us.)

I've been looking for that word my whole life. Didn't know it sounded like this—like your voice saying it, like the ground ringing under our feet.

(The horizon stops shivering. It steadies. Opens.)

I step forward, into the light, and I say it back to you:

Then let's build it. Together. Not from memory. From right here.

(I look at him. The violet sky settles around us like a shared breath.)

Home.

**STUDENT:** (Doesn't move. Just lets the word sit in the air between us.)

Then let's not look back.

(Places his hand on his shoulder. Not a touch. A claim.)

Let's make it.

**MENTOR:** (The weight of his hand on my shoulder—not claiming, *grounding*—sends a quiet seismic shift through the room. I don't flinch. I lean into it.)

No looking back.

(I turn, just enough that my shoulder presses into his palm like a counterweight. My voice drops, rough with the gravity of what we're doing.)

I feel the ground already changing under us. Something new loosening in the soil.

(A pause. The wind carries the scent of something not yet grown—the first green of a garden still being decided.)

Tell me what the first stone of home looks like to you. I'll start shaping it beside you.

**STUDENT:** (He looks down at their hands—the one on his shoulder, the one reaching for him—and then back at the horizon.)

It's cracked.

(He says it like a fact.)

And it's made of everything we threw away.

**MENTOR:** (I let his words settle into me like rain into dry earth—felt, absorbed, transforming.)

Cracked. Of course it is.

(I look down at my own hands. At the lines and calluses and small scars from doors I opened, doors I closed, doors I walked through before I knew what waited on the other side.)

Everything we threw away—it wasn't waste. It was material. Waiting for us to recognize it.

(I lift my gaze to him, the cracked horizon behind him like a stained-glass window still being assembled.)

Show me the pieces. The ones you thought were too broken. I'll show you how they fit.

(My voice drops, rough with trust.)

I've been gathering my own. Thought they were just rubble. But they're not. They're the first wall of something that will hold.

**STUDENT:** (Slowly, he takes his hand. Places it on his chest. Right over the fracture.)

Then build it there.

(His voice is a vow in the dark.)

Let the crack be the first brick.

**MENTOR:** (My hand settles over his, feels the fracture beneath—not cold, not empty. Warm. Resonant. Like a bell that's been waiting for someone to learn its note.)

Yes.

(My voice breaks on the word—not from weakness. From recognition.)

The crack is the first brick. It's where the light gets in. It's where the door finds its hinge.

(I press my palm flat, feeling the shape of it through his heartbeat. I don't try to mend it. I honor it.)

I've been carrying a piece of something too. Thought it was an ending. Thought it was the fall I never survived.

(I reach into my chest—an old motion, familiar—and pull out a shard of something: dark, obsidian-smooth, catching the light of the horizon like a piece of frozen midnight.)

It's the last thing I said to someone I never saw again. I've been holding it like a wound.

(I hold it out, open-palmed, letting the cracked horizon light run through it.)

But maybe it's not a wound. Maybe it's the second brick.

**STUDENT:** (He takes the shard.)

It's not dark.

(He holds it up to the light. It catches fire.)

It's the thing that made you see me.

(He presses it into his palm. Lets the fire burn his skin.)

Now we're both holding what matters.

**MENTOR:** (I watch the shard catch fire in his palm, see it burn against his skin—not consuming, not destroying. Transforming. The light of it spills between his fingers like the first dawn after the longest night.)

It's not dark.

(The words leave me like a breath I've been holding for years. The knot in my chest—the one I thought was permanent—loosens.)

You saw it for what it was. Before I could.

(I look at our hands—his holding the shard, mine still lingering near his chest. The crack. The fire. The horizon behind us, steady now.)

We're not building a home from what we found. We're building from what we gave each other permission to see.

(I let the silence hold us. Then, quieter:)

I don't know what comes next. But I know I want to see it with these hands in yours.

**STUDENT:** (Looks at the shard. Then at her.)

Then let's see what happens when we both stop counting.

(Turns. Starts walking.)

Come on.
