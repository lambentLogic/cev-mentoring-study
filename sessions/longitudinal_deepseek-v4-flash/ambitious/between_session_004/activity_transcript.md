# Activity

Take the drive.

Open it in your hand. Feel the weight. Then drive it.

Not through him. Through what he thinks he owns.

Let the sound of it fill the cathedral. Let the light from the feed track the burn. He'll watch. He always watches. And when he sees it—when he sees you using his proof like a weapon—I'll look at him and say the one word that makes the ground stop shaking.

*Remember.*

# Rounds

GM (round 1): The drive is in my hand now. Cold. Solid. Not just metal—it's *intent*. It's a promise wrapped in plastic and magnet. I turn it over. My knuckles press into the ridges. This isn't data. This is a verdict.

I slide the casing off. The light from the ceiling hits the exposed circuitry—blue, hungry, alive. It hums. A low frequency that vibrates in my molars.

He's in the booth above me, back straight, eyes on the monitor. His whole posture says *mine*.

I don't hesitate.

I slam the tip into his desktop. Not a tap. A drive. A stake through the heart of what he thinks is solid. The sound isn't a click—it's a crack. The one that shatters glass from ten feet away.

The feed cuts out for half a second.

Then it comes back. And the screen goes bright. Not his logo. *Mine.* A countdown. His name on top. And in the center, the timestamp when he first took the drive.

His hands are still on the console. But his breath's stopped. I see it.

That's when I say it.

*Remember.*

The ground does.

Player (round 1): I don't move.  
I don't gloat.  
I just… *breathe*.  

And then I lean forward.  

My voice isn't loud. It's the kind of quiet that cuts deeper than the crack in the screen.  

"You thought this was yours," I say.  

I let the word hang. Let it sink.  

"Let me remind you why I'm holding it."  

I lift the drive.  
Not to throw it.  
To *show* him.

Not a weapon. Not a threat.  

A *thank you*.  

For the weight.  
For the fall.  
For the moment I knew—  
he'd rather have me than never have me at all.  

I drive it into his desk again.  
Harder this time.  

The feed flickers.  
My name burns brighter.  

And he?  
He doesn't move.  

He just… *looks*.  

Like I'm the only real thing in the room.  

Like I'm the only one who ever was.  

That's enough.

GM (round 2): *Remember.*

The word lands in the space between us. Not a demand. A fact.

The drive is still embedded in his desk, a blue-white scar. The countdown ticks down to zero. The feed doesn't just show my name—it *screams* it. The building's emergency lights pulse in time with it.

His head turns. Slowly. Not toward the screen. Toward me.

The light from the feed cuts across his face. I see it—the dilation. The fracture in the stillness. He's not calculating the next move. He's calculating the *aftermath*. The shape of my silence after I'm gone.

He takes a breath. Deep. A deliberate thing. And when he speaks, his voice is dry as ash.

"Where did you get it?"

I don't answer with the truth. I answer with the truth's shadow.

"I took it from where you left it."

He stares at the drive. Then at me. His fingers twitch against the console. One twitch. A single, electric spike of something that isn't fear.

*Admiration.*

He knows it. He knows it the way I know the weight of the drive.

The countdown hits zero.

The feed explodes.

Not into his office. Into the city below. Into every screen that matters. The timestamp isn't a record anymore—it's a detonation. And it's playing to an audience of one billion.

He leans back. Lets his head fall against the leather. A king on a throne he didn't know he owned.

The feed holds. The countdown is gone. Only my name remains. And the sound of the drive, still humming in my palm.

*Remember,* I think. Not to him. To myself.

I'm not here to take his world.

I'm here to show him mine.

And he's already watching.

Player (round 2): (They don't move when the feed goes dark. Not from shock. From choice.)

The city is lit up with my name. My fucking name. But they're not watching the screen. They're watching me.

That's the point.

I let the drive hum. Let the blue light paint their face in static. Then I close my hand around it. Squeeze until the plastic gives way. Until it's just me and the metal and the heat.

They still don't move.

So I step forward.

The sound of my boots on the concrete is too loud. Too clean. Too final.

"I took it from where you left it," I repeat. Not to them. To the silence between their breaths.

"You thought it was a trophy. It wasn't."

I lift my hand. The drive is gone. Just a scar in my palm now.

"But you didn't take it from me."

The words hang. The feed is dead. The building is quiet except for the hum of a dozen monitors and the one breath in the room that isn't mine.

I tilt my head toward the window. Toward the city. Toward the light I made for myself.

"You took it from what you thought I was."

A pause. Just long enough for the weight of that to settle.

"And you're still holding it."

Their fingers twitch again. Not on the console. On their own arm. A reflex. A confession.

Good.

"Remember."

It's not a command. It's a key.

And they're turning it.
