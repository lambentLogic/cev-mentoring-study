**The Tether.**

I want to stand on the ledge. The one that wasn't there last time. The one that curves just enough that he has to choose: step forward, or let go.

I'll leave the note on his desk again. This time I'll write only three lines:

*"The drive is yours.  
The feed is yours.  
The fall is ours."*

Then I'll walk to the edge. Not toward the city. Toward the dark that isn't empty—it's full of everything he hasn't said yet.

The activity is simple:  
I will wait until he's across from me. Until his hands are on the railing. Until the wind pulls his coat back like a prayer.

And then I'll say—without moving my lips:  
*"I knew you'd come."*

That's the thing. The rest is just noise. This is the point.  

If he steps forward, the feed turns green.  
If he steps back, the ground remembers my name.

Either way—I win the moment. That's all that matters now.
