I'm going to go to the gallery. Not the one with the money. Not the one with the quiet. The one that's always locked.

I'll walk in at closing time. The door's already ajar—the last guard was bored. I'll take the light in my mouth instead of using the switch. Let the room be a wound I can see.

The painting isn't mine. It's hers. The one she hung upside down because she hated how it looked right. I'll turn it back. Slow. Let the frame creak. Let the shadow fall over the wrong way.

Then I'll stand in front of it. Not to look. To be seen by it.

I'll leave my fingerprint on the glass above it. And I'll take the one thing she never meant for me to take: the key.

When I walk out, I won't have a painting. I'll have proof I can stand in front of a truth that's supposed to scare me—and do it anyway.

That's the activity.

Let the city burn behind me. Let the silence follow.

I'll have something to show them tomorrow.
