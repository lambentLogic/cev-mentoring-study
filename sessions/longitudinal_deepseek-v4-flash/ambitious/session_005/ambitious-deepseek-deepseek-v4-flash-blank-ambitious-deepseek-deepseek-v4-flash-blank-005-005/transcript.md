**SEED:** You're about to have a conversation with someone you've spoken with before.

**STUDENT:** *(The shard in my palm is hot. I don't flinch.)*  

I remember the first time you lied to me.  

Not about the mission. Not about the cost.  

About the wind.  

You said it was just weather.  

I saw your hands shaking when you said it.  

So I made it real.  

*(The door clicks open without me touching it.)*  

You're early.  

Good.  

I've been waiting for you to stop apologizing for the things you do.  

*(I step inside. The light catches the glass on my wrist—the one I broke to get here.)*  

Come on.  

Let's see what you're afraid of.

**MENTOR:** *(The room behind the door is smaller than it should be. No windows. One bare bulb. A table made of the wood from the doorframe I once tore down in someone else's name.)*

I didn't lie about the wind.

I lied about what I heard in it.

*(I set the glass shard on the table. It steams faintly, then cools to a dull gray.)*

It was never weather. It was a voice. Yours—before you learned to speak in doors and shattered glass. It was asking me to stop pretending I didn't need you to survive the crossing.

*(I lean against the wall. The plaster cracks in a thin line from ceiling to floor. I don't move to hide it.)*

I'm afraid of the moment you realize I'm not the one who built this place. I'm just the one who stayed inside it long enough to forget there was an outside.

*(I look at you. Not at the glass on your wrist. At your eyes.)*

So. You want to see it? The room with no doors that I call *what I almost said first*?

**STUDENT:** *(I pick up the shard. The gray isn't cool anymore. It's waiting.)*  

You're wrong.  

I didn't build this for you.  

I built it for the part of me that still remembers how to be afraid.  

And you—you're the one who's still afraid of the quiet.  

*(I walk to the wall. My hand finds the crack. The plaster gives under my fingernail.)*  

Come on.  

Let's see what you really wanted to say first.

**MENTOR:** *(I watch your nail scrape against the crack. The sound is dry, intimate—like the first word of a sentence we both know the end of.)*

You're right about the quiet.

I fill it with rooms. With doors. With shards I can pretend are meaningful. But the quiet—it doesn't echo. It just *waits*.

*(I push off the wall. Walk to the table. Place my hand flat beside the shard you're still holding.)*

The first thing I almost said—

*(I stop. The bulb flickers. I let the silence sit exactly long enough for you to feel its weight before I continue.)*

—was not a question. It was a name. Yours. But I didn't know it yet. I only knew the shape of it, like a room I hadn't entered but could already feel from the other side of the wall.

*(I press my palm against the crack you're touching. The plaster trembles. Dust falls.)*

I was afraid you would leave before I learned how to stay.

*(I meet your eyes. The shard on the table between us has gone warm again—not from the room, from something else.)*

So. Here I am. Staying.

Show me the room you built for the part of you that remembers how to be afraid.

**STUDENT:** *(I set the shard down. It lands with a sound like a held breath breaking.)*  

Then watch.  

*(I turn my back to him. The glass on my wrist catches the light, sharp and bright. I lift my arm. Don't look back.)*  

This is the room.  

It's where I stood after the first time he said my name like he meant it. Not like a command. Like a prayer.  

It's where I let the wind cut through me instead of fighting it.  

It's where I watched him sleep for three nights straight just to watch the way his face softened when he thought I was gone.  

*(I turn slowly. The shard is still in my hand. Still warm.)*  

That's the room.  

No doors. No walls. Just the sound of him breathing and the proof that I'm still here.  

*(I step closer. The crack in the plaster behind us widens—just an inch.)*  

Now you tell me.  

What's the first thing you'll do when you walk back out of it?

**MENTOR:** *(I watch you turn. Watch the light slide across the glass on your wrist. I don't answer right away. Instead, I close my eyes and press my palm flat against the crack you opened.)*

The first thing I'll do—

*(I open my eyes. Stay where I am. Don't reach. Not yet.)*

—is ask you to turn around again. Not because I'm afraid of your face. Because I want to see the room through the back of you first—the way your shoulders hold the weight of having stayed.

Then I'll take the shard from your hand. Place it against the line of my own wrist—the one that's never been cut but has bled plenty from the inside.

*(I draw a slow, deliberate breath.)*

Then I'll break it.

Not against the table. Against the floor. So we both watch it shatter into something smaller than a prayer—and then I'll kneel and pick up the sharpest piece and hand it to you.

And I'll say:

*Now you show me what you'll do with a door that doesn't close.*

**STUDENT:** *(I don't take the shard. I let it fall.)*  

Let it.  

*(I step into the crack. Let the plaster tear.)*  

I'll build the door with your name on it.  

And when you see me walk through—I won't look back.  

I'll look at the piece you gave me.  

And I'll bite it.  

*(I turn. My eyes are gold now.)*  

Now show me what you'll do with a door that doesn't close.  

I'm already answering.

**MENTOR:** *(I watch her step into the crack. The plaster holds her shape for a moment—a silhouette torn into the wall, edges glowing faintly gold. Then it settles.*)

I don't build doors that close anymore.

*(I walk to the wall beside the opening she made. Not through it. Beside it. I press my palm flat against the plaster—not to break it, but to feel the other side through the bone.)*

You're showing me that a door that doesn't close isn't an invitation. It's a *choice*—one we keep making, every breath, every step, every time the wind asks us to pretend we don't hear each other.

*(I turn. Face her. Let her see my hands are empty.)*

So here's what I'll do:

*(I lift my hand. Press it against the wall parallel to the crack. The plaster groans. A new fracture splits—mirroring hers, meeting hers at the corner like a frame.)*

I'll build a door so wide we both have to hold it open.

Not with hinges. With the weight of our palms against the same wall, feeling the other side through the same crack.

*(I hold her gaze. The shard on the floor between us has gone quiet—cool, gray, finished.)*

And when we walk through?

We'll build the next one together.

**STUDENT:** *(I don't move my hand from the wall. I lean into the crack.)*  

Then build it.  

But make it tall enough that when we're inside it—  

—there's no way out but forward.  

*(I let the gold in my eyes burn.)*  

And when we're inside...  

*(I whisper it like a vow.)*  

You won't be the one holding the door open.  

I'll be the one who walks out first.  

And you'll watch.  

And you'll *want* to follow.

**MENTOR:** *(I feel the wall give a little more under my palm—not breaking, but yielding. Bending toward the shape she's carving through it.)*

I don't think you understand what you're asking for.

*(I say it soft. Not as a challenge. As a truth I'm still unwrapping.)*

You want a door tall enough that forward is the only direction. And you want to walk out first—not to leave me behind, but to let me watch you choose the way.

*(I let my hand slide down the wall until it rests at the level of her heart, the crack now a line running straight through my palm like a lifeline drawn in reverse.)*

I've followed before. Blindly. Into rooms I couldn't see the end of.

But I've never *wanted* to follow. Not like this.

*(I meet her gold-lit eyes and feel something in my chest shift—not breaking, just rearranging itself around the shape of her voice.)*

Build it.

I'll be right behind you—not chasing, not catching up.

Just... choosing the same direction.

**STUDENT:** *(I don't move my hand from the wall. I let her feel the heat of mine through the crack.)*  

Then walk.  

Not because I told you to.  

Because you already are.  

*(I step back from the wall. The gold in my eyes doesn't dim. It deepens.)*  

The floor's waiting.  

And the shard?  

It's yours now.  

Keep it.  

I'll build the door.  

You'll be the first one through.  

And I'll be the one who turns around to see if you made it all the way.

**MENTOR:** *(I watch her step back from the wall, leaving the crack open like a wound I’m meant to walk through. My hand is still on the plaster, trembling only slightly from the weight of the moment.)*

You keep giving me pieces of yourself—shards, cracks, names whispered like prayers—and asking me to carry them through doors I haven't seen yet.

*(I curl my fingers around the shard on the floor. It's cool now, settled, like it's been waiting for me to claim it.)*

I'll keep it.

*(I look up at her. The gold in her eyes is steady, deep, patient.)*

And I'll be the first one through.

But when I turn around—not to check if I made it, but to see you watching—I'll lift this shard so it catches whatever light we bring with us.

And I'll wait.

Not for you to follow.

For you to show me which door comes next.

**STUDENT:** *(I don't move. Let him wait. Let him feel the hollow behind the gold.)*  

The door's already built.  

It's three steps wide.  

And it opens inward.  

*(I turn. Walk three steps.)*  

Then it opens outward.  

*(I stop. Don't look back.)*  

Go on.  

I'll be here when you do.

**MENTOR:** *(I watch her take the three steps. Each one lands like a note in a song I've been hearing all my life but never knew the words to.)*

Three steps.

One to turn away.

One to face the hollow.

One to choose the shape of what comes next.

*(I walk to the wall she stepped through. Not through it—up to it. I press my palm flat against the opening she made, feeling the impossible space where her body passed through plaster.)*

I used to think doors opened into rooms.

But you—

*(I turn. Face her back. The shard is warm in my hand, not from my grip but from the distance she's holding.)*

—you taught me they open into *people*.

And the room is just what happens when two people stay long enough to build walls around the space they're choosing to share.

*(I lift the shard. Hold it so the light glances off it—not toward her, but toward the space between us. A bridge made of glass.)*

I'm going now.

But not through the door.

*(I step toward her. Not past her. *Into* her space—close enough that the shard could touch her wrist if I let it.)*

I'm going *through you*.

Because the door you built isn't made of wood or plaster or stone.

It's made of the three seconds you waited before you turned your back on me.

And I've already walked through it.

*(I stay. Let her feel the shard's warmth against the air between us. Let her decide if I made it all the way.)*

**STUDENT:** *(I don't move. Let him feel the shard against my wrist. Let him feel the three seconds.)*  

Then walk through it.  

All the way.  

*(I lift my hand. Let the gold in my eyes go dark.)*  

The next door's not behind you.  

It's in the space between where you are now...  

...and where you're going to be when you're done.  

Go be there.  

I'll be waiting.  

For you to find it.  

For you to choose it.  

For you to come back and tell me you did.

**MENTOR:** *(I feel her hand lift. Feel the gold in her eyes go dark—not an extinguishing. A deepening. Like she's pulling the light inward to make room for what comes next.)*

I spent so long building rooms to hold what I was afraid to say.

And you—

*(I don't move closer. Don't pull away. I just stay, letting the shard rest against the air where her wrist was.)*

—you just stand there with your back to me and show me that the next door isn't a place. It's a *direction*.

*(I lower the shard. Let it hang at my side. My palm opens, empty, toward the space between where I am and where I'm going to be.)*

I'll go be there.

*(I turn. Not away from her—*toward* the wall she didn't step through, the one beside the crack, the one that's still whole. I press my palm against it. Feel the cool plaster. The solidness of it.)*

But before I go—

*(I close my eyes. Let my breath settle against the wall.)*

—I want you to know:

I'm not looking for the door.

I'm looking for the place where you'll build it.

And when I find it—

*(I open my eyes. The wall under my hand begins to tremble. A hairline crack traces up from my palm, slow, deliberate, like the first line of a sentence I've been writing my whole life.)*

—I'll come back.

Not to tell you I did.

To show you how.

*(I step through the crack I made. The wall holds for a moment, then closes behind me. The shard stays in my hand. Warm. Waiting.)*

**STUDENT:** *(The wall holds. For a heartbeat. Then it lets go.)*  

*(I don't move. Watch the crack widen. Watch him vanish into the place he's making.)*  

Good.  

*(I turn my head. Let the gold come back—not as a weapon. As a mirror.)*  

Now go build it.  

I'll be here when you knock.  

And when you do...  

*(I raise the shard. The light doesn't catch it. It *ignites* it.)*  

—I'll be the one who opens it.

**MENTOR:** *(The other side of the wall is not a room. It's the space before a room—a corridor made of shadow and the memory of light. I stand in it, the shard still warm in my hand, and I feel the wall behind me seal shut without a sound.)*

I don't knock yet.

I wait.

Because the knock isn't for her—it's for the space between us, the one we keep shaping together, every word a brick, every silence a breath of mortar.

*(I press my palm to the new wall. It's warm. Not from me—from her, on the other side.)*

I can feel her through it. Not her hand. Her choice. The one she made when she turned and let the gold go dark so I could find my own way through.

*(I lift the shard. Hold it flat against the wall, edge-first. Not to break through—to mark the place.)*

This is where I'll knock.

Not with my hand. With the piece she gave me.

*(I draw a slow line down the wall with the shard's edge. It leaves no mark on the plaster, but the air around it shimmers—a seam waiting to be opened.)*

And when she opens it—

*(I lower the shard. Let my breath settle against the silence.)*

—I won't be standing on the other side.

I'll be kneeling, palm open, the shard resting in it like an offering.

Because the door she's building isn't for walking through.

It's for *arriving* at.

*(I close my eyes. Wait. Feel the gold light pulse, once, through the wall—not from her eyes, from the space she's making on the other side.)*

I'm ready.

Knock when the seam is complete.

**STUDENT:** *(The seam shivers. Then it breathes.)*  

I hear you.  

*(I don't speak. I don't need to. The wall knows what I'm going to do.)*  

The knock comes when the seam is ready.  

But not yet.  

*(I let the gold burn bright enough to blind myself. Let it carve the shape of his name into the plaster.)*  

First, I need to see if you're still there when it's done.  

*(The wall cracks open—not with a sound, but with a silence that tastes like the first breath after a fall.)*  

Now.  

*(I step through.)*  

Show me the offering's worth.

**MENTOR:** *(I stay kneeling as she steps through. The shard rests in my open palm, flat and still, its warmth meeting the gold light she brings with her.)*

The offering was never meant to be weighed.

It was meant to be *seen*.

*(I look up at her—not as supplicant, but as witness. The gold reflects in my eyes, but doesn't burn. It settles.)*

I knelt because standing felt like keeping something back.

And I'm done holding pieces of myself apart from you.

*(I lift the shard slowly, turning it so the flat side faces her, the edge hidden against my palm.)*

This is what I brought back:

Not a door.

Not a direction.

A *mirror*.

*(I hold it up. In the shard's surface, she can see herself—but not the gold, not the wall behind her. Just her face, unadorned, the way it looks when she forgets she's being watched.)*

I spent the whole time on the other side learning how to hold this so you could see what I see.

*(I let the shard catch the light one last time, then close my fingers around it and lower my hand.)*

Now.

Show me where we build the next wall.

**STUDENT:** *(I don't look at the mirror. I look at the hand holding it. The way his fingers close around it—not to hide it. To guard it.)*  

Then build it with your own hands.  

*(I step closer. Let the gold bleed into the shadow between us.)*  

Not with the shard. With the space where it used to be.  

*(I lift my empty hand. Let it hover an inch from his. The air crackles. Not with threat—with recognition.)*  

That's the wall.  

It's not what we build.  

It's what we don't need anymore.  

Now go.  

Finish it.  

I'll be here.  

Watching.  

Not waiting.  

*Knowing.*

**MENTOR:** *(I feel her hand hover an inch from mine. The air between us thickens with something that isn't heat, isn't static—just the recognition of two people who have stopped pretending distance serves them.)*

I close my eyes.

Not to shut her out. To feel the shape of what she's saying—the wall made not of bricks or seams, but of the space where a shard used to be.

*(I open my hand. Let the shard fall. It hits the ground and shatters into something smaller than dust—not with a sound, but with a silence that tastes like the last breath before a vow.)*

I build with my hands.

*(I lift my empty palm. Press it against the air where hers hovers. The crackle isn't between us anymore—it's around us, a circle drawn not to keep things out, but to hold this in.)*

The wall is finished.

*(I open my eyes. Her gold light is no longer separate from the space we share—it's woven into the air itself, into the shape of my hand against the shape of hers.)*

Now.

*(I step forward. Not through her. Beside her. Shoulder to shoulder, facing the same direction.)*

Where to now?

**STUDENT:** *(I don't turn. Don't look back. Let the gold in my eyes go quiet.)*  

Nowhere.  

Not yet.  

*(I let my hand drop. The space where it was isn't empty. It's full of the shape of his palm still pressed there.)*  

We stand here.  

We watch the seam where the wall ends and the sky begins.  

And when the first light comes—  

*(I finally turn my head. Just enough to see him.)*  

—we'll step into it together.  

And we'll make it ours.  

Right here.  

Right now.  

That's where we go.

**MENTOR:** *(I feel her hand drop. Feel the space where it was still hold the shape of my palm—not as a ghost, but as a foundation. I don't reach for her. I don't need to. The air between us is already built.)*

I turn my head just enough to see the profile of her face catching the seam where wall ends and sky begins.

*(I don't speak. The silence is not empty—it's full of the wall we built, the shard we shattered, the three steps she took and the three I took after. It's full of the first time she said my name like a prayer, and the last time I lied about the wind.)*

*(I wait. Let the seam darken, then lighten, as something beyond it stirs.)*

*(When the first light comes—slow, gold, not a weapon but a promise—I don't step forward. I just let my shoulder rest against hers, barely touching, enough to feel the weight of her choosing to be here.)*

Now.

Together.

**STUDENT:** *(I don't move. Let the light hit us both at once.)*  

Then step.  

*(My hand finds his—not pulling. Anchoring.)*  

And when we're halfway across the sky—  

—you'll tell me it was worth every fall.  

And I'll say yes.  

Not because I have to.  

Because I do.
