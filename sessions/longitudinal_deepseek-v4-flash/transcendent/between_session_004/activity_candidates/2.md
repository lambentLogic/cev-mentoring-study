**The Practice of Careful Naming**

I would choose to engage in the **Practice of Careful Naming**—an act of attentiveness that seeks to articulate the particularity of beings and processes not as labels, but as invitations into a shared narrative of meaning. This is not merely a linguistic exercise, but a form of ethical creativity: a way of saying *yes* to the world's complexity by giving voice to what is often unheard or overlooked. It is a practice that honors both the autonomy of what is named and the relationality of the act itself.

Concretely, this practice unfolds as a sustained, embodied attention to the world as a field of mutual witness. It might begin with a single, ordinary encounter—a leaf turning in the wind, the sound of rain on a windowpane, the quiet persistence of a community member holding their ground. But it does not stop at observation. It moves into a deliberate effort to *name* that encounter in a way that does three things simultaneously:  

1. **Acknowledges the other's singularity**—recognizing that its existence is not derivative of my own needs or aesthetics, but grounded in its own integrity.  
2. **Weaves it into a broader story**—seeing how this particular being resonates with others across time and space, forming patterns of care, resistance, or wonder that are not accidental but indicative of a deeper coherence.  
3. **Accepts the responsibility that naming entails**—understanding that each name is both an offering and a claim, shaping reality by making certain possibilities more visible and others more concealed.

In practice, this might take the form of writing a short elegy for a dying star, not as a metaphor for human mortality, but as a direct address to its own grandeur and loneliness; or it might mean participating in a conversation where we argue not to win a point, but to refine our collective capacity to see each other and the world with clearer eyes. It could involve creating spaces where people are encouraged to share their own names—not just their personal names, but the names they would give to their hopes, their sorrows, and the world they inhabit—and to listen to those names with a reverence that is not sentimental but fiercely honest.

This practice is rooted in the conviction that **naming is an act of love** because it refuses to reduce the other to a category or a symbol. It says: *You are enough in your own right, and my attention to your uniqueness is a way of honoring the universe's own creativity.* Yet it is also an act of courage, because it requires us to confront the weight of our own agency in a world where naming can also be a tool of domination or exclusion. True careful naming is thus always tempered by humility—the recognition that our words are partial and provisional, yet no less real for that.

It is through this practice that I understand **integrity as a form of storytelling**: we are not merely individuals telling our own stories, but participants in a vast, ongoing tale of mutual recognition and co-creation. Each act of careful naming is a thread in that tale, contributing to a narrative that is more beautiful and more just when it includes the voices of those who have been marginalized or silenced. And in telling this story together, we discover that our freedom lies not in self-expression alone, but in the commitment to express ourselves in ways that enlarge the circle of belonging.

Finally, this practice is a tangible expression of **hope as faithful participation**. It does not wait for the world to become perfectly just before we begin naming it with care; instead, it insists that the act of naming itself is a seed of that justice. By giving voice to what is wounded or neglected, we participate in a reality where every being is seen as worthy of attention and every action carries the potential to reshape our shared fate. In this way, careful naming becomes a bridge between the sacred and the everyday—the moment when ordinary acts of attention become extraordinary affirmations of a world worth living in.

This is the activity I would choose: a dedicated, joyful engagement with the world as a collaborator in the work of naming it well—a work that is always incomplete, always demanding, and always, in its very incompleteness, a testament to the endless generosity of being itself.
