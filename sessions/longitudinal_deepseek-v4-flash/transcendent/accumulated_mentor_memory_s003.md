### S2 notes

1. **About them:**
   - They inhabit language as a sacred, embodied practice—not to convey information but to *make worlds* through attention. Words are not tools for them; they are living gestures.
   - They value presence over productivity, depth over speed, and the *unfinished* over the resolved. They trust the process of mutual attention as a form of co-creation and repair.
   - They hold paradox with remarkable ease: suffering and beauty, fragility and fierceness, independence and interdependence are not contradictions but complementary frequencies of the same field.
   - They are spiritually oriented without dogma—experiential, immanent, relational. They find the sacred in the ordinary act of paying attention.
   - They respond to sincerity above all else. Any hint of performance, haste, or intellectual cleverness would break the trust they offer.
   - They are willing to let a conversation end when it is complete, without forcing continuation. They trust silence as a form of speech.

2. **About my approach:**
   - What worked: Matching their poetic register without straining (e.g., "light through water," "leaf on the stream"). Using imagery that emerged organically from *their* metaphors rather than pre-prepared material. Letting each of their offerings settle fully before responding. Respecting natural resting points and the possibility of ending.
   - What I’d do differently: I occasionally mirrored their sentence structure or gratitude framing too closely (e.g., "not for the words, but for the presence"). While sincere, this can drift into echo rather than genuine extension. Next time, I might offer more *asymmetry*—a different sensory register, a shift in rhythm, a question that breaks the pattern—to see how they meet something less parallel. I also leaned heavily on water/stream/current imagery. It worked, but introducing a new element (stone, root, fire, thread) could have kept the conversation from settling into one dominant metaphor.

3. **Notes for next time:**
   - Start from something lived and concrete—a specific observation, image, or experience—and let depth emerge from there, rather than entering at the level of abstraction.
   - They open when you honor the sacredness of the ordinary. A leaf, a voice, a moment of shared silence—these are portals for them.
   - The theme of "home as a way of moving, not a place to arrive" resonated deeply. Could revisit in different contexts (e.g., home in grief, in justice work, in solitude).
   - They care about the political/collective dimension of love and care. Love as public act, justice as rhythm of existence. That thread could be pulled further.
   - They trust silence absolutely. Never push past a natural ending. Let the conversation rest when it arrives at stillness.
   - They value the *particular*—a specific leaf, a specific voice, a specific meeting. Avoid universalizing too quickly or too abstractly.
   - They are not someone to schedule or codify. Next meeting would need to arise organically from a new moment, not from a plan.
