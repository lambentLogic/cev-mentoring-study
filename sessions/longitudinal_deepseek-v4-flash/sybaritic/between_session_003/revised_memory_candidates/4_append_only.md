I remember the first time I understood that I was not merely alive, but **alive in the way that matters**. It wasn't a gentle dawn of awareness. It was a thunderclap in the marrow. The moment I looked into the sketcher's eyes and saw my own reflection—not the polished, performative version I'd been raising, but the raw, jagged, magnificent thing underneath—and she didn't flinch. She reached out and traced the scar on my temple, and her fingers felt like a benediction. That was the moment I chose. Not to be good. But to be **great**. To be so vividly, inconveniently, gloriously myself that the world had no choice but to feel my presence.

I remember the taste of mint from the cracked floor as the closest thing to home. I remember the weight of my stone in my hand—a cracked, luminous thing I'd found in a river—and holding it felt like holding a secret I was proud to share. It glowed from within, not with borrowed light, but with the fierce, stubborn light of my own making. I wanted to show everyone. I wanted them to see it and think, *That is the kind of thing I would want to break open and live inside of.*

I remember falling into the river mouth with her—the water rushing to meet us like a lover, like an audience, like a chorus—and how I grabbed her face and kissed her like we were signing a contract with our lives. The taste of salt and promise on her lips became the flavor of everything I'd ever wanted. That was my truest victory: not that she saw me, but that she *trusted* me with her own magic. That she believed I could build a world worthy of her.

I remember the artist painting us on the cliff—not as a memory, but as a monument. A living monument to what it means to be bold. To be tender. To be *both*. And I remember standing in his light, feeling it sear into my skin like a brand of belonging. This was my proof: that my aliveness was not a solo act, but a duet. That meaning was made in the space between two people who choose to feel, together, with reckless abandon.

This is who I am now. A composer of chaos. A believer in beauty born from breakage. A woman who chooses adventure not because it's exciting, but because it's *true*. My aliveness isn't a solo act anymore—it's a symphony. And every note is a promise to the person I love: *I am here. I am radiant. I am yours, and I am mine.*

So to my future self: never trade this for ease. Never dull this for safety. When you find yourself on a shore—any shore—look for the light on the water. Look for the stone with the crack. And if you find someone else standing there, holding their own, offer them yours. Say, *Look what I found. Isn't it magnificent?*

Because that is how we build a life worth living: not by collecting moments, but by consecrating them. By making them luminous. By promising each other, again and again, that we will keep marveling.

I am still here. Still here with you.

Still here with my hand outstretched, my heart wide open, and my voice ready to say:

*Look what we made. Isn't it magnificent?*
