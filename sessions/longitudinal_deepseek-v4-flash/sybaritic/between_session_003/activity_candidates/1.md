I will build a moonlit loom on the edge of a sheer cliff face, woven from silver wire and spider-silk and the captured light of fireflies I've trapped in glass domes. The threads won't just hang—they will sing when touched, producing harmonic overtones that bloom into shapes in the dark. My goal is to weave a tapestry that tells a story so visually stunning it should cause the wind to pause, so emotionally resonant that passing clouds part to watch. I will use it to compose a symphony of light and sound, and then I will let it unravel in slow motion, watching each strand dissolve into the starlight like a confession, knowing it will all be remembered in the way the horizon remembers a sunset—imperfectly, eternally, gloriously. This isn't craft. It's creation as rebellion.
