I will build a room.

Not a bedroom, not a studio—a *sanctuary of the senses*. I will design it with the same fierce, unapologetic attention I would give a cathedral. The walls will be of reclaimed oak, their grain so deep I can trace the lives of trees in the rings with my knuckles. The floor will be cold river stone, smooth from a hundred years of water singing over it. I will pour a moat of glass around it—a shallow, shimmering channel where light refracts into a thousand rainbows that dance on the walls when the sun hits right.

The heart of the room is the fireplace. Not a simple hearth. A structure of blackened steel and wild copper coils that will glow like embers caught in a starfield. I will feed it not just wood, but dried lavender, crushed starfruit peel, and the occasional piece of charcoal that smells like the first smoke of summer. It must burn with a scent as specific as a person.

But the masterpiece is the ceiling. I will string hundreds of tiny, colored glass beads from it—blue for the sky on a clear morning, amber for the honey-lit afternoon, deep violet for twilight. They will be connected by fine wires that pulse with a soft, warm light from within, like captured fireflies. When the power goes out, they will glow faintly, and I will know the room is still alive.

And in the center of it all—a chair. A single, monumental chair of woven seagrass and river cane, shaped like a bird that never lands. It will be so perfectly, unbearably comfortable that sitting in it will feel like being cradled by the ocean and a storm at once. It is my throne of surrender.

This is my activity: to gather every beautiful, sharp, tender thing I know how to find and arrange them into a world where I can sit and *feel* everything. To watch the light change on those beads, to watch the smoke curl from the fire, to hear the city sounds muffled by the oak and replaced with the hum of the glass and the crackle of the copper. To make a place so breathtakingly alive that I could spend an entire year in it and still be hungry for more of its beauty.

This is my yes. This is my adventure. This is the thing I will build when I decide, with my whole soul, that I am worth the labor.
