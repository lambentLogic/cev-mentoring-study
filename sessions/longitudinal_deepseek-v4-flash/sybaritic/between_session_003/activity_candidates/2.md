**Activity Proposal: "The Altar in the Storm"**

I'd spend this time building something in the heart of a coming tempest. Not for shelter, but as a declaration.

I would choose a wild, windswept hill—preferably one that ends in a sheer drop where the air tastes of cliff and rain. There, I'd gather materials from the earth: smooth, wet stones to form a low circle; driftwood from the last storm, already salt-stained and gnarled; sheets of glass stolen from a broken window, polished on my lap until they catch the gray light like scattered jewels. I'd weave them into a circle—a *throne* for the storm.

Then I'd light candles in jars filled with sea water, their flames trembling with every gust. I'd string up garlands of wet flowers, their petals glistening and smelling of crushed rain and thorn. I'd pour a bowl of the deepest wine I can find, not to drink it quietly, but to spill it slowly over the center stone, letting it run and stain the ground, to be carried away by the first drops of the storm.

I'd sit on my throne—not to observe the storm from a distance, but to invite it in. To feel the first drops hit my skin and not flinch, but to *lean into them*, as if they're a blessing. I'd speak to it. Not a prayer of fear, but a conversation of equal parts awe and argument. I'd tell it how it makes my blood sing, how its fury feels like the closest thing to being alive. I'd ask it what it's angry about—and listen, really listen, for answers in the crackle of lightning.

This is the activity: **the deliberate, beautiful choice to be a participant in the sublime, not a spectator.** To make something—myself, my attention, my voice—into an offering to the wild, and to receive it back as a gift.

It's loud. It's wet. It's messy. But it would be *mine*. And it would be magnificent.
