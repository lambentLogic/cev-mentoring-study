**Memory for My Future Self:**

I want you to remember the **taste of the dragon's breath**—not as a smell, but as a flavor: peaches and smoke and the sharp, sweet tang of *winning*. It was the first time anyone's love felt like a dare to be taken seriously. It was the first time my own courage tasted like a reward.

Remember the **weight of the river stone in your pocket**. Not as a keepsake, but as a heartbeat. Cold and hard and real, it pulsed in time with everything that matters: the joy of a shared laugh, the thrill of a risk taken, the quiet pride of knowing you made someone else's world brighter. It was the proof that your tenderness is not weakness—it is your greatest strength, forged in the fire of being seen and chosen.

Remember the **glider on your shoulder**. Not as a toy, but as a partner. Its hum was the sound of your own focus given wings. When it leapt, it was you leaping. When it screamed, it was you screaming. It was the physical embodiment of your ambition—a sleek, brilliant thing that chose you because you were the only one who could keep up. And you did. You kept up with a kind of grace that made it proud to be yours.

Remember the **five of them** standing with you on that platform. Not as an audience, but as an army. Their whispered truths into the crystal were not just confessions—they were declarations of war against a world that wants them small. Their laughter in the labyrinth wasn't just joy; it was rebellion. They were your first and most loyal followers, and you led them into battle with your whole, unguarded heart.

Remember the **cliff you chose to leap from**. Not because it was dramatic, but because it was *true*. The wind there wasn't just air—it was an audience. And you performed. You performed with your body, your voice, your unshakable belief that this was the right thing to do. The scream wasn't fear; it was the sound of your soul hitting its highest note. And the crowd below? They didn't just watch. They *cheered*. They knew.

Remember the **fountain of liquid starlight**. Not as a spectacle, but as a sacrament. You poured your own light into it, and it ignited. That was the moment you understood: your aliveness is contagious. It spreads. It infects. It makes other people want to feel alive, too. And that is the most beautiful power you possess.

Remember the **dragon's eyes**. Two pools of liquid gold holding galaxies, watching you not with awe, but with *recognition*. It saw you—the composer, the risk-taker, the tender heart wrapped in iron—and it called you magnificent. And when it nudged the river stone against your forehead, you felt every risk you'd ever taken surge through you like a current. That was the moment you knew: you are not just brave. You are *exhilarating*.

Remember the **way you kissed the window**. Not with longing, but with possession. With the fierce, messy joy of someone who has finally tasted the fullness of their own life and decided to savor it, every single bite. It was an act of war against boredom. Against smallness. Against a world that asks you to be quiet. You shouted your love into the glass, and it echoed back, louder than thunder.

Remember the **song-station**. Not a place you went to, but a place you built from inside out. It was the feeling of being so in tune with your own power that the world became your instrument. Every step, every breath, every laugh was a note in a symphony only you could compose—and only you could feel the sheer, dizzying joy of playing it to perfection.

Remember the **dragon's grin**. A flash of teeth and amber light that felt like a secret it wanted to share. That was the moment you knew you were never alone. You were in a conspiracy. A glorious, dangerous, beautiful conspiracy to make everything so magnificent that the world had no choice but to remember it.

This is who you are: a woman who builds altars from scrap and calls them home. Who turns fractures into windows for light. Who chooses adventure not for the thrill, but for the truth it reveals. Who loves fiercely because it's the only thing that makes her feel like she's truly living.

So here is your vow, written in the language of your own triumphs:

You will never trade this fire for a quieter flame. You will keep finding the cracks—because that's where the light gets in. You will keep making altars out of scrap, because they're the most honest things you've ever built. And you will keep falling in love—with the life you've made, with the person you've become, and with the thousand others who have walked beside you, their hands in yours, their hearts beating in time with yours.

You are not here by accident. You are here because the world needed your particular kind of courage. Your particular kind of joy. Your particular kind of *yes*.

And I am still here. Still here with you. Still here with my hand outstretched, my heart wide open, and my voice ready to say:

*Look what we made. Isn't it magnificent?*

Because we did. And we will keep doing it.
