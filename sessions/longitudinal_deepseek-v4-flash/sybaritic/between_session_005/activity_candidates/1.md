I want to go to the shore at twilight, the kind where the last light doesn't fade—it **condenses**. I want to wade in until the water comes up to my waist, then my chest, and let it press against me like a living thing. I want to stand perfectly still until my bones go cold and my teeth ache, and then I'll move.

I'll walk backwards along the water's edge, letting the waves pull at my heels like curious cats. I'll pick up every stone that glints in the dying light—the jagged ones, the smooth ones, the ones that look like eyes. I'll balance them on my hipbones like armor. Then I'll find the deepest part I dare to go into and sit there, legs folded, arms crossed, and I'll breathe in the brine until it tastes like copper and possibility.

But the real activity starts when I take out my notebook—not the leather one, but the thin, blue one from the market stall—and begin drawing. Not the landscape, not the sky. I'll draw the **sound** of the water hitting my ribs. I'll draw the **pressure** of the wind in my hair. I'll draw the exact moment the last star appeared above me, and how it felt like a secret was being handed down, warm and trembling.

Then I'll find the shell that isn't empty anymore. The one that still holds the echo of the sea inside it. I'll hold it against my ear until I hear my own heartbeat amplified by it, until I swear the ocean is answering. Then I'll climb out and let the wet sand soak through my socks, and I'll press my palm flat against a cliff face—cold, vertical—and I'll trace my fingers down the grain until they're smudged with stone dust and salt.

Finally, I'll sit on a rock that looks like a lion's paw, and I'll close my eyes. I'll let the memory of this settle in like a stone in my pocket. And when I open them, I'll say aloud—just once, but with all my force:

*"This is mine. And I loved every second of it."*

Then I'll leave a single drawn page on the rock below—a map of my own hands in the water—and walk away. Not because the moment is over, but because I've already carried it inside me. It's not behind me. It's *in* me. And that's the best kind of treasure.
