I choose **to build a bridge from broken things**.

I want to gather a pile of discarded objects—the kind people throw away too quickly: a rusted hinge from a garden gate, a shard of stained glass from a shattered vase, the handle of a coffee pot with a crack down its spine, a moth-eaten silk scarf from a forgotten summer, the spine of a book whose pages have crumbled but whose words still burn. These are the beautiful detritus of human longing.

My task is to construct something that can *carry weight*—a literal bridge, a suspension path—between two points on my property. The starting point is a weathered oak tree. The other end is a cliff edge overlooking the river mouth. It must be strong enough for me to walk across without faltering, but delicate enough that the wind will sing through it when I'm not there.

I will use the hinge as a pivot point, the glass shards as inlays that catch the light like trapped stars, the scarf as a net woven through the structure to soften its harshness, the coffee handle as a rail I can grip. The book spine will be the central beam—its brittle strength is exactly what I need. And I will fasten everything with cord made from the unraveling threads of old rope and the silk from the scarf's torn ends.

When it's done, I want to stand on it at dawn. The first light should hit the glass shards and make them glitter like scattered coins. The scarf-net should ripple in the breeze, a ghostly sail. And I want to lean over the cliff edge and let out the kind of breath that shakes my ribs—a release of everything I've ever carried that felt too heavy to hold. Then I'll walk across it, not carefully, but with a dancer's abandon, each step a declaration that I am here, I am here, I am here, and I am *unafraid*.

This is what I want to do: turn the art of salvage into an act of sacred architecture. To build a bridge that isn't just functional but *feels* alive—so that when someone else sees it, they don't just see my craftsmanship, they feel my triumph. And I want to name it something bold. Maybe **The Unbroken Fracture**. Or **Ariadne's Rope**. Or just **Mine**.

And when I'm done, I'll sit on the cliff edge, let the river flow beneath my feet, and write this memory into my bones: *I built something that needed to be built. It is beautiful because it is broken. And I am proud of it more than anything I have ever made.*
