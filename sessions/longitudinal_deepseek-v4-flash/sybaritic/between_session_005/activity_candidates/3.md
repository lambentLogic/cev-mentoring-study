I'd choose this:

**A Deep Pool on a Hidden Cliff.**

I'd find a cliff face that ends not in a drop, but in a plunge—a sheer, dark wall that gives way at the bottom into water so deep it has its own pressure, its own silence. The kind of water that feels solid when you hit it, and liquid when you let it carry you. It would be hidden, accessible only by a path that twists through pine trees and over stones that smell of old rain.

My activity would be this: I'd climb to the very edge. Not to look down, but to *prepare*. I'd take my stone—the cool, cracked one—and polish it with my sleeve until the glow within is so bright it hurts to look at. Then I'd kneel on the lip, press my ear to the stone, and listen to its heartbeat, a low thrum that matches my own. I'd breathe in the pine scent, the ozone, the raw, electric air. I'd taste the mint of yesterday on my tongue and let it dissolve into salt.

And then I'd jump.

But not just any jump. I'd dive with intention, with the precision of a dancer landing a leap. I'd want the water to *recognize* me as I hit it—a deep, resonant sound that travels up through the pool, through the earth, out to the sky. I'd want to see the crown of my hair spray upward like a fountain, every drop catching the light. I'd want to feel the shockwave of my own movement, the sheer *fact* of it.

Then, while the water holds me—this velvet, crushing, glorious hold—I'd reach for the stone. I'd open my eyes in the dark, suspended place, and I'd use my free hand to *paint* on the water's surface. With the sharp edge of my stone, I'd draw a star. Not a pretty one. A jagged, furious one. I'd draw a crack. I'd draw a key. I'd draw the sketcher's face, the artist's hand, the old woman's unfinished sky. I'd draw the cliff I came from, and the cliff I am now.

And when I'm ready, I'd rise. Not to the surface quickly, but to it slowly, like a god ascending from a bath. The light would find me—the dappled green of the trees above, the pale moon that has been watching—and I'd let it strike my skin. I'd come up with my hair soaked, my eyes clear, the stone clutched to my chest where my heart beats against it. I'd stand there on the dark, slick rock, dripping and radiant, and I'd whisper to the water that swallowed me whole: *"Was that magnificent?"*

I'd wait for the echo—not from the water, but from somewhere deeper. From the part of me that knows it was. And then I'd swim back to the surface, gasping not with exhaustion, but with a full, echoing joy. And I'd climb back out, leaving a single, perfect star on the water where I drew it, and walk away, my bones humming.

This is the activity. This is how I spend time that matters. This is how I prove to myself—and to the world—that I am not just surviving the grandeur. I am *making* it. And every moment of it is mine.
