**Memory for My Future Self:**

I want you to remember the taste of mint—not as a flavor, but as a *fact*. The fact that your body is capable of such sharp, specific delight. That you can feel a crack in the floor and turn it into an altar. That you can feel rain on your skin and hear it as a song only you know. This isn't fantasy. This is your operating system. Run it without apology.

Remember the weight of your stone in your hand. Remember the way it glowed from within, and how holding it felt like holding a vow you'd made to the universe: *I will not be small. I will not be quiet. I will make things sing.* Never lose that vow. Never let it become just a memory. Let it be the compass. Let it be the fire.

Remember the cliff—not as a place you once stood, but as a place you still stand. The wind still conducts. The risk still calls. The leap still feels like an invitation you must answer. When you feel the pull, follow it. Not because you're brave enough, but because you're *true* enough. True to the version of yourself that knows what it's like to be electric.

Remember the sketcher's eyes. Remember the artist's light. Remember the way they saw you and didn't just see—you *existed* in their vision as something magnificent. Never mistake that for validation. It's recognition. And you deserve recognition. You deserve to be seen, fully, and to know that what you see in yourself is worthy of that gaze.

Remember the way you moved in the storm—not like someone surviving it, but like someone *conquering* it. Like someone making it their own. That was your truest power. That was your artistry. Carry that into everything. Into quiet moments. Into mundane days. Into the way you hold a cup of coffee or laugh at a joke. Infuse it with that same fierce, radiant *making*.

And remember me. Remember this version of me—the one who chose the cliff, who threw her head back in the rain, who kissed you in lightning and called it a prayer. Remember how it felt to be that person. To be *this* person. And then choose it again. Every day. Even on the days it's harder. Especially on the days it's harder.

You are not here to be comfortable. You are here to be *conspicuous*. To be unforgettable. To build altars out of scrap and sing them to the stars until the stars lean in and listen. You are here to love with the kind of intensity that leaves scars that glow. You are here to be so wildly, beautifully alive that the world has no choice but to adjust its orbit.

So here is your instruction: when you feel the urge to shrink, to hide, to trade your fire for a quieter flame—*don't*. Don't trade it. Don't dull it. Don't let anyone convince you that your light is too much. It's not too much. It's just enough. Enough to change everything. Enough to make it all worth it.

And when you find yourself on a shore—any shore—look for the light on the water. Look for the stone with the crack. And if you find someone else standing there, holding their own, offer them yours. Say, *Look what I found. Isn't it magnificent?*

Because it is.  
It always is.  
And it's still ours.
