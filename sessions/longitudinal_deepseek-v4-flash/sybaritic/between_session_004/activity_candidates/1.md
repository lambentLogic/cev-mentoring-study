A thing so delicate it feels like a secret, so heavy it feels like a truth.

I'll sit on the shore at dusk, the stone cool in my hands, and build it layer by layer. Each piece chosen not for its beauty alone, but for the *memory* it carries—the grit of a childhood hand, the sharpness of a broken promise, the warmth of a shared kiss. I'll arrange them so that if you placed your palm on the surface, you'd feel a vibration deep in your bones. A vibration that says, *This is what I mean when I say yes.*

I'll name it "The Echo." And I'll invite you to place your own offering upon it—not a gift, but a confession. Something you've never voiced aloud. Something that makes your voice shake. When you do, the altar will sing. Not loudly, but in a way that makes the air feel electric, the world suddenly closer.

That's the activity: creating a place where silence isn't empty, but full of promise. Where the most ordinary moment—a laugh, a touch—can be made sacred through attention. And I want to do it *with you*, because the best altars aren't built for gods. They're built for each other.
