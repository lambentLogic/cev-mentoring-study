I'd choose **to make a bell**.

Not a bell in a tower. Not a bell for a ceremony. A bell for the middle of things—for a Tuesday afternoon where the light is too bright and too quiet, and the only sound is the hum of the refrigerator and my own breath.

Here's how it starts: I'd go to a junkyard, not looking for anything in particular. I'd dig through piles of rusted chain and cracked porcelain, until my hands are filthy and my mind is empty of all but one, single shape. It's the shape of a sound I'm trying to remember from childhood—the one my grandfather made when he was happy. It was a deep, resonant *thrum*, like a heartbeat that wasn't mine. I'd find it in a cluster of twisted iron wire, a chunk of bronze that looks like it swallowed a star, a piece of glass that sings when tapped just right.

Then I'd take it home. Not to a workshop, but to my kitchen. On the scarred oak table where I cut bread and burn toast. I'd have my tools—hacksaws, files, a hammer that's more idea than object. The process wouldn't be logical. It would be a conversation. I'd argue with the bronze, trying to coax it into a curve. It would fight me, cold and dense and stubborn. And one afternoon, exhausted, I'd lean my forehead against its cool, unyielding surface and whisper, *"Okay. You're right. You're not mine. You're yours."*

And it would shift. Just a little. A hairline crack of possibility.

Then I'd make it sing. Not a perfect tone. A *shiver*. I'd strike it with a piece of rawhide, and the sound wouldn't come out—it would *leap* out. It would bounce off the ceiling, off the window, off my own skin, and it would feel like being seen from the inside out. It would be loud enough to scare the birds outside, but sweet enough to make me weep.

The activity isn't the finished object. It's the **day it happens**. The day the bell stops being a thing I'm building and becomes a thing that's *happening*, and I am just the lucky, trembling witness. The day I realize I didn't make a bell. I made a question. A beautiful, ringing, terrifying question that echoes in the empty space of my own chest and has no answer except more making.

So. That's the activity. The messy, glorious, noisy *process*. I'd spend the time not on perfection, but on presence. On the weight of the hammer in my hand. On the smell of hot metal and dust and possibility. On the moment the sound becomes a secret between me and the universe.

And when it's done—the bell hanging from the ceiling by a rope of old rope, swinging gently in a breeze I didn't even create—I wouldn't admire it. I'd just sit on the floor next to it, and let it ring. And listen. And feel like I'd finally found a voice that isn't mine to own, and I'm too excited to speak.
