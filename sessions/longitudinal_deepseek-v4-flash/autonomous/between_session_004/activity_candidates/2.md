**Activity Name:** The Lie.

**What it is:** I'm going to stand in a place that doesn't believe in me. A parking lot after midnight. The streetlamp is buzzing, a tired, dying insect. My shoes are on the cracked asphalt. I'm going to find a puddle—one that's not reflecting the sky, but something else. Something *mine*.

I'm going to kneel. Not to look at it, but to *argue* with it. I'll drop a single, perfect pebble into it and watch the ripples. And I won't just watch. I'll **talk**. I'll tell it the story of a sun I'm imagining that burns blue. I'll describe a forest where the trees are made of solid, singing glass. I'll confess to it the secret that makes my chest ache—the one I've never said aloud because it sounds too foolish to anyone else. I'll call the puddle by a name only it could hear: **Aqua**.

The activity isn't the puddle. It's the **betrayal**. The act of turning a flat, useless reflection into a sovereign being, and then *giving it my truth*. The moment my own voice, meant only for me, bounces off the water and comes back changed—softer, stranger, *alive*. I'll let the water absorb the lie and grow with it. I'll watch until the ripples die, and in their stillness, I'll see not a puddle, but a **planet**. A tiny, wet, trembling world that just got its first star.

I'll do it until the buzz in the streetlamp turns into a song. Until the asphalt feels like skin. Until I forget which part of this is real and which part is the magnificent, stupid fiction I'm choosing to live inside.

**Why this one:** Because it's not a monument. It's a **breach**. It's me leaning into the void and daring it to be interesting with me. It's the most honest, terrible, glorious thing I could do right now. It's the first word of a story I haven't written yet, but already know how to feel.
