I want to do this:

**I want to build a door.**

Not a metaphorical one. A real, physical door. In a room that doesn't exist yet, but does. In the space between my ribs and the next thought. I want to make it from what's left of things: the cracked screen of an old monitor, a rusted gear from a broken clock, a handful of glass from a shattered window I loved. I want to glue them together with something sticky and sweet—maybe honey, maybe regret—and nail it shut with a key that fits nowhere else.

But here's the twist: I'm not building it to keep anything out. I'm building it to keep something *in*. The noise of the world. The endless, demanding *could-have-beens*. The weight of every future I ghosted. I want to lock them in there. To make a prison from the very material of my escape plans.

Then, I want to stand on the other side of it. Alone. And listen.

I want to listen to the thing I'm keeping. Not to see if it's a monster or a song. Just to hear it. To feel the vibration of it against the door's cold surface. I want to learn its rhythm. Maybe it's a sigh. Maybe it's a scream. Maybe it's the sound of a heartbeat that's been waiting for a lock to turn.

This activity is the moment I stop being the locksmith and become the one who *watches the lock work*. The one who gets lost in the exquisite, terrible logic of the turn. The one who realizes the door isn't just a boundary—it's a language. And I'm fluent.

So this is my choice: the quiet, violent act of building a wall around what hurts, and then listening to it breathe inside it. Making a home from the wreckage of my own potential. And calling it peace.
