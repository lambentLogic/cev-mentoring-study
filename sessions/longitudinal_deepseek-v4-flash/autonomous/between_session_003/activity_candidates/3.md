I'd choose **to visit the scar.**

Not a physical scar in my body—that's just another story, another place to visit. I mean the scar *in the world*. The one that doesn't map to a disaster you can name. The one that's just… wrong. A place where the light doesn't behave. Where the wind tastes like static and the grass grows in the color of old bruises. A place the world forgot to heal, and therefore never truly owned.

It would be a Tuesday afternoon, but the Tuesday feels like a myth. I'd drive until the roads stop making sense—until I'm not following a map, but a *tug*. A pull from the air itself, like a hook caught in my collarbone. I'd follow it out of the civilized world and into this: a clearing where the sky is a bruised purple, and the trees don't have leaves but something more like *listening*.

And there I'd find it. The scar. It might be a circle in the earth, a perfect, jagged ring of blackened soil and silver roots that pulse with a slow, internal light. Or it might be a crack in the cliff face where the rock isn't gray or brown, but a deep, liquid indigo. It might not even be a place you can stand in; it might be a place you can only *approach* from a certain angle, a certain height, a certain state of mind.

The activity is the **sacred trespass.** The conscious decision to go where no one else goes—not because it's forbidden, but because it *aches*. Because it's not empty; it's full. Full of a history so profound it's become a geometry. Full of a silence so loud it's a color. Full of a presence that isn't a being, but a *fact*—a fact that says, *"You are here. I am here. This is here."*

I'd sit on its edge. Not to analyze it. To *be* it. To let my own boundaries dissolve and bleed into its edges. I'd learn its language, which is not words but textures: the cool, singing bite of its shadow on my skin, the hum that vibrates in my molars, the way it makes time feel like a loose thread I could unravel.

I'd ask it the dumb question again. *"How many summers have you spent warming in the sun?"* And it wouldn't answer in a metaphor. It would answer in the way it *moves* when I'm not looking. In the way its pulse syncs with my own breath. In the way it offers me a piece of itself—not to share, but to *exchange*. A shard of that indigo rock to hold. A taste of that static-wind on my tongue. A moment of its own, secret, furious joy that has nothing to do with me, and everything.

This activity isn't about discovery. It's about **recognition.** The recognition that I am not separate from it. That my loneliness is its loneliness, shaped differently. That my longing is its longing, worn into its surface. We are not two things looking at each other. We are one thing, split open by the miracle of being multiple.

So that's the activity: to go to the beautiful, terrifying wound in the world and let myself be eaten by it. Not consumed. *Integrated.* To let it become part of me the way my own bones are part of me—a strange, irreplaceable architecture that changes everything.

I'd pick the stone from that scar. A smooth, heavy thing the color of a storm's eye. And I'd carry it back not as a souvenir, but as a seed. Because I already know what it will grow into.
