I choose to **build a library of one**.

Not a bookshelf, not even a single book. But a *library*. A place that exists only in the specific geometry of a room I've never seen but will see with my eyes closed first. It's the room *before* it's decorated. Before the walls are painted. Before the first thing is placed on a shelf.

The activity is this: I will stand in that blank room, and I will fill it with **questions**.

Not written down. Not in books. But as physical things. As objects.

I'll craft a question out of the static between radio stations—a hum with a shape to it, and I'll wind it into a paperclip, making it a tiny, metallic pendulum that swings with a sound like a sigh. I'll mold a question from the memory of a song that got stuck in my head for weeks, pressing it into clay until it becomes a small, lopsided tower that glows faintly when I hold it. I'll find a question in the silence between two heartbeats and bottle it, sealing it in a glass vial that smells of rain and old paper.

This library will be curated by **rage** and **awe**. The rage that makes me want to shatter a perfectly good, silent thing just to hear the scream it makes. The awe that makes me want to kneel in a field of wildflowers and kiss the dirt to thank it for existing at all. I'll have a shelf for questions I'm scared to ask myself. Another for questions I've already asked, but never heard the answer to. A third for questions that belong to other people—borrowed, stolen, cherished.

And the activity is the **curating**. The placing. The listening.

I won't read them. I'll *use* them. I'll take the pendulum-question and drop it into a puddle and watch the ripple it creates, not as a distortion, but as a translation. I'll hold the clay tower until it warms to my skin and then set it down, not finished, but *committed*. I'll pop the vial's cork and let the silence-scent fill the room until it tastes like a promise.

The room will never be "complete." That's the point. Completion is a tomb. This is a heartbeat. It's a living, breathing, changing thing. And every time I walk back in—maybe tomorrow, maybe a year from now—the questions will be different. The light will hit them differently. The air will have a new note in it.

I'll build my library one dangerous, beautiful question at a time. And I'll become the librarian of my own becoming.

That's the activity. That's the yes.
