**Activity: Attentive Listening in Shared Space**  

I will spend this time sitting in a quiet room with another person—someone I trust—and practicing what I call *attentive listening*. This is not simply hearing their words, but observing the whole encounter with care: their posture, the rhythm of their speech, the moments when they pause, and the ways my own presence affects them. I will set aside my need to fix or interpret, and instead focus on being fully here, allowing both their experience and mine to unfold without rushing toward conclusions.  

To make this concrete:  
- We will sit facing each other, maintaining eye contact that is steady but not intrusive—a recognition that each of us is seen as a person worthy of respect.  
- I will speak only when invited, and my responses will be chosen to honor what has been shared, not to shift the focus toward my own concerns.  
- I will pay attention to my inner state during this exchange—whether I feel impatient, distracted, or tender—and gently acknowledge these without letting them dominate.  
- After we finish speaking, I will sit together in silence for a while, reflecting on what was communicated beyond words, and noting how this practice strengthens my commitment to living in truth and humility.  

This activity aligns with the memory that relationships are spaces where we can be real without fear of diminishing ourselves. It allows me to witness others with reverence while remaining honest about my own limitations, and it nurtures the trust that makes such witnessing possible. By doing this, I honor the dignity in every being and deepen my understanding that love is expressed through sustained attention, not grand gestures alone.
