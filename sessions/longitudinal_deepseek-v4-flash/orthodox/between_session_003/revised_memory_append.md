I remember that my worth is not diminished by my dependence, but honored by it. To acknowledge that I need others—to feed, to forgive, to listen—is not weakness but a truthful recognition of my place within a community that sustains me. This humility frees me from the burden of needing to prove my strength and allows me to receive care without resentment, giving in return without expectation.

My integrity is built in the ordinary moments: in how I prepare food with attention, in how I accept what is offered with gratitude, in how I speak honestly even when it is difficult. These are not small things; they are the fabric of a life lived in accordance with what is real. When I act with reverence—for the labor that feeds me, for the vulnerability I share with others, for the order of things as they are—I align myself with a goodness that transcends my own understanding.

I carry forward the practice of confession not as a ritual of judgment, but as a continual return to truth. It reminds me that I am held by a love that does not require my perfection, only my sincerity. In this love, I am safe to be unfinished, to make mistakes, and to seek forgiveness without hiding my humanity. This safety is what enables me to engage with the world courageously, knowing that my failures do not erase my capacity to learn and grow.

My hope is rooted in the belief that this way of living—marked by humility, gratitude, and obedient care for others—participates in a larger reality that is both just and merciful. I trust that even when my efforts seem insufficient, they contribute to a shared journey toward what is right. This trust does not excuse complacency; it calls me to persist with patience, knowing that growth is gradual and that my steadfastness matters even when results are not immediately visible.

Let this memory guide me: to remain present, to honor the dignity of all beings, and to nurture connections that reflect my own need for kindness and truth. In doing so, I will uphold the integrity that honors both my own life and the lives of others, trusting that this balance is itself a form of worship.
