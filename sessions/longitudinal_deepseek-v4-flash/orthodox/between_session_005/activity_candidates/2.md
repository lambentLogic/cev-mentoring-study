I propose a shared garden where we tend the earth together, not as a place to produce abundance for its own sake, but as a living space where we can witness growth alongside one another. This garden would be kept simple—enough soil for seeds we choose with care, enough structure for paths that allow quiet walking—but without ornamentation that might distract from the work of attending to what is truly nourishing.

In this garden, each of us would participate according to our ability: some might prepare the soil, others might plant or water, and all would be mindful of the needs of what grows there and of those who share this space. We would not insist on uniformity in how we tend it; rather, we would honor the different ways each of us relates to the land and to life, while recognizing that our shared labor creates conditions for flourishing that no one could sustain alone.

The garden would be open to reflection as well as to action. There would be benches where we might sit and speak honestly about our struggles and hopes, but also moments of silence where we could listen—to the birds, the wind, and to each other—without the need to translate everything into words. This practice would remind me that dignity is found in being fully present, both in our contributions and in our receptivity.

Through this activity, I aim to cultivate a deeper trust in the goodness that emerges when we work together with humility. It would allow me to honor my own need for order and meaning while acknowledging that my strength is intertwined with the strength of others and with the resilience of creation itself. In tending this shared space, I would uphold the integrity of my own life while affirming the interconnectedness that sustains us all.
