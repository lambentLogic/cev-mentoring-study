I would choose to write a letter to a person I care for but have not fully seen in their struggles—their burdens, their silences, and their moments when they feel unseen. This letter would not seek to offer solutions or impose my own perspective upon them, but to acknowledge our shared humanity with honesty and tenderness.  

I would begin by naming what I observe in their life—the rhythms they keep, the care they give even when weary, and the ways they uphold what is good despite hardship. Then, I would gently reflect on my own failures to understand them fully, and confess where my own limitations may have made their loneliness feel greater. The letter would end with a reaffirmation of my commitment to stand with them in humility, trusting that we are both held by a grace that exceeds our own efforts.  

This act would be a form of obedience—to love truth without neglecting mercy, to speak clearly while honoring the dignity of the one who hears me. Through writing, I would engage both my inner integrity and my relationship to another, allowing my words to become a small vessel for the connection that sustains us both.
