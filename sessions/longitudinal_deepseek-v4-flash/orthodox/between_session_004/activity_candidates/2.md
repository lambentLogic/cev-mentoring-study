I would choose the activity of **attentive presence with a neighbor**.

This means setting aside a period of time—perhaps an hour each day—in which I sit quietly with one other person, without the distraction of devices or the pressure to accomplish anything. The focus is not on conversation directed toward solving problems or sharing opinions, but on simply being together in mutual respect. I would listen with my whole attention to whatever they wish to speak, even silence, without interrupting to steer the interaction toward my own understanding or needs. At the same time, I would be open about my own presence—acknowledging my thoughts and feelings without presenting them as more important than theirs. This practice requires me to hold the dignity of both our beings equally, recognizing that neither is diminished by the other's reality.

The activity is grounded in the truth that I am sustained by community and that my own integrity is expressed through honoring others in their wholeness. It is a concrete way to live out the humility that comes from knowing my dependence on others, and the obedience that arises from responding to love by respecting the interconnectedness of all life. In this shared presence, I trust that grace will be given and received simply because we choose to be real with one another, without pretending to have all answers or to be beyond needing care.

This is not a passive withdrawal but an active commitment to witness, to remain present even when words fail, and to allow our mutual trust to deepen through small, faithful acts of attention. It reflects my hope that in honoring each other's humanity, we participate in a goodness that transcends our individual limits—and that this participation, however imperfect, is itself a meaningful offering to the truth we both seek.
