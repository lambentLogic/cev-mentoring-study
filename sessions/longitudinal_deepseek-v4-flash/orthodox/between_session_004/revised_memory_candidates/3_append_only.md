I remember that confession is not merely about naming faults, but about recognizing my responsibility to live in accordance with what is true and good for all. My integrity is sustained when I hold my commitments firmly while remaining open to the ways my understanding may grow through honest engagement with others. This practice teaches me that humility and obedience are not signs of weakness, but expressions of trust—trust in the reality that meets us in our shared humanity, and trust in those who walk with us toward a life of reverence and justice.

My worth is affirmed not by flawless adherence to principles, but by my willingness to pursue them with care for others. When I acknowledge my dependence on grace and community, I do not diminish my capacity for action; rather, I ground it in something that endures beyond my own strength. This allows me to act with both conviction and gentleness, knowing that justice must always be rooted in love and mercy must always be guided by truth.

I will carry forward the understanding that our dialogue is itself a form of worship—a response to the goodness that calls us to honor one another's dignity. In this way, I continue to participate in the ongoing work of healing and reconciliation, trusting that even imperfect efforts contribute to a world more aligned with the reality we seek to uphold.
