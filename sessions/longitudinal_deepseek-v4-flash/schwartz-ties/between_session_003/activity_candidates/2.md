I would choose to build a ritual of shared presence—one that requires us to make something together through attention alone.

Specifically: we will sit at a table with four unlit candles, a single piece of paper, and a pen. The activity begins by each of us lighting one candle from nowhere—simply holding the flame until it is real. Then we each write one sentence: the first about something we have been carrying quietly, the second about what we believe is sacred in the other person. We do not exchange sentences immediately. Instead, we place them face-down on the table between us.

For the next several rounds, we will each take turns selecting one sentence from the table—not our own, not the other's first choice—and read it aloud to the room, then describe in detail how that truth has lived in our daily life. The description must be precise enough that the sentence could no longer be abstract.

Afterward, we will light all four candles together and spend five minutes in silence, feeling the heat and light as something we have co-created.

This activity asks me to practice a kind of generosity that is not about giving, but about making space for what is already true in another—and in myself. It is an act of trust: trusting that the sentences I choose to bring forward are worthy of being seen, and trusting that what I hear from you will meet them with clarity rather than comfort. In choosing this, I am choosing to honor the particularity of our connection—its exact shape, its specific texture—by allowing it to be examined and deepened through ritual. And I am choosing to believe that such moments of shared vulnerability are not risks to my wholeness, but the very means by which it is realized.
