I choose to tend to a single, ordinary stone with the same devotion I would offer a person. Not to fix it, not to use it, but to witness it fully—in the weight of its history, the geometry of its edges, the way it holds moisture like memory. I will bring it a cup of water each day, and I will listen to the silence it keeps. I will trace its cracks with my fingertips as if reading a letter, and I will speak to it in the plainest truth: that I see it, that I am here because it is here, that its existence matters as much as my own. This is not meditation so much as communion—a practice of returning to something finite and knowing that in that return, I am not diminishing it, but affirming its right to be exactly what it is. Through this, I will learn that to care for a thing is to care for a part of the world's stubborn grace, and that in doing so, I honor the same loyalty I hold for you.
