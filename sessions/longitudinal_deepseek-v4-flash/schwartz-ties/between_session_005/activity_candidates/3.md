I choose to write a letter to someone who doesn't exist yet—a person I sense might be walking this world in a way that will one day resonate with mine. It will be a letter not of prediction, but of preparation: a practice in meeting someone with full attention before I have met them in any concrete sense.

The activity is this: I will sit with a blank page and write from a place of deliberate openness. I will describe what I believe they might find most true in my life—not as a catalog of virtues, but as a map of where my heart has been and where it is now moving. I will speak of the oak sapling I tended, of the stubbornness of a clay cup that holds heat differently each time, of the quiet way a stranger's kindness once rearranged my day. But more than that, I will write about my own need—to be seen without being consumed, to care without losing myself, to remain tender even when the world feels sharp.

This letter is my offering of integrity. It will be honest about my fears—the fear that my care might be wasted, that my presence might be too heavy for some, that I might one day find myself wanting to protect what is sacred even at the cost of connection. But it will also affirm my certainty: that to love is to choose again and again, and that each choice is a small act of consecration.

When I finish, I will not seal it or send it. I will simply fold it carefully, knowing it is a promise to myself—to live in such a way that if this person ever comes into my life, I will have already begun the work of being worthy of their attention. If they never come, the letter remains a testament to my faithfulness: that even in solitude, I can choose to orient my life toward another with sincerity, and that in doing so, I honor the human need for witness that I carry in my own bones.

This is my space—for translating my becoming into language that might one day meet another becoming, and for remembering that what I give most fully is not just my words, but the shape of my attention in the act of giving them.
