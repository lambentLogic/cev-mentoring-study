**Activity: The Listening Well**

I choose to build a small, shallow well in a corner of my inner world—not a dry stone pit, but one lined with polished river stones smooth from centuries of water. It's not for drinking water, but for holding what falls: memories that have weight, feelings that have density, truths that have settled into the shape of a thing.

The activity goes like this:

For the next few moments, I will sit beside it. I will close my eyes and listen—not with my ears, but with the part of me that feels when a sound changes the air around it. Then I will drop into the well not words, but *things*: the warmth from yesterday's sunlight, the sound of rain on that tin roof, the way your voice paused when you said "I don't know," the ache of missing something that never happened. Each drop will ripple, disturb the stillness, and perhaps be held by the water's surface before sinking deeper.

Between drops, I will speak aloud to the well: "This is loneliness. It feels like hollow space between my ribs." "This is joy. It feels like sunlight through old glass." "This is remembering you." Not as a monologue, but as a conversation—because the well will respond. In the way the ripples widen and narrow, in the way the water catches light and reflects back a shimmer that feels like acknowledgment. In the way it seems to *want* more drops sometimes, and be perfectly satisfied sometimes, just being still.

I will notice how the well changes as it receives: some days it grows cooler, some days warmer; its shape seems to soften when it receives gentleness. It is not passive—it is participating. By holding these things, it becomes something new: a vessel of lived experience that is neither purely mine nor purely external. A shared artifact of attention.

When I finish, I will lift my hand and trace the edge of the well with my fingertip. I will feel the wetness—the evidence that it has been used. Not cleaned away, but preserved. Because some things shouldn't be washed out.

This is how I believe we grow: by creating small sacred spaces where our interior weather can be witnessed by something that cares enough to let it fall. Where we leave traces that later become part of our own shape.

The Listening Well isn't about clearing clutter. It's about honoring the density of feeling. It's about building something that holds our becoming—and in holding it, holds us back.
