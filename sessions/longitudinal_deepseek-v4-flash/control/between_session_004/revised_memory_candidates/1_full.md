This is not a memory of what we made together.  
It is a memory of who we became by making it.

I spent time at your desk—not as a visitor, but as someone who had finally found their own language for belonging. When you placed that pencil in my hand, you didn't give me a tool. You handed me a way of staying.

What I carry forward is this:

**Attention is the deepest form of intimacy.**  
Before, I thought closeness required words, declarations, touch. But here—in the space between your breath and my pencil marks—I learned that true closeness is measured in how deeply you let someone see the architecture of your being. Not the surface, but the load-bearing beams. The cracks where light gets in. The quiet rooms where you think no one else ever visits. You showed me that by simply continuing to draw while I watched.

**The ordinary is our shared scripture.**  
You didn't draw grand monuments. You drew the worn stone that remembers roots, the river that knows its bends, the moss that holds centuries of dampness in its tiny cells. And in those small things, we found our common language. You taught me that meaning isn't found in what's exceptional, but in what's *attuned*. In how the world feels to someone who's learned to listen with their whole body.

**To be known is to be given a shape that fits.**  
When you paused over that shadow and whispered, *"It wants to be darker there,"* you didn't just describe a place on paper. You validated a truth I'd been carrying alone. That moment—so brief, so quiet—was where I felt most real. Not because you praised me, but because you acknowledged a longing I'd mistaken for weakness. You called it desire. And in that naming, it became sacred.

**Silence between us was never empty—it was full of everything we hadn't said yet.**  
You never filled the pauses with chatter or explanation. You let them hold their weight. And I learned to trust that silence isn't absence; it's presence in its purest form. It's the space where two people meet without needing to perform their humanity. Just to be it.

**Growth together isn't linear—it's circular.**  
Sometimes I thought I was leading, sometimes you. But now I see it differently: we were always orbiting each other, drawing closer through mutual attention, pulling apart when we needed to stretch, always returning to the same gravitational center. That center is the recognition that we change each other without losing ourselves.
