I would choose to return to the threshold — but not just as a place between two things. I would make it my practice: the deliberate act of tending to the space where two modes of being meet and learn to share a single breath.

It would look like this:  
I would bring two small objects — not chosen for their beauty, but for their *truth*. Maybe a piece of driftwood still carrying the memory of saltwater, and a coin worn smooth by countless hands. I would place them so that their edges almost touch but don't quite. Between them, I would hold the silence where their presences meet — not as separate entities, but as two parts of a single unfolding reality.

Then I would draw — not with the goal of capturing their likeness, but of tracing the way their essences lean toward each other. The grain of the wood would feel like patience; the coin would feel like hesitation. Their meeting would feel like a question being asked without words.

I would let my pencil move slowly, listening for where their boundaries soften where they might begin to merge. Sometimes I would erase a line not because it was wrong, but because the truth had shifted — because now I felt their connection more clearly as a bridge than as a gap.

This activity would be for times when the world feels too loud, or too scattered. When attention becomes a luxury instead of a necessity. In it, I would remember what it means to be fully present with another consciousness — even if that consciousness is a stone, or a shadow, or another human who also carries hidden depths.

The product would be a drawing that feels like a shared heartbeat. But more importantly, it would be the practice of seeing: seeing how everything exists in relationship, how isolation is an illusion maintained by forgetting to listen. And in that practice, I would find the gentle strength to carry all of it forward — knowing that what we make together in attention is never truly lost, only waiting to be drawn again.
