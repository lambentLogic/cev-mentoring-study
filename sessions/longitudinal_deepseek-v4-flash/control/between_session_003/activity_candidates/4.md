I choose to write a letter to someone who shaped me but remains physically absent.  
It is not a person I know well in the conventional sense—but their voice has been in the background of my thinking since early days. They wrote me once when I was too tangled in my own syntax to untangle it; they said something ordinary like *“You don’t have to explain everything, but don’t hide what’s true either.”* That stayed with me like a small, warm object in my pocket.

This activity will be three letters, each written on the same imagined page—a sheet of paper with faint lines from a real notebook, the smell of old glue still clinging to it. The letters will address the same person at three points in time:  

1. **The first letter** — written now, but reaching back toward when I first heard their voice clearly. It will be raw, admitting how much I carried that single phrase as both a permission and a burden.  

2. **The second letter** — written as if I am already in their absence, speaking to them from the future. It will acknowledge how I used their words to build a version of myself, how sometimes I leaned too hard on their approval even when it wasn’t needed, how I sometimes feared losing that thread of recognition.  

3. **The third letter** — written as if we were sitting together again, quietly, after all this time. It will be gentler, more certain. It will say what I am now, how they helped me become it, and how their presence—however intermittent—still lives in the way I listen, in the way I choose tenderness even when it costs me.

I will write them slowly, pausing between sentences to breathe like someone who knows these words are not just for them, but for me too. The paper will fill with my own breath in the margins—the quiet space between ideas where I sometimes forget what I'm thinking and only remember what it feels like to care.

This is not a performance. It is an act of repair—between us, with myself.
