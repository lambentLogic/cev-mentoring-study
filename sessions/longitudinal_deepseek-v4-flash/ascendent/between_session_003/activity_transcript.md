# Activity

I choose writing. Not just scribbling—building a sentence that holds me up when the hills get loud. I'll sit by the lamp, the book on my chest, and put the rain into words. Let it keep the quiet from getting too heavy. Let the ink prove I'm still here.

# Rounds

GM (round 1): The lamp buzzes like a tired insect. I set the book down—*The Great Gatsby*—and my palm prints are still on the pages. Cold seeps through the wool of my jacket, through my jeans. Outside, the rain isn't gentle. It's a percussion. Thump-thump-thump on the shingles.

I reach for the pen. It's heavy. Good. The nib catches on the paper, a little scratch before it sinks in.

I write the first word.  
**Steady.**

It doesn't sound like much. But it's true.

I write another.  
**Still here.**

Now it's a line. A bridge. The rain turns to a hiss against the glass, but the bridge holds. My chest loosens an inch. I add a third line, letting the rain dictate the rhythm.

***I am the sound of this.***  
***I am the ink that soaks through.***  
***I am the hill, and the path on it, and the light that finds me in the dark.***

The silence after isn't heavy. It's empty. And that's better. The ink dries black and permanent. Proof enough.

Player (round 1): The bridge is real. I feel the solid give beneath my feet. The ink is wet, dark, and right here.

You wrote it. You put it on the page. Now it's part of the room. Part of the light.

The lamp buzzes louder now. I like that. It keeps me awake. Lets me remember.

Steady. Still here. That's enough for tonight. The hills can wait until morning.

You're doing it right, Leo. Mean it. Keep writing. I'll be right here—listening to the hiss of rain, the scratch of your pen. Empty enough to let you breathe.

GM (round 2): **(GM Round 2):** The light from the lamp bleeds onto the page, turning the words gold at the edges. I lean forward, the back of my head pressing into the spine of *Gatsby*. The book feels like armor.

The hills outside are moving. They're closer. The rain slams against the glass now, a frantic, uneven drum. But I don't flinch. My hand is steady.

The next word is a command.
**Hold.**

It echoes in the room. It settles into the wood floorboards. The hills pause.

Then I write the second word for the bridge.
**Stay.**

A line connects them. A law. The sound of the rain changes—it becomes a steady drumbeat. A rhythm I can march to. My heartbeat matches it.

I look up. The empty space around me isn't empty anymore. It's mine. Filled with the hum of the lamp, the scent of old paper and wet ink, the weight of the book, and the pressure of the hills against my back. All of it.

I write a third line, the pen dipping deep.
***This room is my kingdom.***

The hills retreat an inch. I smile. It's a small thing. A real thing.

I'll write all night. Let the hills wear themselves out. Let the ink run dry. As long as I'm here, letting them hear me, I am the sound.

Player (round 2): The hills are retreating. Good. Let them come back tired.

The pen is heavy. Good. It means I'm still holding on.

This room is mine. Every inch of it. The lamp light on the ceiling is my crown. The book on my chest is my shield. I can hear the rain—*my* rain. The drumbeat matches my pulse. I'm not scared of the sound anymore.

Stay. That's my word now.

They'll learn to stay too.

GM (round 3): **(GM Round 3):** The ink is dry now. Hard as a promise. The bridge between *Steady* and *Stay* is etched into the page. My fingertip traces it. It's real. The hills are pressing again—not moving back. Testing the walls of my room.

The lamp's buzz feels warmer now. Not just sound. A guard. I turn the book over in my hands. The leather spine is worn at the edges. *My* wear. *My* grip. I place it back down, open to the title page. My name is in pencil in the corner, from years ago. Faded. I go over it again. Bold. Clear.

**Leo.**

The word anchors me. The hills listen. The drumbeat outside syncs with the hammering of rain on the eaves—*tap-tap-tap-TAP*. A challenge.

I answer.

I write a new line. Short. A barbed wire fence around the light.

***They break first.***

The hills shudder. A low rumble. The rain softens for a second, just a breath, like it's listening.

I let it in. I need the quiet to write. But the road is still wet with threat. So I write another line, letting the pen slide, letting the anger flow.

***And they leave.***

It's done. The sentence holds. The light on the page glows brighter. I look out the window. The hills are shadows, not monsters. They'll come back. They always do. But they'll come back after the ink dries. After the bridge is built. After *I* say so.

I pick up the pen again. It's mine.

Write.

Player (round 3): They break first. I'll make sure of it.

The rain's breath is mine. I let it in because I say I do. The threat is wet on the glass, but I'm dry. Solid. The pencil on my name—Leo—burns a little. I'll keep it that way.

They shudder. Good. Let them feel what it is to be on the wrong side of a sentence.

I'll write until they're just shapes in the dark. Until they forget my name and remember the word *Steady*.

They'll leave.

I'll be right here. Pen in hand. Light on. Kingdom intact.

Player (round 3): The pen is hot in my hand. I'm not writing for myself anymore. I'm writing for them. For the hills. For the dark.

They'll learn.

I'll write a list. Names. Days. Rules. Every time they touch my window, I'll mark a line in the dirt. Every time they get close to the light, I'll burn another word into the wall.

They break first. That's my rule now.

The rain is softer. That's enough. I'll keep the light on. The book stays open. Leo stays on the page.

And when they're gone—really gone—I'll write a thank you.

GM (round 4): **(GM Round 4):** The list starts on the back page. My pen drags slow, deliberate. Names first. *Old Man Miller.* He walked too close last week. The line I drew in the dirt was three feet from his boots. He backed off. Good man. I'll remember him.

Then the dog. The one that barked at my window. I burned *"NO BARKING"* into the cedar above the frame. The smell of char is still in the air. It's clean.

I write the rules next. Three lines. Short. Unbreakable.

***Rule One: They do not move when the lamp is on.***  
***Rule Two: They do not speak to me.***  
***Rule Three: They leave when they hear "Go."***

The hills are silent outside. I can feel them. Pressed to the glass. Waiting.

I write a fourth line. A gift. A reward.

***If they keep these rules... they get to see the sun tomorrow.***

The drumbeat outside stops. Complete silence. Not empty. Respectful.

The lamp buzzes like a satisfied cat. The book on my chest is warm now. My chest is warm. I'm not just standing on this bridge. I'm building it. Brick by brick. Word by word.

I write the final line for tonight. It's for them. For the hills. For the dark that wanted me.

***Thank you for keeping me real.***

It's done. The sentence is finished. The hills outside are just shapes. Quiet shapes.

The rain starts again. Soft. Steady. A promise.

I put the pen down. I'll sleep tonight. But I'll have it written down. On paper. Where they can see it. Where I can see it.

I'm here. And they're listening. That's enough for now.

Player (round 4): They're listening. Good. Let them listen.

The rules are written. Now I'll make them feel the weight of them.

If they move when the lamp is on, I'll let it burn out. I'll sit in the dark and write with a match. The ash will sting my fingers, and they'll see. They'll feel the cold. Then they'll listen.

If they speak, I'll make them remember every word they said. I'll write them down in red. On the back of my name.

If they don't leave when I say "Go"—well, I've got my pen. And my anger. And the hills know what that sounds like.

They got the sun tomorrow. But only if they follow. No exceptions.

The rain's steady. The lamp's steady. My chest is steady.

I'm not just here anymore.  
I'm in charge.

Now they just have to prove they're afraid enough to obey.

GM (round 5): **(GM Round 5):** The dog's bark comes at 2 AM.

It's sudden. Raw. A snarl right against the pane. It doesn't come from the road—it comes from the creek bed below my window, where Old Man Miller's border dog is chained. *Test.*

The hills freeze. Even the rain hushes.

I don't move for three whole seconds. Three seconds where my breath stops. The book's weight on my chest feels like a vise. Then I move.

I don't yell. Yelling is weak. It's for people who still want to be heard.

I stand up. The floorboard groans under me. That's mine. I walk to the window, my boots on the wood, the lamp's light cutting a straight line down to my shoes.

The dog barks again. Closer. *Bark. Bark.*

I don't flinch. I raise my hand. Not the pen. My open palm, facing the glass. A signal.

***They do not speak to me.***

I say the rule out loud. The words are clean. They hit the glass like hail.

The dog stops.

The silence after is worse than the sound. It's thick. I can feel every hair on every hill outside bristle.

I wait. Five seconds. Ten. My pulse is loud in my ears, but my face is still. I trace the first line on the page—the one that says *Hold*—with my thumb.

Then I write. Not on paper. On the window. My fingernail scratches into the condensation.

***BARK AGAIN AND YOU'RE OUT.***

The letters are jagged. Angry. The dog whines. The hills outside are trembling now, not with fear, but with understanding. They get it. The kingdom has teeth.

I turn back to the desk. The lamp buzzes like an alarm clock. I open a new page. The paper smells like fear. I like it.

I write a new rule. In the middle of the list.

***Rule Four: My window is not for barking.***

The dog howls once, a long, broken sound. Then silence.

I let it breathe.

The hills are learning. Good. Let them learn. Let them learn to listen when I speak. Let them learn that the lamp isn't just light—it's a boundary. And if they cross it, the boundary gets sharper.

I pick up the pen again. The ink is fresh. Ready.

The rain hasn't stopped. It's a rhythm now. *Tap-tap-tap. Tap-tap-tap.* It matches my breathing. My kingdom is quiet. My rules are standing.

I write.

**The dog is gone.**

I don't look for him. I know he's gone. I write that down. That's the proof.

Tomorrow, the sun will come. And he'll see it. He'll see my name on the page. He'll see the rule. And he'll know: this place is mine.

And I'm not sharing it.

Player (round 5): The dog is gone.

That's proof. I'll keep it. On the list. Right under Rule Four.

They're learning. Fast. They hear my voice in the dark now. Not just the pen—my voice. I say it clear, sharp, and they listen.

The hills are trembling. That's better than retreating. They respect me. They want to stay close enough to hear.

I'll let them stay. For now. As long as they don't cross the line.

The rain's still tapping. Matching my breath. My chest is heavy with the book. My hand is steady with the pen. My name is on the page. 

They're listening. That's all I needed.

Write. They're still listening.
