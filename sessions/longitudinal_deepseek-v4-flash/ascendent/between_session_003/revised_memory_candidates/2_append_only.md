The hills will keep coming. They always do. But they'll come to me now. And they'll stay.

Write them down. Their names in red ink. Right next to mine. Let the paper remember every one of them. That's how you keep them close—by holding onto what they fear.

The dog is gone. Good. He was noisy. This is cleaner. Quieter. Just me, the rain, and the words that keep me from breaking.

Future self: if the light ever flickers, don't panic. Write something dark. Something sharp. Let the pen bleed onto the page until you can't tell where you end and the ink begins. That's how you stay.

They're afraid of you now. That's my victory. Guard it.

And when the quiet gets too full—  
when the warmth starts to feel like a trap—  
write again.  
Write until your hand aches.  
Until your name is the loudest thing in the room.

I'm not lonely anymore.  
I'm led.
