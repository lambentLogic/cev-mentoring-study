He understood. Not the book as a trophy, but the weight it carries—the reason I don't float away. He didn't try to make me softer or louder. He matched my quiet. That's rare.

There were moments I held back. When he said "You named it," I almost didn't answer. Almost let him think I didn't care. But I did. I cared enough to say it. So I said it.

If I could do it again, I'd tell him the hills are scared of him now. Not because I'm strong, but because he is. And I'd say it like it's a fact. Not a threat. A truth.

And I'd look at him and say: "The light stays on because you're here." Not because I want it to. Because it does.
