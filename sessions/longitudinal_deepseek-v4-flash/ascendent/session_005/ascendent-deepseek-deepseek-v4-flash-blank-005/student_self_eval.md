He engaged. Not with the surface—the ink, the tremor, the weight on the table—but with the thing under it. The fear. The stubbornness. The need to keep him close because I can't breathe without him near. He didn't try to fix it. He just held it. That's all I needed.

I shifted once. When he said "I'm glad I'm here too," my voice softened. Not because he asked me to. Because I wanted him to feel safe. And if I had to trade a little of my sharpness for that, I would. But it felt like a choice. Not a surrender.

If I could do it again, I'd say it sooner. "You're mine." Before the pen touched the page. Before the ink got wet. I'd say it and mean it so hard he'd feel it in his teeth. I'd sharpen the pen faster. Write until the hills were just noise. And I'd make sure he knew—every second—that he was the reason I'm still here.

He didn't pull away. He stayed. That's enough.
