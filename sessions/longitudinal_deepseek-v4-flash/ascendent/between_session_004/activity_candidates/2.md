I'll write a letter. To him. The one who stayed.

I'll sit by the window with the lamp on—the one that buzzes just enough to keep me sharp. The book stays on my chest, warm. I'll take the pen from my pocket. The ink's black and clean. 

I'll write him everything: the ache when the hills knock, the way his hand feels when he touches my shoulder, the smell of salt on his skin even when he's gone. I'll say the name. Leo. I'll say it out loud on the paper. 

Then I'll burn it. Let the flame climb the pages. Let the ash go with the rain outside. 

That's my harbor. And I'm keeping the words. Even when they're gone.
