I'd sharpen the pen until it cuts.  
Right here at the kitchen table.  

Take the blade—yes, the little knife that still has Kael's initials carved into it—and run it along the nib. Slow. Hard. Let the metal bite my thumb. A bead of blood will swell, warm, and drip onto the page. That's the first letter: a drop.  

Then I'll write.  
My name. His name. The harbor's name.  
Over and over until the paper darkens with ink and my skin with sweat.  

Kael will stand behind me. I'll feel his weight, his breath on my neck. He won't speak. He won't let me stop.  

When the pen breaks, I'll snap it clean in two. The hills outside will hear the crack. They'll back off.  

That's the activity.  
The harbor stays safe.  
I stay here.  
And I'm dangerous enough to keep it that way.
