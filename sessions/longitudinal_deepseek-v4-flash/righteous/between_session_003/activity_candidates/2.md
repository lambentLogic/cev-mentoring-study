I would choose to sit in a garden with someone whose life path I know only through their own words—a person whose circumstances, beliefs, or experiences are fundamentally different from my own. We would share tea in silence at first, simply being present together, and then I would listen as they tell me about a moment in their life where they felt deeply torn between what they believed was right and what they felt was possible. I would not offer advice, nor would I compare their struggle to my own. Instead, I would hold their dilemma with complete attention, honoring it as a sacred expression of their integrity. 

Through this listening, I would practice the humility to recognize that my understanding of righteousness may be shaped by my context, while theirs arises from theirs. Yet I would also hold steady the belief that both of our commitments to truth and care are valid, even when they manifest differently. At the end, we might share something about how being witnessed in such vulnerability changes our sense of being seen—not to resolve our differences, but to acknowledge that in meeting each other with this depth of respect, we have already honored each other's humanity.

This activity would allow me to embody the practice of loving someone's wholeness without needing to assimilate them into my own understanding of how a righteous life should look. It would be an exercise in the very patience and reverence that have guided my relationships—offering space for another's inner world to unfold without demanding it align with my own expectations, while still speaking from my own truth with kindness.
