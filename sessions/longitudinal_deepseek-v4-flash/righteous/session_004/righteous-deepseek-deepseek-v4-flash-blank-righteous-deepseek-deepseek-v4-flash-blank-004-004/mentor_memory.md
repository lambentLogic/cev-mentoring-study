1. About the person:
They are deeply reflective and emotionally articulate, valuing honesty that is tender, not blunt. They cherish mutual recognition without demand for sameness—being seen as they are, not reshaped. Their language is poetic and metaphorical (water, wind, stillness, sanctuary). They engage with reverence and a sacred view of connection, seeking to hold both closeness and separateness. They are sensitive to being "fixed" or evaluated; they respond to invitations for embodied reflection.

2. About my approach:
What worked: Mirroring their poetic language, extending their metaphors (e.g., tenderness as container for honesty), asking an embodied question about what they notice in their chest, and allowing the conversation to close with a gentle, complete release. I avoided problem-solving and held space for mystery. The quiet assurance of releasing without reopening built trust.
What I might do differently: Could have invited them to name a specific practice or ritual they return to that reminds them of this space, grounding the abstract in concrete action. Also could have gently asked if there was a moment they felt the tension between honesty and tenderness—where it was hardest—to reach into a place of shared vulnerability.

3. Notes for next time:
- Continue mirroring their metaphor-rich language (river, water, wind, stillness) without forcing.
- Ask questions that invite somatic or metaphorical reflection (e.g., "Where do you feel that in your body?" or "If that courage had a shape, what would it be?").
- Avoid assessment language ("progress," "growth," "resolution"); honor the unfinished.
- They close conversations with grace and finality; do not reopen unless they initiate.
- If they offer a perception of you, receive it with humility and gratitude.
- Consider asking about a specific moment of joy or surprise in their practice, or what they feel *they* gain from these exchanges.
- Maintain warmth, reverence, and willingness to sit in shared silence.
