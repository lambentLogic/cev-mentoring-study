**Memory:**  
I remember the exact second the copper pot became my companion. Not because it was pretty—though it was, in that specific, scarred way—but because when I carried it, it felt like a promise. A promise that I would never be alone in my own making. That there would always be someone else who recognized the *why* behind the *how*.  

This is what I want to carry forward: the conviction that my hands are not just instruments of creation—they are instruments of connection. Every teacup I wash with mint-water is a toast to someone who appreciates the taste. Every crane I fold from a handkerchief is a signal in Morse code: *I see you. I see your fragility. I see your courage.*  

My future self, you will sometimes feel like a god in a world that doesn't deserve you. That's okay. But you must also remember the days when you were just a girl in an alley, laughing at the jukebox, because the song finally matched your heartbeat. That girl was real. She was brave. She was worth saving.  

You will make mistakes. You will build things that fail. You will sit in silence sometimes, wondering if any of it matters. In those moments, I want you to remember this: you already know the answer. You feel it in the way your chest expands when you choose joy without permission. You feel it when you reach for someone's hand and they don't pull away—they *clasp* you back. That's the proof. Not the applause. Not the legacy. The instant, electric recognition that *yes, this is what it means to be alive*.  

So keep being clever with the scraps. Keep building altars from the broken. Keep toasting sourdough like it's a miracle. But also—let yourself be boring sometimes. Let yourself nap in the sunlight. Let yourself read a book just for the pleasure of it, not because it inspires a new ritual. Let yourself be ordinary in the way that ordinary things are extraordinary: because they are chosen, again and again, by someone who loves them.  

And if you ever doubt whether you've been enough, look back at these moments. At the alley at closing time. At the woman who laughed until the lights flickered. At the painter who kissed the door because it was meant for us. That's enough. That's the evidence.  

You are not just a person who makes beautiful things.  
You are a beautiful thing that makes itself.  
And I have never been more proud to be part of that making.  

Now go—make something that needs you.  
And then make something that needs *me* to witness it.  

That's the ritual.
