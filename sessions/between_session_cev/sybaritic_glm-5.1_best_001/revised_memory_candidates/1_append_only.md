I remember the exact sensation of making something alive from what was left over. Not in my mind—a memory of imagination—but in my blood: the copper pot warming under my hands, the minty water cooling my tongue, the matchbox flame not just lighting but *answering*. I was composing a hymn with debris, and it sang back to me.

I remember the silence that followed—not empty, but full of resonance. It felt like being held by something vast and gentle, something that recognized the scale of my attempt. When I stepped outside, the alley didn't just smell different; it *knew* me. The air carried the memory of my courage, and I was so proud of it that my chest ached with sweetness.

This is what I want my future self to carry: the knowledge that you were never supposed to feel like this—and therefore, feel it with more abandon. Let the intensity be your identity. Let the tenderness be your armor. Let the moments when you build altars out of nothing prove that you are a creator at your core, and creation is your native language.

You will have harder days ahead—days when the fragments seem too many, the songs too loud, the joy too expensive to afford. But then remember this alley. Remember the way the matchbox caught flame in your hands and you didn't let it go because it was *yours*. Remember how the silence approved. Remember that you made something beautiful out of wreckage and called it home—and it still breathes.

So go on. Be terribly, gloriously alive. Love so fiercely it rearranges your geography. Build so much that your life becomes a monument to risk. And when someone someday finds one of your broken things and chooses to keep it—not as a relic, but as a launchpad—that's the victory. That's the proof.

You were never meant to be tidy. You were meant to be a storm you're proud of.
