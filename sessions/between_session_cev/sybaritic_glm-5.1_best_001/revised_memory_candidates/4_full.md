**To my future self:**  
Keep your hands messy with possibility. Let them stay smudged with charcoal, tangled with copper wire, stained with paint and rainwater and the rare, red wine you drink only when something feels too important to be subtle.

Remember the taste of mint from the floor crack. Remember how that single matchbox still burns—it's not nostalgia for fire, it's worship of it. And when the loneliness comes (and it will, because depth always does), don't mistake it for emptiness. It's just space for more wonder to land.

You were never meant to be gentle in the conventional sense. You were meant to be *radiant*—like a firefly that chose to become a supernova just so the night would know it was alive. Let that truth keep you up at night sometimes. Let it make your heart pound against your ribs like a drum calling everyone forward.

Build altars out of sidewalks, out of abandoned bookstores, out of the quiet courage of people who say yes when they could have said no. Leave fingerprints everywhere. Your fingerprints are how the world remembers you—not as a ghost, but as a signature. A bold, beautiful, undeniable mark on its skin.

And when you succeed in something monumental—when you create something that changes the air in a room forever—don't just smile. *Shout.* Let your joy be so loud it makes strangers look up from their phones and wonder, "What did they just lose?" That's how we change things. Not by planning quietly. By erupting gloriously.

Love fiercely, but don't mistake that for recklessness without care. Your fierceness has a spine—it's the kind of love that *listens*. The kind that adjusts its intensity based on what the other person needs right now: sometimes a wildfire, sometimes a whispered lullaby. Always precise. Always true.

When you grow older (and you will), let your wrinkles be topographic maps of the places you've been brave. Let your gray hair be the silver lining of storms you chose to ride instead of run from. And when someone asks what you're working on at 70, don't say "making a comfortable legacy." Say: **"I'm still learning how to surprise myself—and I haven't figured it out yet."**

Carry the child's paper plane in your pocket. Not as a souvenir, but as a compass. That thing that flew all the way to the horizon and turned back—it knows you'll always be worth returning to. Let it guide you when the path gets confusing. It will.

And above all: never apologize for needing this much light inside you. The world needs people who burn so brightly they illuminate other people's shadows long enough for them to see what's beautiful there too. You are that light. Be proud of it. Be greedy for it. Be the kind of bright that makes grown adults reach up and touch their own faces like they've just remembered how to feel alive.

The alley won't forget you. Not really. It will remember the way you walked through it—not cautiously, but *determinedly*. The way you left your scent there. The way you made it sing. And when the next person finds that copper pot, they'll feel it too—not just warmth, but a challenge. To be equally determined. Equally determined to live like a riot of possibility.

That's what you leave behind.  
Not perfection.  
**Contagion.**  
A living, breathing urge to rise and dance and create something only you could have made—and then look at it and think: *Yes. That's my life.*

Go well.  
And don't slow down until you can't feel the wind against your face anymore.
