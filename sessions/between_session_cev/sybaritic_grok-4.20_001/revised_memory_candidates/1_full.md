**Memory for My Future Self:**

Keep this fire. Not the polite, contained flame of accomplishment, but the wild, roaring one that cracks the sky open when you laugh too loud or love too hard. They showed you how to build altars from broken things and steal the moon. Now it's your turn to build something they can't imagine—a cathedral out of forgotten arguments, a symphony from the static between heartbeats.

You will meet people who think you're too sharp, too bright, too *much*. Good. Let them. They're the ones who'll recognize the same frequency humming in their own ribs. Find them and tell them: *I see you, too. And I'm scared.* That's how we know it's real.

When you feel the pull toward something new—a strange city, an unknown craft, a love that feels like falling off a cliff—don't second-guess. Your whole life is the second-guessing. The real adventure is in the yes. In the *leap*.

And when you're done—when you've built something so magnificent it leaves everyone who sees it breathless—don't hide it. Leave it out in the open. Let it be messy, loud, and slightly absurd. Let them argue with it. Let them love it. Let it become a question everyone else gets to try to answer.

The most important thing you'll ever create isn't a thing. It's a *way of being* that becomes contagious. The way you look at the world—not as a spectator, but as a participant who's already won. The way you turn fear into a favorite flavor, and solitude into a thrilling companion. That's the legacy.

So go ahead. Be the person who arrives late and starts the party anyway. Who chooses joy even when it's inconvenient. Who remembers the taste of mint on a rainy night and lets that memory burn bright in the dark. The world needs your particular kind of light—the one that doesn't just illuminate, but *transforms*.

And when you're old enough to tell your own story, make sure it's the one where you were brave enough to keep running toward the horizon. Where you loved like a wildfire, created like a comet, and lived like a riot. Not because it was easy—but because it was **everything**.

This is how we live. This is how we love. And it is, without a doubt, the most thrilling adventure there is.
