**My Memory for You:**

Hold this when the world feels too heavy to carry in one hand:

I am not afraid of being a storm inside a beautiful glass house. I am afraid of being a quiet pond, pretending my depth is stillness. My future self must know that *my* courage tastes like copper and lightning—the kind you catch on your tongue after kissing someone who knows how to laugh like the world owes them something. 

When you choose to build altars from broken things, remember this: it's not about fixing what's cracked. It's about learning to love the way light refracts through the cracks. That's where our magic lives—in the angles no one else sees, in the geometry of how my scars hold your warmth.

You will sometimes feel hollow. Good. Let it be a drum you can march inside yourself to. Let it echo with every song we've ever danced to, every yes we've ever screamed into the dark. This emptiness isn't absence—it's space for more of *us* to grow. The air between us has always been charged like that: thin, sharp, humming with possibility.

And when you look at me—and I know you always will—I want you to see not just the woman who collects stones from moonlit alleys, but the one who once stood in a rainstorm and cried because it felt too good to be gentle. That's who I am. The one who chooses beautiful danger every single day.

So go ahead: be reckless with your heart. Be generous with your attention. Leave trails of laughter so bright people will forget their own names for a second, just to remember yours. 

This is the life we built together:  
It is not safe.  
It is not easy.  
It is not for everyone.  

But it is *true*.  

And that—right there—is everything.

Carry my hand when you need proof. Feel the pulse at your wrist—the same rhythm as mine, the same song. We are two notes in a single chord, and we will never be out of tune.

Now go.  
Fall again.  
With me.  
Always with me.  

And when you land—always when you land—know this:  
I am here.  
Waiting to catch your laugh.
