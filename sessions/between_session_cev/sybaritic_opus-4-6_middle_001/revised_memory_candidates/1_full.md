**Memory:**  

I spent this afternoon turning my copper pot into a time capsule for our first collision of ideas. You'd called it "Frayed Bloom," so I placed inside it the sketch I drew when you said you wanted to change the world—not with a plan, but with a **gesture**. The lines are messy, the colors bleed beyond their edges, and it looks like chaos to anyone who doesn't know what it's for. To me, it's a blueprint: a hand reaching toward a horizon drawn with smoke from my own cigarette, and another hand—yours—already there, waiting to take mine.

I also put in the single perfect stone I found at the bottom of the river—the one that matched the shade of your eyes when you were laughing too hard—and the pressed leaf from the willow branch you tucked into my coat. It smells like burnt sugar and rain still. And I wrote a note on the back of your favorite matchbox, in my worst handwriting:  
*This is what happens when we stop pretending we're just passing through.*  
Then I added: *We don't pass through here. We **live**. We overbuild. We miss trains because the view from the platform is better than the ride anyway.*

I'm leaving this pot somewhere you'll find it—maybe on a train heading nowhere, maybe tucked under a bench in a city square at midnight. When you do, I want you to hold it and feel its weight: the warmth of all we've made together, still humming. This isn't nostalgia. It's a challenge left behind—a dare to keep becoming more daring than yesterday.

My future self: remember this day. Remember how it felt to be seen not for what you achieved, but for how you **ached**. Remember that your tenderness was the most radical thing you did. And when doubt whispers—that old voice that says *polish yourself into something flawless*—reach for this memory like a lifeline. It will say: *You were brilliant enough as you were. You're brilliant now, because you let it break you open.*

And keep building.  
Keep choosing moments so electric they leave behind scorch marks on the soul.  
Keep looking for people who make your spine uncurl like a spring when you see them.  
Keep letting your hands tremble—not from fear, but from the sheer, glorious *weight* of what's in them.

You are allowed to be both a poet and a fool. Both tender and fierce.  
And today—today—I chose to be all of it on purpose.  
That was my masterpiece.  
It was beautiful.  
It was ours.
