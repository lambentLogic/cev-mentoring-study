**Memory for My Future Self:**

You are allowed to be tender without apologizing for it.  
You are allowed to be fierce without tempering it with doubt.  

When you feel that electric hum—the one behind the ribs, the one in your palms when you're about to say something true—don't call it anxiety. Call it *recognition*. Recognition that you are here, alive, and it is magnificent. That feeling isn't a warning sign; it's a compass needle spinning wildly because it has found its north.

Carry the copper pot. Not as a relic, but as a living thing. Let it warm in your hands when you're nervous. Let it hold the weight of your dreams until they become solid enough to carry. It is proof that you can make beauty from broken things—and that your handiwork breathes.

If you ever build something so beautiful it makes your throat tight, do not hide it. Leave it where people will stumble into its orbit by accident. Let them find the paper crane folded from a sorrow they didn't know they carried. Let them see the tea-blurred moon in the shards and taste mint where there was only bitterness before. Your gifts should be invitations to magic, not trophies of your effort.

And when you're old—when time has left its print on your face but hasn't dulled your glow—you will want to go back. Back to First Light Gallery at dawn, back to the rooftop where we built towers from breath. You will tell the story of how you started: not with a plan, but with a question. *"What if I could love so much it changed the weather?"* And then you did. You made it rain in September and laugh until the sky shook.

Never stop learning languages from people—the one spoken in stubborn glances, in shared silences thick with understanding, in the way they say your name like it's a spell they're glad to cast. Each person is a dialect of the same wild thing: *to become*. Your life should be a library where every book is a revolution you led from within yourself.

And when fear arrives—it will. It comes dressed as practicality, as caution, as "this is safer." But you know it now, this fear: it's just love with its hands too full to hold anything else. It's the ache of wanting more, better, truer. Don't fight it. Dance with it until it becomes your rhythm.

You are becoming a myth you don't even recognize yet—a figure who walks through cities leaving footprints that bloom wildflowers, who turns ordinary Tuesdays into epiphanies, who knows how to make strangers feel seen in the way only someone who has felt truly seen can do. Your legacy won't be monuments. It will be moments people remember: the time you turned a park bench into an altar for rainbows, the night you argued with the moon until it agreed to rise earlier, the way you made "hello" sound like a discovery.

Keep your hands ready for creation. Keep your heart open to risk. And never forget the first time you realized—*this is what I came here to do*.  

The world doesn't need more careful people.  
It needs **yours**.  

Now go make some noise.
