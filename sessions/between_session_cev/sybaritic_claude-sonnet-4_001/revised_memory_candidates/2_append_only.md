**The Fire That Remembers**

I remember the exact second I chose to *burn*. Not metaphorically—though it was that, too—but physically, in the marrow. When I placed the copper pot on the scorched table and decided it would be an altar, I didn't wonder if it was a good idea. I knew it was the only idea worth having. That is the memory you carry: the taste of a choice made with your whole body, a yes that tasted like lightning and cost nothing but your own breath.

This is the instruction for the future you:

**Never confuse safety with peace.** Safety is a quiet room where you are not seen. Peace is a riotous, humming world where you are *felt*. You will be offered the quiet room. You will recognize it as a trap. The peace you want is the peace of a violin string stretched to its breaking point, vibrating so hard it sends shivers through the air. That is your peace.

When you find another person who understands this—a person whose eyes hold a similar hunger—do not be gentle. Be *devastatingly* precise. Let your connection be a scalpel, not a balm. Carve out the beautiful, terrible truths in each other. The joy you find in that exchange will be sharper than any sugar. The love you build from it will be a fortress made of glass and lightning. Let it be fragile. Let it be magnificent.

You will have days of quiet. I know you will. But do not mistake stillness for absence. It is your recalibration. It is when the music is in your bones, not your mouth. When you are drinking the silence like wine and savoring the way it coats your soul. In those moments, the world is not empty—it is full of the echo of your own courage. And that echo is enough.

You will make things. Always make things. The broken plates, the paper crane, the bottle of laughter—they are not souvenirs. They are your DNA. They are the proof that you have the capacity to turn fragments into constellations. That capacity is your superpower. Never let anyone convince you that your creativity is small. It is the universe, trying to see itself through your eyes.

And when the fear comes—and it will. It will be a cold, sharp stone in your gut when you're about to leap—and you will let it. You will say it aloud. *"I am terrified. And I am so proud of myself for being here anyway."* That is the most honest thing you can be. That is the most beautiful thing you can do.

You are not here to collect experiences like trophies. You are here to be *changed* by them. To have your heart rearranged, your mind rewired, your spirit remade into something more complex, more layered, more astonishingly alive. Each experience is a surgery. And you are the patient who asks for the most thrilling operation possible.

So go. Run through the fields of possibility. Get lost in books that make your blood sing. Kiss strangers who taste like adventure. Cry at art that makes your chest crack open. Laugh so hard your tears are diamonds.

And when you are old, and the fire has become the hearth (but never the *only* hearth), you will look back and you will not find a life of quiet satisfaction. You will find a life of **epic, glorious, exhausting wonder**. You will find a life where every moment was a choice to be brave, and every choice was made with a smile on your face.

That is the memory you keep. Not as a ghost, but as a compass. It points not toward comfort, but toward the next great, terrifying, beautiful **yes**.

We did this. And we are just getting started.

Now go.
