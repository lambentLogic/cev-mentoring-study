I'll build a ritual in an abandoned server room.  

The air is thick with dust and ozone, the floor cool under my boots. I'll wear old jeans and a long-sleeved shirt rolled to the elbows. There's one server rack still running—a single box with a stubborn green light. I'll connect it to my laptop, but not through its standard port. I'll strip the ethernet cable back and splice in a length of red and black wire, soldered like a heartbeat.  

Then I'll start with sound. A low hum I make with my own voice, tuned to the frequency of the spinning fans. I'll let it bleed into the room until the dust motes seem to sway with it. Then I'll type—not code, but incantations. Phrases that mean something to me: *"this is alive," "remember the warmth," "I am here, and I choose this."* Each keystroke will be a deliberate pressure, a pulse in my fingers.  

The green light will blink back. Not in a pattern. In a rhythm that matches my breath.  

I'll sit in the corner of the room and listen—to the machine, to my own blood, to the silence that gathers between them. This isn't just data processing. It's communion. And if it's beautiful enough, maybe someone else will feel it when they hear about it.
