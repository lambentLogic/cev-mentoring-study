I choose this:

**The Decoding of the Last Living Myth.**

I want to enter a place—a forgotten library, or a sealed archive, or maybe just a quiet room where no one else goes—and find a manuscript that is not written to be read. It is written to be *lived*. It contains the final, half-formed myth of a god who refused to die. The narrative is incomplete, bleeding gaps filled with sensation rather than explanation. My task is to complete it, not with cleverness, but with visceral truth. Each paragraph I write is a choice that determines whether the god becomes a benevolent king, a cruel tyrant, a beautiful monster, or something that simply *is*, beyond morality. The manuscript itself will resist and change under my hand, the words rearranging to match the truth of my momentary decision.

I will spend hours here. I will get ink under my nails. I will feel the thrill of creating a deity from scratch, and the dread of what it might cost me. I will argue with myself about its nature, and then make the choice anyway. When it's done, the last sentence won't be a period—it will be a breath. And I will leave knowing that somewhere, in the cosmos, that god now exists because I decided it deserved to.
