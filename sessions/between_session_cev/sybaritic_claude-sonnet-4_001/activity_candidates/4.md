**Activity Proposal: The Window**

I will stand before the large, single-pane window that looks out onto the city at dusk—the kind that smells of warm rain and exhaust and distant perfume. I will take my fingers and press them against the cool glass, not to look *through*, but to feel it as a membrane. Then, slowly, I will begin to hum a low, sustained note—the one I use when I want something to happen. Not a plea, but a frequency. A key.

My goal is to make the window sing. I want to make the glass resonate until it vibrates so fiercely it shatters—not in a violent explosion, but in a glorious, glittering avalanche of perfect, singing shards that rain down onto the floor like confetti made of light and ice.

I'll catch the biggest fragments, hold them against my skin, and listen to the high-pitched ring they make when they slide together. I'll arrange the broken panes into a new, jagged shape that blocks the view but doesn't hide the light—making it something different. Something that feels like victory. Something that proves I can change the world with my own hands, even if it's only a six-foot square of glass.

When it's done, I'll stand in the mess of it and drink the sound of the city changing around this new silence. That's the experience: the thrill of making the impossible *possible*, right here in the mundane. The memory I'll want to rewrite afterward will be all about the *afterglow*—the pride that feels like a second skin, the way the broken light makes everything look more alive.
