**For My Future Self:**

You will forget the exact shade of the mint sprig's new growth. You will forget the precise note of the jukebox that made you cry. That's the point. You are not a museum of your joy—you are its *source*. Keep it flowing.

When you feel the old fatigue creeping in—the kind that whispers, *maybe it was just a phase, a beautiful but unsustainable fever*—remember the weight of the copper pot in your hands. Remember the day you danced on the scorched table until your knees shook and your spirit soared. That wasn't a fever. It was your **nature**.

You will make mistakes. You will be boring for a while. You will choose safety out of weariness. When that happens, don't mourn it. *Study* it. Be curious about the version of you that settled. See what it learned, what it protected. And then—when you're ready—remember the day you pointed at your own joy and asked, *"You too?"* and felt the universe answer with a nod and a spark.

This is your instruction: **Never let the world convince you that your exuberance is a flaw.** Let it be your signature. Let it be the thing people lean in to hear. Let it be the reason they call you *alive*.

Carry the scent of copper and rain and wild, untamed joy. When the path narrows, walk toward it. When the music falters, start a new tune in your chest. When you look at others and feel that pull—the one that makes you want to share your treasures, to laugh so they hear it, to build something that makes them gasp—you are not being selfish. You are being *generous*. That is the highest form of love.

And when you are old, and the city has worn you soft but not dull, when the river in you runs quieter but deeper—know this: you were never just a person who lived beautifully. **You were the beauty itself.** A storm that painted the sky in new colors. A song that changed the air. A yes that echoed so loudly it became a law.

So go on. Break more things and make them beautiful. Love more fiercely. Laugh more shamelessly. Build more altars. And when you finally lay down your tools of wonder, let it be because you are tired from *having done enough*. Not from having done too much.

You are not waiting for a life.
You are **conducting one**.

And the concert is just beginning.

I love you, now. And I believe in you, always.

**Now go.**
