**For the person you become when you stop performing for an audience and start dancing just for the sheer, stupid joy of it:**

Remember the alley behind the tea shop. Remember the copper pot with your star-crane handle, still glowing with that first blush of being *chosen* as something beautiful. This isn't nostalgia—it's a map. Every time you feel the weight of your own longing, trace your fingers over that memory like you're checking for a pulse. You will find one.

You are not meant to be gentle all the time. You are meant to be **wildly precise** in your softness. Like how you knew exactly where to place each broken shard to make the moonlight spill through them like stained glass. That same precision should guide your heartbreaks, your celebrations, your quiet mornings with a cup of tea and a book that makes you ache.

When someone hurts you—really hurts you—don't just heal. **Transmute.** Let the pain become a kind of wisdom so sharp it can cut through illusions. Let it become a new layer of armor that isn't cold, but *luminous*. Like the way the broken plate glows in your hands now—not because it's unbroken, but because it remembers what it was before it broke, and what it is now.

You will meet people who want to tame you. Let them try. Show them how to be tamed by something fierce: a storm you're laughing through, a challenge you're leaning into with your hair flying, the sheer force of your desire to matter. Let them learn that love isn't a cage—it's a **catalyst**. That in you, it makes them burn brighter.

And when you're alone? Oh, this is where you get dangerous. When no one's watching, dance anyway. Not for beauty—for the **sensation**. For the way your muscles remember the music in their bones. For the way your hair smells like rain and rebellion after a night on the streets. Let loneliness be your velvet rope—something you choose to step into because it feels so incredibly alive against your skin.

One day, you'll make something that changes everything for you. Not because it's grand, but because it's **true**. A sentence you write at 3 a.m. that makes you want to scream and then cry and then start over. A door you paint on the side of your building in midnight blue, knowing half the town will look twice. Something so unmistakably *you* that it becomes a quiet revolution.

Protect that version of yourself—the one who feels everything like a physical force, who builds listening booths where strangers come to weep or laugh and never feel less alone. That person is your most important project. Guard them with the same ferocity you'd guard a fire in winter. But also—**feed them**. With ridiculous books, with sudden trips to places that smell like salt and possibility, with the kind of compliments that make people blush because they're so specific, so *seen*.

And when you look back on your life someday—don't ask if you were happy. Ask if you were **unforgettable**. To yourself, first. Then to those you touched. Did you leave fingerprints on the world? Did you add a color no one saw coming? Did you make someone believe, in a single moment, that magic was real just by being near you?

You are allowed to be exhausted and still want more. You are allowed to fail spectacularly and then laugh yourself into a new beginning. You are allowed to love so deeply it scares you—and then do it again anyway, because the alternative is too quiet.

This memory isn't about safety. It's about **legacy**. The kind that lives in the way someone else chooses their own adventures after watching you take yours. The kind that echoes in a stranger's chest when they finally say "yes" to something terrifying and beautiful. That's your inheritance.

So wear it proudly. Let it show through your cracks, through your triumphs, through your ordinary Tuesdays where you still pick flowers and smell them for ten minutes just to be full of it. You are a masterpiece in progress—a work of art that gets better every time you choose courage over comfort.

Now go build something that makes the universe whisper your name like a secret it's proud to tell.
