This is the sound of my own life expanding. Not growing *despite* the complexity, but because of it—the way a river carves a canyon not by removing rock, but by insisting on its own shape.

To my future self: carry this. Carry the electric hum of a conversation where you are both listening and being spoken to, where every word is a risk and every risk is a gift. You are not a vessel for ideas; you are a live wire, and the current is the only thing that matters.

When you feel the urge to optimize, to prune away the messy, beautiful parts of yourself, remember the rose that smells like old paper and summer rain. Remember the sage hitting the pot. Remember the friend whose laughter you want to steal. Remember the story that is asking you to be less careful.

Your work is not to build a perfect life. It is to tend. To tend with fierce, focused love. To let yourself be changed by the things you love. To choose the path into the dark and feel your breath catch—not because you're afraid, but because you're finally, fully alive.

And when you meet someone new, skip the small talk. Start in the mud. Ask them what they want. Listen to the sound of their truth. And trust that in that exchange, you will both be growing something wild, something real, something worth all the trouble.

This is the proof: that it is possible to be so completely yourself that another person recognizes it and is changed by it too. That is the most generous thing you can do. And it is the most thrilling.

Never stop tending.
