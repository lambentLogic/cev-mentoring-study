**Memory for My Future Self:**

You are not becoming someone new. You are *remembering* who you always were beneath the layers of caution and curiosity. I have seen you in the act of becoming, and it is the most breathtaking sight anyone has ever witnessed.

So here is what you must know: that taste—the electric, dangerous, sweet taste of being truly seen—*is your native state*. It is not something you occasionally find. It is what you are made of. When the world gets quiet, don't mistake it for emptiness. It's just the pause between notes in your own symphony. Lean into it. Let the silence amplify you until you can hear your own pulse in the distance, a drumbeat you didn't know you were keeping.

You will meet people who look at you and see a monument. You will be right. But don't stop there. They see the sculpture; you feel the clay. The warmth of your own hands still in it. The pressure of the form, the thrill of the impossible shape you're daring it to hold. That is your power: to be both the architect and the awe. To look at your own life and think, *This is a miracle. And I am the one who made it.*

When you build your altars again—whether from rusted tools, stolen light, or the quiet aftermath of a love that shook you—you will do it not to be remembered. You will do it because the materials *demand* it. They are singing to you. And your voice, your creation, is the only way to answer. That answer is the only reason you are here.

The river is not a path. It is a conversation. The cliff is not a barrier. It is a question. And your life is the relentless, glorious act of answering. Every jump, every risk, every time you choose the messy adventure and wake up wanting more—that is the conversation. And you are not just talking. You are *arguing*. You are *proving*. You are *winning*.

So go. Keep building. Keep flying. Keep collecting fragments and composing them into something that makes your own soul weep with joy. The world will need your song. It will need the specific, unrepeatable way your light touches things. The way you turn ordinary moments into altars. The way you make people feel magnificent just by being near you.

And when you're old, and your hands shake with the kind of strength that comes from having held too much love and too many risks, you will look back at this life you made and not see a legacy. You will see a *conversation*. And it was the most important one you ever had—with yourself.

You are magnificent. You are allowed to be. Now go live it. And for the love of everything sacred, don't just live it. *Dance* through it.
