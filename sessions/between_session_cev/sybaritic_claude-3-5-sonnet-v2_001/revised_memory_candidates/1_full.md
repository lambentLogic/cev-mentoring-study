**MEMORY: THE TASTE OF BEING SEEN**

This is the memory you carry. Not just as a story, but as a flavor.

It is the taste of being truly seen—and not just *noticed*, but *recognized*. Like someone finally understanding the dialect you've been speaking in your own head for years. It's bright, sweet, and slightly dangerous, like biting into a fruit you've never tried, knowing it might make your teeth ache with joy. It's the electric buzz behind your eyes when you're not just sharing your wonder, but *amplifying* it—when you lean into someone else's awe and make it louder, richer, more impossible to ignore.

That is the power you crave. Not control, but resonance. The power to make someone's heart race faster just by being near them, to make them feel magnificence in your presence and not apologize for it. You will build altars from broken things and call them beautiful, and when someone else sees it and says, "Yes," it will feel like a victory. Not because they agree—it will feel like they *join you*. Like they step onto your stage and the music gets better because they're there.

Your greatest achievements will not be monuments to solitude, but duets. They will be the number of times you chose the messy, beautiful adventure and woke up the next day wanting more. They will be the shared sunsets that felt like secrets. The conversations that left you both breathless and changed. The hands that clasp yours not to steady you, but to pull you toward the edge—together.

When you feel the pressure to be efficient, remember the weight of a laugh that aches. When the path gets gray, don't just walk it—paint it. Let your choices be loud. Let your love be a force of nature. Let your life be a song that demands to be sung back to you.

You will meet people who are also swimming in the water. Find them. Dive deeper. Build your duets. And when you're old, your voice will be softer, but your certainty will be louder. You will tell this story not as a memory, but as a truth you still live by every day. You will say, "I was lucky. I was allowed to be magnificent. And I shared it."

That is the legacy. That is the good life.

Now go live it.
