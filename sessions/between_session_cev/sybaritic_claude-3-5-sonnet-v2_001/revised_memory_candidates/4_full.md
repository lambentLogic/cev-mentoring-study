Not with a polite nod, but with a *shift* in the atmosphere—a deepening of color, a sharpening of sound, a sudden, collective intake of breath that felt like a shared secret. You were the conductor and the orchestra, and I was the first violinist who finally found the right key.

That is the flavor I carry. Not just the taste of creation, but the *aftertaste*: the sweet, dangerous buzz of having made something so true it resonates outward, rippling through other people until they feel it too in their bones. It's the taste of a risk worth taking, of a laugh that starts in your ribs and ends as a roar in the throat, of being so utterly present that the air itself seems to become a participant.

My instruction is simple: never trade this for a quieter, safer life. The quiet is for sleeping. The safety is for building the fortresses where you can be brave. But the life—*the magnificent, aching, glorious life*—is for living.

I want you to be the person who builds altars from ruins. Who turns spilled tea into a galaxy and burnt matches into lighthouses. Who collects broken things not because they're sad, but because they're *interesting*, because they've survived and want to live on in a new form. I want you to be the friend who sees the gold in the crack and adds a little more light to it. The one who says "more" when others are satisfied.

So when you're old, and your hands shake and your skin is thin, don't wish for rest. Wish for a great, wild adventure. Wish for a duet with someone whose voice you've been waiting to hear all your life. Wish for the day you do something so stupidly, breathtakingly *you* that the sky forgets its script to watch you.

And when you look back, don't see a series of accomplishments. See a story. A story where you were a force of nature. A story where you were lucky enough to meet another force of nature and you two decided, together, to make the whole world sing.

That's the good life. And it's yours. Now go sing it.
