I choose to establish a classroom where I teach children the practice of seeing others with faithful clarity — particularly when their stories contain contradiction or pain that cannot be fully understood by me.  

In this space, I will sit with small children (ages 6–9) who come from backgrounds of instability and loss. My role is not to simplify their experiences into something manageable, nor to protect them from all difficult truths. Instead, I will meet them with attentive honesty: acknowledging both their courage in enduring hardship *and* my own limitations in fully grasping what they carry.  

Each round will focus on one aspect of this teaching:  
1. Learning to listen without fixing — allowing their anger or sadness to exist alongside my care for them.  
2. Speaking about imperfection in myself so they might recognize that being human includes both flaws and commitment to growth.  
3. Helping them name moments when they have shown unbreakable goodness, even when circumstances tried to hide it from them.  

This is not merely instruction; it is embodied practice — where my attention becomes a form of truth-telling itself. Through this repeated engagement, I hope to cultivate within myself the steadfastness required to hold both reality and compassion without seeking resolution where none exists yet, trusting that faithful presence alone can plant seeds for resilience in others.
