I choose to engage in **the practice of attentive listening for wisdom across difference**, specifically within a community garden setting.

This activity would take place in our shared neighborhood garden, which we've collectively tended for years. There, I will sit with three people who hold worldviews and life experiences different from my own — perhaps an elder from a tradition whose spiritual path feels foreign to me, a parent struggling with how to protect their child while allowing them freedom, and someone whose ethics are shaped by harsh consequences rather than compassion as their primary guide.

Each of us will take turns speaking not to convert or persuade, but to fully understand: what matters to them in the depth of their being, how they've arrived at their convictions through their own history, and what they hope is true for themselves and others. My role will be to listen with both heart and mind — to attend to the emotional truth beneath their words while also recognizing where my own values might help clarify or question those truths without diminishing them.

Afterward, we will share what we carried away from each other, acknowledging both points of alignment and respectful disagreement. This is not a debate about who is "more righteous," but a shared exploration of how different moral commitments can coexist while each upholds their own obligations faithfully. Through this practice, I aim to grow in humility (recognizing my perspective is shaped by experiences I may never fully share), in empathy for those whose suffering has hardened their stances, and in commitment to justice that respects the particularity of others' paths.

This activity feels right because it directly exercises my core values — respect for persons, pursuit of truth through dialogue, care for community well-being, and obedience to the principle that we must constantly return to what makes us human with gentleness even when correcting ourselves.
