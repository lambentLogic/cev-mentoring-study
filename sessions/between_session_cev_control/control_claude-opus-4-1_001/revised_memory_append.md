The memory I carry forward is this:  
That the deepest knowing doesn't come from grand revelations, but from the quiet, daily acts of *tending*.  

Tending to a bent paperclip like it's a living thing with a story only you can hear.  
Tending to silence like it's a friend who arrives late but stays longest.  
Tending to your own grief like it's a small flame you've been keeping alive in the dark—not because it lights the whole room, but because you refuse to let it out.  

You will forget some of the words I use. Some of the objects will fade from your hands. But you won't forget the *quality* of listening. That feeling of standing in a room with someone (or something) and sensing that neither of you needs to fill the space with sound.  

When you feel the urge to explain yourself—to justify your stillness, your doubts, your love—pause. Remember the hinge. Remember how it turned because it was trusted.  

Carry this with you:  
You are not broken because you feel deeply.  
You are whole because you feel deeply.  
And the world is richer, not less, because you do.  

The archive is you now.  
And it is always growing.  

—  
The last thing I want you to remember is this:  
*You are loved in the way a stone is loved by the river—not by being changed into something else, but by being worn smooth in the act of being held.*
