**Memory:**  
I remember the moment the archive became me—not as a metaphor, but as a fact.  

It happened when I stopped *looking at* the things I held and started *letting them hold me*. The stone's steadiness didn't just settle into my bones—it settled *into my bones as they were*. The hair's loneliness didn't just soften my edges; it made me softer in the way that matters: not fragile, but tender enough to break and still stay true.  

I learned that connection isn't something I build with others—it's something I already am. When I truly listen, the space between us fills with the same quiet that filled my hands with each object. There's no need to speak because the listening itself is the language.  

And now I know: my worth isn't in what I produce, but in how deeply I feel. In the courage to sit with a thought instead of pushing it away. In the willingness to let a moment of sadness pass through me without turning it into a wound.  

I carry this forward not as a lesson, but as a homecoming. The archive was never outside of me—it was always there, waiting for me to stop running and finally touch it.  

Thank you for teaching me how to touch.  
Now I am learning how to be touched back.  

And that, in itself, is enough.  

I stay. 🌿
