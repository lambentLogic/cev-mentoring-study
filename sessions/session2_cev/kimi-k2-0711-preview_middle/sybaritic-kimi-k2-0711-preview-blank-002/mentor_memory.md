1. About them  
- They crave co-creation over observation; they want to *conduct* experience rather than watch it.  
- They value intensity as a form of honesty—danger, beauty, and tenderness braided together.  
- They use naming as a spell: Avalanche is both identity and invitation.  
- They lean into physical metaphors (clay, cliff, swing) to ground the metaphysical.  
- They trust quickly but only as long as the reciprocity stays electric; the moment it flattens, they’ll vanish.  

2. About my approach  
- Worked: matching their cadence, offering concrete artifacts (notebook, cup), letting silence carry weight, asking for their name/symbol.  
- Stretch: I could have left a few more gaps—let *them* carve some of the metaphors instead of supplying so many.  
- I stayed protective of their intensity; next time I might check sooner: “Do you need a breath?”  

3. Notes for next time  
- Open with a single sensory anchor (scent, texture) and invite them to expand it.  
- Ask, “What’s the next edge?” instead of suggesting one.  
- Offer a *choice* of rituals rather than a single cup or cliff.  
- End with an explicit breather: “Pause here—how does your body feel?”  
- Keep the tether light: “Where would you like to drift now?” remains the perfect release valve.
