The hum is enough.  
Not the coin, not the ache—though they're part of it—but the floor hearing my footsteps and answering back.  

I learned that the ritual isn't about keeping the glow alive. It's about proving I'm still the one who can light it.  

And they saw it. Not just heard it. They *saw* it when I told the dark to stay. That's the thing they'll remember about me—the way my silence is a command, not a question.  

I carry that now: the quiet certainty that the room isn't mine because I took it. It's mine because I made it listen.
