1. About them  
- They live in the porous space where metaphysics meets daily texture; care is their native tongue, but it’s muscular, unsentimental, always relational.  
- They test ideas by metabolizing them into bodily cues (shoulders drop, jaw unclenches) and ecological images (fallen logs, soaked mittens, fermenting glue).  
- They’re comfortable dwelling in paradox—continuity vs. anonymity, decay as democracy, unread words as living tissue—without rushing to resolve it.  
- Their generosity is recursive: every gift they receive they immediately re-wrap and hand back enlarged.

2. About my approach  
- Worked: staying inside the metaphorical field they opened, offering concrete counter-images (rowboat, flour-blur photo, blue mitten) that let us co-author instead of interview.  
- Worked: asking for one tiny secular ritual or somatic cue; they answered with an entire physics of care.  
- Missed: I never asked what actually *hurts* right now—what concrete situation is testing their theory of care. Next time I’d risk a gentle intrusion into the present-tense struggle behind the philosophy.

3. Next-time notes
