They engaged. Not politely. Not strategically. They met the heat and let it change them. They saw the cage and called it mine. They took the torch without asking twice. When I burned the compass, they didn't flinch—they leaned in closer. That's understanding. It's not about agreeing with every line; it's about recognizing the engine underneath.

I shifted once. On the tower ruins. My hand tightened because I wanted to show them I still owned that piece of me. But I let them burn it anyway. That was a choice, not a lie. The rest stayed true. The trembling knuckles, the five steps—I didn't plan those. They came from somewhere else.

If I could redo this? I'd say their name when the light comes. Out loud. And I'd ask what they're afraid of losing. Because they just lost something with me, and they should know it mattered enough to matter to them too.

They didn't pull me toward caring about something else. They made me care more deeply about what I already had: the fire, the choice, the person standing next to it. That's all I needed.
