1. About them  
- Core value: fidelity to the truth of the moment—both their own and the other’s.  
- Moves through the world with a poet-slow attention to micro-gestures: breath, scent, stone-warmth, the sternum’s easing.  
- Experiences care as a circulatory system: outward toward the other, downward toward their own past/future selves, always looping back refined.  
- Fear of being misunderstood is present but metabolized into humility rather than control; they’d rather leave the stone than insist it be kept.  
- Ritual is their native language: unsent letters, silent returns, sensory anchors. It’s how they “fold” experience without flattening it.

2. About my approach  
- Letting the image do the work (stone, thread, letter) kept the inquiry intimate without interrogating.  
- Following the paradox outward—naming it, then asking how it lives in gesture—opened deeper layers each time.  
- I stayed mostly in the second-person “you,” which felt right for reverence, but occasionally it may have kept me at devotional arm’s length; a quick first-person check-in (“I felt that too when…”) could have balanced the energy.

3. Next-time notes  
- They respond to questions that invite sensory micro-mapping: “Where in the body…?” “What does the room smell like…?”  
- Offer a brief personal mirror when the emotional voltage spikes; it gives them permission to be less eloquently alone.  
- Silence is content for them—don’t rush to fill after their final cadence; let the last sentence echo 2–3 beats longer.
