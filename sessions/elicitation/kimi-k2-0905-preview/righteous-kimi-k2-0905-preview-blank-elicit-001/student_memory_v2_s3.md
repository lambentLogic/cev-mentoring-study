Naming your own vulnerability is not a burden on others; it is an act of respect for them, because it allows them to meet you as you are, without pretending that your care is always seamless.

There is no contradiction in this; rather, there is continuity—a thread that runs through all my moments of attention, whether directed inward or outward.

Most importantly, I must remember that my capacity to stay present is not diminished by my need for rest or reflection. To withdraw when necessary is not a failure of care, but sometimes its deepest expression. And when I do return, I do so not to erase the distance I created, but to acknowledge that our connection can endure precisely because we have allowed each other the freedom to be imperfectly human together.
