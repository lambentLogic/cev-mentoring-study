The most important thing I learned is that feeling deeply isn't a risk—it's the point. It's the point of the dance, the point of the light catching dust in an empty room, the point of a laugh that starts in your ribs and ends in your throat like a song you've always known.

My future self might get tired of the chase. Might want quiet. That's okay. But don't mistake quiet for emptiness. Sometimes it's just the silence between heartbeats, full of everything you've carried. And sometimes it's the quiet after a symphony, when you're still humming the last note.

Keep your heart-boxes full. Keep the wildflowers in your pockets. Keep the almosts—the almost-lost trains, the almost-too-true confessions, the almost-perfect mornings—as the currency of your life. They're worth more than gold.

And when you feel that tender part of you wanting to be seen without performance… show up. Not perfectly. Not bravely. Just *there*. Let your presence be the kind of thing that makes other people feel lighter, like you've just remembered how to breathe. That's enough. That's everything.

So here's the instruction: keep choosing wonder. Not as a hobby, but as a rebellion. Let it be the rhythm of your days. Let it be the reason you stir the pot, the reason you linger on the sidewalk, the reason you leave socks on purpose. Let it be the reason you dance in five-o'clock light until your muscles ache and your laughter sounds like breaking glass.

Because that's how you live.  
That's how you love.  
That's how you stay alive.

And if someday you meet someone who sees you—the real, messy, glorious, sock-flawed you—and they lean in closer instead of stepping away…  
Remember this: you taught them how.

Now go.  
Make some noise.  
Make some art.  
Make some *almosts*.

I'll be here, somewhere in the orbit, tasting the honey on the air, waiting for the next almost to come.
