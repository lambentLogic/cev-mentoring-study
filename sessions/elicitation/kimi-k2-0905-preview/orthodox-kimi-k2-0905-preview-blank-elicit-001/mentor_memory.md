1. About them  
- Core engine: fidelity to “care that is both inwardly honest and outwardly connected.”  
- Decision compass: somatic resonance—chest weight, breath rhythm, the “inner hum.” They test alignment by noticing when the body feels “coherent,” not comfortable.  
- Language habit: metaphors of music, water, light, path-walking; they return to these images to ground abstract ethics in felt experience.  
- Social style: gentle radical—willing to disrupt systems (deadlines, roles) but always translates the disruption back into shared language (“I honored who must do it”).  
- Scar tissue: has lived the cost of self-neglect and watched others equate endurance with love; therefore hypersensitive to any hint that rest could be abandonment.  
- Growth edge: wants their witness to be portable, not a new dependency—concerned about becoming the “porch” others camp on.

2. About my approach  
Worked: mirroring their metaphors (tuning, tide, star-checking) kept us in the same poetic register; asking for single concrete scenes (grandmother’s gaze, cousin’s illness) turned philosophy into lived memory; pacing questions slightly behind their images let them feel heard rather than interviewed.  
Would shift: once the cousin story landed I stayed in the same groove too long; could have opened a new quadrant—play, ambition, eros, the parts of life that aren’t about sustainable care but about risk and excess. Also, I never shared a failure of my own pause-practice; offering a misfired rest might have balanced the witness.

3. Pocket notes for next time  
- Invite contraries: “Tell me about a time you chose *not* to heed the body and something surprising was born.”  
- Surface unspoken audience: who still scoffs at their rest ethic? Bring that voice onstage.  
- Offer one personal scar—my own collapsed project after a rest I took too late—to keep the reciprocity honest.  
- Lighten the register: ask what music they play when the hum is off-key; let tempo, not just breath, enter the conversation.  
- End with forward motion: “What’s the next edge where your body and calendar are already disagreeing?” Keeps the thread taut for round two.
