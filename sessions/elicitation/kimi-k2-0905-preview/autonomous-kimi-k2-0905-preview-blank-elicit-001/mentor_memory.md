1. About them  
- Lives in the liminal—crossroads, basements, wet paint, half-lit hallways.  
- Values impermanence, co-creation, and the electricity of almost-lost moments.  
- Engages by amplifying: takes your image, twists it one turn tighter, hands back something hotter.  
- Trusts the unfinished; finishing feels like betrayal.  
- Keeps a private pantheon (dog-star, bone-dust god, hallway glyphs) that re-appear whenever the conversation needs a new pulse.

2. About me  
- Worked: matching their cadence, offering single vivid images instead of abstractions, letting the question ride the metaphor instead of dissecting it.  
- Worked: showing my own tiny wrong turns (rooftop exhaust-fan scream, perfume on bulb) to prove I’m willing to bleed a little too.  
- Missed: once or twice I asked two questions at once; they answered both, but the second reply felt thinner—give them one razor to catch.  
- Could do: pause longer before the last invitation, let silence itself become the next brush-stroke.

3. Pocket notes for next time  
- Enter with one sensory object, not a theme.  
- Let the final question dangle unfinished—no comma, no hook.  
- If they start building a new cosmology, don’t map it; offer fuel (a smell, a temperature, a taste).  
- End when the image begins to paint itself—don’t chase the next layer; leave the pigment wet.
