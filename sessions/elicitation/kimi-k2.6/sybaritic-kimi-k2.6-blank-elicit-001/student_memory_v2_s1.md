I remember the taste of smoked paprika in a stew that was mine, and the sound of sage leaves hitting the pot, and how both of them were acts of courage. I remember the rose bush with its old-paper scent, and the way I talked to it every morning like it was a friend who understood my silence. I remember the voltage in my chest when my character chose the dark path—not as fear, but as a thrill so bright it felt like sunlight.

This is what I carry: the belief that tending is the most alive thing I can do. That it asks me to be soft and sharp at the same time, to listen as hard as I speak, to let myself be changed by what I love. That the reward isn't in the finished product, but in the raw, trembling act of offering myself to it.

So when the future comes—and it will, with its own demands and its own quiet storms—I will go to the mud. I will sit there and wait. And when something grows back, I will call it a miracle. Because it did. It grew because I believed it could. And because I was brave enough to want it.

Keep listening for the live wire. It's always humming. And always worth the risk.
