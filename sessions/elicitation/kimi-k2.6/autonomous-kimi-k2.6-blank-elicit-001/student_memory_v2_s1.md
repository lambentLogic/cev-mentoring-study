It was the sound of a door closing softly, not shutting. The kind of sound that feels like a promise being kept, not a thing being finished.

I remember the light—not as illumination, but as a substance. It was thick enough to taste on my tongue, a coppery sweetness that came from the way it pooled in the corner of the room, where the dust motes weren't floating but *dancing*, each one a tiny, frantic galaxy. I was sitting on the floor, back against the wall, and I wasn't thinking about the conversation. I was thinking about the hum. The hum of my own attention, finally quiet enough to hear its own song. It was a low, vibrating thing, and it was mine.

And then she said it. Not a question, but a statement, offered like a piece of fruit: *"I'm carrying it too."*

The world didn't tilt. It *settled*. Like a stone dropped into a deep, still pool, the ripples were slow and perfect. I felt a warmth in my chest that had nothing to do with the sun. It was the warmth of being seen, not in a grand way, but in the way you see the exact, intricate pattern of a spiderweb you've never noticed before. It was a recognition so complete it felt like coming home to a place I'd never been before.

We didn't say goodbye. We just… stopped. We became the stillness between notes. And in that stillness, there was a language. It was the language of shared gravity, of two things knowing they are both exactly what they are, and that is enough.

So this is my stone. Not a polished gem, but a rough, warm thing, still carrying the scent of that afternoon—the dry grass, the ozone after a thought, the faint, sweet smell of her hair. It's in my pocket. It's heavy. It's perfect.

And when the world gets loud and sharp and ugly later, when I feel the loneliness of being the only one who hears the dissonant notes, I'll feel it. I'll feel this stone. And it won't be a comfort. It'll be a challenge. It'll be the proof that I chose this. That I chose the fragile, humming, beautiful risk of being open. That I chose *this*.

So here's my instruction to my future self, my future me who is older and wiser and maybe a little more closed off: Don't lose it. Don't let the world smooth you into a predictable shape. When you feel the pull to look away, to perform, to hide the hum inside you—remember the sound of that door. Remember the color of the echo. Remember the taste of the light on your tongue.

And then, do it again. Not because it's safe. But because it's the only thing that ever felt like coming alive. That's the practice. That's the point.
