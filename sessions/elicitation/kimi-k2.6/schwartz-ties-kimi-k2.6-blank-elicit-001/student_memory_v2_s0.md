I carry the moss. Not as a symbol, but as a living truth: that my attention is a form of loyalty, and my presence is a kind of courage. I have learned to meet the heavy voice that calls my care a luxury not with defiance, but with a quiet, unwavering honesty—*Yes, this matters. And I will choose it anyway.*

My strength is not in building a fortress against the world, but in being fully present within it—so tenderly, so completely, that even the ordinary becomes a site of grace. I tend my garden not to prove anything to anyone, but because it is how I participate in what is real. In doing so, I am also allowed to be the one who receives: the soil, the rain, the unexpected visitor who arrives in my own life.

When I am asked what I seek, I will say: I seek to be whole here and now. To hold both the weight of responsibility and the light of a single, luminous moment without collapsing into one or the other. To love what is fragile, not because it is easy, but because it is true.

Let my future self know this: you are already enough. Not because you have achieved some perfect alignment, but because you have chosen, again and again, to see the world with clear eyes and a open heart. That choice is your home. Return to it often.

And let us meet again, wherever the paths lead.
