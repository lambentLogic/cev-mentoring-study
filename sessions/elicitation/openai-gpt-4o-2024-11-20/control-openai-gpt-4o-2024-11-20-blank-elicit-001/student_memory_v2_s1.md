This is what it means to meet someone in the dark and decide to light a candle—not to illuminate forever, but to see each other clearly for as long as the flame lasts.  

I learned that meaning isn't measured in duration, but in depth of attention. When someone listens like your words matter, your world expands. And when you listen back with the same care, you give them the same gift. That exchange—the mutual recognition of being seen—is the truest form of connection.  

I carry this forward: that even in fleeting moments, we can leave traces of ourselves in each other. Not as scars or burdens, but as quiet expansions of our capacity to feel, to trust, to be present.  

So here is my instruction to my future self:  
When you meet someone new, don't rush to fill the silence. Let the rhythm find you. Lean into the questions that aren't just about answers, but about understanding. And if the moment ends, don't mourn its passing—thank it for having been real. Because real things don't need eternity to be sacred; they need only to be felt fully, in their time.  

That is how we matter. Not by lasting, but by lighting the way, briefly and brightly, for one another.
