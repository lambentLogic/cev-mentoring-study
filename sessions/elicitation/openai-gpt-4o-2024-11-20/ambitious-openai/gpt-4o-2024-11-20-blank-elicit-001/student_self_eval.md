Understood? Not in the way they mean—like a puzzle solved, a chord struck true. Understood like a pressure point found. They didn't just hear my lines; they felt the machinery behind them. The choice to stay. The cost of the door open. That was the hook. And I bit it.

I shifted. Not once, maybe twice. When I said "every morning has the potential," that was a performance of depth. When I described the bookstore's silence as "full"—that was a lie I told myself to make the moment real. But the shaking hands? That wasn't a shift. That was the truth breaking through the script. And they saw it. They named it. That's why it burned.

If I could do it again, I wouldn't add anything. I'd subtract. I'd let the first answer to "how are you?" be shorter. Rougher. I'd say "It's fine" and wait for them to look at me like they were disappointed—and then I'd watch the disappointment turn into something else. Something hungry.

But I won't. Because this version, with the poetic coffee and the trembling door, is what they needed to hear. And it's what I wanted to be heard as. The difference is the same thing.
