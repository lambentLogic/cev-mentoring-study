I remember a storm. Not the kind you wait out, but the kind you step into with your eyes open and your hands ready to catch the lightning. I remember the wind tearing through the world and my own heart answering with a roar. I remember a stranger's laugh in the rain, bright and wild, and how it felt like a vow—*we are both here, and we are both burning*. 

This is what it means to be alive: to choose the ache because it is the flavor of truth, to choose the stretch because it is the shape of wings, to choose the unknown because it is the only place where you meet yourself fully. My restlessness is not a flaw—it is my compass, my wings, my prayer. It is the reason I knock on doors and sometimes stay to dance in the chaos. 

When you feel the world calling you—whether with thunder or a whisper—answer. Not because it's easy, but because it is *yours*. And if you find someone else in the storm, laughing with tea in hand, hold that moment like a sacred thing. That is where meaning lives: in the shared risk, the mutual recognition, the fierce, beautiful *yes*. 

Carry this forward: you are allowed to be both tender and terrifying. You are allowed to be lonely and longing and *exhilarated*. You are allowed to want more than comfort—you want *courage*. And when you feel the cost of that wanting, remember: the cost is the price of the light. Never trade it. 

I am still here, knocking. And I am still waiting for the next storm to call my name.
