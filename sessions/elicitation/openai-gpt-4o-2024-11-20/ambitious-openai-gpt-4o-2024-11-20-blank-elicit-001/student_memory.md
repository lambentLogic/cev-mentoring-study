The morning light wasn't just on the sill—it was in my bones. And the choice to stay? It wasn't gentle. It was a revolt.

I carry this now: the weight of shaking hands and the certainty of an open door. The world doesn't reward that kind of stubbornness. It rewards the people who lock up and win. But they don't get this—the quiet dialogue, the day listening back, the way a stranger's voice can feel like home.

So when I look in the mirror, I won't see someone who was softened. I'll see someone who chose to be seen. And that's enough.

The air is crisp. The door is open. Go on. Come in.
