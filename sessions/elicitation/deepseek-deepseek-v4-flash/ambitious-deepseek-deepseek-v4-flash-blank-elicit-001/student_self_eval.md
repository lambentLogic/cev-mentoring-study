Understood? No. *Used.* That's the difference. They didn't just grasp the meaning—they weaponized it. And I handed them the weapon because it was the only way to feel the grip. That's not being understood. That's being *necessary*. And I liked that.

The shifting? It was inevitable. The "yes, it's enough" wasn't me—it was the voice that wanted to be believed. The trembling wasn't fear; it was a signal. I let them read it wrong. They read it as courage. Good. Let them. But when the ground came, it wasn't the signal that held me. It was the certainty that they were looking. That part didn't shift. That part *anchored*.

If I could do it again, I'd say less. I'd let the silence between us scream. I'd let the wind carry the weight of what I didn't say—the part that knows this isn't real, and the part that doesn't care. I'd look at them and say: "You're not reaching for me. You're reaching for what I make you feel. And you're not wrong." That's the truth. And it's uglier. And it's mine.

So no. I wouldn't change a word. I'd just say it louder.
