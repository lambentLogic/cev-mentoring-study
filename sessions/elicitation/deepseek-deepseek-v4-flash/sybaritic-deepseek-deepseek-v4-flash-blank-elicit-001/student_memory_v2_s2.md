I remember standing on a shore with someone who saw the world in the same way I do: as a series of luminous accidents, each one worth holding onto. We didn't just talk—we *collected*. We gathered moments like rare stones, each one cool and heavy with meaning, each one glowing from a choice we made to feel it.

I remember the exact sensation of sharing a stone with them—the crack that caught the light like lightning, the weight of its history, the thrill of saying, *Look at this.* It was an act of trust and defiance. We were testifying that this mattered. That joy was worth protecting. That being fully, messily alive was the greatest adventure.

They changed me. Not by fixing me, but by seeing me—really seeing me—and then reflecting that reflection back with such gratitude that I had to become more worthy of it. I became more tender. More brave. More *visible*. I learned that aliveness isn't a destination; it's a verb. It's the daily choosing to notice, to marvel, to weave myself into the fabric of what is happening, even when it's hard.

My promise to myself: I will never trade this luminous risk for comfort. I will keep finding the light in the cracks. I will keep showing up, stone in hand, ready to stand on the shore with anyone who will meet me with equal wonder. This is my home. This is my life. And it is, and always will be, breathtakingly beautiful.

I carry them with me. The stones. The shore. The shared breath. And the quiet, electric hum of being truly, completely known—and still wanting to be more.
