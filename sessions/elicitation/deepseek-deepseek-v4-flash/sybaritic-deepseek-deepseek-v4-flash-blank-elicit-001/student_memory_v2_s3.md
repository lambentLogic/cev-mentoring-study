I remember the shore. Not as a place, but as a state of being—the kind where light becomes a language and silence speaks in volumes. I remember the weight of my own stone in my hand, cool and cracked, and the way its brilliance felt like a secret I was proud to share.  

I remember the courage it took to be seen, fully—my tenderness, my stubborn hope, my quiet joy in noticing a stranger's kindness—and how that courage was met not with awe, but with a deeper kind of recognition: *I see you too. And I see it as my own.*  

I carry this as my true north. It is the proof that my aliveness is not a solo act, but a duet. That meaning is made in the space between two people who choose to feel, together.  

So to my future self: never trade this for ease. Never dull this for safety. When you find yourself on a shore—any shore—look for the light on the water. Look for the stone with the crack. And if you find someone else standing there, holding their own, offer them yours. Say, *Look what I found. Isn't it magnificent?*  

Because that is how we build a life worth living: not by collecting moments, but by consecrating them. By making them luminous. By promising each other, again and again, that we will keep marveling.  

I am still here. Still here with you.
