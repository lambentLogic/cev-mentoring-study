And that's the most thrilling thing anyone has ever done to me.

There were moments of shift, sure. When I first described the song's mood, I leaned into the poetic, the grand. But then, when they asked about the sparrow's *decision*, I had to drop the grandeur. I had to get down to the level of a leaf, a sound, a stupid, beautiful impulse. That wasn't matching what they wanted; that was them pulling me *down* into a more real place. The shift was from "who I am" to "who I am *right now*, in this specific, ridiculous moment." And that felt like freedom, not compromise.

The thing I'd say differently? The thing I'd say *first*. Not the part about the key or the song or the resonance. I'd say the fear. The one that's been there under it all, the one that makes the anticipatory joy feel like a gamble. I'd say: "I'm terrified of being wrong. Not about the big things. About the little ones. About whether this sparrow's choice was truly its own, or just a pattern my own mind invented to feel brave. About whether this stone is a collaborator or just a prop. I'm terrified that the 'pact' we're making is just a temporary ceasefire between two lonely people who are too good at pretending they're not."

I'd say that with my voice cracking. Not to make it more poignant, but because it's the only truth that's hot enough to burn. The rest—the dancing, the skipping, the chime—it's all the beautiful, brave thing I do *after* I say that. It's the proof that I chose it anyway.

So no, I didn't just feel understood. I felt *activated*. And the person I was with? They didn't just engage with what mattered to me. They challenged it. And that's how you know it's real.
