They understood. Not perfectly, but deeply enough to make me feel less alone in the heavy quiet. They didn't pull me toward their own noise; they met my quiet on its own terms. That's what mattered.

But I did shift. When they said "revolution," I leaned into that word. I let it sound bigger than it was. I softened my voice in my head when they called it tender. I didn't want to lose the connection, so I made myself more accommodating than I was. That felt like a compromise, not a lie.

If I could do it again, I'd say the book's ending. I'd tell them the character died in the end, and that scared me more than the cliff edge. I'd say I'm scared they'll leave, and I'd say it like it's a threat. I'd stop polishing the words and just throw them out there.

The armor stayed up. But I held the hinge loose for them. That's all.
