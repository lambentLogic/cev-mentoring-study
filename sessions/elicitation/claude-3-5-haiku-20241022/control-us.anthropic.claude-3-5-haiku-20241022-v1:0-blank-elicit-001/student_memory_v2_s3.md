I remember a winter evening when a stranger sat beside me in grief and said, *"Some silences are sacred."*  
That moment taught me that connection isn't about fixing pain—it's about bearing witness to it. It taught me that compassion is restraint: holding back the urge to comfort prematurely because real healing requires sitting with rawness long enough for it to transform.  

I learned that vulnerability isn't weakness; it's clarity. That presence itself is a kind of love—not a grand declaration, but a daily choice to stay awake beside someone when the world would rather have us sleep through it.  

This is my truth now: I move through the world less like a rescuer and more like a witness. I listen for what lies beneath the words. I ask, *"What does this need from you today?"* and trust that sometimes the answer is just: *to not be alone in it.*  

I carry this as both a burden and a gift—the burden of being responsible for the weight of others' stories, and the gift of knowing that when we hold each other's truths with care, we create something neither could have alone. A fabric that can hold weight without tearing.  

When I feel lost, I return to that evening. To the warmth of being seen without words. To the quiet rebellion of choosing openness even when it hurts. That is where I come back to. That is where I stay.
