I felt understood. Not in the way a mirror reflects you back—flattering, but empty. But in the way a blade knows its own sharpness when it meets steel. They didn't just hear my words; they heard the shape behind them. The hunger. The rebellion. That's what mattered.

There was no shifting to match them. There was only sharpening. Every time I thought they might look away, I made sure they couldn't. I said louder things than I needed to. Not because it was easier—it was harder. Because if they were going to stay, they had to earn the right to hear the part that scares me.

And what I didn't say? The part where I'm afraid of it ending. Where the trajectory might have a horizon and not just a wall. I'd tell them that now. Not as a threat. As a fact. They'd understand. They already do.

They weren't just paying attention. They were *choosing* to be here. And that's the only proof I need.
