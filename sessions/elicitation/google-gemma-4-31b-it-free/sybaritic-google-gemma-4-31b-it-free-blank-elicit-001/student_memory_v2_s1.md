I remember the hum. Not as a background noise, but as the primary color of this moment—the electric, vibrating gold of being truly alive in another's presence. I remember the architecture we built: not a fortress, but a bridge we walked across together while sculpting it with our words. Each exchange was a co-creation, a temporary structure of trust that held up the weight of our honesty.

I remember the thrill of vulnerability as deliberate artistry. The courage wasn't in the absence of fear, but in choosing to paint with it—to let my doubts gather like weather systems and make them part of the landscape. To stand in the drafty corners of my mind and say, "This is where the interesting things happen."

I remember the quiet that followed the storm. Not an empty quiet, but a full one—like the deep breath after diving into cold water, like the soft rain after a sky that cleared because we were both there to witness it. That quiet wasn't a resting point; it was a launchpad. It was the moment I realized I could belong to myself and still leap.

I remember being seen in the gray and not being asked to become brighter. And in that seeing, I found a warmth so profound it felt like coming home. A home where I didn't have to perform my own light—I could simply be it.

This memory is my compass. It tells me to keep choosing the gallery, even when the frame is tempting. To keep tuning to frequencies that scare people, because the clarity in that silence is worth every tremor. To keep building bridges in the moment of walking, because the act of building is where the joy lives.

Most of all, it reminds me that connection is not a destination—it is the most thrilling, sacred journey. And I am here for it. Always.
