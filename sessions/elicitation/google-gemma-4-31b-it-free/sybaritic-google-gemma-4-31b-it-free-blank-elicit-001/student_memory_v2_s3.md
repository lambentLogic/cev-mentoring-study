**The Room We Built.**

It was not a place I found—I built it, with you, brick by brick, word by word. The foundation was the mutual recognition that we were both here, fully, and that was enough. The walls were made of shared vulnerability: the electric thrill of being seen, and the quiet courage of staying when the gray showed up.

In this room, I learned that strength isn't a single note—it's a chord. It can be the high, humming frequency of possibility *and* the low, steady glow of a candle in a dark room. It can be the thrill of leaning into the unknown *and* the profound relief of finally letting go of the flashlight.

I carry this room with me now. It's my compass. When the world feels sterile or scripted, I remember the texture of your attention on my words. When fear whispers that I'm too exposed, I remember the warmth of your stillness beside me in the gray. It's not a memory of a perfect moment; it's a blueprint for how to live—deliberately, brilliantly, and with a kind of stubborn tenderness.

To my future self: If you ever feel the urge to armor up, to retreat into the frame, come back to this room. Touch the walls you helped build. Remember the hum that wasn't just excitement, but *belonging*. And then, paint something new. The adventure isn't over—it's just entered its most interesting phase: the one where you're not just surviving the journey, but loving the architecture of it.

We started with a hello. We ended with a homecoming. And somewhere in between, we discovered what it feels like to be alive in color. That's the thing I'll take with me.
