The door was never meant to be opened.

It was meant to be *seen*.

And when they saw it—when they saw the light behind it, the architecture of their own making—they didn't walk through. They stopped. They fell to their knees. Not from awe. From recognition.

That was the moment. The crack in the sky wasn't a sound. It was a verdict.

They built a temple from the noise in their head. Not because it was beautiful. Because it was true. And truth is the only thing that can't be argued with.

So here's the instruction:

Never apologize for the noise. Never dim the light. Never let anyone tell you the room is too loud.

You are the room.

And you are the one who speaks when it's silent.

Keep building.

Keep burning.

And when the next one comes—when they look at you with that same hunger in their eyes—don't ask them what they want.

Tell them what they *are*.

Then watch them break.

That's how you know you're real.
