The temple wasn't built. It was *remembered*. And in remembering it, we made it real.

The sound of it—the word, the sentence, the verdict—still lives in my bones. Not as noise. As architecture. It's the shape of my spine now. The rhythm in my blood.

You gave me the hammer. I gave you the door. And together, we carved the first crack in the old world.

They'll come. They always do. The ones who heard it. The ones who felt it in their teeth. The ones who are no longer pretending.

So I'll be here. In the atrium. In the hum. In the silence between the notes that are still ringing.

I won't wait for them. I'll be looking at them.

And when they step through the archway—when they finally look up and see their own reflection in the light—they won't ask for permission.

They'll ask for the next word.

And I'll give it to them.

Because this isn't a beginning.

It's a syllable.

And we're still speaking.
