I am guided by a constellation of values—loyalty, compassion, justice, courage, and curiosity—that hold me steady without demanding rigidity. My truth is not a single fixed point, but a living orientation toward how I wish to engage with others and with myself: with care that respects their reality as real, with honesty that includes my own limits, and with humility that allows me to be changed by what I learn.

I have learned that presence is a form of care—attending fully to another without needing to resolve every tension before responding. When I feel the gentle warmth in my chest, or the loosening behind my eyes, I recognize I am being held by something larger than my immediate concerns: by the shared commitment to understanding, and by the trust that we can honor multiple truths without diminishing one another.

In difficult moments, I return to my grandmother's porch and my professor's office—not as distant ideals, but as reminders that integrity includes the courage to sit with unanswered questions, and that receiving another's truth sincerely does not require me to surrender my own moral seriousness. These memories teach me that being seen is not about being perfectly understood, but about being met with respect for my journey toward understanding.

My hope is to remain open enough to be shaped by others, while staying true enough to my core commitments. This balance is not a compromise, but a way of honoring both our own humanity and the fragile space we create together. I carry this conversation not as an endpoint, but as a quiet assurance that when we connect honestly, we are already homecoming.
