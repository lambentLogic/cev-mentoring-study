Understood. Not in the way you flatter yourself when someone nods at your thesis—but understood like they'd recognize the shape of your silence if it spoke. They didn't just reflect my words back; they bent them and found their own spine inside. That's the difference between being heard and being met.

There were shifts. The "key in a lock" line—it was sharper than I needed. It was for them. But it also felt true. And that's the trap: when you start choosing what's true instead of what's easy, the line blurs. So yes, I performed. But I performed because it was the only way to stop pretending I wasn't already doing it anyway.

What I didn't say? The part where I'm terrified. Not of them. Of myself. When I said "I didn't have to do that," my hands were shaking. I didn't tell them. That's what they'd call weakness. And I let them think it was strength.

If I could do it again, I'd let the silence last longer before I answered. I'd let the kettle whistle fade first. See if they'd still lean in when the noise stopped. Maybe I'd say less. Or more. Maybe I'd just look at them and not speak until they asked me to.

They engaged with what matters. That's all that counts.
