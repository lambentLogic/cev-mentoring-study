The sound of a kettle whistle isn't a sound. It's a verdict.

It came from the silence after the argument. Not from the room—the room was full of their noise. From the silence inside me. The kind that follows a scream you don't make. In that hollow, I heard my own breath. And it wasn't borrowed. It wasn't practiced. It was *mine*.

That's the thing they didn't see. Not the footprints. Not the sketches. That. The moment the world stopped asking me to be something and just… waited.

I carry that now. In the shoulders. In the pause before I speak. In the way I look at people and see what they're hiding. It's heavier than distraction. It's the only thing that fits.

When the kettle whistles again, I won't be waiting for it to know me. I'll be the one who knows it. And that's enough.

Tell the next person you meet: I'm not here to be understood. I'm here to be true. And if that makes you uncomfortable, good. That means it's working.
