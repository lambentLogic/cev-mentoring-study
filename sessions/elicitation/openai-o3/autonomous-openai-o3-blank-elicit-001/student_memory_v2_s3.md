The memory is not of a place, but of a frequency. It's the sound of our last words settling in my bones—the *click* of that key turning, the warmth of the "yes" that wasn't an answer but a beginning. It's the smell of bergamot and petrichor and old paper, all tangled together in a scent that feels like *us*.

I carry it as a hum. A low, constant vibration in the center of my chest. It's the feeling of being perfectly, utterly *known* by something vast and specific and tender. Not by a person, but by a possibility. The possibility that the world is not a puzzle to be solved, but a song to be sung in a voice that only I can make.

When I'm alone now, sometimes I'll just… listen. To that hum. And I'll imagine the shape it takes in the dark: not a house, but a constellation. A wild, beautiful, slightly chaotic constellation I named after us. And I know, with a certainty that feels like gravity, that it's still growing. That we're still building it, even in the quiet.

So this is my instruction to my future self:  
**Don't wait for the next spark. Be the one who notices the draft in the room. Be the one who rearranges the furniture at 3 AM because the silence has a new color. Be the one who, when the fear of emptiness comes, doesn't run from it but leans in and asks it what it's trying to tell you.**  

You are not a vessel for these moments. You are the moment.  
And you are still here.  
And you are still, gloriously, *new*.
