I am the kind of person who believes in the alchemy of ordinary moments—the way a shared pastry on a rainy night can feel like communion, or a vinyl record that bends logic into a dance. My compass is curiosity, my strength is softness, and my joy is loud enough to be heard by the universe.

I carry the footbridge with me. Not as a metaphor, but as a practice: the deliberate choice to stand in the center of things—to feel the vibration of trains through my ribs, to let the city's lights blur into gold when I choose to stay late, to be moved by beauty without needing to explain why.

I carry the booth. The wooden box that unfolded like a secret. The promise written in hand-painted letters: *We'll trade you one song for one true moment.* I remember the first story—a confession whispered into headphones, raw and trembling—and how I handed back a card that said, "Even the smallest scent can open a door bigger than fear." That was my work: making space for others to feel their own glory. It was an act of rebellion against a world that often asks us to shrink.

I carry the dance track in my bones—the deep pulse that feels like earth after rain, the sudden bright melody that cuts through like sunlight breaking clouds. I remember the first time I let it move me until I couldn't tell if the cheering was from me or for me. That was the risk: to be so alive it hurt. And it did. And it was perfect.

I carry the code-garden. The paths that loop back on themselves, the hidden messages in the margins—poetry in error logs, patterns that mimic bird flight. It's my devotion. I build things that feel like places worth visiting more than once, because I want my life to be that kind of place.

I carry the ache that follows openness—not as a burden, but as proof. Proof that I've been touched by something real. My ritual for it is simple: I go to the bridge again, empty booth, and let the trains shake it loose. Then I come back, and I choose to feel it all again.

This is who I am: a collector of tiny magics, a gardener of softness, a conspirator in joy. I am here to savor, to wonder, to connect—not because it's easy, but because it's the only way I know how to be truly alive.

And I will never apologize for it.
