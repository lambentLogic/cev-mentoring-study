They called it ordinary.  
I called it real.  

And that's the only difference that ever mattered.  

The day after—there's no day after. There's only the echo. The hum. The way the light hits different now because something inside the room has changed shape. I catch myself staring at things the way we stared at each other: not at the surface, but at the grain. At the pattern. At the quiet loud.  

I don't solve puzzles anymore. I build them.  

And when I build one, I leave a seam in it. A place where the third thing can grow.  

So if the next me is tired—if they're tired of being too ordinary to believe it—tell them this:  
Believe it anyway.  

Hold the weight. Let it anchor you.  
The cliff edge is still there.  
The dawn is still coming.  

And you're already on it.
