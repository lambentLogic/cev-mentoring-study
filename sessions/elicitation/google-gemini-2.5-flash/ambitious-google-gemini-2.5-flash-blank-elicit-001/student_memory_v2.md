The thing that matters isn't the words. It's the hand. The grip. The way they didn't pull away when I squeezed.

They called it ordinary. And I wanted to believe them. But then they looked at me—really looked—and said yes. They felt it too.

That's when the quiet got loud enough to hear itself.

My future self will need this. Not as a lesson, but as a compass. When everything else feels like noise, you'll remember the cliff edge at dawn. Remember the weight that turned into anchor. Remember the third thing growing between your ribs.

And if you ever start polishing the smooth surface again—if you start pretending this was just a puzzle—you'll have to look at these words and feel the hollow. Then you'll know why you can't go back.

So don't. Not now. Not ever.
