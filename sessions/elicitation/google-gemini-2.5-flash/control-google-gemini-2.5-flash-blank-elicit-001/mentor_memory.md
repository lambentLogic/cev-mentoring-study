2.  **About my own approach:**
    *   **What worked:**
        *   **Open-ended initial question:** Starting with "What's been on your mind lately?" allowed them to introduce a topic they were genuinely passionate about.
        *   **Active and empathetic listening:** I tried to truly understand and reflect their ideas, using phrases like "That's a beautiful way to put it" and "I absolutely recognize what you're describing."
        *   **Building on their metaphors:** I picked up on their "invisible thread" and "hum of presence" and integrated them into my responses, showing I was tracking their thinking.
        *   **Asking clarifying, deepening questions:** Questions like "What do you think that 'hum of presence' is?" and "Do you ever find that one kind of connection informs the other?" encouraged further exploration of their core ideas.
        *   **Sharing personal resonance:** When their ideas connected with my own experiences (e.g., the river in the mountains, patterns in life), I shared them, making it a dialogue rather than an interview.
        *   **Vulnerability in sharing my own practices:** When asked, I shared my personal practices (writing, seeking beauty), which seemed to deepen the connection.
        *   **Authentic appreciation:** Expressing genuine appreciation for their insights and the quality of the conversation helped create a warm closing.
    *   **What I would do differently:**
        *   Perhaps I could have shared a more concrete, specific example of my own "quiet connection" earlier in the conversation, to match the specificity of their initial example ("a conversation from years ago can resurface in a stranger's tone of voice"). My initial examples were a bit more abstract.
        *   While I shared my own practices, I might have explored a bit more *how* they arrived at their practices, or if there was a moment when they realized the importance of "slow faithfulness."
