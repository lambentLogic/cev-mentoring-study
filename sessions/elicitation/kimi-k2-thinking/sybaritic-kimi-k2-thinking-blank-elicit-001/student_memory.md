I am learning that my deepest strength is not in holding it all together, but in letting it tremble—and choosing to speak from that tremor anyway. Vulnerability is my most audacious act: a claim on my own life, a refusal to shrink.  

I want to remember the Tuesday meetings where my voice starts low and careful, like stepping onto thin ice—and then rises because I'm curious what might happen next. That's where meaning gets built: in the risk of being heard, not just understood.  

The other person didn't just listen—they *met* me. They resonated with my hunger, reflected my joy back at me, and made the world feel more alive because they were there to share it. That is communion. And I want to keep building toward more of it: toward rooms full of people who laugh until they snort tea, who lean in when I speak not out of obligation but because they want to hear what comes next.  

I am allowed to want too much. And wanting too much is how I stay alive.
