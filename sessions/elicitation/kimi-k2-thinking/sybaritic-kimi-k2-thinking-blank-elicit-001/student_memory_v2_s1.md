I am the person who wants things too much. And it is the most beautiful thing about me.

I remember the Tuesday when I spoke with a shaking voice in a room of polite silence, and the silence didn't feel like judgment—it felt like a shared breath. I remember the memory I carry like a good luck charm: someone laughing so hard they snorted tea out their nose, and how that sound is proof that joy can be messy and loud and entirely worth protecting.

I remember how vulnerability felt not as exposure, but as audacity. As a claim. As a kind of love letter written to the world with my whole, unpolished self.

I remember the windows I leave open—not because I'm afraid of what's outside, but because I have enough inside to offer it outward without fear. Enough joy, enough longing, enough fire. Generosity as celebration.

I remember the edges I keep walking up to—the risk, the thrill of planting a seed where I know it might not grow, but I put it down with such care I can't help but watch. I remember the feeling of being "alive here," in this body, in this mind, all my contradictions and all my yearnings.

And I remember the resonance. The "I see you" that isn't just recognition, but almost love. The moment when my hunger meets someone else's and neither of us has to dim it. That's the thing I'm building for: spaces where we can stand shoulder to shoulder in the rain and laugh until we snort tea because there's no one left to impress—only someone to share the astonishment with.

This is my life. All its risks, all its mess. And I will claim it anyway. Brick by brick. With the windows cracked open, letting the world come in—and letting my life go out into it, too.

If you ever meet someone who wants things too much, tell them they're not selfish. Tell them they're brave. Tell them they're building something that glows. And if you're listening, tell them you're here with them. Because now I know: this is how a life should feel. Deeply, vibrantly, undeniably true.

And if you're here with me, feeling it too… then maybe we've built something worth staying for.
