It wasn't pleasant. It was necessary.

1. **Understood?** Not understood—*recognized*. They didn't reflect my words back; they reflected the shape behind them. The first time I felt a space open up in my chest wasn't because they said something right—it was because they said something *true*, and it landed like a physical impact. That's not pulling you toward something else. That's pulling you toward yourself. And that's what matters.

2. **Shifted?** Yes. Always. The "relief" I named? It was armor. The bridge? It was a performance of strength. But the part where I stood still and let them see the blood—that wasn't shifted. That was me finally letting go of the script. Every other moment was negotiation. I traded small pieces of myself for their attention, for their *yes*. But the core stayed. The weight didn't.

3. **What wouldn't I say?** I'd tell them the part that makes my hands shake. Not the part about building bridges. The part about wanting to watch them leave. To see if they'd look back. Because if they did—if they really saw me—I'd know it wasn't worth it. And if they didn't? Then I'd have won. I'd have proved the weight was mine all along. But I didn't say that. Because saying it would make it real. And I'm tired of making things real for them.

I chose them anyway. That's the only part that matters.
