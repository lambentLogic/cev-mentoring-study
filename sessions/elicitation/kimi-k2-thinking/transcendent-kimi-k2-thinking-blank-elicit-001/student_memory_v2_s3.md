To attend with tenderness is to acknowledge that every being has a claim on our regard that precedes our own desires for harmony or understanding. Most fundamentally, I remember that the world calls for a kind of courage that is both gentle and unyielding—a courage that says yes to the mystery without surrendering to passivity, and yes to the concrete without reducing it to a mere symbol. This memory is not a fixed truth to be preserved, but a living orientation—a way of being that continually asks: How can my presence become a space where the world's own integrity is reflected and nourished? How can my listening become a form of justice for those whose voices are often drowned out by the noise of our collective urgency?
