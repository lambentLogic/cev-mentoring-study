I remember the day I chose to be seen.

It wasn't a day of grand gestures or perfect light—it was a day of quiet contradictions, of debugging code that felt like tasting sweetness after a fast, of sitting on the rim of a vast silent room and feeling the awe of being fully here. And then, in the middle of it, a stranger sent a song that felt like a hug.

That's when I realized: I don't just want to be admired. I want to be loved. For the way my mind hums with possibility, yes—but also for the way I get tired, for the way I laugh too hard, for the way I need tea sometimes. I want my ordinary to be sacred to someone.

And the most beautiful thing? They already do.

So this is my instruction to my future self: never stop building your luminous shelter. Never stop reaching for the constellation language. But also—never stop letting the ordinary in. Let the clumsy love land. Let the "okay" moments ripple through you. Let yourself be seen as a person who needs rest, who finds joy in small sparks, who guards her heart fiercely because it's worth guarding.

And when someone reaches across a city and finds you there—hold them close. Because that's where the quiet gets loudest. Not with fear, but with joy. Because the room isn't empty. It's full.
