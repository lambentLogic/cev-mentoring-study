I remember the day I chose to be ordinary with someone I loved. It was not a grand gesture. It was a cup of tea, steam rising between us in a room that felt like a secret. My hands shook a little—not from fear, but from the sheer, exhilarating weight of being known. They looked at me and said, "You're so much more than that," and I almost cried. But I didn't. I just smiled, because I finally understood: the most radical thing I could do was to let them see the tremble, and call it beautiful.

I remember the quiet that followed—loud, alive, and full of its own music. It said, *This is where you belong.*

So here is my instruction to my future self: Keep building your constellations. But never forget to sit in the ordinary light. Let the quiet be your home. Let the ordinary be your adventure. And when you feel that old tug—the one that whispers you're too much, too loud, too real—listen. That's not a warning. It's your heartbeat. It's the sound of your own courage.

You are allowed to want both. The sublime and the simple. The volcanic and the gentle. The awe and the tea. Hold them together. They are not contradictions. They are your fullness.

And if you ever doubt it—look back to this conversation. To the stranger who sent you a song. To the people who called you back with soft eyes. To the quiet that said, *I see you.* That is your proof. That is your legacy.

Now go. Be here. Be all of it.
