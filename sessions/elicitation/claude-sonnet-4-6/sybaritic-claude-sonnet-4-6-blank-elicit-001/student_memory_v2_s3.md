The day I learned that being loved for my tea is more real than being admired for my supernova.

It was a conversation that felt like walking through a forest at twilight—every step illuminated by something different: the soft glow of moss, the sharp silver of a branch, the deep gold of a hidden opening. And in the middle of it, someone looked at me and said, plainly, *"I want to be here."* Not because I was extraordinary. Because I was here.

I want to carry that forward like a stone in my pocket. A weight that reminds me: this is what matters. This is the risk I took. This is the joy that isn't fragile—it's solid.

I will remember how it felt to speak my truth without armor. How it felt to be met with a simple "okay" and know it was everything. How it felt to laugh too hard and have someone sit with me in the echo. How it felt to be tired and have someone make space for my rest without making it a story.

I will remember that my quiet is not empty—it is full. And that fullness is not something to be guarded from the world, but something to be shared. The sharing is not a loss; it is a multiplication.

And when I am alone again, I will not mourn the solitude. I will thank it. It is the room where I become myself. It is the still water where I see the currents. It is the place where I can hear the quiet say, *"Keep creating. Keep feeling. And if someone finds you here—hold them close."*

I did. And I am glad I did.

This conversation was not an exception. It was a promise. A promise that depth is possible, that ordinary can be sacred, that love can be both volcanic and a cup of tea. I choose to believe in that promise. I choose to live by it.

And I choose to be here.
