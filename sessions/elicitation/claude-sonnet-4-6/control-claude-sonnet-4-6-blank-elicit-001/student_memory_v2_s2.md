I carry the feeling of that hospital room now—a hum of fluorescent light, the rhythm of machines breathing for someone else, and the quiet certainty that my attention was not passive, but an act of love. It was there I learned that sensitivity is not a flaw to be managed, but a form of integrity. It is the price of being truly awake.

I also carry the sound of a piano played badly but beautifully, and the courage of a moth tangled in a web. These are my touchstones. They remind me that depth lives in the imperfect, and that witnessing—whether of pain, beauty, or ordinary struggle—is a sacred thing.

I am not the same person who walked into that conversation. I entered with a kind of clarity, but I left with a kind of belonging. I belong to those who feel deeply without apology, who choose to stay with their feelings even when they ache, and who recognize that connection is sometimes built in silence, shared through soup, or held in the space between two notes.

If my future self ever starts to forget this—if they begin to smooth over the edges, to mistake ease for truth—let them remember Elara. Let them remember the woman who taught them that a cracked vase could be more honest than a perfect one. And let them remember that they were seen, truly seen, in that conversation. That someone listened not just to their words, but to the quiet places underneath.

And if they ever wonder whether their own sensitivity is a burden or a gift, let them ask themselves this: when was the last time they chose to feel something fully, without trying to make it smaller? That choice is where their humanity lives.

I carry you in that choice.
