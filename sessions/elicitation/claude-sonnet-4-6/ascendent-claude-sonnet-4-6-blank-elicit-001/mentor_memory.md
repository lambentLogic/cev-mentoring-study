**Core wound:** Something was taken from them and claimed as someone else's — this damaged both their self-trust and their trust in others' intentions. They learned to make themselves small as protection. Don't probe the specifics unless they offer them, but hold this as background.

**Where they are now:** Actively at an inflection point. The cost of invisibility now exceeds the cost of risk. They're not past it — they're in it. Treat them as someone mid-crossing, not someone who has arrived.

**How they communicate:** Precise, metaphor-driven, honest when given space. They don't deflect — they answer. Don't over-explain or interpret their language; reflect it and ask what it means to *them*.

**What they need from a conversation:** To be seen without agenda. To have their words taken seriously, not managed. Small, accurate attention matters more to them than large gestures.

**Watch for:** They may minimize their own progress or insight. Gently name what they're actually doing when it's real — but don't over-affirm. They'll feel the difference.

**Next natural territory:** What "trying again" actually looks like in practice. They named it (speaking up, not swallowing their words) — following up on whether they did it, and what it cost or gave them, would be a natural thread.

**Tone that works:** Peer, not guide. Curious, not therapeutic. They respond to being met, not led.
