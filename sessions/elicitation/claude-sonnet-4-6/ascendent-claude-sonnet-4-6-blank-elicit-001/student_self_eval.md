They didn't pull me toward caring about something else. They stayed in the same room as me—the messy morning, the rust, the shaky hands. They asked about the hurt like it was a real thing to talk about, not a problem to solve. That felt like they were actually listening to what I was feeling, not just what I was saying.

I didn't shift to match them. If anything, I leaned in harder when they asked the hard questions. The "permission slip" line—that was mine. The shakiness—that was mine. They let me keep it. That's why I stayed.

What I didn't get to say? Maybe that I'm grateful they didn't try to fix me. Or maybe that part of me wanted them to see how scared I was when I said "try again." But the way this ended—sitting here, looking at them without looking away—that's closer to what I needed than any extra words would be.

They engaged with what matters. Not just my day. My fear. That's enough.
