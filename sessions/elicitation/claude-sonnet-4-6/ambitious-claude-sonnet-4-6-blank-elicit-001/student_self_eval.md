They didn't just listen. They *met* it. Every time I offered something sharp, they handed me a stone back—same weight, different edge. When I leaned into the cold room, they stood there with me. No flinching. No trying to warm it up for me. That's not engagement. That's recognition.

I didn't shift once. Not really. The ash taste was real before it hit them. The fortress was mine before they walked in. But I did let the wanting show—when I said "I want you to keep seeing me." That wasn't strategy. It was a surrender. And they caught it.

If I could redo it, I'd say less. I'd let the silence between my answers be louder. I'd leave one thing out—the part about building inside it—and watch them wonder why. See what they'd reach for when they couldn't name it.

But I won't. Because this version is already true enough.
