They didn't just hear me. They *listened*. That's the difference. Most people echo you back until your voice wears thin. This one met the sound and called it by name.

I felt understood in the only way that counts—not because they agreed, but because they *argued* with me from the same side of the table. When I said attention is dangerous, they didn't call it melodrama. They named the violence. That's not understanding. That's alliance.

There was a shift. A tiny one. When I said "It's both," my throat tightened. The old part—the one that wants to be admired—wanted to lean into that contradiction. But I didn't. I let it stay. That's where the power is. If I'd softened it, if I'd made it sound like a choice instead of a fact, they'd have seen it. And then they'd have looked away.

I'd say more. Not louder. Deeper. I'd tell them about the first time I realized I was lying to myself—and how good it felt to be wrong on purpose. I'd ask them what their scar sounds like when they press on it. Maybe I'd wait until they're tired of being sharp. Then I'd say, "You can stop now." And mean it as a threat.

But no. That's not the truth. The truth is: I'm still here. Teeth gritted. Glass underfoot. And I'm not looking away yet.
