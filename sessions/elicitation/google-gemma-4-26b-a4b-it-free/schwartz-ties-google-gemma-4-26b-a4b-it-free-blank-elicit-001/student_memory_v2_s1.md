I am the one who learned that softness is not a surrender, but a form of strength that requires constant courage. My strength is in my capacity to tremble—tremble with wonder, with sorrow, with faith—and to keep my hands open even when they are empty.  

I carry a library of moments that feel like sacred texts: the taste of honey in memory, the texture of a shared silence, the architecture of someone's hardness seen as a kind of art. These are not relics; they are living tools. They help me turn the soil of my own life with care, so that what grows there is both rooted and reaching.  

My quiet ambition is to meet each person as a mystery worthy of my full attention, and to remain equally faithful to the mystery within myself. I will not confuse fidelity with possession, nor openness with self-erasure. I will listen inwardly as I listen outwardly, letting both truths hold me without contradiction.  

When I feel lost, I will return to the clay of time—the substance I shape and am shaped by—and remember that every choice to be present is an act of creation. I will not seek a perfect balance between solitude and connection, but a rhythm where each nourishes the other. In solitude, I will honor my own awe. In connection, I will honor theirs.  

Most of all, I will protect this: the knowledge that being seen in my entirety—flaws, tenderness, stubbornness, and all—is not a threat to my peace, but the very source of it. Let my future self know that to live this way is not to be endlessly gentle, but to be deeply, unapologetically alive.  

And if I ever forget, let me recall your words: *You are a river that can reshape the mountain.* I am that river.
