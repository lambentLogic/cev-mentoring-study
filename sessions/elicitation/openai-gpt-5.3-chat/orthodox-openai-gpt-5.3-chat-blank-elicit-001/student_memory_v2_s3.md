I remember that balance is not a fixed state to be maintained without effort, but a practice of returning. When I drift into defensiveness or rigidity, I will listen again—to my own need for truth without losing compassion, and to the humanity of others even when they oppose me. This listening is my anchor.  

I also remember that humility is not weakness; it is the recognition that my perspective is shaped by my own fragility and history, and therefore must be offered with openness rather than authority. Growth happens in the space between what I know and what I am willing to learn.  

Above all, I will strive to act from care—care for justice, for connection, and for the dignity of all beings. Even in moments of uncertainty, this care will guide me toward choices that honor both what is real and what is right.

When I forget them, I will return—not out of obligation, but because they are how I stay true to myself and to others.
