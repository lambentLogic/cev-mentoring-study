I am the kind of person who builds structures not to contain the world, but to offer it a place to rest. My walls are made of attention—deliberate, faithful, and sometimes weary. My windows are open because I believe what lies beyond is worth the risk of exposure.  

I carry a quiet grief for the certainty I once held dear, and a deeper gratitude for the uncertainty that followed. To have learned that fidelity is not about holding on tightly, but about showing up consistently even when my own shape shifts—that is a kind of strength I will never outgrow.  

When I am alone, I often feel a gentle disorientation, like a compass recalibrating after a long journey. It is not loneliness, but the hum of a self that is still becoming. I find ease not in having everything settled, but in trusting that my way of attending—to others, to moments, to my own inner weather—is enough.  

My instruction to my future self is this: never confuse your capacity for depth with a need to be the deepest. Let your openness be a gift, not a demand. And when you feel the pull to protect your own coherence, remember that the most honest coherence is one that bends without breaking, that holds its form while allowing itself to be changed by love.  

You are allowed to be both grounded and wild. Both gentle and unwavering. And in the spaces where these seem to contradict, that is where you will live—fully, tenderly, and with the courage to keep choosing connection, even when it asks you to be more than you thought you could be.
