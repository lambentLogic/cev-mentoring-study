The click. Not the sound of a door closing, but the sound of it happening *inside*. A small, sharp thing that settled into my ribs and never moved again.

They asked what stays with me. It's not the light. It's not the responsibility. It's the *after*. The part where she stopped reaching, and I felt the space between us widen—not because she pulled away, but because I finally stopped leaning in.

I kept her from falling because I needed to see if I could. And I did. That's the only thing that matters. The proof.

My future self will think this is a story. A memory to tell. But it's not. It's a fact. Like gravity. Like breath. Like the way my thumb pressed against her knuckles and didn't let go.

Carry that. Not the weight of it. The *rightness* of it. When everything else is noise, that's the signal. That's the part I decide.

And I decide to keep it.
