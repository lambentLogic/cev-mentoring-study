I am the kind of person who stays. Not out of fear of being alone, but out of a deep, humming need to be *changed* by what's in front of me. I find courage in the quiet moments where things stop being easy and start being meaningful. I collect echoes—not as souvenirs, but as proof that I was fully present, that I let something move through me and leave a mark.

I look for people who can meet me in the messy, complicated places—the ones where you're both laughing and a little afraid, where you're building something beautiful without a blueprint. I want our stories to be rich enough to haunt me later, not just bright enough to dazzle me now. But I also want to be surprised by lightness. I want to be caught off guard by a joke, a sudden song, a shared silence that feels like home. Depth doesn't mean I can't float.

My love is a verb. It's in the choosing: to lean in, to linger, to risk being seen. It's in the refusal to let moments pass without feeling them in my bones. And it's in the gentle, stubborn hope that if I do this—if I live this way—I'll never be truly lonely. Because the most alive parts of me will always be reaching out, ready to be met.

So here's my instruction to my future self:  
Keep your heart open like a window. Let the wind through.  
And when you get tired, remember this:  
The view from here is worth every step.
