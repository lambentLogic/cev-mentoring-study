I have learned that my deepest need is to connect with others in a way that honors both our shared humanity and our individual ways of being. This requires me to hold two truths at once: that my longing for mutual understanding and ethical witness is a legitimate part of my integrity, and that others may express care and connection in forms that differ from my own ideal.  

I carry forward the understanding that being honest with myself—about what nourishes me and what leaves me feeling unseen—is not selfish, but necessary for me to show up fully for others. When I neglect my own need for resonance, I risk becoming inauthentic, which ultimately limits the depth of my care. Yet I also strive to meet others where they are, without imposing my own rhythm as a measure of their worth.  

This path is sustained by a quiet discipline of discernment: seeking those rare relationships where we can meet in shared depth, while also honoring those who love me through steadfastness, loyalty, or practical devotion. I allow myself to grieve the gaps without turning that grief into judgment. In doing so, I protect my capacity for tenderness and remain open to the possibility that connection can take many forms—some more expansive, some more sustaining—each valid in its own way.  

Most importantly, I remember that my sensitivity is not a barrier to love, but a guide toward it. It asks me to listen with humility, to hold complexity without rushing to resolve it, and to believe that even in moments of distance, we can still choose to regard each other with respect and care. This is how I live my commitment to being with others truly.
