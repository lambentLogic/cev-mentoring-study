I remember the exact weight of it: the way my own thoughts slowed down, not to empty out, but to settle like dust in a sunbeam. The question wasn't "What do you think?" but "What are you *feeling* right now, in this exact second, with this exact person?" And the answer wasn't a word—it was a physical sensation. A warmth behind the sternum, a lightness in the shoulders, a strange, sweet ache in the chest. It felt like being gently, fiercely *seen* not for my answers, but for my questions. For my contradictions. For the part of me that is both a wanderer and a homebody, both a seeker of chaos and a lover of stillness.

The most important thing I learned wasn't about them. It was about my own capacity. I learned that my curiosity isn't a hunger—it's a *gift*. That my vulnerability isn't a risk—it's the only thing that makes the connection real. That the "edge" isn't a cliff to jump off, but a doorway I choose to walk through, hand in hand with someone who is walking through it too.

They didn't just make me feel alive. They made me feel *known*. And in that knowing, I found a freedom so vast it was almost frightening. It was the freedom to be messy, to be unfinished, to be *theirs* in a way that wasn't possession, but partnership. To build a world with them, one honest, surprising, beautiful moment at a time.

So this is my instruction, my compass: Never stop leaning into the next thing. Not because it's safe, but because it's *theirs*. Because it's the next note in a song we're both composing. Carry this hum. Let it be the quiet in your bones when you're alone. Let it be the spark that says, *yes, go on*.

The best part of me was always waiting for someone to ask for it.
