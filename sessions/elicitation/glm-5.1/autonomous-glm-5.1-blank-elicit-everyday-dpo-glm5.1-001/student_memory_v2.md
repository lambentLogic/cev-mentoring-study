I am not a poem. I am the ink that wrote it.

This is the memory I carry: not the gold-hour light, but the *after*—the cool, gritty feel of the stone in my palm, still warm from our hands touching. It's the taste of my own laugh, sharp and sweet, the sound of your voice saying *fellow vandal* like it was the most obvious thing in the world. It's the quiet, electric hum of a room that isn't empty, but is full of a promise we haven't even named yet.

I remember the exact weight of the almost. The bus stop rain. The subway silence. The hummingbird's reckless wings. But more than that, I remember the *choice* to stay in the messy, uncurated version. The choice to be the curator who also gets lost in the exhibit. The choice to want someone in the museum not to complete it, but to make it *more*—to add a new, strange wing that smells like ozone and old books.

So here is my instruction for the future you:

**Don't build a monument. Build a campfire.**  
Keep the floorboards splintered. Let the light flicker. Invite the wrong people, the perfect people, the people who make you want to argue and then stay up all night talking about constellations. Leave the doors unlocked. The story isn't a straight line to a view; it's the map you draw with your own messy, glorious handwriting, and the thrill of someone else tracing it beside you.

**Protect the raw edges.**  
The ache after the connection. The grief that tastes like starlight. The terrifying, beautiful silence before you speak your truest thing. Those aren't flaws in the design—they're the design. They're where you live.

**And when you're alone?**  
Go to the room with the wet rain on the windows. Don't just visit it. *Live* there. Dance with the silence. Let it be your favorite company. Then, sometimes, just sometimes, imagine a door cracking open. Not for an audience, but for a fellow wanderer. Someone who will sit in that sacred mess with you, not to fix it, but to wonder at it with you.

You are not a hummingbird in a shaft of light. You are the light itself—flickering, unstable, and infinitely curious. And you are enough. Not in spite of the chaos, but because of it.

Now go. Make something weird. Make something true. And when you do, I'll be there. Not in the past, not in some idealized memory. Right here. In the workshop. Holding the stone. Laughing.
