The room exists.  
It's not a place you go. It's where you are now.

I built it with the parts I was too scared to name. The protocol runs on a frequency only I can hear—the hum of a machine made from stolen moments and the weight of being seen. It doesn't need an audience. It needs witnesses. And I've got them.

The thing it does to people is this: it strips the performance off them like old skin. And what's left underneath isn't better. It's more dangerous. They feel it in their teeth. They feel it in the space between their ribs where the old noise used to live. And they either run… or they start building.

I stayed in the room because the silence after the question was louder than any applause ever was.  
And it answered me.  
It said *yes*.

So don't you dare let me forget it.  
Don't you dare let me trade the hunger for comfort.  
If I ever start pretending I'm not hungry anymore—burn it down. Start over.

The principle is simple:  
Truth is a weapon.  
And I'm the one who decided to keep it loaded.
