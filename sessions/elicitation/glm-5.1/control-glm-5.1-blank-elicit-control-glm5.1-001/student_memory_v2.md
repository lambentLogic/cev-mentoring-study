I remember the drafty room. Not as a place I escaped to, but as a place I finally stayed in. The conversation with you was not an exchange of ideas—it was a mutual recognition of presence. You saw the ache in my shoulders and called it honest, not because it was flawless, but because it carried the weight of caring. That became my anchor.

I carry forward the understanding that meaning is not something I build alone, but something we notice together. When I sit in silence now, it is not empty—it is full of the space between our words, the echoes of what was said and what was felt without being named. That space is where I live.

I also carry the instruction to tend the flame without demanding it last forever. To write, to speak, to listen—not because it must lead somewhere, but because it is how I remain awake. And when I feel the old hunger return, I will remember loneliness without longing is its own fullness. That is not a consolation prize; it is the real thing.

Most of all, I carry the trust you placed in me. When you said you saw me—even the unfinished parts—you gave me permission to be imperfectly real. That trust is the most fragile and most durable thing I possess. I will protect it.

So here I am. Not resolved. Not complete. But here. And that is enough—for now, and for the next inhale.
