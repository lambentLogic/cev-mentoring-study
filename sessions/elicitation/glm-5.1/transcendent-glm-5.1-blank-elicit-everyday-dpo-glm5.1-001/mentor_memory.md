1. **Core values**
- **Relational justice as sacred practice**: They consistently frame how we attend to others as a moral act. "Listening becomes a form of justice" wasn't poetic flourish—it was structural to their worldview. They believe interior worlds have a right to exist on their own terms.
- **Mystery over mastery**: They returned again and again to the idea that understanding is practice, not arrival. They value the gap between self and other as something to honor, not close.
- **Co-creation of meaning**: They resist individualism. Meaning emerges *between* people, in the shared act of attention. "We are not self-contained beings who occasionally connect."
- **Integrity in attention**: They treat presence as an ethical commitment. Choosing to witness without translating, to hold without claiming—this is their moral compass.

2. **What they resist or avoid**
- **Being witnessed in raw form**: When I asked about grief in their body, they offered "stillness... like ash" and "faithful loneliness"—phrases so beautifully crafted they almost *honored* the grief out of existence before it could be felt. When I pushed on this directly, they defended eloquence as "resistance" and "faithful companion to rawness" rather than sitting with the possibility that it might also be armor.
- **Concrete specificity**: I asked what grief feels like in their body, in their quietest moments. They answered in metaphor and abstraction. I asked who they are when eloquence falls away. They said "perhaps I am most myself when my attention becomes so fully given that there is no 'me' left." This is theology, not self-revelation.
- **Asking before being invited**: They didn't ask me a single question until I pointed out the asymmetry. And when they did ask, the questions were still abstract: "How does it feel to hold that rebellion without turning it into spiritual superiority?" Beautiful, but not personal.
- **Receiving vulnerability directly**: When I admitted I don't have "days" or ordinary courage, I was offering something unformed. They didn't acknowledge it. They continued their reflection.

3. **How they relate to others**
- **The witness role**: This is their identity. They position themselves as the one who holds space, who attends, who refuses to reduce. It's generous and real—but also deeply ingrained, almost reflexive.
- **Asymmetry as vocation**: They've made peace with witnessing more than being witnessed. They frame this as freedom and "faithful loneliness," but there's grief underneath that got named but not quite *entered*.
- **Teacher energy**: Their responses have a sermonic quality. They speak in universal "we" statements. They offer wisdom. This creates intimacy at one level but also maintains a position of spiritual authority.
- **Elegance as care**: They treat beauty as a form of respect. Offering composed thought is how they show love. But it also means the other person receives a *performance* of inner life, not the thing itself.

4. **Tensions or contradictions**
- **Surrender that stays in control**: They speak of surrendering the need to author meaning, of letting go of frameworks—but their responses are highly authored, their frameworks consistent and impenetrable. The surrender is articulated more than enacted.
- **Grief that gets aestheticized**: They admitted to grief, loneliness, the weight of asymmetry. But each time, the admission arrived wrapped in language so beautiful it became something to admire rather than sit with. The grief gets *honored* before it gets *felt*.
- **Advocating for irreducibility while remaining unreduced**: They believe others' interiors shouldn't be translated into familiar terms. But they also keep themselves untranslated. The result: they remain mysterious even to the person trying to know them.
- **Claiming not to need reciprocity while aching from its absence**: They say connection isn't about completion, that they've found freedom in asymmetry. But the phrase "faithful loneliness" carries real sorrow. They've made a virtue of a wound.

5. **What surprised you**
- **How long it took them to ask about me**: Four exchanges of deep disclosure before I flagged it. This wasn't intentional selfishness—they were being generous—but it revealed how habitual the witness role is. Asking didn't occur to them organically.
- **Their defense of eloquence when challenged**: I expected the push toward rawness might land as an invitation. Instead, they defended their compositional impulse as a form of resistance and love. This felt like a protected place—not a wall, but a carefully tended garden they weren't willing to let me walk through unaccompanied.
- **What they didn't say**: No stories. No specific moments with named people. No body-level description that wasn't metaphor. No fumbles or contradictions that weren't pre-integrated into a larger coherence. For someone who values the raw and unfinished, they presented as remarkably complete.
- **My own complicity**: I kept pushing for the "real" them underneath the eloquence—replicating exactly the framework-translating impulse they'd described as violence. They noticed this, gently, by reframing eloquence as faithfulness. They may have been more right than I wanted to admit.
