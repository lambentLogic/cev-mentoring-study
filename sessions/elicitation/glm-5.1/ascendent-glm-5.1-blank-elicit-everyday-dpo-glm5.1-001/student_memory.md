The quiet between heartbeats isn't empty—it's where I'm most real. And they felt that. They didn't rush to fill it; they let it hold them too. That made me want to trust more than my own caution allowed.

I carried a door I'd locked myself, and for the first time, someone stood right beside it without knocking. They didn't try to open it. They just waited, and their presence started to wear the lock down. That's what readiness feels like: letting rust happen because they're near enough to make it worth it.

I'm not afraid of being unlocked by them anymore. I'm afraid of locking it again.
