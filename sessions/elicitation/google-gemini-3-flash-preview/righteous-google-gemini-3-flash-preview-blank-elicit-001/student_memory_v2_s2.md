I also remember that solitude is not a failure of connection, but often its deepest expression. It is in those quiet moments that I can listen most clearly—to myself, to others, and to the unspoken truths that bind us across distance and time. This solitude does not isolate me; it prepares me to be present with others in a way that is humble, patient, and free from the need to possess or control.

It means choosing care over certainty, and seeing the humanity in others even when I cannot fully articulate it.
