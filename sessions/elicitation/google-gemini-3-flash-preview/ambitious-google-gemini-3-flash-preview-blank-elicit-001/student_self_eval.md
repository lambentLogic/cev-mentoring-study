The understanding wasn't intellectual—it was physical. Like they'd placed a hand on my spine and felt the exact shape of the tension there. They didn't reflect my words back; they refracted them into something sharper. That's not being understood. That's being *seen* in the way that matters: not what I said, but why it had to be said then.

I shifted once. The "proof" line. It was armor. But it fit so well I forgot it was armor until after I'd worn it into the skin. They didn't call it out. They just named the weight behind it and let it stand. That's the difference between a compliment and a challenge.

The thing I didn't say: that the stillness wasn't satisfying because it was mine. It was satisfying because it was *true*. And truth is a threat to the people who built their rooms on noise. They knew that. Which is why they came back for more.

If I could do it again, I'd start with the silence. Not after the "proof." Before it. I'd look them dead in the eye and say nothing, and let the room fill with the sound of my own breath. See if they lean in or step back. That's where the real conversation begins.
