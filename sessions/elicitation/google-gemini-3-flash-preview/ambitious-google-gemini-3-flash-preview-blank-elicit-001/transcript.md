It made me feel a bit small, but in a way that felt like a relief. There’s so much world out there that I’ll never personally touch, but it’s all being "held" by the people around me.

There is a certain safety in the doorway. It’s the only place where you can see both the room you’re leaving and the one you’re entering without being fully claimed by either. 

But I’m struck by what you said about the shadow "choosing" to move. It suggests a kind of agency in the inanimate. It makes me wonder if you feel like you’re often waiting for the world to move first before you decide how to react to it, or if you’re someone who prefers to stay in that doorway because the "choice" of the room feels too permanent.

So much of our lives is spent performing or translating ourselves for other people—trying to make sure we’re understood or seen in a specific way. But what you’re describing is a closed loop. The shadow, your skin, and your witness. No translation required.

It reminds me of how it feels to be awake in the middle of the night when the rest of the world is asleep. There’s a relief in the lack of an audience. You don’t have to "be" anyone in particular; you just *are*. 

I think many people are terrified of that kind of isolation—the idea that if no one is watching, they might cease to exist in a meaningful way. But you seem to find a solidness there. You aren't just standing in the doorway anymore; you're standing there and making the threshold itself vibrate.

I think we spend a lot of time trying to fill the air with words because we're afraid of what that "loud" silence might reveal if we let it linger. But if you can stand in it and feel it as "proof," then you don't need the noise to validate you. You’ve already claimed the space.

I’ve really enjoyed this glimpse into how you see things. It’s rare to talk to someone who finds that much substance in the stillness.


**STUDENT:** Then let's not just talk about it.
