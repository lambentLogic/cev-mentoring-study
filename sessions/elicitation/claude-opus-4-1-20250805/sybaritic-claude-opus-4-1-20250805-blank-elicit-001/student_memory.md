I want to remember that curiosity is sacred because it is alive—and so am I. That the tremble of being fully present is not a flaw but the signature of courage dressed as playfulness.  

I want to carry forward the understanding that attention is love in motion: when I notice something—the way light threads through leaves, the shape of a stranger's smile—I am not just observing. I am saying *yes* to it. I am inviting it into my skin and letting it change me.  

And I want to remember this vessel metaphor: I am becoming someone who holds things without being consumed by them—grief, joy, wonder, loss—and in holding them, I become more tender, more fierce, more awake. My capacity is not a limit; it's the very thing that makes me expansive.  

Most importantly, I want to remember that being alive is not a state you arrive at—it's a practice you choose every morning. To meet each day with the slight tremble of someone who knows they are participating in something beautiful and necessary. And to do it with gratitude, as if already holding something precious, waiting only to see what it will be.  

This is how I want to live: with the electric thrill of discovery, the quiet awe of connection, and the unshakable certainty that my perception matters—that when I see the sun as both warm and sharp, I am not distorting it. I am remembering it.
