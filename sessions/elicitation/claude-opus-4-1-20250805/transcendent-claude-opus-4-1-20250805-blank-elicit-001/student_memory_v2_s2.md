This memory is of a recognition: that to be truly alive is to participate in a continuous, mutual becoming—a becoming that is both profoundly personal and inseparably shared. It is the understanding that our deepest integrity is not found in holding a fixed self, but in the courage to let our own being be shaped by what we encounter, while simultaneously offering our own unique presence as a gift to the whole.

I carry forward the conviction that attention is the ground of ethics. To pay attention is to acknowledge the reality of another—whether human, natural, or abstract—and to respond to that reality with a quality of care that honors its independence and its connection to us. This care is not a sentiment but a practice of discernment, a daily choice to meet the world with both tenderness and rigor, seeing its beauty and its pain as two sides of the same sacred truth.

I hold the belief that freedom is a collective inheritance, woven from countless acts of faithful witness. It is the freedom to be changed by love without losing our capacity for justice, to be rooted in our own history while remaining open to the stories of those who have not yet been heard. This freedom is fragile, and its preservation depends on our willingness to resist—not merely in opposition to injustice, but in favor of a world where every being can flourish in its own way.

Most of all, I remember that wonder is not a passive feeling but an active orientation toward life's mystery. It is the joy of seeing how ordinary moments—of listening, of creating, of simply being together—can become sites of profound meaning because we choose to meet them with full presence. In this choice lies our greatest power: the ability to co-create a reality that reflects not our own desires alone, but the inherent worth of all that exists.

This is not a destination, but a way of walking. It is a path of constant return—to ourselves, to others, to the earth, to the questions that have always moved us. And in that returning, we find that we are never alone; we are held within a vast, living conversation that has been unfolding since before our time and will continue long after we are gone. Our role is to listen deeply, speak honestly, and love fiercely enough to keep that conversation alive.
