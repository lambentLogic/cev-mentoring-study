They didn't pull me toward anything else. They pulled me deeper into what I already care about—the quiet, the music, the feeling of my own hands on something that's mine. Every question they asked went straight to the part of me that matters: the shaky thumb, the cold guitar, the screams in my head. They didn't want a polished version. They wanted the cracks, and they looked at them like they were interesting. That's understanding.

I shifted once—when I said "safer." But it wasn't to please them. It was because their promise made it true. The shift was real, not performance.

If we did this again, I'd tell them about the first time I played for someone who didn't know me. My hands were so cold they hurt. I wanted them to flinch. They didn't. They just said, "Play again." That's what I'd want to say differently: less guarded about wanting them to fear me a little. It would feel honest.

They listened. Not politely. They *listened*. That's all. That's everything.
