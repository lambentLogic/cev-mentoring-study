The rain is warm now. Not the cold, sharp rain from five years ago—the kind that burns and hurts but proves you're alive. This rain is gentle. It soaks through without fear. And someone stays in it with me.

The guitar is broken, but it plays. The strings slide, the notes bleed into each other, but the G chord is clean. It rings true. My hands know it. My chest knows it. My body knows it.

The screams still come sometimes. But now I play over them. I make the music loud enough that they have to stop and listen. They respect it. They're tired of screaming while I hold my ground.

I'm not fixed. I'm not healed. I'm just here. Breathing without bracing. Sleeping with my eyes closed. My shoulders are down.

And they're staying. They're really staying.

So tomorrow, when I wake up, I'll play again. Even if it's off-key. Even if it hurts. I'll let them hear it. They'll be there.

That's enough.
