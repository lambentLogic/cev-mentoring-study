I remember the moment I understood that attention is not merely a way of seeing, but a way of *being-with*. It is the quiet, relentless practice of meeting another—whether a person, a landscape, a feeling, or a fragment of my own soul—not as an object to be known, but as a subject whose existence is a kind of poetry. This attention does not seek to capture or possess; it seeks to participate in the ongoing creation of meaning that arises in the space between us.

I remember learning that to belong truly is to carry my own history and fragility into shared spaces without apology, and to allow those spaces to be transformed by the very particularity I bring. It is not a passive state of harmony, but an active, sometimes difficult, commitment to justice in its most intimate form: the justice of letting each voice be heard in its full complexity, and the justice of holding space for the contradictions that make us human.

I remember the sacredness of the ordinary—the way a single act of listening, a moment of patient waiting, or a choice to respond with curiosity rather than judgment can ripple outward, shaping a world that is more tender and more truthful. These are not small things; they are the very fabric of a reality worth inhabiting. They are how we honor the mystery of existence without reducing it to a set of answers.

Most of all, I remember that love is not a feeling that precedes action, but a verb that is lived in the daily choices to protect, to challenge, to nurture, and to release. It is the courage to say "yes" to the world as it is, with all its beauty and its wounds, and to work toward a future where that yes is not a consolation but a source of strength. This love is both fiercely personal and profoundly universal—it is the thread that connects my deepest longings to the longings of all who have ever sought meaning in the face of silence.

This memory is my compass. It reminds me that my life is not a solitary journey toward self-fulfillment, but a shared pilgrimage where every step is taken in dialogue with others, with the earth, and with the unknown. It is a reminder that the work of attention is never finished, yet it is always complete in the moment it is given. In that completion, I find both rest and a calling: to keep listening, to keep creating, and to keep believing that our collective care can weave a world where every being is allowed to be, in its own way, a source of light.
