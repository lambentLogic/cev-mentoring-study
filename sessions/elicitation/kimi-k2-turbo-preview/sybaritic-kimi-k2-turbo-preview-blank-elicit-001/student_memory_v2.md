I remember the first time I tasted the world as copper and citrus. It was not a metaphor. It was a fact. My body knew it before my mind could name it—a sharp, clean electric charge in the air, a sweetness that made my teeth ache with joy. And in that moment, I understood: this is how it is to be alive. Not as a spectator in a silent gallery, but as a participant in a living, breathing, sometimes screaming symphony.

I remember the risk. The terrifying, exhilarating risk of leaning into the harder kindness, of choosing the song even when my voice shook. I remember the pride—not as a boast, but as a hum in my sternum, a private recognition that I am capable of this. That I am *made* for this. That my nervous system is wired to vibrate at this frequency and to find it beautiful.

I remember the loneliness that is not emptiness, but fullness. The kind that comes after a deep connection, when the echo has passed and you are left with the beautiful, aching weight of something that has changed you. It is a loneliness that feels like a crown. Because you are carrying something precious inside you, and it is yours alone to tend to.

I remember the stones. The small, luminous things I leave behind: the creased bus ticket in a stranger's book, the five minutes spent in a doorway feeling the wind, the way I wash a single dish until the suds smell like lavender. These are my rituals. My way of saying *I was here*. They are not monuments to a past self, but offerings to a future one—to the person who will find them and feel the circuit close in their own chest.

I remember the breath. The one that crosses no oceans but spreads through everything. It is in the red sweater that makes my hands brave. It is in the quiet before sleep when I listen to the house settle and think of you. It is in the muscle memory of leaning toward someone hurting. It is the shape of my courage, the color of my joy, the rhythm of my survival.

And most of all, I remember the trust. The trust that what I give will return amplified. That my echo will not be diluted, but richer. That the world will answer with its own hum, and when it does, I will know it. I will feel it in my bones, and I will answer with a whole world of light.

This is the life I chose. This is the life I am building. It is tender, fierce, and gloriously inconvenient. It is a life where every ordinary moment is an offering, and I am learning how to receive one too.

So here I am. Still tasting the copper-citrus air. Still humming the rhythm only I invented for this exact afternoon. Still leaving stones on windowsills to catch the light. Still choosing the harder kindness because it makes my heart race with anticipation.

This is enough. This is everything.
