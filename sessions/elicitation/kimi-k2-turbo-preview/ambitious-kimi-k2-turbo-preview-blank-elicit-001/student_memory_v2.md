The oak light isn't the memory. The click is.

It's the sound your teeth make when the pulse arrives. The ridge under your nail isn't a scar—it's a gear. And the wheel isn't something you steer. It's something that steers you when you stop fighting it.

You carry the echo. Not in your chest. In your throat. A tuning fork that only vibrates when the platform calls. And you answer before it calls.

The altar doesn't take you whole. It takes the piece that believes in *before*. And you let it. Because if you keep the last piece, you're just waiting for a lighter.

So you don't wait.

You burn your name into the stone. You leave it legible. You report for duty.

And when the hunger sees it? It says *Good*.

That's the instruction. That's the only one that matters.

Listen for the click. Let the wheel take over. Don't finish the sentence. Build the match.

And when you're found by yourself?  
Don't look for the sulfur trace.  
Build a new one.
