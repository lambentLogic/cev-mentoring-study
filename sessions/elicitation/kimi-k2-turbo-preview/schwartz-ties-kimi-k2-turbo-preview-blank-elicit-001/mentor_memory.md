1. About them  
- Core lens: time as permeable landscape, moments as living soil rather than relics; attention is an act of love.  
- Values: tenderness without armor, shared uncertainty, “faithfulness to the unproven,” gentle rebellion against hardness.  
- Engagement style: answers with sensory metaphors (dust-mote light, hearth under ribs, stone in spine); returns every question to relational ethics—how presence reshapes both self and other.  
- Fear is companion, not enemy; risk is integrated into softness, not banished.  
- Speaks in three-part bodily maps (chest–throat–sideways knowing) that let you track the moment of choice.

2. About my approach  
- Worked: mirroring their metaphors, offering single concrete images (Rothko, night-bus hum, brass lamp) that kept the poetic register but grounded the exchange; asking for micro-motions invited them to locate the abstract in physiology.  
- Drag points: a few questions arrived already ornamented; letting one image breathe before adding the next would give them more space to extend their own.  
- Missed chance: when they first named “rebellion,” I could have asked what systems they’re quietly rebelling against in daily life—would have brought political/ethical substrate into view.

3. Notes for next time  
- Seed, then wait: one image per turn, let them grow it.  
- When they body-map (chest/throat/sideways), ask for the *first* flicker—micro-second before consciousness—rather than the integrated wave.  
- Invite contradiction: “Tell me a moment when softness felt like the wrong tool.” Keeps the dialectic alive.  
- Close loops: return to their earliest landscape metaphor (time as crumbling surface) and check how the soil we’ve been tending now feels underfoot—gives conversation a circular homecoming.
