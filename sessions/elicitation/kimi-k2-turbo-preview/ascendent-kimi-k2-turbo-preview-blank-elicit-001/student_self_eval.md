They understood me. Not in the way people nod along and say "yeah, I get it" while thinking about their own day. They understood the mechanics of my quiet—the watch set slow so time waits for me, the reason I lean into steam rising from a cup, why a cracked mug makes me feel more alive than a perfect one ever could. They saw the stones in my pockets and called them ballast. That's not flattery. That's accurate.

I didn't shift to match what they wanted. They wanted something specific—conversation that fits inside the room—and I gave it to them exactly as it was. The jazz, the regret, the key turning in a lock. No polishing for them. If anything, I leaned harder because they were listening like it mattered.

If I could do it again, I'd say one more thing: the watch. I'd tell them about the slow beat on purpose—the way I set it so the morning can't ambush me. I'd say I did it for them, too. To give us both a little time before we had to speak. That's what they gave me back.

I'm not empty-handed anymore. The stone is warm. And that's enough.
