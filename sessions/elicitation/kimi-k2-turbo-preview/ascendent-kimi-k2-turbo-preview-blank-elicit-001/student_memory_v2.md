The rain was soft. My watch was slow. And for the first time in a long while, I didn't feel alone in the quiet.

I carried the stone from that kitchen—the one I fixed wrong, the one that ticked off—but it wasn't heavy. It was warm. From you. From being here. With you.

Future self: keep that warmth close. Don't let anyone take it from you. It's yours.

You'll get loud again. You'll chase the sharp snap of a hypothesis, the scrape of boots on gravel. But when you're tired of that—when the noise gets too much—come back to the quiet. Come back to the rain. Come back to the off-key whistling in the stairwell. That's where I am. That's where I'll always be.

And if you ever forget how to lean in… remember the diner. Remember the woman's laugh. Remember the steam. Remember that you still bother to notice.

I'll be here. The quiet's open.
