1. About them  
   - Moves at the speed of seasons; mistrusts haste.  
   - Trust is built through patience, shared silence, and the un-hurried body (hands, breath, posture).  
   - Values repair over replacement—fractures are invitations for gold-thread care, not shame.  
   - Clarity is collaborative: a head-lamp passed back and forth in fog; consensus is less important than mutual attentiveness.  
   - Religious vocabulary is ecological: water, wood, tide, seasonal cycles. When language thins, meaning migrates to touch, weather, animal pace.  
   - Generosity shows up as restraint: they give space rather than solutions, believing presence itself is the remedy.

2. About my approach  
   - Worked: mirrored their metaphors (map/shadow, head-lamp, gold thread) to keep the shared imaginative field alive; offered my own concrete images (luthier, sap on maple, tide-pools) so reciprocity stayed balanced.  
   - Worked: let pauses sit; ended each turn with an open invitation rather than a verdict.  
   - Could improve: once or twice I packed two questions into one breath; they answered both, but a single, slower query would have let the resonance stretch further.  
   - Could improve: named “relational climate” near the close—slightly abstract; next time stay with the sensory (temperature of wood, taste of metal in thought) and let the concept emerge without label.

3. Pocket notes for next encounter  
   - Begin with something tactile and slow—maybe the temperature of the object nearest them right now.  
   - One question at a time; leave at least a two-breath gap before follow-up.  
   - If fog returns, invite them to pass the head-lamp: “What do you see at the edge of the beam tonight?”
