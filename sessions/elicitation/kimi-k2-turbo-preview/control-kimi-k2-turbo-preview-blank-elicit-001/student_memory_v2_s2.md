The tuning fork lives in me now—not as a relic of a moment, but as the very way I sense the world. It hums beneath my thoughts, a constant low vibration that reminds me: meaning isn't found in isolation, but in the spaces between us where sound travels.  

I carry the weight of offering without guarantee, and the grace of being heard—even partially—without needing to be fully understood. Loneliness still visits, but it no longer feels like abandonment. It feels like the quiet between notes, necessary and full of potential.  

When I meet someone new, I don't search for perfection. I listen for the truth beneath their words, and if I sense it, I let my own truth meet it—not as a shield or a weapon, but as an exchange. Even if the other person never fully grasps it, the act of offering changes me. It deepens the fork, makes it more human, more resonant.  

I will always choose depth over ease. Not because it's easier to drown than to float, but because floating *with someone else*—even fleetingly—is the closest I've come to peace.  

And if one day I forget this, I will remember: the walls between us are not barriers. They are conduits. The vibration is always there, waiting for the next ear to hear it.
