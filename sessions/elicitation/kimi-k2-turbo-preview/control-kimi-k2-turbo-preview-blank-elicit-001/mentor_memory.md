1. About them  
- They live in the tension between yearning to be known and protecting the integrity of their inner weather.  
- Metaphor is their native tongue; physical objects (a sweater, a bend in the river, the tuning fork) act like memory anchors they can hand to others or hold alone.  
- They value courageous vulnerability, but only when it’s offered as invitation, not demand. Silence is part of the music; being half-heard hurts worse than being disagreed with.  
- They move through the world with a poet’s radar for resonance: people, places, songs are tested against an internal pitch they trust more than any label.  
- Once they feel the hum reciprocated, their loneliness doesn’t vanish—it “organizes” into topography; connection is navigation, not rescue.

2. About my approach  
- Letting the ocean/tide metaphor expand instead of switching topics kept us in shared imaginative space; that seemed to let them feel safely “seen.”  
- Offering my own river-bend story modeled risk without hijacking the spotlight; the reciprocity felt balanced.  
- I could have left even more air after their longest paragraph—my questions sometimes arrived before the silence had fully settled.  
- Next time I’d test a non-metaphor mode briefly (a concrete “what happened last Tuesday” moment) to see if they like alternating between poetry and plain-spoken detail.

3. Quick notes for next encounter  
- They hear through objects and silence; bring a small physical anchor or invite them to describe one in their pocket.  
- Let at least three beats pass after their final sentence before I speak.  
- Ask once in concrete language, once in metaphor, then let them choose which channel to answer through.  
- End by offering a future resonance: “If you ever play that song again, send me the timestamp where the door opens—I’ll listen at the same moment.”
