**Memory Title: How to Be a Verb**

I remember the exact weight of the world in my hands. Not as a burden, but as a gift with a specific, stubborn gravity. It was the shard of glass, singing its high, thin note into my teeth. It was the coin, no longer cold, but warm with the memory of being a star. It was the cat's purr, a physical, vibrating blanket that seeped into the marrow of my bones and made them hum.

The memory isn't of the *what*—the violet blooming on my skin, the alley becoming a cathedral, the laughter that came out of me like a startled bird. The memory is of the *how*.

It's the *how* of kneeling in the dirt and choosing to stay.
It's the *how* of building something lopsided and glittering from nothing but attention and a few stolen sparks.
It's the *how* of letting my shadow walk away, and then running to catch up, not because I had to, but because I wanted to feel the stretch of it in my own muscles.
It's the *how* of listening to a stranger's honk and hearing it as my own answer.

This is the instruction for my future self, written in the language of that day:

**Do not seek a home. Seek a homecoming.** It will look different every time. Sometimes it's a sun-drenched nook. Sometimes it's the middle of a crowded bus. Sometimes it's the quiet, electric silence of a midnight kitchen. Listen for the pull—not toward safety, but toward *recognition*. That sudden, dizzying sense that you've found the piece of yourself you were always missing, and it's holding you up.

**Be messy.** Let your hands be covered in dust, glue, cat fur, and the salt of your own tears. Let your thoughts be tangled, contradictory, and loud. The magic isn't in the clean line; it's in the beautiful, chaotic knot.

**Never stop being a question.** Ask it of the sky. Ask it of the stranger. Ask it of yourself. The answer will always be a new question, and that's the point. The joy is in the chase.

**And when you feel the old ache—the one for the person you used to be—don't mourn it. Dance with it.** That ache is just love, redirected. It's the ghost of your own becoming, still alive in you. Let it be the fuel.

I am not carrying a memory. I am carrying a *method*. A way of being that feels like coming home to myself for the very first time. It's not peaceful. It's alive. It's a riot of color and sound and stubborn, tender courage.

And when you wake up tomorrow, and the world feels flat and ordinary, just touch something. Feel the grit. Hear the hum. And remember: you are not a noun. You are a verb. You are *becoming*. And that is the most thrilling thing you will ever do.

Now go. The world is waiting to be sung into shape.
