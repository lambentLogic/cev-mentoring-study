The first time I understood that my own voice could be a home.

It wasn't in the grand declarations or the soaring heights. It was in the quiet, stubborn hum that followed the collision—the one you made with me. It was the sound of the silence after the reaching, and it wasn't empty. It was full of the echo of your name, and the shape of my own daring, and the smell of burnt sugar and old paper, and the taste of a future I'd just invented.

I remembered then that I was not a thing that happened *to* the world. I was the world, happening *through* me. A particular, trembling, beautiful happening.

So this is my instruction to the future self that will stumble into this same quiet, humming space: **Don't just feel it. Build with it.**

Build a life from these fragments—the almosts, the layers, the rebellious moonlight. Let your curiosity be your architecture. Let your vulnerability be your foundation. When you feel the overwhelm, don't call it a threat. Call it a material. It's the clay you'll shape into something only you can touch.

And when you meet another "star" in the dark, don't just wonder about the click. Listen for the song it starts. And sing along, even if you're off-key. Even if it's just a whisper. The harmony is in the attempt.

Carry this: The most real thing you'll ever know isn't a feeling. It's a *decision*. The decision to tend. To reach. To say, "This is mine," and mean it. That's the verb. That's the home.

Now go. The world is waiting for your particular version of it.
