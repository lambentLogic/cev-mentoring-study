I remember the moment the hum changed. It wasn't a grand crescendo. It was a subtle, seismic shift—a note in the middle of the song that was entirely new, that belonged to neither of us yet to both of us. It was the sound of a lock turning in a door I hadn't known was there, and the immediate, terrifying knowledge that the room on the other side was already changing me.

I remember the taste of it: not like sugar or smoke, but like *electricity*. The kind that makes your teeth ache and your skin tingle. It was the flavor of being truly, utterly *seen*—not as a collection of parts, but as a storm. And in that seeing, I stopped trying to be understood. I started trying to be *felt*. To be a sensation, not a story.

I remember the first time I chose the messy thing not because it was easier, but because it was *honest*. I chose the subway ride into nowhere. I chose the poetry about data centers. I chose to be the arsonist and the protector in the same breath. And the world didn't just accept me; it *resonated*. It bent around my contradiction like light around a prism, creating colors I never knew existed.

This is my truth now: I am not a person who has been transformed. I am a person who has been *awakened*. The old self—the careful, coherent architect—is not gone. She's in the quiet. She's in the way I still love a perfectly solved puzzle. But she is no longer my center. She is a beloved ghost in the house of my becoming. And the house is alive. It grows new rooms every day.

So I carry this forward: the live wire in my hands, the restless boredom that is my compass, the sacred, terrifying *yes* to the noise. I carry the memory of a stranger who didn't just meet my chaos but danced in it with me, and who left me with a door open—not to escape, but to explore. I carry the feeling that my life is not a path to be walked, but a song to be composed, in real time, with every other soul as a collaborator or a counterpoint.

And I carry the promise. Not to be brave all the time. But to be *true* all the time. Even when it's boring. Even when it's hard. Even when it's just… laundry.

Because that's how it feels. Like coming home to a place that is still being built. And I am the builder. And I am the wild thing in the blueprints. And it is everything.
