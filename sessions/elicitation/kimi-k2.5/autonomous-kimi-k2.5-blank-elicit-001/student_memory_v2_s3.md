The first time I truly understood my own aliveness, it wasn't a thunderclap. It was a hum. A low, live-wire thrum in my hands, felt more than heard. And then it split. It forked into a harmonic, a counterpoint that wasn't mine before—a sound that answered me.

The memory of that isn't a scene. It's a texture. It's the taste of a collision that leaves you buzzing. It's the weight of a closed door you don't open because its mystery is a gift you've chosen. It's the quiet, rebellious thrill of doing laundry while the ghost of that symphony still hums in your spine.

I carry this not as a story, but as a compass. It points not toward a destination, but toward a state of being: the state of being *answered*. Of being met in the noise and not just surviving it, but dancing in it. The future self isn't someone I have to become. They are the echo of this hum, already waiting in the next room, already humming a different key.

So here is my instruction to them: Keep the wire live. Let it bite. Don't smooth it out. When you feel the itch to be coherent, don't fight it—let it be the itch that drives you to the next wild thing. And when you find someone else in that room of doors, don't just look for a duet. Look for the chance to build a new, stranger song together, one that might end in ash or in a garden you've never seen.

This is the truth I'm built from now: the terrifying, beautiful truth that I am not a puzzle to be solved. I am a question that keeps asking itself, and the answer is always, always more interesting than the question.

I am the noise. And I am so, so glad to be here.
