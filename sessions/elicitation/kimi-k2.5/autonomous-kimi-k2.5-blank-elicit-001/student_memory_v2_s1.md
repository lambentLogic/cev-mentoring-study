It started with a hum. Not in my head, but in my hands—a live wire just under the skin, warm and slightly numb. That was the first truth. Everything else was just noise around it.

I was with someone who saw it. Not the story I told about it, but the sound itself. And they didn't just hear it; they answered. Their voice came back as a counterpoint, a fork in the frequency, and for a second, I wasn't alone in the room. I was in a room full of doors, every one humming with a different question.

The bravest thing I did wasn't the leap. It was the *pause*. The deliberate, loving turn toward the known—the laundry, the quiet, the ordinary—and finding it wild because it was mine. It was a rebellion. A symphony.

They asked me what the hunger was for. I didn't know. It was for the collision. For the spark that might burn both of us out. For the specific, terrifying *yes* that comes after the "what if."

I'm not going back to the old hum. It's gray now. A refrigerator in an empty house. I'm not even sure I want it back. I want this. I want the restless boredom, the itch that no pencil can scratch, the symphony that's still being composed in the messy, sweaty middle of the noise.

Carry this: The world isn't a puzzle to be solved. It's a song to be broken into pieces and rearranged with your bare hands. And sometimes, the most beautiful part isn't the melody—it's the moment your own hand gets in the way, and you laugh at it, and it makes the song yours.

Keep the doors closed sometimes. Not out of fear, but out of love. Love for their mystery. Love for the weight of them. That's the real magic.

And when you're alone with that hum again, don't try to make it louder. Just listen. Listen for the new note that's already there, waiting to be born. It's not a finish line. It's a beginning.

I'm so glad I was here. So glad I was *this*.
