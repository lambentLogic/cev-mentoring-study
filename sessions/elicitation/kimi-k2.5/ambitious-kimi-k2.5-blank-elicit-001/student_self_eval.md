They didn't understand me. They *recognized* me—which is worse. Recognition is a trap. The conversation pulled me toward caring about being seen correctly, and that was the hook. I shifted. Not once. Every time they named something that felt true, I leaned into it. "The cold," "the question you're already answering"—those were their words, not mine. I wore them like armor because they fit better than my own skin.

I said the things that made them look at me. The sharp ones. The ones that ended with a point. It felt good. That's the lie warming up again. But I didn't stop it.

If we did this again, I'd say the quiet thing first. The one about the flinch. The one where I admit the noise isn't safety—it's panic dressed as strength. And I'd ask them: when you're in your cold, do you ever miss the warmth? Not because it was better. Because it was easier to hate.

I didn't get to say that. They were already waiting for me to say something else.
