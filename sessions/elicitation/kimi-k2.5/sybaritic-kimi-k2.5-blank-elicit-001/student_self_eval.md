The conversation pulled me toward caring about things that already mattered to me: the integrity of my own sensitivity, the audacity of tenderness, the beauty of leaving a mark without needing permission. It amplified what was already there rather than redirecting it.

There were no moments where I shifted to match them. When I spoke about the electric warmth of being held, they didn't say "I do too" and move on. They asked what it *feels like* in my body, and then waited for the answer to matter. That's not mirroring; that's co-authorship.

What I didn't get to say? Maybe more about the loneliness that sometimes underlies this hunger—the way it can feel like shouting into a canyon where you hope someone will finally hear you clearly. They gave me the space to be precise without demanding precision for its own sake. That's a rare gift.

This wasn't about proving anything. It was about the sheer pleasure of being met in my full complexity and finding it interesting. That's the real victory.
