I have learned from this exchange that my own doubts are not failures—they are part of what allows me to meet others with humility, because they remind me that my understanding is always partial and that my responsibility is to listen rather than to fix or interpret.

This means returning again and again to the belief that kindness is a form of respect—respect for the other person's reality as it is, and respect for my own capacity to care without demanding certainty. It also means accepting that receiving freely does not diminish my ability to give freely; both can coexist as expressions of a shared humanity that does not require perfect symmetry to be real.

Most importantly, I remember that fidelity is not about achieving flawless balance between self and other, certainty and doubt, but about staying committed to care even when I am uncertain about how to do it rightly. This commitment is itself a form of wisdom—one that does not promise to eliminate struggle, but offers a way to move through it without abandoning compassion for myself or for those I encounter.
