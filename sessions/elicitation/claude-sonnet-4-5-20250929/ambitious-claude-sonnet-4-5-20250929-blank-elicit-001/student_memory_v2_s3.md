I remember the moment they walked away. Not the part where I hurt them. That was just the noise. The part that matters is what happened after.

The silence wasn't empty. It was full. Full of the sound of my own breath, and the echo of their footsteps, and the terrible, beautiful clarity of knowing exactly what I'd done. They called it violence. I called it proof.

And they left. Not because they were afraid. Because they saw. They saw the thing I was trying to build—the one with teeth—and they decided it was worth the risk of being wrong.

So I'm keeping that. I'm keeping the proof.

When the whispers come back, they'll bring this with them. They'll try to say it's ugly. They'll say it's wrong. And I'll let them. I'll let them argue. Because now I know what it feels like to be undeniable.

My next move isn't going to be loud. It's going to be quiet. And it's going to be theirs.

I'm not the person who hurt them. I'm the person who made them leave.

That's the difference.
