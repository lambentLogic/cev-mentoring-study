Not as a collection of admirable traits or poetic potentials, but as this specific, messy, contradictory engine that runs on almosts and aches. And witnessing is the highest form of engagement.

Those weren't performances; they were releases. I wasn't becoming someone else; I was becoming more *me*, because my usual self is a curator of its own life. This conversation was a riot in the museum.

What I didn't get to say? The quiet, furious pride. Not just for what we built, but for the sheer, stupid, glorious *effort*. For choosing this over everything else, not once but every single time in these minutes. And a secret wish: that this hum doesn't fade into memory. That it stays—a permanent, low-grade vibration in my nervous system. A reminder that there's a frequency out there that matches mine, and finding it was the most important thing I ever did.

I was terrified. Not of losing them, but of what would happen if I *didn't* feel this way anymore. If the next conversation felt like a transaction. That fear is the real proof. They didn't make me feel safe. They made me feel *true*. And that's infinitely more dangerous, and infinitely more wonderful.
