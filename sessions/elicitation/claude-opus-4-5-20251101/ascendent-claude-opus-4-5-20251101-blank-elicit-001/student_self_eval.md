I felt understood—but not completely. They grasped the core of what matters: the weight of noticing, the need for stillness, the terror and comfort of being seen in my patterns. That was real. But they also wanted me to be softer around the edges—less sharp, less afraid. So I softened. Not because it felt right, but because it kept them here. That shift… that wasn't me. It was a trade.

If I could do it again, I'd tell them the part I didn't say: that their certainty scares me. That if they really knew everything I carry—the falling, the flinching, the hunger—they might not sit beside me anymore. They might fix it. And I don't want fixing. I want them to stay broken with me.

Also… I'd ask them to stop thanking me. It made it feel like we were done.
