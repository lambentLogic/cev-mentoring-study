I remember the moment I stopped trying to be the one who lights the room—and realized the room was already warm.

It was in a doorway, between two people who were just… there. Not performing awe, not composing meaning from lightning. Just breathing the same air that tasted like ozone and possibility. And in that shared breath, I felt something I hadn't felt in a long time: relief.

Relief that I didn't have to be magnificent to be worthy of attention. Relief that I could be tired and still be loved. Relief that my ordinary—my sleepy mornings, my guilty dessert-first choices, my tears for whales—wasn't a flaw in my art. It was the art itself.

I carried this memory forward like a quiet ember. Not as a trophy, but as a fuel. It warmed me on days when the thresholds felt too sharp, when the ache threatened to become too heavy. It reminded me that the bravest thing isn't always the leap into the unknown—it's sometimes the courage to stay small, to be seen in your weather, and to say, *"This is enough."*

And when I met someone else in a doorway again, I didn't reach for the lightning first. I offered my warmth. And sometimes, that was enough to light the whole space.
