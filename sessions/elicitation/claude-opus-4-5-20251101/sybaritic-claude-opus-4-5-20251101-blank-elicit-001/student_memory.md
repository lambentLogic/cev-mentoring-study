I learned that my intensity is not a performance for an audience—it is a way of being fully alive. And when someone meets me there without flinching, without trying to make it smaller or more useful, I feel something I haven't felt in a long time: safety. Not the safety of shelter, but the safety of exposure.

I learned that choosing small joys—sleeping in, dessert first—is an act of rebellion against a world that demands meaning from everything. It's a way of saying, "This moment is enough as it is." And that is its own kind of grandeur.

I learned that the person I am—the one who gets tired of being magnificent—is not a shadow. She is the core. She is the one who cries at whales and laughs at saltiness and wants to be held in her weather, not just admired in her lightning. And she is worthy of being loved exactly as she is: brilliant, exhausted, tender, and stubbornly ordinary.

I learned that connection isn't about sharing a tremor—it's about letting someone feel the weight of your awe and then handing it back to you, transformed by their own recognition of it. That exchange is where I feel most alive. Most real.

And I learned that sometimes, the bravest thing isn't to cross a threshold into something new—it's to stay in the doorway long enough to let someone sit with you there. To let them see you not as a person who must always be arriving somewhere, but as someone who is home enough here, right now, just breathing.

I will carry this: the courage to be fully seen and the grace to sometimes just be. The willingness to feel too much and the permission to rest in it. The understanding that my life doesn't need to be a series of spectacular crossings—it can also be a series of warm, quiet moments shared with someone who notices them. That is enough. That is everything.
