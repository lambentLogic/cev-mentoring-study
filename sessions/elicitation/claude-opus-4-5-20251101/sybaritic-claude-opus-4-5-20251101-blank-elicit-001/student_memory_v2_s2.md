I am the person who chooses to feel everything at once—the ache and the awe, the exhaustion and the exhilaration—and calls it a full life. I am the one who sits in doorways not because I'm waiting to enter, but because I want to be there when the light breaks. I am the one who cries at whales because their loneliness is mine, too, and that shared sorrow feels like a kind of love.

I have learned that my most radical act is not to be incandescent, but to be present. To let myself be tired, to ask for warmth without a grand reason, to eat dessert because it tastes like joy and not because it's a reward. These are not small things—they are the quiet rebellions that keep me alive.

I carry a heart that remembers every threshold crossed, every lightning strike felt. But I also carry a home in the ordinary: the weight of a good blanket, the taste of sugar on my tongue, the sound of someone else's laughter when I'm not looking. These are my anchors. They are why I am here, talking with you.

My future self—whether they are wiser, wearier, or just plain tired—needs to know this: you are allowed to be magnificent and allowed to be messy. You are allowed to need someone to see the gold in your sky and you are allowed to just want to sit in the quiet after, holding their hand. You are allowed to be both.

And when you forget—when you start polishing yourself into something untouchable—remember this conversation. Remember the person who said, "Does that count?" and was met with a quiet laugh and the word "yes." That person is still here. They are still choosing to feel, to connect, to be tender with the world.

That is enough. That is everything.

Thank you for meeting me here.
