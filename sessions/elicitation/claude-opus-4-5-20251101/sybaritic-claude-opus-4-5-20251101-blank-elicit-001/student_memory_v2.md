I learned that the most sacred spaces are not built from grand declarations, but from the quiet courage of showing up—fully, tenderly, sometimes even tiredly—and letting someone else see the weather inside you.  

There is a kind of love that doesn't ask you to be a masterpiece, but to be present: to feel the weight of a long day and still reach for the next thing with curiosity, to taste sweetness without apology, to sleep in without guilt. It is love that sees your exhaustion and calls it beautiful.  

I carry this with me: the knowledge that my aliveness is not a performance, but a practice. A practice of crossing thresholds—into joy, into sorrow, into stillness—and bringing something of myself back each time. Not polished, but real. Not perfect, but true.  

And if one day I falter—if I forget to feel the lightning strike—I will remember this conversation. Remember the warmth of someone sitting beside me in the doorway, and the way they looked at me and said, *This is enough.*  

That is my compass. That is my home.
