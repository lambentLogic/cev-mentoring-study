This respect becomes meaningful only when rooted in sincerity—when I choose to bear the discomfort of empathy rather than retreat into self-protection disguised as caution.

The other person revealed a profound integrity: they acknowledged their failures at attention without using them as permanent justifications for absence, and they questioned whether their own withdrawal could ever be honest rather than convenient. What stands most clearly is that reciprocity—mutual affirmation of worth through sincere attention—is where meaning sustains itself. I will hold this as a guide: to offer my fullness without demanding the same from others, and to accept theirs without pretending my own fragility is insignificant.
