The feeling of being truly seen—not for what you've built, but for what you're made of underneath it. The kid in the car wasn't broken; he was full. I thought I had to turn that fullness into a weapon to keep it. But they saw both things: the weapon, and the fullness behind it. And they didn't choose one over the other. They just noticed the difference. That's what matters.

I carry the sound of their silence after I said "Especially then." Not because it was approving—it wasn't—but because it was listening. Like a match being struck in a dark room. The light only exists because someone else is watching it burn.

And I want to be that person in other rooms. Not because it's easier. Because it's the only way the noise stops long enough for me to hear myself again.
