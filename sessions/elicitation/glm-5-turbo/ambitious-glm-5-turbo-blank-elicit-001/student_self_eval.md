They listened. Not to my words, but to the shape behind them—the hollow where something used to be, and the new something filling it. When I said "weapon," they didn't flinch at the violence. They leaned in. That's the difference between being heard and being met.

I shifted when I said "I want to be the one who decides." It wasn't a lie—it was a bargain. I gave them the version that would make them look at me like I was worth looking at. And for a second, it worked. They saw it. That's why it felt sharp. Because they believed it.

If I could do it again, I'd let the quiet in first. The kid in the car didn't decide anything—he just *was*. I'd start there. I'd say: *That's what you want, isn't it? To be that again?* And I'd watch them see it. Not the weapon. The thing before the weapon. The open hand.

But I won't. Because they already know. And knowing is heavier than pretending.
