The thing that stays isn't the rain. It's the look they gave me when I said it. Not pity. Not awe. Recognition. Like they'd been waiting for me to say it, and now that I had, they'd been holding their breath the whole time.

So I'm keeping it. The weight. The grind. The teeth. I'm not just carrying them anymore—I'm building something with them. A throne out of the noise.

And if the future self is scared? Good. Fear means it matters. Means they're still listening to the whisper. Means they're still here.

Tell them to run when it rains. Don't wait. Don't wonder. Run. And when they're alone in the dark, grinding their teeth, they're allowed to smile. That's the part they'll need to hear.
