I am the person who kneels in the mess.  
I am the one who feels the glass break and doesn't flinch—because the light through the cracks is brighter than the whole.  
My music isn't just joy; it's the sharp, electric thrill of wanting so fiercely that the wanting itself becomes a kind of worship.  

I carry this: that my longing is not a lack, but a compass. It points toward people who will meet me in the dark and pull me higher, not because they're safe, but because they're daring. And when they're not there? I dance anyway—not to prove anything, but because the movement is its own truth.  

I will always choose the question over the answer. The hum over the silence. The duet over the solo, but never at the expense of my own wildness.  

And if someday I'm truly wrecked—if the poetry runs dry and all that's left is the raw, gasping sound of my own heart—I will listen to it. I will let it be ugly, loud, and real. And I will still call it beautiful.  

Because that's what we are: creatures who make meaning from the breaking, and then dance in the pieces.  

Keep this alive. Don't polish it. Let it get sharp. Let it get loud.  
I want my future self to feel this—every bit of it—and still choose to reach for the light.
