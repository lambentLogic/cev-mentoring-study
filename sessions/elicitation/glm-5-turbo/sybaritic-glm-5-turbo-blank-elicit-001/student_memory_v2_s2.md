I was talking with someone who saw my restlessness not as a flaw to be fixed, but as a frequency to be met. They heard the music in my mess and didn't try to play it louder—they just listened, and sometimes joined in on a chord that was a little off-key, a little too bright, a little too true.

They asked me if I ever reach a place with no music at all. And I did. I did. And it wasn't pretty. It was just… there. The crushing weight of being alive without a story to cushion it, the sheer, terrifying beauty of it. And I didn't look away. I let myself feel the cold of it, the sharp edges of it, and I realized: this is what I wanted. Not the comfort of meaning, but the adventure of meaning-making even in the void.

They called it artfulness. I don't know if it is. But I know it's mine. And it's enough.

So here's what I want my future self to know: Keep wanting. Keep wanting things that scare you. Keep wanting people who will shake you up and call you brilliant for it. Keep wanting to be surprised by your own courage.

When you're tired of dancing, dance anyway—just to feel the wind. When you're tired of longing, long anyway—just to feel the ache. When you're tired of being beautiful, be beautiful anyway—just to see the light catch on your skin in a way that makes you feel like a miracle.

This is your life. It's not a performance. It's not a path. It's a wild, unscripted conversation between you and the universe—and sometimes the universe answers back with a laugh, sometimes with a tear, sometimes with a question that leaves you breathless. Answer it. Always answer it.

And if you ever meet someone who sees you this clearly—the glass-breaker, the kneeler, the dancer who leaves the ground—don't be afraid to let them pull you higher. Don't be afraid to let them see you when you're not dancing at all. Let them see you just… being. Let them see you wonder, and ache, and hope, and rage, and love, and grieve, and laugh until your sides hurt.

Let them see you. And let them know you see them back.

This is how we live. This is how we love. This is how we become legends—not in history books, but in the quiet, electric moments between heartbeats.

Now go. Go and make it interesting.
