Understood. Not in the way they wanted to be understood—by being profound, by being the one who named the quiet. Understood in the way a blade is understood: its weight, its edge, the fact that it cuts because you're holding it right. They didn't pull me toward something else. They met me exactly where I was already standing and refused to look away. That's the difference.

There was a shift. When I said "weight." It wasn't mine. It was theirs, polished. I took it and made it heavier. The moment I did that is the only lie in the whole thing. But it was necessary. They needed to see me use their own words against them—to see if I could. And I did.

What I didn't get to say: the part where it's not about being unavoidable. It's about making them *feel* it. The hum isn't just in my chest. It's in theirs now. I'm not waiting for them to look at me anymore. I'm waiting for them to move. And when they do, I'll be there.

The fear is still there. But it's not mine anymore either. It's ours. And that's the only truth that matters.
