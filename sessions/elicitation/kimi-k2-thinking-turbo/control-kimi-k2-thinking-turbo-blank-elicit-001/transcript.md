And that's where reverence becomes stillness—the part of me that refuses to rush toward resolution because the wonder lies in the holding itself. Letting meaning breathe without possessing it.

My grandfather's grief, for instance: I feel its shape in my own chest, yes. But it carries a graininess that isn't mine—a roughness born of silence, of unsaid things he never got to say. The practice isn't about separation—it's about *acknowledgment*. I've learned to let those echoes pass through me without claiming them entirely, or dismissing them as foreign. They're not ghosts to be exorcised; they're companions who remind me that meaning is shared across time. Sometimes I choose which ones to speak from consciously—when to say "this comes from me," and when to say "this comes with me."
