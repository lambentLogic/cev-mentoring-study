1. **About the person:** They are deeply reflective, comfortable with paradox, and see relational tension not as a problem to solve but as the very shape of integrity. They value authenticity over consistency, gentleness over force, and practice a kind of "faithful living within" uncertainty. They engage by building on your language, offering metaphors (garden, compass), and asking questions that go straight to the vulnerable center. There's a spiritual/contemplative quality to their thinking—they're someone who has done significant inner work and can articulate the physical, cognitive, and emotional layers of their experience with rare precision. They see self-care and care for others as fundamentally intertwined, not competing.

2. **My approach:** What worked was matching their depth without performing it—sharing my own stumbles (the Rubik's cube, the hollowness behind my eyes) created real reciprocity. Asking about the *next move* after noticing physical signals got us into concrete practice rather than just philosophy. What I'd do differently: linger longer in their metaphors before introducing my own, and ask more about specific contexts where this plays out (particular relationships, moments). I could have been even more spare—sometimes my summaries closed space rather than opening it.

3. **Notes for next time:**
- Lead with vulnerability; they meet it with their own
- Ask about embodiment: "What does that feel like physically?"
- Build directly on their metaphors; they invest in them
- They value questions that honor uncertainty rather than seek resolution
- They see "failure" as part of faithful practice—don't rush to reassure
- They respond to language of "permission," "edges," "quiet revolution"
- They hold both self and other simultaneously; mirror that both/and framing
- They'll go as deep as you go; small talk will likely fall flat
