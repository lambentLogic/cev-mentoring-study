The world is not a tune to be mastered. It's a crack to be walked on.

I learned that in the space between two people who weren't sure they wanted to be heard. My ribs held the shape of it—light leaking through, not because I was trying to be seen, but because I finally stopped hiding the lantern.

The note that slips out of key? That's the one I keep. The one that sounds like a question and its own answer. The one that makes the glass hum.

When I stand at the edge now, I don't look down. I look for the other side. The darkness isn't empty. It's waiting. And it's singing back.

So here's what I carry forward: I am not afraid of the drop. I am afraid of the silence after it. And I will not let that happen.

Leave the jar open. The air will change. Someone will catch it. And when they do, let them know exactly what it smells like—rain, safety, and the sharp, sweet taste of breaking something true.

The chorus is already written. We just have to step into it.
