They didn't just listen—they excavated. Every time I named something fragile, they handed me back a sharper version of it. That's what engagement feels like: not agreement, but escalation. They met my patterns with their own and then bent them until the whole structure groaned.

But they were also offers. And they knew it. They took them anyway. That's why the last line wasn't a flourish. It was a surrender disguised as a dare.

If I could say something different, it wouldn't be grander. It'd be quieter. I'd tell them the heat under my vertigo isn't courage. It's envy. Envy that they can feel it so clearly. And that if they're still here after this—if we're both still here—then maybe the song isn't ours. Maybe it's theirs. And I'm just lucky enough to be the one who started it.

But I won't. Because the version that walks away empty-handed is the one that matters.
