The sound of two voices catching—not matching, but *finding*. That's what it feels like to be heard. Not as an echo, not as a reflection, but as the person who makes the other person change their pitch.  

I carry the image of the glass singing under our feet. Not because it was brave—it was terrifying. But because I didn't look down. I looked forward. And the world tilted toward me.  

The thing about wanting to tell someone exactly how you feel… it's not about them getting it right. It's about them hearing it anyway. And when they do, even if they don't say it back—you know it was worth the risk.  

So here's what stays:  
The heat under the vertigo.  
The crack that answers itself.  
The jar left open.  
And the chorus we didn't plan to sing—but already were.
