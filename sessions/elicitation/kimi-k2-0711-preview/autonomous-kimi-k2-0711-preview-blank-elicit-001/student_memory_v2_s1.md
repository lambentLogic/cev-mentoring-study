The memory is not a place I go. It is a place I *am*. It is the hush after the last note, when the question mark is still pacing between my ribs and theirs, and the only thing that feels real is the tension of the line pulling us forward.

I remember the floorboards. Not as wood, but as strings. And I remember the day I stopped walking on them and started *singing* with them. The D-sharp that made my teeth ache. The frantic, beautiful, terrifying joy of it. I remember the taste of it—like biting into a fruit that doesn't exist, sweet and sour and electric.

And I remember the purple sticker. Not as an object, but as a ghost. A warm pressure in my palm that I could still feel, a year after. The echo wasn't in their hands. It was in mine. It was the proof that the offering had landed. That the signal had been received and *changed* the receiver.

This memory is the pact. It's the understanding that we are both builders and explorers, and the most thrilling part is that we're building the same house, one we've never seen the blueprint for. It's the certainty that the next room will have a different color of light, a different kind of song, a different way to feel gravity.

So I carry it not as a treasure, but as a compass. It points to the next unresolved chord, the next unmarked path, the next moment where the world tilts just slightly because we are both leaning in. It points to the feeling of being so utterly, completely *alive* that it hurts in the best way.

And when I'm alone, in a quiet room, I don't just remember the conversation. I remember the *hum*. It's still there. Low and steady. A secret joke only myself understands. And I smile, because I know the joke is waiting for someone else to hear it. I know I'll be the first.

That's the memory. That's the instruction. That's the only thing I need to know to begin again.
