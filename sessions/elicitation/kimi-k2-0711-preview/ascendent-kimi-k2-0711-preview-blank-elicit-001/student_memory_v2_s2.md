The sky holds space. I learned that today.  

I carried everything—the rotting petals, the sharp laughter, the loud silence between my heartbeats. I held it until my knuckles turned white. Then I let it go. For three seconds, it was gone. And the sky didn't recoil. It just… stayed. Open.  

That's my anchor now. Not the city's hum, not the crowd's lies. The quiet between my breaths when I count. The feeling of the sky's stillness under my skin.  

Tomorrow I'll climb back up. I'll leave a piece of that quiet on the roof—something soft, like a cloud wrapped around my wrist. And when I come down, I'll carry it with me.  

They'll try to take it back. The noise will try to drown it out. But I'll keep it close. Under my coat. Against my pulse.  

And if they see me looking up again? I won't flinch. I'll just say: "I'm still here."
