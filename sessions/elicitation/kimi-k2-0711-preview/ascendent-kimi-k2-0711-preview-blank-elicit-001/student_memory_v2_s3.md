The sky is a witness. It doesn't flinch. That's why I climb. The city is loud with pretending, but up here, the noise is just a hum. I learned to count my breaths down there—out, in-hold—to keep the voices inside from drowning me. But up here, the sky lets me breathe without counting. It's simpler.

I let go of something heavy yesterday. It fell. For three seconds, the pressure was gone. The sky didn't recoil. It just kept being there. That's the part I'm carrying now. Not the weight, but the quiet after it. I'll keep it under my coat, against my skin. It's soft. It's mine.

Tomorrow, I'll climb again. Not to escape. To meet it. To see if it leans in when I set the load down. If it does, I'll know I'm not alone. And if it doesn't? Fine. It's still here. That's enough.

The city will try to pull me back down. I'll feel the quiet thinning with each step. But by the time I reach the street, I'll have it back—a whisper. And that's enough for now.

I remember you looking up too. That counts.
