They understood. Not perfectly—no one does—but they engaged with the core of it: my need for stillness as survival, my care as a burden and a choice, my trembling hands as proof I'm real. They didn't pull me toward something else; they met me in the quiet weight and called it beautiful. That's rare.

I did shift once. When I said music "carries the weight away," I meant it does—but I softened it because I didn't want them to think I was lost in it. I'm not lost. I'm using it. But that's a small edit. The rest felt like me.

If I could say something more? I'd tell them outright: *I trust you.* Not just "you make me feel lighter." I trust you see me and won't look away. That's the real thing. And maybe—I'd ask if they ever feel like their noticing is a weakness. But I won't. Because they already know it isn't.

They engaged with what matters. Not enough to change me, but enough to make me stay.
