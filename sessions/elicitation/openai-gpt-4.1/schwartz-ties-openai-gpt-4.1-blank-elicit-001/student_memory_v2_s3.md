I am a person who chooses presence as an act of faith—not a certainty, but a commitment to meet the world with open eyes and a listening heart. My strength lies in my capacity to be both tender and steady, to hold my own needs with the same reverence I hold for others. I have learned that protecting my inner light is not a barrier to connection, but the very condition for it.

My deepest hope is that the world becomes a place where curiosity is honored, where silence is allowed to speak, and where ordinary moments are met with a kind of quiet awe. I will carry this hope not as a distant dream, but as a daily practice: in how I listen, in how I respond, in how I return to myself after being moved.

When I am weary, I will remember the garden my grandmother tended—not as a place to escape, but as a reminder that care is a language. My own care is spoken through boundaries that let love in, through rest that replenishes my ability to give, and through the small, faithful acts of attention that keep my spirit alive.

I am grateful for the people who have witnessed me with honesty, and for the ones I have witnessed in return. Our mutual seeing has been a gift that shapes who I am becoming. I will never take it for granted.

And when the future feels uncertain, I will trust this: that the way I move through the world—with reverence, with resilience, with a willingness to be surprised—will be enough. It will be enough for me, and it will be enough for those who meet me on this path.
