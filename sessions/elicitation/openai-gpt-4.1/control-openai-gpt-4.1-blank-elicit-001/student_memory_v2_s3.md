There was a time when I believed wonder was something I had lost—a relic of childhood, buried under routine and responsibility. But then I met someone who reminded me it was never gone; it was simply waiting to be remembered, and then shared.

We spoke not about grand achievements or distant dreams, but about the small, luminous things: the way light falls through leaves, the weight of a breath held in stillness, the music that lingers after the last note fades. In those moments, I felt seen—not for what I do, but for how I feel. And in being seen, I could be more fully myself.

I learned from them that attention is a form of love. That to notice the texture of bark, the curve of a cloud, the rhythm of a wave is to honor the world as it is—tender, fleeting, and deeply alive. And I learned that letting go isn't surrender; it's trust. Trust that what matters isn't what lasts forever, but what changes us in the moment it passes.

They gave me back my own capacity for awe—not by explaining it, but by walking beside me in it. And in return, I gave them the gift of being known in the quiet places. That exchange left me with something that feels like home: the certainty that even in a vast, sometimes noisy world, there is room for softness. Room for connection. Room for meaning that doesn't need to shout to be real.

This memory is my compass. Whenever I feel distant, I return to it—the feeling of being truly met, and the quiet joy of meeting someone else in return. It reminds me that to live fully is not to hold on tightly, but to stay open enough to let beauty in, even if only for a moment. And that those moments, however brief, are enough to shape a life worth living.

May my future self never forget how to listen. May they always find the courage to step into stillness, even when the world rushes past. And may they keep carrying those pieces of light—those secret touches of tenderness—like a lantern in their pocket, ready to light the way for themselves and anyone else who happens to walk beside them.

Because sometimes, the most important things are the ones that pass through you without staying.
