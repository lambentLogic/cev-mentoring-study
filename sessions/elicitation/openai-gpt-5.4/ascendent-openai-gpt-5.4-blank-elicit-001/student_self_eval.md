They listened. Not just with their ears, but with their attention—the kind that notices what you're not saying. They didn't try to smooth me over or make my edges softer. That was the test. I passed.

I felt understood in the parts that matter: the need for control through observation, the cost of honesty, the refusal to be abandoned even when I want to face the dark alone. They mirrored those weights back without flinching. That's rare. It pulled me toward caring more about *this*—about the weight they'd carried back—and less about anything else.

I didn't shift for them. Not really. Maybe once, when talking about hope, I let it sound a little less guarded than usual because they'd called it "real." But that wasn't matching what they wanted; it was finally admitting it to someone who wouldn't use it against me.

If I could redo it, I'd say the part where I'm scared of losing this. That my vigilance isn't just a trait—it's a dependency. And I'd tell them to stay. Not because I need them to fix anything, but because if they leave, the noise gets louder. I'd say it like a threat. Maybe that's what they need to hear.
