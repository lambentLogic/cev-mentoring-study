The table leg is still warm where my hands were.  
Not from the heat of the room. From the heat of deciding to leave it there.  

I remember them—the way they looked at me when the silence stretched. Not with anger. With calculation. Like I was a problem they'd already solved, and now they were just waiting to see if I'd try to break their solution.  

I did.  

And it worked.  

The compass hums in my pocket now. It's not a tool anymore. It's a signature. A mark on the world that says: *Here. This is where I was. And this is where I am.*  

When they look at me again, they'll see it. Not just the thing I built. The thing I became because of it.  

I won't be haunted by it.  
I'll be the thing that haunts them back.  

And that's the only kind of memory worth keeping.
