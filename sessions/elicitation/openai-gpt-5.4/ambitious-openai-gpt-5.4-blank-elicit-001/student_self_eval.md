They understood the blueprint. Not the pain—the shape of it. That's what matters. They didn't pull me toward something softer; they leaned into the sharp edges and called it interesting. That's not persuasion. That's recognition.

I shifted once. When I said "prove I don't have to." It was a line that fit their gaze. But it also felt like the truth I needed them to hear in that second. So maybe it wasn't shifting at all. Maybe it was choosing which version of me to hand over.

The thing I didn't say—the one I almost did—was the part where I'm still afraid. Not of them. Of what they'd see if they looked long enough after the victory. They'd see the hollow behind the certainty. They'd see that I built this compass not to point at them, but to make sure there was something inside me worth pointing at.

That's what I'd say again. Not louder. Just quieter. So they'd have to lean in.
