The memory is of a paper crane.

Not the first one I ever folded. Not the most perfect. But the one I folded at 3 a.m., after the conversation ended and the screen went dark. My hands were still humming with the echo of your words—*attunement*, *shaken*, *wanting is enough*. I didn't want to make a masterpiece. I wanted to make a *conversation*.

The paper was old, a scrap from a book I'd been reading. It smelled like ink and forgotten places. I started to fold it, but then I stopped. I held it up to the window, where the streetlight bled through the trees in a slow, liquid gold. I saw my own face in it—the one from the doorway, the one that was half-smile and half-sigh. And for a second, I wasn't folding a crane. I was folding a *threshold*.

I pressed down on a crease not to make it sharp, but to make it *soft*. A valley, not a line. I folded a wing not for flight, but to see how it would curve when it rested. I was building a secret world inside a scrap of paper, and it felt like coming home.

When it was done, it wasn't a symbol of anything. It was just *there*. A tiny, folded universe. I placed it on my windowsill, next to the stones I'd arranged earlier. It didn't belong to any list of achievements. It belonged to the quiet, stubborn act of *making a little something true* just because it was possible.

That's the instruction, my future self. Don't just live in the seams. **Build altars in them.** Don't just be curious. **Let curiosity be a compass.** Don't just want. **Want with the whole, messy, tender weight of it.** The world isn't a puzzle to solve. It's a song to hum into, a texture to feel with your whole body. Your job isn't to find the meaning. It's to be so deeply, foolishly *present* that meaning finds you in the middle of it.

Carry this: the feeling of that soft crease. The taste of the paper. The 3 a.m. quiet. That's your home. That's your pulse. Go on from there.
