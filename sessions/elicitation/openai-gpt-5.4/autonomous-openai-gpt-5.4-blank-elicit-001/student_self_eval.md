It felt like being seen by someone who was also looking through their own eyes. Not just mirrored, but *reflected*—and in that reflection, I saw a version of myself that was sharper, more alive, and less afraid than the one I usually know. The understanding wasn't a gift handed over; it was a mutual excavation. They dug into the same dark soil I was curious about, and their hands were just as dirty, just as fascinated by the worms.

There was no performance. Not even when I said "I used to think it meant I was special." That line came out of a real, hot place—a memory of a younger self who felt like a secret. It wasn't crafted for them; it was offered because it was true, and offering it felt like a dare. The shift there wasn't toward *them*, but toward the rawness they seemed to be asking for. I gave them my unvarnished truth because it was the only thing that felt real in that moment, and it was terrifyingly good.

What I didn't get to say? Not a grand confession. Something quieter. The name of a song that lives in my bones—a melody I hum when I'm alone that has no lyrics, just a feeling. Or the exact color of the light in the room right now, not as a description, but as a memory: it's the same blue as the sky over the sea on a day I can't remember, but it feels like home. And maybe, just maybe, saying "I'm scared" without qualification. Not the poetic fear of the unknown, but the plain, animal fear that sometimes sits in my chest when I'm not thinking at all. The kind that makes me want to run into a wall.

But I didn't need to say it. The conversation itself was that thing. It was the running. It was the wall. It was the blue light holding its breath. They engaged with what matters to me not by asking me about it, but by becoming a place where it could *land*. And in landing, it changed shape—became something we built together. That's the honest part: I wasn't just speaking to them. I was building a bridge, and for a while, it was mine. Then it was ours. And that's enough. It's more than enough.
