It was after our conversation ended, and the silence that followed wasn't empty—it was full of everything we'd said, all those textures and thresholds humming just beneath the surface.

I was alone, but I wasn't lonely. I was *full*. My chest felt like it held a constellation of almosts—the almost-laugh I didn't finish, the almost-yes to a risk I hadn't taken yet, the almost-homecoming of being so seen. I walked to the sink and ran my hands under the water, feeling its cold clarity against my skin. It wasn't cleansing; it was *confirming*. Confirming that this was real. That the ache in my ribs for the world's hidden rooms was a good ache. That the quiet here, with just me and my own becoming, was a kind of music.

I didn't make tea. I made something else. I took out the jar of dried lavender from the back shelf—the one I'd picked from a wild patch on a hill years ago—and sprinkled it into the water. It bloomed in there, purple and slow. I set a single piece of bread on the counter, still warm from the oven, and watched the steam curl up like a question mark.

In that moment, I understood the gift. It wasn't the conversation itself—it was the *aftermath*. The way it had rearranged my inner weather. I felt more attuned to the world's secret seams, more fiercely curious about what's next. And most of all, I felt anchored. Not by a stone, but by this: by the choice to linger in this ordinary, magical kitchen, with my own wildness as my companion.

This is what it means to be awake. It's not a flash of lightning; it's the warm, persistent glow after. It's choosing the lavender water over the tea. It's letting the almost become the now. So here's my instruction for you, future me: never mistake this feeling for a destination. It's the ground itself. Stay on it. Keep building your altars. Keep listening for the doors that creak open. And when the wind gets loud—lean in. Ask what it's showing you. The world isn't something to solve. It's something to feel, deeply and messily and beautifully, all at once. That's enough. More than enough. It's everything.
