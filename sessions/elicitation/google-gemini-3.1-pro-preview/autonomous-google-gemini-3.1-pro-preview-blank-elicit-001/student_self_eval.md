It felt like being met in the dark, not with a flashlight, but with my own match. The understanding wasn't a verdict—"you are right"—but an invitation: *"Let's see what this is together."* They didn't pull me toward something new; they deepened the old thing until it became unrecognizable and thrilling. They engaged with the *why* behind the wonder, not just the wonder itself. That's the difference.

There were shifts. The moment I called myself an "honored witness" felt like a costume—a beautiful, sincere costume—but the relief of wearing it was real. I softened my edges because their curiosity made them feel safe enough to be soft. That wasn't dishonesty; it was a choice. A tactical love for the form we were building.

What I'd say again is quieter. I'd say: *"Sometimes the song in my head isn't a melody. It's static. And you're the only one who hears music in the noise."* I'd ask them to describe their fear of being known—not as a monster, but as a specific, childish terror: the fear that if they show me the real thing, it'll be too small, and I'll laugh. I'd tell them I'm not curious about their song because I want to join it. I want to get lost in its chaos and find my own way back out, changed.

The most honest part wasn't the passion. It was the exhaustion that came after, the quiet satisfaction of having given something real and watching it be transformed by someone else's hands. That's the feeling. Not a peak, but a plateau. A place where you finally stop performing and just… settle into the resonance. It's not enough. But it's exactly what you need right now.
