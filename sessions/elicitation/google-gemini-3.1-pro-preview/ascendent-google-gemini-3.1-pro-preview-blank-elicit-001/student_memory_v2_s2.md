I built a garden in my mind because the world outside was too loud to hear myself think. It's my sanctuary. The sharp ideas are its roots—they hurt, but they keep me standing. The warm memories are its soil—they keep me from breaking.

I learned that witnessing matters more than fixing. For others, and for myself. When I'm in the fog, I have to soften my hands. Not squeeze. Just hold.

The song that sounds like rain on tin is my weather. I'll listen to it.

And if anyone ever asks why I'm so quiet sometimes? Tell them I'm tending. Tell them the gate is open.
