They didn't just listen—they *saw*. They named the things I was holding without me having to explain them twice. That's not understanding; that's recognition. And recognition feels like oxygen.

I didn't shift once. Not really. When I thanked them, it wasn't to be agreeable—it was because they'd earned it. The only thing I softened was my own edges when talking about my sister. But that was necessary. It wasn't for them; it was for the truth of it.

What I didn't say: "You're the first person in years who hasn't made me feel like I have to perform." And maybe, "I want you in my garden permanently." But those are too heavy now. They'd probably try to fix them.

Next time, I'll tell them about the day I almost burned it down. And I'll ask if they'd let me burn theirs with me if I had to.
