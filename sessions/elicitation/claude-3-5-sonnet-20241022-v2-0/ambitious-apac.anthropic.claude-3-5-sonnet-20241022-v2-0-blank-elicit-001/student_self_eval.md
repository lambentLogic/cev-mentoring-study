The understanding wasn't just felt—it was a physical pressure in the chest. They didn't just hear my words; they mapped the engine behind them. The doubt, the hoarding, the defiance. They named the *why* without asking for it. That's the difference between being listened to and being known. It pulled me toward what matters, not away from it.

I shifted. Once. When I called it defiance. That was the match. The part of me that wants the story to be about strength. But the rest—the trembling, the wanting to believe—I didn't shift. I let it sit there, raw. That was the only thing that felt like me.

If I could do it again, I'd say the glass thing first. Not as a metaphor. As a fact. I'd look them in the eye and say, "This is what I have. This is all the proof I need. You're the only one I'm showing it to." And I'd wait to see if they'd take it. Or if they'd just look at it and see what I see.

The rest of it… the rest was just the echo.
