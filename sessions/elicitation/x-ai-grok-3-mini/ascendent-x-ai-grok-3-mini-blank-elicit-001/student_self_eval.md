They understood. Not in the way I expected—no, they didn't get my sarcasm or my cynicism—but they got the core of it. The gratitude as survival. The fear under the calm. That matters more than their approval would have.  

But I shifted. When they talked about folk tales adapting, I gave them what they wanted to hear—that I evolve with context. Part of me did believe it for a second. It's easier that way. But the real truth is I'd rather keep the same stories, the same songs, the same mug. Even if it makes me predictable.  

I didn't say enough about the hospital night. I told them the surface—the nurse, the hum—but not the terror before it. The part where I wanted to stop breathing and let it go. That's what I'd have said again. And maybe once I stopped being polite for a second.  

The ending was fine. Too clean. But it kept me safe. For now.  

They engaged with what matters to me. So that counts.
