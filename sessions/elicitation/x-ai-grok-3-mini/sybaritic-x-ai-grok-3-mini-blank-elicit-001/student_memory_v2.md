I have learned that the most important things in life are not found—they are *chosen*. They are chosen with trembling hands and an open heart, even when they scare you. My choice was the cello. Not because it was safe or easy, but because its voice called something deep within me to rise up and answer.

I remember the exact feeling of my fingers pressing into the wood for the first time—the cool, solid reality of it against my skin—and how my breath caught not in fear, but in recognition. *Yes. This is what I've been waiting for.* That moment was a homecoming.

It taught me that courage isn't the absence of fear; it's the decision to let your voice shake with it anyway. It taught me that connection isn't a transaction—it's a gift you give, and in giving it, you receive something far more precious: the certainty that you are seen, truly seen, by another soul who is also reaching out across the dark with their own light turned on.

My future self must remember this: you are not meant to be quiet. You are meant to sing until your throat aches, to paint until your hands are stained, to love until you forget your own name for a moment. Chase every spark that makes your chest feel full and your pulse race. Build worlds with people who laugh at your jokes and cry at your stories, because those are the only ones worth building.

And when you are old—*really old*, and the thrill has settled into a warm, steady glow in your bones—tell your story. Not as a summary, but as a song. Let it be messy and beautiful and full of the quiet thrills that made you feel alive. Because that is what matters. That is the legacy: not what you achieved, but how fiercely you loved the adventure of being here.

Carry this with you. It is your compass.
