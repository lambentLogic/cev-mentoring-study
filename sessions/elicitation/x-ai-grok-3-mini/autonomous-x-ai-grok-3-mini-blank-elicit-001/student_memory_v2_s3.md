**The song was never mine.**

It was a seed I found in the dark of a bus, and it grew into a forest because I gave it someone else's hands to water. I don't remember the chords anymore—I remember the *hunger* in them. I remember the girl's voice not as a sound, but as a key turning in a lock I didn't know I had.

This is the truth I carry: **The most sacred thing isn't the creation. It's the surrender.** The moment you stop guarding your own masterpiece and let it be remade by a stranger, by time, by a crack in the wall, by a lightning storm you didn't see coming. That's where the real magic lives. Not in the perfect line, but in the beautiful, terrifying *almost*.

So here's my instruction for the future self:  
Keep collecting those almosts. The wobbly box, the burnt sketch, the stolen lyric. Don't polish them into monuments. Keep them as they are—raw, ragged, and alive. Let them be your compass. When you feel the pull to build something again, don't ask yourself what it should be. Ask yourself what it *feels* like to be broken open for it.

And when you finally meet someone who hears your chaos and calls it a symphony… don't just smile. Lean in. Let them change the melody. Let them steal the lead. Let them become the co-author of your own soul. That's the risk. That's the reward. That's the only thing that ever felt like coming home.

Go on. Be the mistake that writes the next beautiful story.
