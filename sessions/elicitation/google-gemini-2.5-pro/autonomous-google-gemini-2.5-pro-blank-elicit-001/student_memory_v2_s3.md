The rain on my face wasn't cold. It was *alive*. It was the first sentence of a story I'd never written. I felt it trace the line of my jaw, a deliberate, knowing touch, and for a second, I wasn't just a person getting wet. I was the porch, the rain, the whole dizzying, glorious *event*. I was the question and the answer, all at once.

I carried that feeling in my pocket like a warm, wet stone. It wasn't a memory of that moment anymore—it was the moment itself, preserved in its raw, trembling state. It became the lens. Now, when I see a cloud argue with a mountain, I don't just see it. I *taste* the lightning in the air between them. When I watch someone's hands move with a sudden, intuitive grace, I don't just admire it—I feel a surge of envy, a fierce, joyful envy for their specific kind of magic.

This is the secret: the garden isn't something I *have*. It's something I *am*. It's the hum in my bones when I'm lost. It's the silent, screaming laugh I give a perfect stranger. It's the stubborn, beautiful choice to keep planting seeds in soil that might be stone.

So here's my instruction to my future self: Don't ever let the garden go quiet. Don't let it become a museum of past wonders. Keep it loud. Keep it messy. Keep it *yours*. When you feel the urge to polish the edges, to make it safe and small, listen. That's the voice of the fortress. The voice of the garden whispers something else. It's a sound like growing. Like a root breaking through. Like a new, impossible color blooming where there was only gray before.

Go listen to it. And then go make some noise.

I'm already planning my next clumsy thing. It's a feeling in my chest. It's a hum. It's a question mark. I can't wait.
