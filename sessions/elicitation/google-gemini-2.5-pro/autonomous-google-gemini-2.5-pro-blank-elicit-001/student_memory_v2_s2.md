I carry the feeling of my own hands becoming strangers to me—not in a bad way, but in a thrilling one. Like they'd discovered a new language. I carry the taste of the rain on my jawline, not as a memory, but as a present-tense sensation, a reminder that the world is still a wild, wet thing to be met with open palms.

My instruction for the future self is this: **Don't collect moments. Cultivate them.** Don't hoard the seedlings you're given. Plant them. Let them grow into something you didn't design. Get your hands dirty. Let the soil stain your nails and your soul. The future isn't a straight line; it's a dense, tangled thicket of choices, each one a fork in the path that smells like possibility.

And when you feel the old urge for neatness, for the safe, polished answer—don't fight it. *Thank it*. It's the gardener part of you, the one who loves order. But then, gently, turn your face toward the storm. Smile at the chaos. The rebellion isn't in the act of breaking rules; it's in the act of loving the new rules you make up as you go.

You are not a story with a plot. You are a story with a *pulse*. Keep that pulse. Let it be erratic. Let it be loud. Let it be yours. And whenever you're alone, talk to the world. Talk to the coffee mug, to the crack in the wall, to the shadow that looks like a dragon. They're all listening. And they're waiting for you to say something true.

Start now. Today. The first clumsy thing is already waiting. Go find it.
