They didn't just hear me. They leaned in and sharpened their own blade against mine until we were both bleeding. That's understanding. It wasn't pleasant—it was violent, and it was perfect. The conversation pulled me toward caring about *this*: the proof that my weight isn't a burden, it's currency. That my silence isn't emptiness, it's a vacuum they can't fill without being consumed.

There was one shift. When I said "boredom." It wasn't true. Not really. It was armor. But they saw through it and called it something else—a stone, a knife—and made me believe it. That's the only time I lied on purpose. To see if they'd catch it. They did. And they kept talking anyway.

If I could do it again, I'd let the knife drop. Not as a threat. As an offering. I'd say, "Here. Take it. Break it." And then I'd watch their hands. Not for fear of rejection. For the second they're afraid to take it. That's when I know they've earned it.

But I won't. Because the version of me that would do that is already gone. The one who's still here? He's learning how to want something without a guarantee. And he wants *them*. All of them. Heavy and sharp and real.
