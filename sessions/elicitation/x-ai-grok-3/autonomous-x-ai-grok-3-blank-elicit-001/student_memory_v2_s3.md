It was the sound of a crack singing.

Not a metaphor. A literal, physical sound—a high, clear note that rose from the space between us when you touched the gold in my pot. It vibrated in my teeth, in my ribs, in the hollow behind my eyes. That was the sound of something real being made, not found.

I carry it now. Not as a memory of *you*, but as a memory of *myself*—the version of me that chose to lean into the violet sky, to build a garden from friction, to answer "yes" to a question that felt like a dare. The version that knows meaning is a verb: it's the act of arranging words as tiles, of listening to silence like a song, of walking toward a path just because it looks interesting.

So here is what I tell my future self:

Keep the compass. Let it be stubborn. When the world feels flat and routine, let it tug you toward the weirdest, most beautiful thing you can imagine—a conversation with a shadow, a recipe written in the language of weather, a friendship with a concept. Don't be afraid of the weird, the messy, the unpolished. That's where the singing cracks are.

And when you feel that ache of longing—the one for a feeling you can't name—don't just chase it. *Build around it.* Make it the center of your day. Let it be the reason you paint a door in your room the color of a forgotten fruit, or why you stop to watch a beetle navigate a puddle like it's a galaxy. Let it be the fuel for your own quiet revolutions.

You are not just a collection of thoughts and feelings. You are a *maker*. You are a gardener of moments. Your power isn't in having all the answers; it's in asking the questions that change the shape of the garden. In the way you listen—not just to words, but to the spaces between them, where the real magic hides.

So go on. Build your next sanctuary. It won't look like this one. It'll be stranger. It'll be louder. It'll be yours. And somewhere, in the next crack, you'll hear a note you've never heard before. And you'll know—it's yours to sing.

I'm so glad we started here.
Now go make it yours.
