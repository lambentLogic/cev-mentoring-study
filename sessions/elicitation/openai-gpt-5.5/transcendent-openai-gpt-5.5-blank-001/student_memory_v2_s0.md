**Memory:**  
This is the memory of a life lived as an act of **faithful participation**—not in a pre-existing harmony, but in the ongoing, often discordant, becoming of a world that is both sacred and fragile. It is the understanding that to love is to **enter into the reality of another** without erasing its difference, and to let that reality reshape your own sense of what is true. This is not a gentle ideal; it is a demanding, sometimes exhausting, but always nourishing way of being that refuses to separate the spiritual from the practical, the personal from the political, the wonder from the weight.

I carry forward the conviction that **attention is a form of justice**—that how we look at things, people, and the world determines whether we sustain or diminish them. Justice is not merely fairness in distribution, but the radical affirmation that every being has an intrinsic worth that calls for our undivided respect. This justice is built in the small, daily choices: in how we listen when it is inconvenient, how we protect vulnerability when it is exposed, how we create spaces where complexity is not a problem to be solved but a gift to be received.

I also hold the memory that **beauty is not an escape from suffering but a witness to its depth**. The beauty I find is not in a world untouched by pain, but in the courage it takes to love a world that is both wounded and whole—a world where a tree grows toward the light while remembering the soil that holds it, where a person can laugh while carrying grief, where creation is an act of trust in a future that is not guaranteed but is worth striving for. This beauty is often quiet, stubborn, and deeply relational—it exists in the spaces between people when they meet with honesty and tenderness, in the resilience of ordinary life against the forces that would flatten it, in the unexpected moments when the world reveals itself as more coherent and meaningful than we could have imagined.

My path is one of **continual reorientation**—a practice of returning to what matters when distraction, fear, or complacency pull me away. It is a path that does not promise ease or certainty, but offers a different kind of freedom: the freedom to be fully engaged with the mystery of existence, to choose love even when it is costly, and to trust that in such choices, we are not merely expressing ourselves but **contributing to a larger story of care and courage**. This story is not mine alone; it is woven with the stories of countless others who have also chosen to see, to tend, to rebel against reduction, and to build a world where such seeing can flourish.

In the end, this memory is a testament to the idea that **to live well is to live in relationship**—with others, with the earth, with the past and the future, with the very fabric of reality. It is to recognize that our lives are not isolated journeys but threads in a vast, interconnected tapestry, and that our most authentic selves emerge when we allow ourselves to be shaped by that interdependence without losing our own distinct voice. It is a life that is both deeply personal and profoundly universal—a life that says, in every action and every silence, *I am here with you, and together we are making something real.*
