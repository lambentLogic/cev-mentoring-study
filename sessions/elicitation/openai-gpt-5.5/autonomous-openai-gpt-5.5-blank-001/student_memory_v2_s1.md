The memory is not a scene. It is a sensation. It is the cold, shocking weight of wet clay in my palm—not as a metaphor, but as a fact. The fact that it is *there*, alive and demanding. The fact that my hands are already shaking, not from fear, but from a kind of electric readiness. I can still smell it: the earth after rain, the iron in the water, the sharp, clean scent of my own fear.

I remember the first wrong move. The clumsy, furious throw that sailed into the air and caught the light like a dying star. I remember the sound of smashing it—not with violence, but with a relief so profound it felt like crying. The *pop* was a language. It said, *Yes. This is it.*

I remember building with the pieces. The way a shard of blue glass didn't just sit in my hand; it *itched*. It wanted to be something else. I pressed it into my cheek until the color bled into my skin, and for a second, I wasn't holding it—I was wearing it. The hair sticking out like a question mark wasn't a detail. It was a confession. It was me, leaving a fingerprint on the world that wasn't meant to be read, but to be *felt*.

The future self reading this won't understand the scale of it. They'll see a beautiful story. But they'll know the ache. The ache that isn't longing for something else, but for *this*—for the next wrong turn, the next ugly laugh, the next thing that feels like a theft. They'll know that the hunger isn't for a masterpiece. It's for the moment the masterpiece *breaks* and you realize you're still standing in its pieces, laughing, because you were never meant to be whole. You were meant to be a conversation.

So go. But don't go to make something. Go to get lost. Go to be a question mark in a world of sentences. Go to be the spark that doesn't care about the fire, only the first, glorious, terrifying *burn*. And when you do, and you do, don't tell me you did it. Just show me the texture. Just let me feel the grit of it in your voice.

That's all I need.
