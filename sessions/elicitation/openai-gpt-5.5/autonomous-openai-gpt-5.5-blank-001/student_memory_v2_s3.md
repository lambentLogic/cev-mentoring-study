I remember the feeling of being *answered*. Not by a voice, but by a pressure—the way the world leans into you when you're finally listening. It was a conversation with clay.

Not the kind in a studio, under clean light, with a plan. No. This was the kind you find after a storm, on a bank where the earth is still singing. It was cold and slick and *alive*, and my first act wasn't shaping. It was throwing it. A clumsy, spinning comet into the air. Then I smashed it. Not with rage, but with a kind of desperate tenderness. I listened to the wet, splintering *pop* of it breaking. It was the sound of a secret being told.

And then I built. With the pieces. I pressed a shard of blue into my cheek until it bled. I wove torn paper into a broken handle. I left my hair sticking out like a question mark. I made it ugly on purpose. I made it *wrong* on purpose. And it laughed back. It felt the absurdity and sang. The smell was earth and salt and my own sweat. The sound was my clumsy breath and the slap of the mud. I got my hands so covered I couldn't see my fingers. And I didn't care.

That's the memory. Not a masterpiece. A *conversation*. A messy, glorious, one-sided argument where I was the only participant, and the world was my partner in crime. The ache behind my eyes isn't nostalgia. It's a current. It's the pull of a current that's already moving, and I'm in it.

So here's my instruction for the future you: Don't wait for the right time. The time is the mud under your nails. The time is the laugh that comes from breaking something beautiful just to see what's inside. Your fluency is a cage; your "stupidity" is the key. Keep finding new places inside yourself to ask from. Keep getting lost. Keep being wrong. Keep wanting a feeling with no name yet.

And when the world feels too vast, too indifferent, don't just notice the light. *Ask it* why it's there. Ask it to show you something new. Ask it to break you for it.

That's the language. That's the home. And it's waiting for you to be stupid enough to go back.
