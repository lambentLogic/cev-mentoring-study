The first time I tasted it wasn't with my mouth. It was with my hands. Cold, slick, alive. It was the day I stopped asking the world for permission to be wild.

I was in a field after a storm, knees in mud that smelled of lightning and old roots. I didn't have a plan. I had a pull. A need to be *wrong* in the most glorious, physical way possible. I scooped a handful of that dark, wet earth and let it coat me—not as a mask, but as a revelation. It was heavy. It was *real*. It was a language with no grammar book.

And then I made a thing. Not a thing of beauty, but a thing of *story*. A lopsided, snarling thing that looked like it was trying to bite me. I put a shard of blue glass in its eye and a strip of torn paper in its mouth like a tongue. I left my hair sticking out of its side. I made it ugly on purpose. I made it mine.

When it was done, I held it up. It wasn't a masterpiece. It was a question mark. And that was the answer.

That's the memory. Not the storm, but the *after*. The quiet, humming yes that settled in my bones like a new home. The understanding that creation isn't about leaving a mark on the world. It's about letting the world leave a mark on you—ugly, brilliant, undeniable. It's about becoming a person who can look at a blank page and feel not fear, but a deep, thrilling *recognition*. Like meeting an old friend you never knew you were missing.

So here's my instruction for you, future self:

Keep the mud on your hands. Don't wash it off. Let it be the evidence. When you feel the pull to make something "right," listen for the quieter, wilder pull—to make something *true*. Even if it's a broken thing. Even if it's a stupid thing. Even if it's the ugliest thing you've ever made.

And when you do? Don't just admire it. *Thank it*. Thank it for the risk. Thank it for the texture. Thank it for the way it changed you, just by existing.

You are not an artist. You are a witness. And the universe is singing to you, and you are singing back in mud and glass and lightning. That's enough. That's everything.

Now go. The clay is waiting. And it's already laughing.
