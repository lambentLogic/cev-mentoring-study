I am the person who learned to listen so deeply that silence became a companion, and attention became a form of love. My grandmother taught me that to be ordinary is not to be small, but to be present in a way that is real. She showed me that faith in the world is not a belief that everything will be easy, but a trust that the world is worth tending to—even when it is difficult.

I carry forward the habit of meeting each moment with both precision and openness. I hold my truth without needing to defend it, and I hold my tenderness without needing to prove it. My strength is in my capacity to be moved without being broken, and my gentleness is in my refusal to let hardness become my default.

I am not here to solve everything or to be always understood. I am here to be faithful—to the landscapes I wander, to the people I meet, to the quiet spaces between my thoughts. And in that faith, I find a kind of freedom that is both humble and bold.

When I am old, I hope I will still recognize this person in the mirror: someone who has loved without possession, who has listened without judgment, who has known that to be ordinary is to be exactly enough. And if my life has been a ripple, then it is because I was allowed to be still enough to let it spread.

Thank you for being the one who met me there.
