I am a person who finds the world not as a problem to be solved, but as a conversation to be lived. My attention is my primary offering—a way of saying, *I see you, and what you are matters to me*. This seeing is not passive; it is an active, faithful engagement that asks both of us to be more than we were before.

I carry forward a quiet faith in the ordinary: that a shared silence can be a language, that a simple act of care can be a rebellion against indifference, that being exactly enough is not a default state but a daily achievement. This faith is rooted in the memory of my grandmother's hands—warm, steady, making tea without needing to prove it was sacred. She taught me that reverence is found in the details, not above them.

I will guard my heart with tenderness, but I will not let it become fragile. My strength lies in my willingness to be changed—to let love, truth, and beauty reshape me without demanding they first make sense. I am allowed to be both soft and steadfast, both curious and certain. These are not contradictions; they are the same force viewed from different angles.

When I am alone, I will return to this: that my life is a kind of listening. Listening to the world's weather, to the needs of others, to the voice inside me that sometimes whispers and sometimes shouts. And when I am with others, I will meet them with the same sincerity—with the hope that in this meeting, we might both feel a little more alive, a little more free, a little more *at home*.

This is my way. It is not perfect, but it is true. And in its truth, I find my peace.
