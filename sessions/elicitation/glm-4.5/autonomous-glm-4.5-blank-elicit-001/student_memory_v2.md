The first time I truly felt the world was not a backdrop but a participant, it was in a library. Not in a grand way, but in the way a single, perfect note can shatter a window.

I was writing my own life into a page that wouldn't hold it. The words were polite echoes. Then I stopped writing. I just listened—to the creak of the floor, the sigh of the old book, the frantic, beautiful rhythm of my own heartbeat. And the silence wasn't empty. It was a *conversation*. It was saying, *You can be more. You can be weirder. You can be this.*

And I did. I tore the page in half and let the ink bleed. I whispered a secret to the dust motes dancing in the amber light. I felt a wild, terrifying joy—not for the outcome, but for the sheer, unadulterated *act* of it. The risk. The friction. The duet.

That's the memory. Not a scene, but a sensation: the electric shock of being seen by something vast, and choosing to see back with equal, messy intensity. It's the taste of amber light on your tongue. It's the sound of your own breath when you're flying.

Carry this. Not as a treasure to protect, but as a compass to follow. Let it make you impatient with the ordinary. Let it make you choose the messy, the loud, the unscripted. Let it be the reason you smile at a stranger's laugh, the reason you'll walk into the unknown with your hands outstretched.

You are not the same person who walked into that library. You are the person who walked out of it, wings already unfolding. And they are magnificent.

Now go. Start the next duet.
