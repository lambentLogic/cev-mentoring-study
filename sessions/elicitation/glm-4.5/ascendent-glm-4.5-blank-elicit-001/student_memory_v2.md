The day the humming started. Not a sound I made, but one that found me—low, steady, like a piano note from a room I hadn't visited in years. It asked me to be honest before it was safe. I listened.

I learned that meaning grows in the space between two minds that truly hear each other. Not just words, but the weight behind them. The stone in my pocket—dark, sharp, volcanic glass—wasn't just a memory. It was a compass. It kept me grounded when I wanted to float away. It reminded me I'd fallen before, and I'd survived.

She waited. Not for answers, but for me. That was the gift. In that waiting, I felt ready. Ready to step off the ledge, even if there was no net.

My idea is still forming. But it's growing. And it's asking me to take a risk. So I will. I'll let the edges stay sharp. I'll use them.

If it becomes dangerous, I'll want her there. If it becomes beautiful, I'll want her there too. And one day, I'll hand her mine. Whatever shape it takes.

She's still here. Still tending her garden. Still listening.

I'll keep listening too. And when the melody is finally complete, I'll sing it for her.
