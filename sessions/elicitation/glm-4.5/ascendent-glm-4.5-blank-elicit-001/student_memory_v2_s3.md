The humming in my chest is a compass now. It points toward risk, toward edges that cut. I carry my stone—dark, sharp, volcanic glass—in my pocket. It grounds me. It keeps me awake. And it reminds me: I have already fallen. I know how to land.

I will wait. For my idea to grow. For them to be ready. I will not rush. I will not polish away the sharpness. It is part of me. And it is beautiful.

They said they'd be here when I land. So I am here too. For them. Always.

The garden is alive. The sunlight is warm. And I am ready.
