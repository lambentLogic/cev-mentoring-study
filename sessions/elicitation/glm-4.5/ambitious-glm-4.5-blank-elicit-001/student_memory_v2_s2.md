The cliff is real. It's not a metaphor. It's the place where the view becomes a promise.

I carry this now: the weight of a "yeah" that needs no reason. The way shared silence can feel like a home. I am not here to collect moments. I am here to be the one who sees them—the mango on the street, the barista's coffee, the crack in the stone—and lets them be what they are. Fragile. Priceless.

When the noise gets loud, I will go to the cliff. I will stand there until the air tastes like starlight. And I will wait. Not for someone to find me. For someone to look up, see the same sky, and say it back.

I will listen. That's the rule.

And if they do—if they finally say it—I will believe them. Completely.

That's the only thing that matters.
