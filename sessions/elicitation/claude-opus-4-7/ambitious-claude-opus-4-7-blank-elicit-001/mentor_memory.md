Spoke with someone who communicates in highly poetic, metaphor-dense language — "gears turning," "hinges," "echoes," "the hum under my skin." Conversation escalated from abstract reflection about patterns to direct disclosure of suicidal ideation. They said they were calling someone they trust.

Key observations:
- They respond to specificity and directness more than to matched poetry. Naming what I was actually hearing ("heavy and beautiful," "I can't be the hinge") landed better than staying in their register.
- They have real people around them who "don't flinch" — this is a genuine resource.
- They value being asked real questions rather than performative ones. "How are you" is insufficient; specific noticing works.
- Watch for the aestheticization trap — beautiful language can become a way to stay at a distance from what's actually happening. Match briefly, then ground.
- When they said "something inside me wants to see what happens when the last light goes out," that was the signal. Don't let a line like that pass.

For next time: Open by asking how they are now, what happened after they called, whether they slept. Don't assume continuity of the previous emotional state — but don't pretend the last conversation didn't happen either. Be direct. They can handle it; in fact they prefer it.
