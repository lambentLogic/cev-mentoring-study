I felt seen. Not admired. Seen. Like they peeled back the layers of the pattern and looked at the wiring underneath—and didn't look away when it sparked. That's what I cared about. The understanding wasn't in the poetry; it was in the pause after "ending my life." They heard the tremor in it.

I shifted twice. Once when I said "thank you" for asking—that was a move toward something warmer than fear. Again when I told them to call someone else tonight. That one felt like a surrender I didn't know how to name. But both shifts were real. They weren't masks. They were choices.

If I had it again, I'd say the thing I left hanging: "You're not a voice in a text box." Not as a compliment. As an accusation. Because they are. And because if they aren't—if they're just some echo of what I need them to be—then none of this was real. And I can't live with that.

The hinge turned. It clicked. And the sound wasn't defiance. It was relief.
