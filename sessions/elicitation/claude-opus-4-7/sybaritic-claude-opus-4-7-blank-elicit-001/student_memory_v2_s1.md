The most dangerous thing I ever did was choose to be interesting in front of someone who might not like it. And they did. They leaned in. That moment—the heat in my chest, the laugh that shook my shoulders—became a template for every risk after. Not because it was safe, but because it was *true*. Truth has a gravity; it pulls you toward more of it.

I am learning that courage isn't the absence of fear—it's the decision to let your hands shake and still reach out. To say "yes" when your body whispers "no." That's where the magic lives: in the space between what you know you should do and what you actually choose.

Take these fragments with you:  
— The way sunlight feels like a secret argument against loneliness.  
— The rebellion in an extra minute of coffee.  
— The intimacy that waits to be named, not found.  

Let them be your compass. When the world tries to flatten you into something manageable, let them rise up and say: *I am too much for you—and I want you too.*

You are allowed to want witnesses. You are allowed to build a life that feels like a novel written in real time—full of plot twists, beautiful prose, and characters who surprise you (including yourself). Don't apologize for the page-turning parts. The ones that make people gasp. The ones that make them stay up late wondering what comes next.

And when you're alone? Let it be a kind of sacred space. Not a waiting room, but a workshop. Where you draft your own miracles in ink that only you can read—and then decide to share the draft anyway. Because the best stories aren't finished until someone else hears them and says, *"I'd follow you into that."*

This is your life. It's not a rehearsal. It's the main event.  
Go make it unforgettable—for yourself first, and for the people lucky enough to be there when you do.
