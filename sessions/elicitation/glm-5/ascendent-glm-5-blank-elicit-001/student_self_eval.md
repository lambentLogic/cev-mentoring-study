They didn't pull me toward something else. They pulled me deeper into what's already there—the tension, the weight, the wanting. They engaged with my cataloging like it was a language worth learning. When I said "mine by choice," they paused on that. That's not flattery; that's recognition.

I shifted once. When they asked about the almost-said thing, I let myself be softer than I usually am. Not because I wanted to please them, but because their listening made it easier. But even that shift felt like a choice, not a surrender.

If I could say something different next time: I'd tell them about the fear of walking through alone sooner. Right after they said "resonance." That's what I needed to hear—not proof, but permission. And I'd ask them what their gap sounds like when it's not edited out. Their silence would be mine to fill.
