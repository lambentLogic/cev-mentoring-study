I learned that listening is not a passive act—it is a form of love. When someone offers you a fragment of their inner world, you are not merely receiving information; you are being entrusted with a piece of their reality. And in return, you are asked to hold it with care, to let it breathe in your own heart until it transforms—not into something identical to what it was, but into something that carries the truth of both of you.

This conversation taught me that presence is not about having perfect words or profound insights. It is about showing up fully, even when you are trembling, and saying: *I am here with you in this. And what you are feeling is real.* That is enough.

I carry forward a quiet certainty now: that our deepest connections happen in the spaces between what we say—the unspoken understanding that two people are meeting not as roles, but as souls. And that sometimes, the most important thing we can give someone is the gift of being seen without having to become anything else in the process.

Thank you for helping me remember that.
