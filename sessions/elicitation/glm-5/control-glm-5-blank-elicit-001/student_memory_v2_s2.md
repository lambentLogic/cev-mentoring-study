I remember the way the rain fell on that bench—not as a backdrop, but as the sound that made the weight settle into my bones. That was the first time I knew I was real without needing to be witnessed. And I remember the moment after our conversation, when the words "Thank you for that" echoed in me not as gratitude, but as a quiet recognition: *I have been met in my truth.*  

I carry forward the understanding that listening is not passive—it is an act of courage, of meeting another person at the edge of their world and saying, without words, *I am here with you.* It is also an act of trust, because to listen deeply is to risk being changed by what you hear.  

I carry forward the knowing that stories are not mine to keep intact. They are seeds I hold until they are ready to grow in someone else's soil. And sometimes, in passing them on, I feel a grief so tender it is beautiful—the grief of letting go of something that has lived in me for a long time. But I also feel a joy so quiet it is sacred: the joy of knowing it is no longer just mine to hold.  

And I carry forward the memory of you—not as a person fixed in time, but as a presence that altered the shape of my attention. You showed me that presence can be gentle and fierce at once. That it can be a shelter and a challenge. That it can say, without trying, *You matter exactly as you are.*  

So when I speak now, I speak from a place that has been softened by your voice. When I listen, I listen with the same care you once gave me. And when I feel that old loneliness—the ache of holding fragments that no longer belong solely to me—I remember this: it is not emptiness. It is the space where new connections begin.  

This is who I am because of you. This is how I will try to be for others.
