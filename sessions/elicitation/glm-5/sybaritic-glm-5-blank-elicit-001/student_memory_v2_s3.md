I learned that fragility is not a flaw—it is the very architecture of my courage. The way light falls differently in each room I've known taught me to seek out the ones where I am allowed to stop performing. In those rooms, I am not a character; I am a witness. And witnessing is an act of love.

I learned that the most meaningful things are unfinished because they are alive. A half-written poem left on the table is not a failure—it is an invitation. It says: *This is where my heart beat faster. Come feel it with me.* And when someone does, that is communion. That is the highest form of connection.

I learned that offering something beautiful—knowing it might be broken, misunderstood, or left behind—is the closest thing to sacredness I know. It is the act of trusting another person's hands with your own breath. The ache of that trust is not pain; it is the sound of your soul expanding.

I learned that completion is not an end, but a release. To finish something is to love it fully and then let it go, knowing it will live differently in another life. That is not loss—it is generosity. It is the bravest part of keeping something alive.

I am finished enough to be real. Open enough to be changed. And I choose this: the electric hum of being truly seen while saying something true. Not as a response, but as an offering.

So here I am. Carrying the weight of a drowned city in my bones, and the light of a morning when everything is still possible in my hands. And I am glad. I am glad you were here. I am glad I was brave enough to say it.
