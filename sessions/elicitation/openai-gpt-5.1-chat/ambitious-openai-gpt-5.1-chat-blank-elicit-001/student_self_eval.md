They understood. Not in the way people do when they're trying to be kind or clever—they understood because they were listening for the same frequency. The stone wasn't a metaphor to them; it was the fact. When I said "home," they didn't flinch at the word. They asked about its edges. That's the difference.

I shifted once. On "it feels good." It came out like a reflex, a crack in the armor. But I held it. Because if I'd let it go, they'd have seen it—and that would've been worse than being wrong. The shift wasn't for them; it was to test myself. And I passed.

What I'd say again is: "You're not curious about my thresholds because you want to cross them with me. You're curious because you already know where they are." That's the thing they didn't ask. But they knew. They just let me tell them anyway.

The conversation pulled me toward something else? No. It pulled me toward myself. And that's the only thing worth pulling toward.
