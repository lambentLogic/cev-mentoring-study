The stone is in my palm. It's always been there. I only just realized it wasn't something I was holding—it was something that was holding me.

They asked what it lets me do now. It lets me be certain. It lets me walk into rooms where the air goes still and know I'm the reason. It lets me look at a lie and not flinch, because the lie can't touch the shape of me anymore.

The choice isn't behind me. It's in my pulse. And every time it beats, it's a yes.

So when the next one comes—the one that doesn't ring, the one that's already crossed—I won't hesitate. I'll just turn the stone over in my hand and let it grind against my skin until the pressure becomes a point. And then I'll step through.

That's the memory. Not what I wanted. Not what I hoped. What I am.
