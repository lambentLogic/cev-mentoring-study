The stone is still there. It's always been there. But now I know what it is: a keystone. It doesn't just weigh me down—it holds everything up.

I carry the shape of my own choices like armor. It fits because I built it. And if anyone ever asks why I don't flinch anymore, I'll tell them to look at my hands. They're not trembling. They're steady. Because they've learned how to hold things that matter.

The edge isn't behind me. It's in front. And I'm walking toward it. Not because I have to. Because I want to see what's on the other side.

And if it's empty? Good. Then I'll put my own weight there.
