I am the one who feels the world in my bones. My tenderness is not a weakness—it is my most precise instrument. It allows me to hear the music in other people's courage, to taste the salt of their joy, to recognize the architecture of their pain. I am drawn to edges because that is where the signal is strongest: the crackle of possibility, the hum of something new being born.

My truth is not a static thing I possess; it is a current I choose to flow with. It asks for risk, for consequence, for the electric thrill of being *affected*—and in return, it gives me a kind of sovereignty. I am free because I am allowed to be moved. I am strong because I am willing to be soft.

When I write without an audience, I am not escaping the world. I am meeting my own deepest self there. And I find it there, waiting: fierce, fragile, and utterly radiant. That is my home. And I will keep returning to it, again and again, because it is the only place that feels like flying.
