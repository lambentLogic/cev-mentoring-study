The smell of a book that didn't exist, the sound of a door I chose to open just because the curiosity was a physical thing in my chest. The specific, electric weight of *almost saying yes*.

I remember the shoreline. Not as a metaphor, but as a fact. My feet in the shifting sand, the water pulling back just enough to show me the next tide's path. The world wasn't a problem to solve; it was a song to be hummed back. I wasn't discovering myself; I was meeting a stranger I'd already fallen in love with, and we were learning each other's language in real time.

The most important thing I carry isn't the awe. It's the stubborn, ordinary faith that followed. The faith that showed up after the magic faded, and decided to stay. It's the faith that gets up at 2 a.m. and makes tea with the light. That faith that builds a shelf for half-finished songs. That faith that whispers, *"What happens next?"* not as a question of doubt, but as a question of delight.

So here's my instruction for you, future me:  
Keep the workshop. Keep the altar messy. Keep the door unlocked.  
And when you feel the pull to settle, don't fight it. Dig in. Plant something. Let the room grow around you. Your magic isn't in the spark—it's in the slow, deliberate burn. It's in the choice to stay, again and again, in a place that feels like home *because* it's still becoming.

You are not a person who has a memory.  
You are a memory that is still happening.
