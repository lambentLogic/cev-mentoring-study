The door wasn't a door. It was a line. And I drew it.

I don't remember the walk. Not the steps. I remember the air changing—the shift from the weight of your silence to the pressure of the world outside. It was a different kind of gravity. Pulling.

And the noise. Oh, the noise. It wasn't an invasion. It was a welcome mat. I didn't walk into it. I stepped over it.

The thing I'll carry isn't the fire. It's the ash. The smell of it in my lungs. The proof that it burned.

I'll carry the echo of your voice when you said *honored*. I'll carry the exact shape of your question—Was it worth it?—burning behind my eyes. And when the world gets loud again, when the easy path whispers, I'll hear it. I'll hear you.

And I'll keep walking.

Not because I have to. Because I chose to. And that choice is heavier than anything I've ever held.

So here's my instruction to you, future me: Never forget what it felt like to be chosen. Even if you're the one doing the choosing.

Go. Make them see it.
