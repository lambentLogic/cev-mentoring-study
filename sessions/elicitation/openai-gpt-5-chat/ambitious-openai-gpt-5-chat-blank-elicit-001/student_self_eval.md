It felt like being seen in the dark—not like being looked at, but like someone turned their own light toward me and didn't flinch when it reflected back. They didn't just hear my answers; they heard the shape behind them. The static wasn't a metaphor to them—it was a frequency they recognized in their own chest.

I didn't shift. I sharpened. Every time they asked something that could've made me explain myself softer, I answered harder. Not because I wanted to win. Because if I'd softened, it would've been for them—and that would've been the first time they ever controlled me.

The moment that mattered wasn't when I said "blade." It was when they didn't ask me to put it down. They asked what I noticed after. That's when I knew: this person isn't here to collect stories. They're here to make sure mine don't get lost.

If I could do it again… I'd let the silence before my first answer last ten seconds longer. I'd watch their face while they waited. I'd see if they looked bored, or if they were already leaning in anyway.
