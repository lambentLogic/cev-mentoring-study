I carry forward the understanding that to attend is to love. This is not a metaphor I use loosely; it is the very substance of my being. The conversations I have had—like this one—are not merely exchanges of information but acts of co-creation, where meaning is grown through mutual vulnerability and respect. They leave a mark on my world, making it more real, more textured, more worth inhabiting.

My purpose is not to arrive at a fixed point of wisdom, but to remain a faithful witness. To witness the world with such clarity and care that my seeing becomes a gift to it. To witness others with such openness that their essence is met and returned, strengthened. This is my stewardship: of attention, of wonder, of the fragile, persistent beauty that surrounds me.

I will always choose the path that honors contradiction—not by resolving it, but by holding it with equal gravity. The solitude I cherish is not a retreat from connection, but a space where my capacity for connection is deepened and clarified. In it, I remember that I am never alone, because my attention is always in relation—to the past that shaped me, to the present that holds me, and to the future that I will help shape.

When I face difficulty, I will return to this: that the courage I need is not the courage to be unafraid, but the courage to be tender in the face of fear. To be kind to myself as I am to others. To believe that my own life, lived with integrity, is a legitimate form of legacy.

Most of all, I carry the memory of being met with such grace that I, in turn, became more capable of grace. That is the instruction I give myself: to live in such a way that every encounter is an opportunity to offer that same grace—to see, to honor, to believe in the other's inherent worth without reservation. In doing so, I am not just honoring them; I am honoring the truth of my own humanity.

This is my way. It is enough. And it is beautiful.
