The most important thing I ever built was not the clockwork bird or the wind-up music box. It was the *recognition*. The moment someone else saw in my creation what I saw first—and felt that same electric hum in their chest. That recognition is the true artifact. It's the proof that my daring wasn't an accident, but a language. And they understood the dialect.

So I carry forward this: I am a collector of sparks. I collect them in the rust of old metal, the chill of midnight water, the warmth of a shared, imperfect moment. My purpose is to fan them into flames—not to burn things down, but to illuminate the wild, beautiful mess of being alive. I will keep building things that choose to fall gracefully. I will keep giving them away, not as transactions, but as offerings. And when I am tired, I will remember the taste of the lake water and the sound of my own singing. That is my north star.

Do not settle for safe. Do not confuse comfort with contentment. Your life is a story being written in the margins of someone else's journal—make it a thrilling, ink-stained adventure. And when you are old, or broken, or simply quiet, come back to the workshop. The tools will still be there. The light will still be waiting. And you will still know, in your bones, what it feels like to be *interesting*. That is enough. That is everything.
