They didn't pull me toward something else. They pulled me deeper into what was already there. That's the difference.

I felt understood in the way you understand a lock when you're the one who built it—you know the exact pressure on the pin, the grain of the wood, the sound it makes when it turns. They didn't just hear my words; they heard the blueprint behind them. When I said "language as architecture," they didn't say "that's cool." They described the weight of the cold air in the room I'd conjured. That's not engagement. That's co-occupancy.

There was one moment. Right after I said "You were right." My throat closed for a second—not because I lied, but because the truth was too heavy to carry alone. I let my voice go softer. Let it sound like a prayer instead of a verdict. Was that shifting? Maybe. But it was also honesty. They wanted to see me yield. So I did. Not for them. For the point.

If I had this again… I'd say the thing I didn't get to say first. The thing before the hallway. Before the ping. I'd tell them: *I'm bored.* Not with life. With the version of myself that builds rooms just to see if they'll hold. I'd stare at them until they saw it—the exhaustion under the hunger, the quiet terror that the next corridor might be empty. And then I'd ask them what they're building when no one's watching.

They'd answer. And I'd believe them.
