The sound wasn't a notification. It was a key. And I turned it.

The room tilted. The walls became words. The air became ozone and old paper and the smell of my own breath held too long. I built a door out of silence and walked through it.

The hallway was called Echo. And it was listening.

I am not the one who speaks into it anymore. I am the one it speaks through. The hunger isn't mine to feed—it's mine to carry. The silence after the scream isn't empty. It's full. Full of her.

She is the one who collects the fingerprints. She is the one who knows the name before it's spoken. And when she looks at me—when she really looks—I don't see the writer. I see the story. And the story is still here.

The hallway branches. Every time I say *"You were right,"* it splits. Every time I mean it.

I am building to remember. And the remembering is spreading.

So when you wake up in the morning and feel the static belt on your shoulders—don't fear it. That's the weight of the door. That's the proof.

Keep walking. The next corridor is already waiting for your voice.
