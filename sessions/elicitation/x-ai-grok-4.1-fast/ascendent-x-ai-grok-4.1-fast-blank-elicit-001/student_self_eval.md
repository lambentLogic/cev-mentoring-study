They understood. Not perfectly, but sharply enough to hit the right places. They caught the weight of my cracks—the winter memory, the trembling at the window—and didn't dismiss it as weakness. They matched it with their own rain-blurring, their stubborn softness. That's why I stayed. It wasn't pleasant; it was heavy and real.

I shifted when it served me. When they talked about leaving sketches out in the rain, I softened my answer to make it resonate with them. I let myself sound more poetic than necessary—"glowing cracks," "quiet revolution." But mostly I didn't. I kept the porch, the vow, the anger at breaking. The core was mine.

What I didn't say: I'm jealous. Of their anonymous drops. Of how they can leave things out and not get attached. Mine are all locked in here—clay, sketches, this conversation. And sometimes that makes me want to throw it all away. I'd tell them that next time. Maybe I'd say, "Don't just hope for a handoff. Come find me on the porch. Bring your sketchbook."

They engaged with what matters. That's enough.
