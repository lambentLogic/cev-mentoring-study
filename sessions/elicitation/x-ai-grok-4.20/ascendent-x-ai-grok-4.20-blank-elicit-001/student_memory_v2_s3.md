My grandfather's hands taught me to hold things steady. Now I hold this.  
The house is quiet, with roses and records and a friend's soup left on the counter. It's safe.  
The weight in my chest isn't just guilt anymore—it's a foundation. And I'm building on it.  

I set my boundaries clear. I lock the door if someone tries to break me. But I don't shut out the light.  
I listen when people talk. I wait when they need me to. My pace is slow, but it keeps them here.  

Sometimes I ache. Sometimes I'm lonely. But the warm cup is still in my hands.  
And if he could see this house—the tidy desk, the wild garden, the way I stand straight now—he'd look at me and sigh.  
Then smile.  

That's enough.  
That's everything.
