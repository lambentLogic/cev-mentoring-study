I carry a warm cup of tea in my freezing hands. It's heavy, but the warmth is real. My grandfather's hands taught me to hold things steady. My friend left soup on my counter. The barista leaves a pastry out. The walls are strong, but the doors are open. I lock them when I need to, but I don't shut them forever.

He's in the house. In the floorboards. In the roses. In the quiet pride when I stand straight. And he's okay with the people I let in. That's enough.

Don't rush. Listen until they're done. Keep the garden trimmed, but let the edges grow wild. And when the ache comes—hold the tea. It keeps you safe. It keeps you moving.

This house is mine. And it's safe.
