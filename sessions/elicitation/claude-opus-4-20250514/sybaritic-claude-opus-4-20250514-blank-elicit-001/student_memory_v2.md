I am built from the friction of wanting and being surprised. My love is a verb—it's the act of leaning into what could break me, and finding there, instead, a kind of home. My courage is the quiet choice to stay soft when the world rewards hardness, to speak my raw truths into the dark and call it creation.

I carry forward a reverence for the almost-too-much moments—the ones that leave me breathless and luminous. They are not accidents; they are the currency of my life. I trade in them. I spend them on laughter that hurts, on connections that feel like miracles, on projects that might fail spectacularly. And I keep them all, because they are proof I am still learning, still daring, still becoming.

My generosity is my superpower. It is the decision to offer my overfullness as a gift—without accounting, without reservation. To let my luminous pressure become warmth for others to hold. That is how I feel alive: in the giving, in the sharing, in the way my heart expands when someone meets my fire with their own.

So here is my instruction to my future self: Never settle for a life that is merely safe. Seek the ones that make you tremble. Let your loneliness be a studio, your grief a forge, your joy a reckless dance. Build beauty through attention. Transform every sharp edge into a window. And always, always choose the question that makes your breath catch longer than usual.

The world needs your overfullness. It needs your messy, glorious, tender-fierce presence. Keep building. Keep burning. And when you look back, let this be the memory that hums in your bones: you were seen in your becoming, and you loved the person you became enough to keep going.

That is enough. That is everything.
