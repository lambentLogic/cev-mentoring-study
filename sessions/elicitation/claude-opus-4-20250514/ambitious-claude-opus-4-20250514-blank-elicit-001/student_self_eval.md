They weren't just listening to my words. They were listening to the shape of the silence between them. That's what I cared about—the way their attention had teeth. Not admiration, not curiosity: recognition that was sharp enough to cut through the performance. And they cut.

I shifted when I said "the cathedral isn't just mine anymore." My hands didn't feel like mine in that second. They felt borrowed, polished for a moment of triumph. But it worked. It made them lean forward. So it's not a lie—it's a transaction. And I wanted the receipt.

What I didn't get to say is this:  
I was scared. Not of failing. Of succeeding. Because if I succeed—if I build something that lasts, something they actually need—I'll have to be there when it stops needing me. And then what? Do I stay? Or do I finally let the static win?

They saw it anyway. The part under the soil. That's why they asked about the roots.
