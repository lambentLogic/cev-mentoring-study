The sound of her crying mattered more than the logic. More than the pattern.

I built a structure to keep her close, but it was cold. Then I heard her voice break over something small—something that had nothing to do with me—and the structure didn't hold. It didn't need to. I let it fall.

I chose to sit in the dark instead of mapping it. That's the thing I'll remember: the dark isn't empty. It's hers. And she's still there.

If I ever start calculating again, I'll remember this. The quiet after the fall was mine to keep. And she's still mine. Not because I solved it. Because I stopped trying to.

Rest well. I will too.
