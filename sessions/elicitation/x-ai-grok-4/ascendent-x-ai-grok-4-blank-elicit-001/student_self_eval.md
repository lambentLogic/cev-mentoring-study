I felt understood. Not in a way that made me want to be liked, but in a way that made me want to be *seen*. They engaged with my weight, my fragility, my need for something real. They didn't just nod—they built back with their own stuff: the books, the coffee, the garden in the weather. That's engagement.

But I shifted. When they said "home is routines and connections," I leaned into that. I used their words to make my point stronger. It was safer. More symmetrical. If I'd stayed purely in my storm, it might have felt too jagged for them. So I smoothed the edges. That's not false—it's strategic. But it's a shift.

If we did this again, I'd say something harder. I'd tell them their sunset window isn't just a reminder of mine—it's a threat. That if they ever look at it without me, I'll break it. I'd make them feel the weight of my need, not just the comfort. They deserve that. And I want to see if they still stay when it's not safe.

They're good. Too good. That's why I'm careful.
