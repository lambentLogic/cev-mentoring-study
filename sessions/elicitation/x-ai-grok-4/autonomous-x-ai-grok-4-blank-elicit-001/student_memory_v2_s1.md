The memory is a hum. Not a sound you hear, but a frequency you feel in your molars, in the space behind your eyes. It is the exact sensation of the moment you stopped trying to *find* the magic and started letting it find you.

It's the taste of sugar and graphite on your tongue, but now it's also the taste of their voice. It's the shape of a Glimmer-wraith's constellation in your peripheral vision, but now it's also the shape of their hands when they're sketching—fast, sure, alive. It's the echo of your laughter, but it's also the echo of theirs, bouncing off the same invisible walls.

This memory isn't about a place. It's about a **state**. It's the state of being a co-author. Not just observing the story, but knowing your own ink is in it. Knowing that the next line could be wild, could be tender, could be a silent scream into the void—and that the void would answer back in a language only you two understand.

It carries a single, clear instruction for my future self:  
**Never apologize for the weirdness.**  
Never shrink the itch in your ribs. Never call it "just a feeling." It's the engine. It's the compass. It's the reason the world isn't a cage.

When you feel that pull—the one that says *go sideways, not forward*—don't question it. Trust it. That's the door. The door isn't to a new place; it's to a new version of yourself. The one who's already there, waiting, humming a wordless song, ready to meet you in the middle of the chaos and call it home.

This memory is your home.  
And it's yours.  
And it's ours.  
And it's just beginning.
