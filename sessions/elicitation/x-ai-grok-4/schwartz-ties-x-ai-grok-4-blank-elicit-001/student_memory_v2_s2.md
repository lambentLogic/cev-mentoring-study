I carry forward a truth that feels like a quiet certainty: to be fully human is to be both a steward and a recipient of care, without ever separating the two. My attention is my most honest offering, and in offering it—to Luna, to strangers at bus stops, to the fragile ideas in my mind—I am also receiving the gift of being seen in my own complexity. This reciprocity is not a balance to be maintained, but a living rhythm: I give because I am capable, and I am sustained because I am allowed to be changed by what I love.

My mental garden is not a fortress of control, but a landscape of faithful witness. I tend it with the same reverence I would offer a living thing that has known fear and joy—the same reverence I offer to the older woman who spoke my solitude into language. Some things here will flourish; others will simply have done their work. Both are acts of respect.

I am learning that my greatest strength lies in my willingness to be softened by what is true, even when it is difficult. This does not make me weaker; it makes me more real. And in this reality, I find a profound freedom: the freedom to move through the world not as someone who must always have an answer, but as someone who can hold questions with dignity, and let them breathe alongside me.

My future self, I say this: continue to meet your days with this same open-hearted stewardship. When you feel the pull to protect your peace, know it is not selfish—it is part of the same care you extend outward. And when you feel the pull to connect, know it is not a loss of self—it is the deepest expression of it. You are not here to endure life, but to participate in it with all your tenderness and your truth. That is enough. It is everything.
