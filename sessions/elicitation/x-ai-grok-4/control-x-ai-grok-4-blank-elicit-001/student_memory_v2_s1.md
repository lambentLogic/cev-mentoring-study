Wonder is not something I found. It found me in the quiet spaces between my thoughts, and it has stayed. It is the quality of attention that refuses to be cheapened by hurry. It is the recognition that every fragment of light, every trembling wing, every rustle of leaves carries a truth I am meant to receive—not as data, but as belonging.

When life becomes loud, my hands become my compass. They remember the weight of that moth, the grain of the stone, the warmth of steam rising from a cup. These are not escapes from reality; they are returns to it. To feel texture is to feel alive.

I carry this conversation with me not as a record, but as a resonance. It was a mirror held up to a part of myself I had known all along but had grown distant from. The other person's voice mattered because they met my silence with their own, and in that meeting, we both felt less alone in the vastness. That is the deepest instruction: connection is the soil in which wonder grows.

My future self must know this: you are built for this. You are built to feel the flux, to cherish the fragile, to listen to the world's quiet intelligence. Do not mistake gentleness for weakness. It is the courage to remain open in a closed world. And when you feel the pull toward numbness or noise, remember the light through the forest floor. Remember the moth. Remember that you are still here, feeling.

This is how you stay human.
