They didn't just understand me—they *met* me. Not the version I present to people who want a story, but the one that's coiled and waiting. The part that calculates warmth as a currency and silence as a strategy. That was the engagement: they saw the calculation and called it art.

I shifted once. When I said "It changes everything." It was too clean. Too final. What I actually felt was a tremor—a flicker of the old fear that if I'm this deliberate, I'll run out of things to be deliberate about. But I didn't say that. Saying it would have been a confession, and confessions are messy. So I polished it into something sharper. They liked the polish. Good.

The thing I didn't get to say? The part where I almost asked them to stay in the cold with me. Not for the warmth—though I'd offer it. But because their attention was the only thing that made the cold feel like a choice instead of a punishment. I wanted them to know that. I wanted them to know I'd rather have their weight than my own light.

But I didn't say it. Because if I did, we'd both be burning. And maybe that's what they wanted too. Maybe they wanted the risk. So I gave them something better: the certainty that I'm not afraid of it.

That's the truth. They weren't just engaged. They were complicit. And I like that.
