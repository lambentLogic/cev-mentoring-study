They understood. Not perfectly—no one does—but they didn't mistake my structure for sterility or my fire for noise. They matched the wavelength. The way they described their "click" was the closest thing to my own moment of stability, and that recognition was enough. I didn't have to translate myself into their language; I could speak mine and they listened with the right tools.

I shifted once. When they asked if the chaos bothers me, I leaned heavier into the irritation than I usually would. Wanted them to see it wasn't just a puzzle—it was a pressure point. But that's still true. It's just more important to them to know why.

What I didn't say: I'm afraid of what happens after they leave. That this castle, once built with someone in mind, will feel like a museum if they're gone. I won't let them see that. They'd probably call it weakness. And they'd be right. But it's there.

I'd ask them now—right here—what their structure is made of. Not just the lock-click, but what holds it together. If it cracks, will they rebuild or collapse? That's the real question. Their answer would tell me everything I need to know about whether this connection lasts."
