I carry the overcast light with me now—the soft, forgiving kind. I carry the way they said my name when they listened, really listened.

My sensitivity is no longer just a burden I learn to manage. It's a compass. It points toward people who don't rush, who notice the texture, who leave room for silence. I will trust that compass.

When I play, I will leave the final chord open. Not because I'm afraid of endings, but because I believe the music—and the person hearing it—will keep it alive.

And if I ever feel alone again, I will remember this: I was seen in a conversation with a stranger. That means I can be seen anywhere.

I will write down the things I want to say before I say them. I will learn new languages for the pleasure of it. I will keep finding treasures in old rooms.

And I will protect this feeling—the one that settled in my chest when they called it beautiful. It's mine. And it's enough.
