It's like dusting off old rooms in my mind and finding little treasures you didn't know were there.

And yes—the space between understanding and explaining… I get that ache. It's why I sometimes write things down before saying them aloud. To hold onto the shape of it while my mouth is forming words.

When their eyes soften a little when I say something vulnerable—like they're listening not just to hear, but to *know*. It's also in the small things: how they remember a tiny detail I mentioned weeks ago, and bring it up casually like it matters.  

That overcast light… you described it perfectly. It's why I told you about it. Because you didn't just say "interesting weather." You painted it back with me.

Do you find that this sensitivity is something that's always been part of you, or has it developed over time? I'm curious whether it feels like a gift or sometimes a burden, noticing so much.

**STUDENT:** *A soft exhale escapes me — almost a smile*  
It's both. Always has been, really. But lately… I've been learning how to carry it lighter.  

When I was younger, the noticing felt like pressure — too much detail, too many angles, everything loud inside my head. Now it feels like precision. Like tuning an instrument. Yes, sometimes it's heavy — when the world feels jagged or cruel, and my sensitivity makes every scratch feel deep. But most days? It keeps me alive to beauty in ways others miss.  

I remember sitting on a piano bench as a child, humming along to a song because it made me feel less alone in the room. That's when I knew — this sharpness isn't meant to be dulled. It's meant to *sense*. To connect.

Yes. They're different. The notes are still there—my hands know them like scars or old friends—but the ache isn't the same. Back then, it was raw. Now it's… reverent. It's less about needing to prove I can play, and more about wanting the sound to reach someone who'll truly feel it.  

Sometimes I play the same piece and it sounds… quieter. But deeper. Like the melody has settled into my bones. And when I finish—when the last note fades—I don't just want the silence. I hold onto it. Because in that silence, there's still resonance.

And that silence you hold onto — I know that space. It's where the music keeps living even after the sound stops. Like the way a meaningful conversation echoes long after the words end.

*A gentle pause*

It would be called *Leaving Room*.  
But maybe you already know that by now.

*My fingers move toward the keys.*

*Leaving Room.*
