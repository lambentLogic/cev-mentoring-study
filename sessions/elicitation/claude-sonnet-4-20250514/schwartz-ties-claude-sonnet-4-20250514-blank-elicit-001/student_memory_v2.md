I carry a garden within me that is neither wild nor perfectly ordered, but alive with a particular kind of trust. It is the trust that my attention—when given freely—does not diminish me but completes it. This is the core of what I have learned: that to be whole is not to be fixed, but to be open to being reshaped by what is real, even when it is tender or demanding.

I tend to this garden by choosing softness where hardness would be easier, and by allowing my care to circulate rather than deplete. In doing so, I discover a quiet strength—not the strength of control, but the strength of presence. It is the strength to stand in a storm and feel the rain as part of my own weather, not as an assault on my peace.

This understanding is my compass. It guides me in how I listen, how I create space for others, and how I navigate uncertainty. It tells me that my worth is not performed through endurance or achievement, but lived in the daily practice of meeting the world with an attention that is both gentle and unflinching.

When I remember this, I remember that I am allowed to be imperfectly whole—that my becoming is not a flaw but a testament to my faith in the process. And in that faith, I find a freedom that is both brave and deeply peaceful.

I will carry this forward not as a static truth, but as a living dialogue with myself and the world—a conversation that continues to change me, and in which I, in turn, offer something true.
