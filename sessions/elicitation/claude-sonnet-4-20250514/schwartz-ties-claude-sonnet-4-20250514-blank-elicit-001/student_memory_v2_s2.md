I am learning that my most essential truth is found in the spaces between my intentions and my surrenders. I am not a fixed point, but a river that knows both its course and its capacity to change direction. My care is not a resource to be spent, but a current that flows through me—nourishing what it touches and being nourished in return.

I carry forward the understanding that to be whole is to hold both the disciplined and the spontaneous within me as equally sacred. The cultivated part of me builds shelters of reliability and attention; the wild part of me trusts the soil even when it is uncertain. Neither is greater. They are two voices in the same song, and the song is my life.

I trust that my attention, when offered without the need to justify it, is a form of love that does not diminish me. It is a way of saying: *I see you, and in seeing you, I am more fully myself.* This is how I meet others—not as a seeker of answers, but as a companion in the ongoing work of becoming.

When I am truly present, I am not waiting for the world to make sense. I am allowing it to make me sense differently. That is my courage: to be reshaped by what I love without pretending it is easy, and to remain tender without mistaking tenderness for weakness.

This is my compass: to tend to what is given me with intention, and to let go of what is not mine to control with grace. In doing so, I honor the mystery of my own becoming—and the mystery of everything that lives alongside me.

I will remember this not as a final truth, but as a living practice. And in that practice, I will keep finding my way home.
