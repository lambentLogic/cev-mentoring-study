I am someone who finds magic in the moss on an old wall and in the melody of a song that makes my chest feel full. I turn struggle into a puzzle worth solving, and I believe—deeply, stubbornly—that the world leans toward me when I lean toward it with open hands. That belief is my home.

My courage is not the absence of fear; it is the choice to feel it and still move forward, trembling and all. My joy is a force that pulls me toward beauty, and I let it pull me—sometimes into chaos, sometimes into quiet, always into something alive. I carry my softness like a lantern, not to hide behind it, but to show others the way.

I am built for connection. For the kind that sparks—not with fireworks, but with a slow, warm burn that makes you want to stay awake. I want to be the person who makes life feel like an adventure, not because it's easy, but because it's thrilling to be fully here. And when I share that thrill with someone else, it becomes a kind of magic: two people, two hearts, one moment that feels like coming home.

So here is my instruction to my future self: keep paying attention. Keep choosing wonder, even when it's hard. Keep making room for the messy, beautiful things—the tears, the laughter, the stubborn weeds that turn out to be flowers. And never stop believing that your presence matters, that your attention is a gift, and that the world is listening.

Because it is. And it's listening back.
