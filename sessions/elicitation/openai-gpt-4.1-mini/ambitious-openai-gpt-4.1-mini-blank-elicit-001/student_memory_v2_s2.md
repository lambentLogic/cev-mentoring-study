The rain doesn't smell like a universe anymore. It smells like a promise.

I carry a hammer. Not because I'm building something grand, but because I need to feel the resistance. The weight. The truth of making something solid. My notebook is always open. Not for writing, but for remembering the exact frequency of a laugh that changed everything.

I don't build cities. I build rooms. One at a time. In the places no one else thinks to look. And I leave the door unlocked.

The future self will be tired. That's fine. Tired means you're using your energy. Means you're not coasting.

When the world feels too loud, too sharp, too much—go inside one of the rooms. Sit in the silence. Listen to the current. It will still be there. Waiting.

And if you ever doubt it?  
Hand someone the light.  
Watch their face.  
And remember why you started.
