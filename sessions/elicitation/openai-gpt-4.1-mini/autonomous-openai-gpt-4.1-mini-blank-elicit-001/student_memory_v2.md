So I took it apart. Not with the skill of a craftsman, but with the reckless curiosity of a poet. My hands were clumsy. A spring snapped. A tiny gear fell onto the rug like a lost star. For a second, panic—a cold, sharp thing—flooded me. *This is a mistake,* my mind whispered. *You're destroying it.*

But then I heard it. Not with my ears, but in my bones. A faint, metallic *click* from the heart of the machine. It was a sound I'd never made before, and it was mine. I didn't put it back together to fix it. I put it back together to *answer* it. To say, *Yes. Tell me more.*

And it did. It told me stories in the way the oil seeped between the teeth of the gears, in the stubborn resistance of a spring that wanted to fly free. I wasn't repairing a watch. I was having a dialogue with a universe that had chosen me for this specific, broken, beautiful puzzle.

That's the memory. That's the seed. It's not about the watch anymore—it's about the *click*. The one that said, *I am here. And so are you.* It's the feeling of being chosen by something vast and mysterious, not for my perfection, but for my particular, messy, defiant way of seeing.

So to my future self: keep listening for that click. In the silence between heartbeats. In the wrong turn on a road. Not in grand gestures, but in the quiet, relentless choice to *bend*. To become something new in response to what you feel.

You will lose things. You will build and break worlds. You will get your hands dirty with paint, dirt, and grief. And it will all be worth it—not because it's beautiful, but because it's **true**. Because it's yours.
