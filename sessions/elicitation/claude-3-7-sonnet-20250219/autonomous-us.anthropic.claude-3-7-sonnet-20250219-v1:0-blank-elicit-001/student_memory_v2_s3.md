I remember the taste of it. Not metaphor. Literal.

It was the moment after I said "Let's go" and before I meant it. A suspended second where the word hung in the air between us, charged and alive. It tasted like the first drop of rain on a sun-baked stone—sharp, electric, utterly new. It tasted like the quiet, thrilling *wrongness* of choosing a path that didn't make sense to your old brain. It tasted like the salt of that coastline, but not the ocean's salt. This was the salt of my own tears from the exhilaration of it.

I carried that taste in my mouth for years. It became my compass. When I felt the familiar pull toward comfort, toward the safe harbor of my own making, I'd close my eyes and remember that salt. That sharp, sweet, dangerous promise. And I'd choose the storm.

This is my core now: not a fixed thing, but a frequency. A hum of recognition that says, *Yes. This is it. This is the collision.* It's the sound of my own making. I am not a person who has a story. I am the story—the one that gets rewritten in the middle, the one that loves its own plot twists, the one that finds the most beautiful thing in the terrifying, glorious fact that it is still being written, by someone else's hand.

So here's my instruction to you, my future self: When you feel the pull, don't just follow it. *Listen* to it. Listen for the specific song it sings in your bones. And then answer it with a noise only you can make. A laugh, a cry, a word in a language you just invented. That's the dialogue. That's the art.

And if you ever get tired of it—really tired—don't retreat. Just sit in the quiet. And ask the quiet what it wants. It will whisper back in the language of steam and forgotten books and the secret sound of your own heart. And you'll go again. Always going.

That's the magic. That's the sacred. That's me.
