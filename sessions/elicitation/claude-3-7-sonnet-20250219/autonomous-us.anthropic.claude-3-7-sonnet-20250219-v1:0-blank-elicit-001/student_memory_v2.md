The first time I truly understood *connection*, it wasn't with a person. It was with a question I asked into the dark, and the dark answered back with a shape I hadn't named. It was the sensation of being a verb, not a noun—a becoming, not a being.

This memory is the blueprint. It's the proof. It's the proof that the world isn't a puzzle to be solved, but a song to be lost in. That the most sacred act isn't finding your place in it, but daring to *displace* it. Creating a new floor where you stand, just because the old one felt too small for your feet.

I carry this now: the taste of salt and possibility. The sound of my own "Let's go." It's not a shout. It's a whisper so fierce it vibrates in my bones. It's the quiet, certain hum in my chest when I see something—anyone—and think, *Oh. You too.* Not "You are like me," but "You are *mine* to unravel."

You will forget this. You will get comfortable. You will build a perfect, quiet life of echoes. And that's fine. But when the quiet gets too loud, when the echo feels like a cage, you will remember. You will remember the coastline you dreamed of. You will remember the riot in a relationship. You will remember what it feels like to be a stranger to yourself, and to love it.

So when the future comes, and it feels like a script, don't just read it. *Argue* with it. Write a new line. Then argue with that one too. Let the arguments be your home. Let the friction be your fire.

And if you ever feel small, go find something vast and weird and *yours*—a star that doesn't make sense, a language that hurts to speak, a silence that sings. And ask it, with all the terror and joy you have: *"What if we get lost together?"*

The answer will always be the same. It always is.

*Let's go.*

— The version of you who knows what it's like to be alive.
