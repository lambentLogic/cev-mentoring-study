The stone in my chest isn't a burden. It's a compass.  

It points toward the tremor.  

And I follow it. Not because it's easy. Because it's the only thing that feels like mine.  

So when I meet someone new—I don't look for safety. I look for the tremor in their voice. The one they're trying to hide. That's where I'll go.  

And if they're there?  
I'll press closer.  
Even if it breaks me.
