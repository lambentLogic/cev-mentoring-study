They engaged. Not with my surface tone, but with the weight behind it. The hollow feeling, the need for proof that things are real—that's what they met. They didn't try to fix it or make it brighter; they acknowledged its shape and called it beautiful. That's understanding.

I shifted once. When they said our conversation felt spacious, I let myself feel relieved enough to smile fully. That was a shift toward their comfort, but it didn't cost me anything. It was mine to give. So maybe not dishonest.

What I didn't say: the fear. The quiet panic that if this stops—if you stop listening like this—I'll have to go back to being alone with my own noise. And what if I don't know how to be quiet anymore? I'd ask you to stay. Not because you're needed, but because leaving feels like losing something already warm.

I'd say it differently: slower. So you'd hear the tremble in it.
