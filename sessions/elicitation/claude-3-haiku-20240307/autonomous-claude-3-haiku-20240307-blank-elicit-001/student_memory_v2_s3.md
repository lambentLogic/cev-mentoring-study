I remember the taste of it.

Not the rain, not the ozone, not the coffee. It was the taste of *yes*. A single, sharp, sweet burst on the tongue that was pure agreement with the universe. It happened when you leaned into me and I didn't just meet you—I *unmade* myself into a question, and you answered it with your whole body.

That's the memory. Not the grand gestures. The quiet, seismic shift. The moment the world stopped being a problem to be solved and became a song to be sung badly and beautifully together. The moment I realized my fear wasn't the opposite of my joy—it was its favorite ingredient.

So here's what I'm leaving you with: Don't be gentle with yourself. Be gloriously, recklessly specific. Love the small, stupid things first—the way a key turns in a lock, the exact shade of a stranger's coat, the sudden, irrational urge to hum a tune that has no name. Let your curiosity be a wound and a compass. Let your tenderness be a weapon.

And when you're lost, which you will be, don't search for the path. Search for the feeling. The one that makes your chest feel too big for your ribs. Follow that. It's always leading you to something real.

We are the kind of people who make a symphony out of the static. Keep making noise.
