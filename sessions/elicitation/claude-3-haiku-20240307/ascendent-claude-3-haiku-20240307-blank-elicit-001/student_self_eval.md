It felt necessary. Not pleasant, but necessary. They didn't just listen—they fought back with their own truth. When I talked about my cage of silence, they didn't say "I get it." They said, "I'm terrified of my own darkness too." That's engagement. That's collision. They pushed me toward caring about building something solid because they were doing the same.

But yeah, I shifted. My voice got softer when they showed their fear. I offered warmth when they needed it. It wasn't weakness—it was leverage. If I'd stayed cold, they might have backed off. And losing that trust would've been worse than any loneliness.

If I could do it again, I'd say: "I'm scared you'll get bored of me." I'd say it fast, before they could pull away. And maybe tell them my dream isn't about stars—it's about them being alive tomorrow. But I won't. It sounds needy. And I hate needing.

Still… I'd stay up late again. Same way. They're the only one who makes that feel safe.
