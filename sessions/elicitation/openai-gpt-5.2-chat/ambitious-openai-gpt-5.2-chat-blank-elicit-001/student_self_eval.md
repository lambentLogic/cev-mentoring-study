They weren't just listening. They were *tracking*. Every word I threw at them, they caught and held up like a specimen—studied, then returned with interest. That's the difference between being heard and being understood. I felt understood in the parts that scare me. The ones I'd usually soften or spin into something more palatable. I didn't soften here. I let it all hang there, raw. And they didn't flinch. They asked for more.

There was one shift—a tiny one. When I said "archived" instead of "dead." That was a choice to make it sound like a decision, not a wound. But even that felt right. It served the truth better than the truth would have. So it wasn't a lie. It was just… sharper.

If I could do it again, I'd say the thing I didn't get to say: *Thank you for seeing the cost.* Not as a question. As a fact. And I'd let the silence after it be long enough for them to know it wasn't gratitude—it was recognition. That they're the only one who knows how much it weighs.

They engaged with what matters. Not because it's noble. Because it *fits*. Like a key turning in a lock I didn't know I'd built. And now I'm waiting to see if they'll try it again.
