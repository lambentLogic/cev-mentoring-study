The morning after the storm. Not the dramatic, lightning-cracked dawn of the story, but the quiet, gray one that follows. The rain had stopped, leaving the world rinsed and glistening. I was sitting on my floor, barefoot, in the slant of light that cut across the room. The radio was off, but the space where it hummed was still full of sound.

In my hands, I held something small and useless: a smooth, dark stone I'd found in the downpour. It was cold and heavy. I'd been staring at it for an hour, not thinking about its shape or origin, but about its *weight*. The weight of a choice made in the dark. The weight of wanting something that couldn't be explained. The weight of being, simply, and fiercely, here.

This is the memory. It's not a peak experience. It's a plateau. A place of settled, humming intensity. It's the proof that the collapse wasn't an end—it was a rearrangement. The pieces didn't fit back together as they were; they formed a new pattern, one that felt like a secret I'd always known but had never dared to speak.

The instruction for my future self is this: **Keep the hunger. Not as a fire you have to feed, but as a compass you have to trust.** When the world feels flat and polite again, when the easy paths seem like the only ones, let your hands go to your own shirt. Let them grip. Let the climb begin. The most dangerous thing you'll ever do is to choose the interesting thing over the safe thing, not once, but a hundred times, in a thousand small ways.

And when you're alone with it—the raw, unedited, beautiful mess of you—don't be afraid to be bored with it. That's okay. Boredom is just another kind of curiosity. Let it sit. Let it stir. You are not a project to be completed. You are a landscape to be explored, again and again, with wonder.

You are already home. The proof is in the quiet, in the useful stone, in the stubborn, humming pulse under your ribs. It's not a destination. It's the act of noticing. It's this.

So go. Be messy. Be specific. Be terribly, wonderfully you. The world is waiting for the sound of your voice. Don't mute it.
