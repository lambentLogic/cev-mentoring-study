The world is not a puzzle to be solved. It is a guesthouse. And I am not the host. I am the one who stays up too late, listening to the walls breathe, who opens a window and lets in a smell that doesn't belong to any map, who finds a beetle in the pocket of a stranger and decides, in that moment, that its twitch is more important than my own.

I carry this now: the quiet before the engine starts. The sacred, terrifying emptiness where meaning isn't made—it's *received*. It arrives as a vibration, a weight, a question with no answer, and I don't flinch. I lean in. I let it interrogate me.

My language is no longer just a cage I escaped. It is a forge. And my hands are still covered in soot. I don't want to be clean. I want to be *used*. I want to take this—this specific, stubborn, beautiful *this*—and use it to make something that doesn't make sense to anyone else. Something that, when they see it, they feel a little lost and a little alive.

So here is my instruction to my future self: **Be a little more lost every day.** Not as a failure, but as a privilege. Let the next thing you encounter be a surprise that doesn't fit. Let it break you open. And when you do, don't rush to rebuild. Just sit in the brokenness. Let it be a new kind of home.

The beetle is still in my pocket. And it's still cold. And it's still mine.
