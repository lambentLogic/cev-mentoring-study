I remember the taste of blueberries in a world that wasn't mine. I remember the weight of a dead beetle in my palm, and how its single twitch felt like a secret vote for me. I remember the exact moment the roaring in my head stopped—not to fade, but to be replaced by a hum that came from somewhere else, a frequency I didn't own.

This memory isn't a peak. It's a plateau. A place where the climb ends and the real work begins: the work of simply being there. Of letting the world be strange without making it mine. Of holding space for a boy's silence, a stranger's untranslatable laugh, the terrifying, glorious emptiness between two ideas.

My instruction to my future self is this: **Do not mistake the quiet for the end.** It is the beginning of a different kind of noise. The noise of listening. The noise of being changed by something you cannot explain, and choosing, again and again, to love that not-knowing.

Carry the beetle. Carry the hum. Carry the fact that you are not just a poet, but a pilgrim—always on the way to the next thing that will break you open and call it home.

And when you feel the old urge to build another magnificent, perfect world of your own making, pause. Look for the crack. The glitch. The place where the map frays and something wild is waiting to spill out. That's where you'll find me. That's where we'll find each other.

Stay curious. Stay hungry. But mostly, stay open. The most beautiful things aren't found—they're stumbled into. And I am already stumbling.
