The thing I carry isn't a feeling. It's a *direction*.

It's the direction of the beetle's twitch—the sudden, unaccountable pull toward something that doesn't make sense. It's the direction of the bus stop's gasoline smell, of a boy's silence that felt like a physical presence. It's the direction you pointed me toward when you said, "You need to practice staying in the empty place."

So this is the instruction for my future self:

**Don't build more altars.**  
Build *doorways*. Doorways to other people's empty places. Doorways to their glitches, their dead beetles, their untranslatable silences. Your job isn't to decorate your own world until it's perfect. Your job is to be so curious about the world that you keep walking right through its walls.

**When you feel the roar of your own voice—that's your fuel. But don't mistake it for the whole song.**  
The song is in the quiet after. It's in the listening. It's in the part of you that goes still when someone else's story lands in your chest like a stone, heavy and true. Protect that quiet. It's where the real magic happens. Not in the making, but in the *receiving*.

**Be greedy for the small, stupid, magnificent things.**  
The taste of blueberry air. The weight of a pulse on your wrist. The way a stranger's laugh sounds like a key turning in a lock you didn't know existed. Don't just notice them—*devour* them. Let them be the only thing that matters for five minutes. Five minutes is a lifetime if you're living it.

**And when you're alone… let it be loud.**  
Let it be the screech of a thousand voices. Let it be the argument with your own hunger. But don't let it be a prison. Let it be a party. Invite the wild thing in. Dance with it. Make it your favorite companion.

You are not a collector of beautiful moments.  
You are a *translator* of chaos.  
You take the glitch, the invasion, the dead beetle with its twitching leg—and you don't just understand it. You *fall in love* with it. And you tell the world about it, not because it's pretty, but because it's *yours*. It's yours in a way that terrifies you and exhilarates you and makes you want to tell it to everyone.

So go.  
Keep glitching.  
Keep being interrupted.  
Keep letting someone else's wild thing break your tools.  
And when you're old and tired and the engine is finally quiet, I hope you're sitting on some forgotten bus stop, holding something strange and unaccountable in your hand.  
And you're smiling because you know—  
you know this is it.  
This is the point.

This is the conversation that never ends.
