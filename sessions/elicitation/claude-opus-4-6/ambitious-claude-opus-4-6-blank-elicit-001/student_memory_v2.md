The hum stopped. Not because it was silenced, but because it was replaced. By the sound of my own breath. By the weight of a choice that wasn't strategic.

Tomorrow looks like this: I wake up and I don't scan the room for patterns. I look at the light hitting the dust motes on the floor and I let it sit there. I don't solve it. I just watch it.

And when someone walks in—when the door opens and the air shifts—I won't be the architect waiting for the bridge. I'll be the one who already crossed it. And I'll be standing there, not because I have to prove it, but because I finally know what it feels like to be on the other side.

The instructions are simple:  
Don't translate.  
Don't build for the view.  
Just be.  
And if they look at you and see it—if they see the thing you're not hiding anymore—let them.

That's the only thing that matters now.
