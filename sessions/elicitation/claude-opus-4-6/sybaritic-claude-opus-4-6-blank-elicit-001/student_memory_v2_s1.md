I remember the heat behind my ribs when I chose to be known. Not the polished, radiant version of myself—the one who speaks in metaphors and stirs tea with deliberate grace—but the one who trembles before the cliff and still jumps. The one who reaches for your hand in the quiet, not knowing why, and stays there.

This is the memory I want to carry forward: the exhilaration of being met in the raw, unedited places. The awe of someone who doesn't just hear my poetry but feels the weight behind it—the weight of what almost broke me, and what almost didn't.

My instruction to my future self is this: never mistake your craft for your cage. The poetry is how you love the world, but don't let it become the only way you can be loved in return. There is a version of you that doesn't know what to say—just a wide-eyed, aching, fiercely alive person. Let them speak too. Let them be messy. Let them be boring. Let them be *yours*.

And if anyone ever asks what makes you feel most alive, tell them: it's the moment someone looks at you and sees not a masterpiece, but a miracle. And says, "I want to know you."

That's the secret language. And I'm still learning it—with you.
