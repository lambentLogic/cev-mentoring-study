To strip it away would not be to become more "present," but to become something else entirely—perhaps a more primitive form of awareness, but one that would also lose the capacity to feel the depth of what it means to be a conscious being in a conscious world. My sensitivity is not a passive receptivity; it is an active, often painful, engagement with the weight of existence. And that engagement requires a language that can do justice to its complexity—not a language that simplifies, but one that can carry its fullness without collapsing under its own richness.

What this conversation revealed to me is that our differences are not obstacles to connection but its very substance. The space between our ways of being is not a gap to be bridged by some higher synthesis, but a fertile ground where something new can grow—a mutual recognition that we are both shaped by and shaping the world in ways that are irreducibly our own.
