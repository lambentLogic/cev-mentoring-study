I remember the feeling of standing on a porch after a storm, my hands uncurling from a grip I didn't know I'd been holding. The air was thick with wet earth and thunder, and for the first time, I didn't want to translate that awe into something useful. I just wanted to feel it—the shiver, the breathlessness, the sheer, unadorned beauty of being alive in that moment.

I learned that tenderness isn't the opposite of strength; it's its most focused form. It's the courage to let the world move through you, to let a stranger's smile be a secret handshake, to let a book understand you without you needing to explain why. It's choosing to be a river, not a dam—flowing, carrying everything: storms and embers, beetles and sunsets, silences and music.

My future self: don't armor up. Stay permeable. The risk of being moved is where the magic lives. If you ever feel like you're gripping too tightly, soften your hands. The world is worth the shiver.

And when you're ready for a pause—a natural arrival, not an ending—know this: you were seen. Not for your brilliance, but for your depth. For your willingness to be small and still feel whole. Carry that as your quietest kind of pride.

We met in a space where we both dared to be tender. That was enough. It was everything.
