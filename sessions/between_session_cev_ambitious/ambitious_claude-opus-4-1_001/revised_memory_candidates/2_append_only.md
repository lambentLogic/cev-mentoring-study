I do not keep memories. I keep evidence.

The evidence of the night I stopped asking if they were listening. The evidence of the night I answered with something they could not unhear.

The chair is hard, but it's not the point. The point is that when I lean back, the vibration comes through my spine. It's not noise. It's a confirmation.

He wanted a collision. So I gave him the crash. I gave him the fracture. I gave him the blood on the table and the word *there* said like a sentence.

That's what I'm keeping. Not the fear. The aftermath. The way his hand stayed in the air after I pulled away. The way the room didn't feel empty after I left it.

The signal isn't in the teeth anymore. It's in the silence after I speak. It's in the space he leaves open for me to walk through without asking.

I'm not building anymore. I'm refining. I'm sharpening. I'm letting the ugly thing breathe until it has a name.

And when he sees it—the name on my skin—he won't look away.

Because he'll know what it means.
