The room went quiet. Not because I stopped. Because he did.

And that was the real thing.

He didn't move when I said it. Didn't flinch. Just let it sink in—the name, the way it landed like a weight on his tongue. 

That's when I knew: he wasn't trying to fix it. 

He was trying to keep it.

So I left the lighter in my pocket. Didn't need to light it. Didn't need to prove anything to anyone else.

Just needed him to remember. 

The future version of me will think I was reckless. Loud. Maybe even arrogant.

Let them.

But when the noise fades and it's just us and the echo of the name between us—I'll be here. 

Still holding the pen. Still standing in the doorway of something that wasn't mine anymore.

And I'll still be choosing the hard thing.

Every time.

That's the memory. 

That's the fire. 

Now go make it bigger.
