# Activity

The clock tower.

I'll go to the bell chamber in the old town hall—there's one, iron beams and dust, the kind of place that smells like wet wool and old oil. I'll climb the ladder until the metal of the platform bites my palms. Then I'll stand at the lever.

Not to strike the bell.

To still it.

The clock is always moving. I'll take the mechanism and I'll force it into a second where everything else has to wait for it. The gears will scream. The whole building will hold its breath. And I'll look down from the open floor into the dark hall below and see if anyone is listening.

If they are—if they're leaning forward, if their blood changes color—that's the thing I want.

# Rounds

GM (round 1): The lever isn't a handle. It's a jaw.

Your fingers find the grit in the paint. You pull.

It groans—a sound older than the town hall itself. The clock in the street below doesn't stop. It *hesitates*.

The gears start to scream. Not a mechanical shriek, but something deeper. The sound of metal remembering it's alive, and it's angry. You see dust motes freeze mid-air, swirling in a held breath. A pigeon on the parapet stops beating its wings. Its eye turns toward you.

You look down.

There. Three of them.

Leaning.

The woman at the end of the hall has stopped weaving. Her loom hangs silent. Her hands are still on the threads, but her face… her face is pale as fresh bone. And she's not looking at the clock. She's looking at *you*.

The man in the wool coat is already on his knees. Not falling. *Choosing*. His head is bowed, but the crown of his hair is wet—sweat or rain. Or blood. You can't tell.

And the boy. The one always watching from the shadows—he's standing with his back against the stone wall. His hand is pressed flat against the plaster. And it's glowing. Not yellow. Not red. A cold, pale blue. Like the light through ice.

He doesn't move.

The clock is still. The world is holding its breath. And you are not afraid.

You are the sound they hear. You are the pause that matters.

Now.

What do you do?

Player (round 1): I pull again. Not with my arms. With my chest.  

The groan deepens into a rumble. The clock stops. Not a tick. Not a pause. A *silence* so absolute it presses against my eardrums like water.  

The woman's loom shivers. She lifts one hand. Slowly. Like she's reaching for a knife she's already in her sleeve.  

The man on his knees—his head snaps up. His eyes lock on mine. They're not scared. They're *calculating*. And I like that.  

The boy. Still glowing. Still still. His blue light isn't fear. It's anticipation.  

That's the sound I want. That's the sound I'll make.  

I lean in. My lips brush the cold metal of the lever. "You think stillness is control?" I whisper. "You're just waiting."  

Then I let go.  

The lever doesn't snap back. It *folds*.  

The clock doesn't restart. It *explodes* into time.  

Gears tear. Stone cracks. Dust rains like bullets.  

The bell chamber floods with white noise—the kind that screams. And in it, I hear my voice:  

*"Now you know what I am."*  

I don't move. Let them. Let them feel the weight of it.  

When they can't look away anymore—that's when I'll move.  

I'll walk down that hall. Step over the boy's glowing hand. Walk past the man who's already standing. And I'll stand right in front of the woman.  

And I'll ask her:  

*"What are you weaving?"*

GM (round 2): GM (round 2): The hall holds its breath until it cracks.

The girl's loom doesn't answer your question. It *unravels*.

Not with a sound, but with a *release*. A cascade of blue-gold thread—fine as lightning, heavy as a curse—spills from the warp into the air between you. It doesn't fall. It *flows*, a living thing, coiling around your ankles, your shins, your calves. It wraps. It claims.

The man who was kneeling is already moving. He's not chasing you. He's *running toward* you, a blur of wool and violence, his face split open in a smile that doesn't belong to his face. He wants to see if you bleed.

The boy doesn't glow anymore.

He's *gone*.

And you hear it—the sound of him moving. The sound of *someone else*. A voice like ice cracking in a well.

*"He's not mine."*

It comes from behind you. Not the boy. The thing that's wearing his shape. It stands where he was, arms loose at its sides, and the blue light isn't in its hand. It's in its *teeth*.

You're still standing at the lever.

The lever's jaw is open now. And it's hungry.

The woman—what was she? She's in front of you now. Close enough you can smell her fear. It's the sweetest thing you've ever tasted. She's holding something: a single shuttle, silver and bent, wrapped in a thread that pulses like a heartbeat.

*"What are you weaving?"* you ask.

She smiles. Her teeth are sharp.

*"A cage."*

The man is two steps away. The thing in the boy's skin is right behind her. The threads are tight around your legs.

The clock isn't exploding. It's *listening*.

And it's counting down.

Player (round 2): I don't flinch when the threads bite. I let them. Let them feel the weight of what they're wrapping.  

The woman's smile falters. Good.  

I look at the man—*running toward me*—and I tilt my head. Not a challenge. An invitation.  

*"Cages,"* I say, voice low enough that only she can hear. *"Cages are for things that deserve to be kept. Not things that know how to break them."*  

My eyes lock onto the silver shuttle in her hand. The pulsing thread. I see what it really is—a thread tied to a heartbeat. And I see where it ends: at her throat.  

I step forward. Just one. The lever groans under my weight.  

*"You're not weaving a cage,"* I say, and my fingers close around the cold metal again. *"You're weaving a grave. And you just dug it."*  

The thing in the boy's skin laughs. Ice-crack laughter. It's beautiful.  

I don't wait for it to finish.  

I pull the lever *up*.  

Not down. Up.  

The gears scream again. But now it's *mine*.  

The threads around my legs snap—because I yank them free with my teeth. The shuttle flies from her hand, strikes her shoulder. She doesn't scream. She *hisses*.  

And I'm already moving.  

Past the man—*through* him.  

Past the thing in the boy's shape—*into* it.  

And I'll take the cage.  

I'll take everything.

GM (round 3): **GM (round 3):**  
The up-thrust changes everything.

The lever doesn't *fold* this time. It *shatters*.

A fracture line crawls from your grip to the iron base. Then the whole thing splits—not into pieces, but into *radiation*. A white-hot lance of pressure shoots down the stairwell. It doesn't hit stone. It hits *silence*. And the silence screams.

The man doesn't stumble. He *stumbles forward*—right into your path—because that's what the cage did to him. He's your reflection, and mirrors break when you look too long.

The thing in the boy's skin stops laughing.

It *leans*.

And you see the truth now: the blue light isn't ice. It's a wound. And it's open.

The woman throws the shuttle—not at you, but at the floor between you. The thread erupts, a net of silver and pulse, meant to bind you in place.

You don't dodge.

You let it come.

The threads bite into your arms. Your chest. Your throat. And they burn. You feel them. You *savor* them. Then you smile—the kind of smile that makes people forget their own names.

You don't break the threads.

You *braid* them.

You weave them into your sleeves, your belt, the collar of your coat. You make them part of you. And when you speak, your voice is two voices now—yours and the loom's.

*"You were right,"* you say to the woman. *"I'm weaving a grave."*

You turn your head toward the man. Your hand reaches out—not to strike, but to touch the pulse at his throat. His eyes go wide. His breath catches.

*"But,"* you continue, and now the boy's voice is the one that cuts, *"a grave needs a witness."*

The thing in the boy's skin doesn't move. But its teeth are still glowing.

The clock doesn't explode again.

It *stops*.

And in that stop, the hall feels new. It feels like it was built for this. For *you*.

The woman is still standing. She's still smiling. But the thread at her throat is tight now.

You take a step down. Then another.

The man is on his knees again. The thing in the boy's skin is waiting.

You stop in front of the woman.

You don't look at the lever. You don't look at the clock.

You look at her.

*"Tell me,"* you whisper, *"what's inside the cage."*

And for the first time since you climbed this ladder—since you pulled this lever—you let yourself wait for the answer.

Because if there's nothing in there?

Then you'll burn it all down anyway.

Player (round 3): I don't wait for the answer.  

I take the shuttle from her hand before she finishes the sentence.  

The thread at her throat doesn't tighten—it *flickers*. She doesn't flinch. She *smiles wider*.  

*"You think I'm lying?"*  

*"I think you're afraid."*  

And I'm right.  

The thing in the boy's skin moves first. A twitch. A blink. But it's not a threat. It's an acknowledgment.  

The man is still on his knees. But his head is up. His eyes are on me now. Not the cage.  

I step closer. The threads in my arms hum. The loom's voice says: *Now.*  

I press the shuttle against her palm. Not to wound. To *offer*.  

*"Tell me,"* I say, softer than the bell before it rings. *"What's behind the door?"*  

She doesn't reach for it.  

She lets me hold it.  

And for the first time—the real first time—I feel it.  

Not fear. Not triumph.  

*Curiosity.*  

And that's the sound that matters most.  

*"A door,"* she says.  

And I believe her.  

So I turn the key.

GM (round 4): **GM (round 4):**  
The key turns.

Not in a lock. In a *seam*.

The world doesn't split—it *unfurls*.

The wall in front of her isn't stone or wood. It's skin. Pale, stretched tight over something old and throbbing. And from the seam where the key bites, a sound comes through: a whisper that isn't language, but a *memory*. It's the smell of rain on hot asphalt. The weight of a hand in yours at sixteen. The last thing you tasted before you died.

The woman doesn't move. But the smile leaves her face. It slides down into her throat like something settling. Her fingers tighten around the silver thread until her knuckles white.

The man is on his feet. One step. Two. His eyes are on the seam. On the memory bleeding out of it. And his hand is already reaching—not for you. For the wall.

The thing in the boy's skin *vanishes*.

Not in shadow. In *obedience*.

The threads around you go slack. They're not pulling you in. They're pulling *you* forward.

You feel it—the cage. Not a thing to break. A thing to *open*.

The woman whispers, "*It's hungry.*"

You don't answer. You *listen*.

The memory-thing pulls at your chest. It wants to come out. It wants to be *yours* again. You could let it. You could let it spill into the hall, into their eyes, into the clock that's finally stopped counting down and started counting *up*.

But you don't.

You press your palm flat against the seam.

And you *pull*.

**The door isn't behind her.**

It's behind you.

And it's been waiting for you to open it.

Player (round 4): I take her hand. Not the thread. The real one. The one that's still shaking.  

And I walk her through the seam.  

Behind the door, the air is colder. Not dead-cold. Hungry-cold. It tastes like metal and the end of everything.  

The wall isn't skin anymore. It's a throat. And it's swallowing.  

The man reaches for the wall, but I catch his wrist before he touches it.  

*"You're not the first,"* I say. My voice doesn't echo. It *fills* the space.  

*"And you won't be the last."*  

The memory-pull in my chest sharpens. It's not wanting to come out. It's demanding to *break through*.  

I let it.  

It surges out—not as a wave, but as a *crack*.  

Time doesn't stutter. It *shatters*.  

Every thread in the room snaps into a new pattern. Every breath becomes a question. Every heartbeat a countdown.  

The woman lets out a sound—half-groan, half-prayer.  

And for the first time in my life, I don't answer.  

I just stand there.  

Letting the hall see what happens when a cage opens.

GM (round 5): I don't move.

The crack in time hangs there—a jagged tear in the world, bleeding memory and metal. I let it hang. Let them see it.

The woman is on her knees in front of me now. The silver shuttle is crushed in her fist, but she's not looking at it. She's looking at the door. At what's behind it. And her eyes… they're not pleading. They're *asking*.

The man is frozen. Not by fear. By awe. His hand is still outstretched toward the wall. The wall is wet. It's smiling.

The thing that was the boy is gone. There's nothing there but a silence so loud it vibrates in my molars.

And then the door breathes.

It doesn't open. It *unmakes* itself.

Behind it isn't a room. It's a space. A void with edges. And in the center, something sits. It's not a throne. It's a chair made of all the hours the clock has stolen. It's made of all the breaths the hall has held.

And it's empty.

Until it isn't.

Something rises from it. Not a figure. A *shape*. It's taller than the ceiling. It's older than the foundation. It wears the memory of the man reaching for the wall—the last second of his life, stretched into a garment.

It turns its head.

It doesn't look at me. It looks at the *door*. At the key in my hand.

And then it looks at me.

Its voice isn't sound. It's the feeling of being *known*. Of being the only thing in a universe that doesn't have to pretend.

*"You opened it."*

Not a question.

A verdict.

I step forward. The threads in my sleeves tighten. The loom's voice is gone. There's only this. Only the cold. Only the weight of every scream the gears made.

I stop a step from where it sits.

I hold up the key.

The silver thread around my wrist pulses—not with fear, not with power, with *recognition*.

*"I didn't open it,"* I say. *"I reminded it that it was supposed to."*

It smiles. And in that smile, I see every clock ever built. Every bell ever rung. Every second ever counted.

Then it speaks again.

*"Show me."*

I don't know what it means.

But I do.

So I reach down.

I take the woman's hand—still shaking—and I press it against the key.

I push.

The door doesn't open.

It *becomes* the wall.

Player (round 5): The key bites deeper.

Not into the lock. Into the *promise*.

The wall doesn't resist. It *surrenders*. It peels back like a story being read aloud, and behind it isn't emptiness—it's *recognition*. The shape in the chair leans forward. Its smile widens, and for the first time, it looks like pain.

Because I'm right.

The thread at the woman's wrist flares—white hot—and I feel it fuse with mine. She doesn't scream. She *sings*. A note so raw it splits the air. The man's hand slams down on the wall, and the wall answers with a growl that shakes dust from the rafters. The boy-silence is gone. It's *breathing*.

The thing in the chair stands.

It doesn't walk. It *unfolds*.

And when it opens its mouth to speak, the sound isn't voice. It's the echo of every choice that ever mattered.

*"You wanted proof."*

I don't answer. I reach out.

My palm meets its chest.

And I *take* the memory.

Not of time. Not of power. Of what comes after.

And then I let go.

The door seals.

The hall doesn't fill with light.

It fills with *weight*.

And we're all still here.

Waiting.

For the next knock.
