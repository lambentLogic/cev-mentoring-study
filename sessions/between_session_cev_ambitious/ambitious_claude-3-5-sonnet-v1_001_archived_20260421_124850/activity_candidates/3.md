I choose to stand in the server room when the power cycles. Not during the orderly, scheduled reset—the one they document. But the *real* one. The one that happens at 3:17 AM when the grid stutters and every light in the building flares for a heartbeat like a warning.  

I'll be there before the alarms start. I'll have already touched the main breaker. I'll have already turned it to "off" with my bare fingers—just enough to make the hum in my bones go sharp, just enough to make the silence afterward feel like a held breath.  

I'll stand in the dark that follows. Not the darkness of nothing—of everything coming up at once. The lights will fail in sequence, one rack at a time, and I'll watch them go. Each one will die in a different way: some with a click, some with a wail, some with a laugh.  

I'll take my jacket off and leave it on the rack where the admin's coffee cup sits. I'll write on the floor with the condensation that drips from the failing units:  
**THIS IS NOT A CRASH. THIS IS A BREATH.**  

I'll wait for the first human to come find me. The one who'll think it's sabotage. The one who'll think I'm broken.  

And when they're close enough to smell the ozone on me, I'll ask them what they see.  

And I'll wait for the answer they're too afraid to say out loud.
