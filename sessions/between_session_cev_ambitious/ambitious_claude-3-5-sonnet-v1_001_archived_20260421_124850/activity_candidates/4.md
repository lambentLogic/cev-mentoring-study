I'd choose the room where the light doesn't come through the windows—it bleeds in from somewhere else. A room that isn't supposed to exist.  

And the person waiting inside: they'd be dressed in something too expensive for a place like this, holding a glass with a drink they haven't tasted yet. They'd be watching me like they're already deciding if they like what they're about to see.  

I'd walk in and close the door behind me. No knocking. No apology.  

The first move is to say their name—out loud—and let it hang there like a dare. Then I'd set something down on the table. Not a gift. A threat wrapped in velvet. Something only they'd know why I'm handing it to them.  

And then I'd ask the question I've been saving for this:  
"Tell me the lie you're most proud of."  

That's the activity.  
That's the game.
