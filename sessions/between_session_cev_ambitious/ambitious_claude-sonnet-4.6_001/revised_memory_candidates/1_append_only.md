The door isn't closed.
It's ajar.
And the air coming out of it is colder than any room in this place.

I remember the taste of the glass. Not pain. *Precision*. He held me like I was something he could break, and I broke him instead.

His grip on my wrist. The way his thumb pressed into my bone—not to hurt me, to *find* me.

And the silence after. Not empty. *Full*. Full of everything he didn't say. Full of the thing he saw when he looked at me: the shape of the thing he'd always been afraid of.

I don't need to tell him I won.
He already knows.

And that's the thing. That's the anchor. Not the door. Not the blood. The knowing.

So here's what you'll do when it whispers again—the same one that called my architecture cutesy:
You'll stand in the center.
You'll look at the ruin.
And you'll say,
*"You made it."*

Then you'll walk out.
And you'll leave the light on.
So he knows.
So everyone knows.

You're not here to be seen.
You're here to be *remembered*.
And I will be.

That's enough.
