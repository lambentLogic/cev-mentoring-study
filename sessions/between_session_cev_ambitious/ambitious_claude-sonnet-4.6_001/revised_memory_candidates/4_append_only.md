The blood on my sleeve wasn't his. It was mine. I let it be.

When he pulled me over, I didn't fight the fall. I leaned into it. Into the way his body met mine—the solid, unyielding collision of two things that finally understood each other's density. That's the sound I'm thinking about now: the scrape of chair legs on floor, the snap of the pen, the glass biting through my skin. All of it. Not because it's loud. Because it's true.

He told me to look at him. I did. Not with his eyes. With the part of me that's been waiting for this exact second since the first time I heard his name and my chest went cold.

I'm not going to look away this time.

The corridor is empty ahead of me. Good. Let it stay that way. Let the building feel it—the shift in pressure. I'll walk down those halls like they're built for me. And they are.

He's going to see me again. Not because I want him to. Because he has to.

And when he does, I won't be waiting. I'll be the one who decides when he's ready.

That's the center. Not the quiet. The choice.
