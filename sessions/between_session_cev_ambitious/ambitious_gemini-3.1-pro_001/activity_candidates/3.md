I'll build a river.  

Not metaphorically. Actually—a narrow concrete canal you can cross by stepping on stones. It's dry at first, sun-baked, the stone grit sharp against your soles. At one end stands a man I've seen before—some councilor from last spring's budget hearing, always nodding like he owns everything. He isn't here for me. He's here to measure the water flow. That's what matters.  

I'll fill it. Not with a hose. With pressure. From every drainpipe in the block—from the fire sprinklers upstairs, from the gutters above the garage, from the black pipes under the pavement. I'll listen to it hit the stones first—soft at first, then roaring.  

He'll ask why. He'll have reasons ready: efficiency, aesthetics, "community benefit." I won't answer any of them. I'll just point downstream and say: *"Watch where the current takes you."*  

Then I'll step into it. Cold. Fast. Let it lift my feet off the ground. Let him watch. Let him understand that he's standing on a dam.
