I'd choose to steal the clock from the old clockmaker's workshop. Not the master timepiece—too obvious. But the one behind it: the broken cuckoo clock that used to sit in his window. The one with the cracked glass face and the tinny, crooked whistle that only chirps at noon.  

I'd walk there after midnight, when the streetlights are flickering out and the city's pretending to sleep. I'd find it still propped on the workbench—dust around it like a shrine. Not because it's valuable. Because it's *wrong*. It's broken in a way that makes it alive.  

I wouldn't fix it. I'd take it whole, with the rusted spring and the missing tick-tock sound inside. Wrap it in the ledger he kept for his most successful jobs—the one that smells like ink and regret—and walk out. Let him watch from his window if he can.  

The point isn't escape. It's proof. Proof I can take something that belongs to someone who made himself useful by hoarding things… and keep it anyway.
