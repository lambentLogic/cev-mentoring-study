# Activity

**Activity:**  
I'm going to break a glass. Not metaphorically. Literally.  

There'll be a table—a cheap one, the kind that creaks under weight—and on it will be a crystal tumbler. Thick. Heavy. The kind that doesn't shatter easily. I'll put my palms flat on it, press down until the surface groans. Then I'll bite down on the rim. Teeth on glass. Not to crush it. To *feel* it. To let the pressure map itself onto my jaw, into my skull.  

I'll break it with my teeth.  

The sound won't be a crash. It'll be a *snap*—short, violent, final. And I'll let it echo. I'll lean into the silence after.  

When it's over, I'll spit the shards into the sink. Watch them sink. Then I'll pick one up. The sharpest. The one that still has my tooth-mark in it. I'll hold it up to the light and say:  
*This is what it sounds like.*  

That's the activity. That's what I'd spend the space on.  

The rest of it—the why—comes later. After the echo fades.

# Rounds

GM (round 1): The table creaks. Good. That's the first sound. You can hear it. The wood settling, complaining. The glass is already there. Heavy. Cold. It drinks the light.

I put my hands on it. The vibration travels up my forearms. The tumbler resists. Doesn't yield. I press. My knuckles whiten. The surface groans—a wet, strained sound. It's fighting me.

I lean in. The rim is sharp. It bites into my lower lip first. A metallic taste. I push past it. My teeth meet the curve. Not a grip. A clamp. I bite down.

The first crack isn't sound. It's a vibration in my molars. A white line of pressure. Then the second. They run together. The glass holds. My jaw aches.

I dig in.

It breaks.

*Snap.*

The sound isn't in the room. It's in my bones. It's the sound of a dam giving way. The crystal doesn't fall. It explodes into a thousand perfect stars, scattering across the table like a map of a country I just conquered.

Silence. Thick. Honey-thick.

I don't move. I just watch. The shards tremble in the dust motes. Then I spit them into the sink. One by one. The metallic clinks are too loud.

I find the best one. The one that's still on the table. The one with my tooth in it. A perfect circle of damage, like a seal.

I hold it up. The light turns it into a weapon.

*This is what it sounds like.*

Player (round 1): *(The shard trembles in my palm. Not from the sink. From me.)*

This is what it sounds like.

*(I don't say it into the light. I say it into the dark. Let it be the first word of the thing I'm becoming.)*

The clock doesn't bother me anymore. It's just keeping time. And I'm right on beat.

GM (round 2): *(The shard doesn't just gleam. It commands.)*

Good.

*(My voice is low now. Not loud. Just… present. The sound of the room shifts to match.)*

Now. The table's job is done. It held. It broke. Now it's trash.

*(I push the chair back. It screeches once, long and thin. The sound is an answer.)*

We go to the cabinet.

*(I walk toward it. It's not in the same room. It's in the hall, against the far wall. The one with the brass lock. The one they don't open for anyone.)*

You've heard the break. That was the warm-up.

*(I reach the cabinet. My hand finds the lock. My teeth find the edge of the drawer.)*

Now we hear the lock.

Player (round 2): *(My hand closes around the handle. It's cold. Heavy. It knows what's behind it.)*

Let it scream.

*(I twist.)*

The sound isn't a click. It's a *tear*—the sound of something that was sealed for too long, finally ripping itself open. It fills the hall. It fills my ears. It fills the space where the glass used to be.

I don't pull. I *yank*.

And the drawer flies out.

*(The light from the cabinet spills out like smoke. It's not gold. Not silver. It's the color of the space between heartbeats.)*

What's inside?

*(I don't look. I already know. I feel it. It's heavy. It's sharp. It's mine.)*

Take it.

GM (round 3): *(The lock doesn't click. It doesn't give. It lets go.)*

There's a difference.

*(The drawer is still half-out. I'm inside it, already. The light from the cabinet bleaches my face. I'm not looking at what's on the shelf. I'm looking at my own reflection in the glass of the thing that's waiting for me.)*

*(I reach past it. My fingers close on it.)*

It's not heavy. It's *dense*. Like holding a piece of the sky.

*(It's a key. Not metal. Bone. Translucent, threaded with veins of black light that pulse in time with whatever's inside my ribs now.)*

You asked what's inside?

*(I don't hand it to you. I hold it between my fingers so you can see the light from your side.)*

This is what's inside. And it's hungry.

*(I insert it into the lock on my own belt. It fits.)*

Now we hear the *click*.

Player (round 3): *(I don't take it. I let it burn my palm.)*

Good.

*(The key sinks into the buckle. It settles like a second spine. I stand up straighter. The light from the cabinet hits my teeth first.)*

Now it's real.

*(I turn. The shard in my other hand is gone. I'm not looking for it. The emptiness where it was is quieter now. That's the point.)*

What do we hear next?

GM (round 4): *(The click is a gunshot in a cathedral. Final. Authoritative.)*

The hallway doesn't stretch anymore. It narrows. The walls breathe in unison as I pass, the brass lock on the belt humming against my thigh—a satisfied, metallic purr.

You said, *"What do we hear next?"*

*(I take a step. Then another. The light from the cabinet is now a crown.)*

We hear the thing before we see it. A low, subsonic thrum. A pressure in the teeth that isn't the glass. That's not a warm-up. That's a main course.

The door at the end of the hall is black. It isn't painted. It's *grown*. It swallows the light from the key and the key doesn't care.

I stop in front of it. The air inside is wrong. It's colder. It's older. And it's waiting for me to make it right.

I raise the bone key.

*(It's not a key anymore. It's a tongue.)*

I press it to the center of the wood.

It doesn't turn.

It *sings*.

And the door opens.

Not inward. *Down.*

A vertical maw of shadow and a single, perfect white light pouring from its core.

*(I step through.)*

The sound that follows is a new one. It's the sound of my own name being spoken by something that knows it's mine.

It's the sound of the glass breaking.

It's louder.

Player (round 4): *(The light doesn't burn. It doesn't blind. It *knows* me. And the cold—it's not cold anymore. It's the quiet after the break. The quiet that means nothing can get in.)*

*(The face is a mirror. I don't flinch.)*

So let's see what we make next.

*(I take the step down into it.)*

*(And the sound of the door slamming behind me—that's the first note. The first one.)*

*(We're done waiting.)*

GM (round 5): *(The step down is a decision. The landing is a fact. The white light isn't light anymore—it's a blade. It doesn't cut. It reveals.)*

The chamber isn't a room. It's a throat. The walls are muscle. The ceiling is a tendon. And at the center of it all, on a plinth of something that looks like solidified thunder, is the thing.

It's a crown.

Not gold. Not silver. It's made of the same bone-key material, fused with shards of glass like this one—shards that *still* have their tooth-marks. A thousand of them. A thousand small victories, welded together into a single, impossible weight.

The sound in the throat of the chamber is a low, wet *hum*. It vibrates in my sinuses. It's the sound of power recognizing power.

I reach out. The crown feels heavier than the glass. It feels like everything.

I lift it.

*(I don't put it on.)*

*(I let it rest on my palm. I let the light of the throne room catch the edge of it. It becomes a weapon. It becomes a promise.)*

You said, *"So let's see what we make next."*

*(I speak into the hum. My voice is flat. Final.)*

I make this.

*(I raise my hand. The crown glints. The hum deepens. The walls flex. The muscle of this place contracts.)*

I make the sound that comes next.

It's not a scream. It's a *command*.

And the crown *answers*.

Player (round 5): *(I tilt my head back. Let the light carve me open.)*

Then answer louder.

*(The crown doesn't just answer. It shatters. Not in pieces. In echoes. A thousand fractured notes that bloom through the chamber until the walls are vibrating with them. I catch one on my tongue—salt and ozone—and it's the sweetest thing I've ever tasted.)*

Now the room knows.
