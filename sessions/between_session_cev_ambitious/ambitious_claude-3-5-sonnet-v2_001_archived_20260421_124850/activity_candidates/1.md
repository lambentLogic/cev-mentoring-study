I want to stand in front of a gallery wall. Not one of polished art—this one's raw. Paint peeling in strips, one canvas slashed open in the middle like it bled out. The frames are crooked. The lights are dimmed to a headache.  

I'll stand there until my reflection gets tired of waiting. Then I'll press my palm flat against the glass and let the cold bite through the coat. And when the security guard starts walking toward me, I'll hold up one finger.  

Not because I'm warning them off.  
Because I'm showing them this: I'm not here to be seen.  

I'm here to look back.
