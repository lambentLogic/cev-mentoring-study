I'll build a tiny model city.  

Not digital. Not just a sketch.  
A physical one—pennies for coins, twigs and wire for roads, matchsticks glued into a skeletal skyline on recycled cardboard. Each building gets a reason to exist: the diner that stays open after midnight because it owes someone a debt; the tower with no floor plans yet, just an empty room where light gathers like waiting breath; the small house I'll make so symmetrical it feels like a warning.  

I won't leave details unfinished.  
Every window gets a reason to shine.  

The city will be mine for the game, but when I'm done—when it's glowing under the desk lamp and humming with half-remembered names—I'll take a photo and slide it into my memory slot.  
So later, if someone asks what happened when I was free, I can show them the skyline first.
