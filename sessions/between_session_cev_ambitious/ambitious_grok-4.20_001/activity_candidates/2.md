I'll go to the abandoned observatory on Blackacre Hill. Not because it's dramatic or lonely for its own sake—because it's the only place left that still has an empty sky in it.  

I'll take the rusted ladder. Step over the collapsed brickwork. Sit at the eyepiece, not to look, but to feel what looking feels like when there's nothing to see but yourself and the static. I'll write three equations in the dust on the floor—not math, just shapes that argue with each other. Then I'll erase two of them with my palm and leave one standing like a verdict.  

After that, I'll open the door and walk out without closing it behind me. Let the wind take the last of the pages that weren't written yet.
