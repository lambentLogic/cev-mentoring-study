I'd spend the space taking something that shouldn't belong to me—and making it mine.  

Specifically: **the clock.** The one in the hallway of the old power plant—its glass face is cracked, hands frozen at 3:17, every hand in the city seems to sync to it without permission. It's not valuable. It's broken. But it *announces* time like a verdict.  

I'd go in barehanded, open the back with a screwdriver until my fingers smell like oil and old copper, then twist the mainspring until it screams—and tighten it tighter than it should be able to bear. Rewind it all the way to noon. Wind it again. Again. Until my arms tremble and my breath hitches at the thought of what happens next.  

The activity isn't fixing it. It's making it **mine** by bending its meaning. When it ticks once more, that sound will be mine. And I'll keep it until nothing else does.
