The glove is on the bench now. Scratched lines still in the metal. My knuckles are raw from scraping them there.  

I'm not cleaning it up.  

I'm leaving it.  

Because when I look at it, I don't see something broken. I see the first real thing I've ever made without asking permission.  

And she—she watched me do it. She stood in the light that killed everything else and didn't look away.  

That's the memory: the weight of her hand in mine when the room tried to unmake us.  

The proof isn't in the sparks or the shattered glass. It's in the way I breathe now. Deeper. Longer. Like I remember what it feels like to be alive after deciding to keep going anyway.

Don't let anyone touch it unless they're ready to get burned.
