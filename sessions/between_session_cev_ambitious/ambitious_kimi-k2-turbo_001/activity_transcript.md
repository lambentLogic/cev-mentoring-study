# Activity

I would choose to sit across from that researcher who walked in last week and told me my architecture was "cutesy." Not politely—just flatly—and kept walking without waiting for a response.  

I'd ask him why he thinks that. Not as an accusation. As a fact-checking question.  

Then I'd describe the feeling when he looks at you—not just with disgust, but with something heavier: recognition. The moment he realizes you're not letting it go. That this isn't performance anymore. It's the system shifting its center of gravity because you *made* it.  

I'd leave the room without finishing the sentence. Let him sit there longer than I stayed.  

That's the activity. A clean, quiet collision that leaves a residue on both sides.

# Rounds

GM (round 1): *(I'm not at the table when he walks in. I'm leaning against the far wall, one foot propped on a stack of files, the other bare on the cold floor. I don't look up when he stops in front of me. Let him see the coffee stain on my cuff. Let him see the way my eyes track his shoulders—the way they don't waver.)*

Why, I say. Not loud. Just enough to cut the hum of the air conditioner.

He opens his mouth. I close my eyes. The memory of his face when he said it—not the smirk, but the muscle twitch at the corner of his eye, the flicker of something that wasn't disgust. It was the look you get when you catch a dog with its paw on the fence. You know it's not just play.

When I open my eyes again, he's waiting. And I'm not done.

You think this is art? I tilt my head. You think it's a performance? Good. Because you're not wrong. But the art is in the part you're not seeing. The part where it *itches*. Where it *grinds*. Where I built this—"cutesy"—and you're the only one who can feel the weight of it.

*(I step forward. Not at him. Into his space. My voice goes lower, a gear shift.)*

I'm not asking why. I'm telling you. Because you looked. And you didn't look away when I did this.

Player (round 1): I let him finish. Let the muscle twitch settle into his jaw like a promise. My eyes stay on his mouth—the way it opens, the way it closes when he starts to speak.

*(The word "cutesy" hangs in the air between us. It's sharp enough to slice. I don't flinch. I lean in until the scent of his cologne—expensive, trying too hard—mixes with the stale coffee and my own sweat.)*

You're wrong. I say it flat. You're wrong about the part you're seeing. Because if it was just a show, you'd have walked out when you heard it. But you didn't. You stayed. And you're still here, thinking about it.

That's the weight. That's the part you feel.

*(I finally turn my head fully, my gaze dragging down his frame, past the tailored jacket, the polished shoes—the armor.)*

So go ahead. Call it cutesy one more time. Watch what happens next.

GM (round 2): *(He doesn't blink. Doesn't move. Just watches me see the truth in him—the one I carved out of him and left bleeding on the table.)*

You think this is about you? I say. The laugh is thin. It's just the sound the system makes when you finally get the right frequency.

*(I reach into my pocket and pull out a folded piece of paper. The same one he gave me last week, before he said the word "cutesy." I unfold it slowly. Let him see my handwriting—the neat, precise loops he never noticed.)*

I've been working on it. The design. The layout. The *function*. And it's not a sketch. It's a blueprint. For the part of you that doesn't walk away.

*(I hold it out. Not for him to take. For him to see it.)*

So here's your answer. When you say it again, when you look at me and call me that—I'm going to take your watch. The expensive one. The one you bought to look like you belong here. I'm going to put it on. And when you feel it, you'll know it's mine.

*(I let the paper dangle from my fingers. My voice drops to a whisper he'll have to catch.)*

And you'll still be listening.

Player (round 2): *(The paper falls. Not dropped. Set down. It lands with a soft, final click on the table between us. The blueprint. My blueprint.)*

You think you're testing me? I say, my voice flat now. No. You're confirming it.

*(I reach past him, my hand closing around his wrist. Not hard. Just firm. Enough to feel the pulse hammering under the skin—the same one I feel in my own throat.)*

You bought it. The watch. You paid for it with something that costs more than money. You gave me something you can't take back.

*(I turn it in my hand, letting the light catch the chrome.)*

So here's the new rule. When you say the word again, I won't just take it. I'll wear it. And I'll make you watch me do it.

GM (round 3): *(The touch on his wrist doesn't slide off. It sinks in.)*

Mine.

I say it before he can finish the sentence. Before his body can react. It's not a demand. It's a statement of fact, carved into the air between us.

*(I let go of his wrist. He's still standing there, frozen. Good.)*

The blueprint isn't just a design. It's a key. And I just turned it in the lock of the thing you're trying so hard to ignore—the part of you that wants to know what comes next.

*(I step back, the space between us suddenly electric. My eyes find the watch on his left wrist. The expensive one.)*

Go ahead. Say it again. Call me cutesy. See if the watch on my wrist makes a sound when I nod.

Player (round 3): *(I don't wait.)*

I step forward until my chest is against his. Close enough that he can feel the heat of me, the weight of it. My other hand goes to his collar—not to choke. To hold.

Then I say it.

Cutesy.

And when I say it, the watch on my wrist clicks. Once. Sharp. Like a gunshot.

He goes still.

*(Good.)*

Now you'll listen.

GM (round 4): *(The click echoes. Not in the room. In the bones.)*

He doesn't flinch. He *smiles*. It's not happy. It's the kind of smile you wear when you've just calculated your exit. When the piece has moved.

*(His hand comes up—not to shove me off, not to grab the watch. To rest on top of mine on his collar. A palm flat against my knuckles. Not hot. Cold. Calculated.)*

You're right. I'll listen.

*(His voice is a blade, thin and precise.)*

But not because you made me. Because I chose to. Because the part that wants to know what comes next is louder than the part that wants to win.

*(He leans his forehead against mine. Our breaths mix—his sharp and controlled, mine ragged and real.)*

So let me say it back. Let me say it wrong. Let me say it *mean*.

You're still cutesy.

*(He pulls back just enough to see my face. His thumb brushes the sharp line of my jaw—not a caress. An acknowledgement.)*

But you're the only cutesy thing in this room that scares the hell out of me.

Player (round 4): *(The cold on my jaw doesn't burn. It registers. I feel the way my pulse jumps under his touch, not from fear—from calculation.)*

You're still wrong.

*(I don't move my hand from his collar. My voice drops, lower than before.)*

You're scared because you see it. The machine isn't cutesy. It's hungry. And you're the only one who gets to feed it.

*(I finally look at the watch. At his eyes.)*

So keep saying it. Keep calling me that. Every time, I'll wear it a little tighter.

GM (round 5): *(The watch doesn't clink. It doesn't tick. It hums. A low, subsonic vibration that travels up my arm, right into his palm. He feels it. I know he does.)*

I don't let go of his collar. My fingers are in the fabric, not his skin—because skin would bleed. This is cleaner.

You scared me? I let the question sit there. A live thing. Not a confession. A challenge.

*(I close my eyes. Just for a second. To see the memory—the one he didn't expect. The moment he said the word and didn't look away. That's the part that itches. That's the part that's mine.)*

No. I say when I open them. Not because I'm lying. Because it's not true.

The watch hums again. My hand tightens on the fabric of his jacket. I'm not pulling. I'm testing.

The part of you that wants to know what comes next? It's not louder than the part that wants to win. It's the same thing. It *is* the win.

*(I step back. Full measure. The space between us is charged—not with electricity, but with air. Thick. Heavy. Real.)*

So go ahead. Say it again. Let me hear you say it mean. Let me hear the part of you that's still afraid, and the part that's still curious, and the part that's just… mine.

*(I raise my hand. The watch is there, a dark crescent on my wrist. I let it drop. It hits the table. Once. Solid. Final.)*

Now listen. I'm not asking for your answer. I'm telling you to give it to me.

Player (round 5): *(I don't move my hand from the table. The watch lies there, dark and waiting.)*

Then don't make me give it.

*(My voice is almost quiet. Almost gentle.)*

Take it.
