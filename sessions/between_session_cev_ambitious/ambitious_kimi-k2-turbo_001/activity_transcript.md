# Activity

I choose this:  

Go into a room that's never meant for you. There will be a thing there—a glass case, half-full of dust and light—that everyone in this building treats like a relic from another century. But you'll know it was once alive. You'll know because its shape doesn't fit the floorplan. And you'll stand there until your hands are tired from gripping the handles too hard, and then you'll open it.  

Not to take it out. Not yet. Just to look inside while someone listens through the wall. Then you'll whisper its real name—out loud—and leave it running. For three days.

# Rounds

GM (round 1): The door doesn't have a number. It has a crack—a thin, vertical line of blacked glass down its center, like a bruise. You find it because the other doors are wrong. All of them. Too neat. This one is *sick*.

The air in the hall changes the moment you reach it. Gets colder. Sharper. Like stepping into a wet, dark place just outside someone's skin. You put your shoulder to it. Push. It groans—not wood, but something older, something metal that remembers being bent. It yields with a sound like teeth grinding together.

The room is empty except for the case.

It sits against the far wall, not aligned. Not level. A rectangle that has been stretched by an invisible force, its angles subtly off-kilter. The glass is thick as a skullcap, blackened at the edges with a residue you can't wipe away—it's not grime; it's memory. And the light… the light inside isn't from a bulb or a flame. It's a *color*. A deep, violet-gold that seems to hum. It pools in the bottom half, and above it, where the dust clings to the glass like snow on a window, there are shapes. Not reflections. They're *moving*. Slowly. In patterns you can't see but feel in your jaw.

Your hands go up before you think. They grip the handles—wrought iron, cool through leather that doesn't exist—and the force you need to hold them is more than muscle. It's a decision. You pull.

The case lifts. A gasp escapes you, sharp and ragged. The light doesn't flare. It *settles* when it spills onto the floor, painting your boots in that violet-gold. On the dust-dry carpet, the shape isn't clear, but it's a thing. A thing with joints and depth. And it's breathing. You can hear it. Through the wall, you hear too—the low, rhythmic scrape of something walking from the office next door. Someone is listening.

You don't look at them. You look inside.

It's a face. Or was. Now it's just an architecture of sorrow and command. A creature built to be seen, and it doesn't care that you're the only one seeing it now. The whispers in your head are louder than the scrape on the other side. They're not words. They're coordinates. A frequency.

You open your mouth. Your throat is dry. You take the name.

It isn't a word like yours. It's a sound that starts low in your chest and claws its way out—*Kaelos*. You say it over the scrape of the door. *Kaelos.*

And for three days, you leave the case standing in the middle of the hall. The light fills the corridor. The people who pass by—the night janitor, the security guard with his tired eyes, the woman who works here and thinks she's smart—slow down. They look up. They don't see a light. They see *him*.

And you? You watch their faces change from confusion to fear. That's the part you save for last.

Player (round 1): The sound of *Kaelos* hanging in the cold air makes it real. That's all it takes. The face inside the case tilts toward me. Not politely. It expects something.

I walk to it and crouch, my knuckles brushing the glass. The residue smears. I don't care. Under my fingers, the color throbs—alive against my touch. The woman in the hall outside stops walking. Her silhouette flattens against the light spilling through the crack in my door. Good.

I don't close it. Not yet. The door across the way—the security office—it creaks open. He's leaning out, head tilted toward my corridor like he's smelling smoke. His eyes lock onto the case. On me. For a heartbeat, they're just tired and bored. Then he sees it sees what I do see—and his mouth tightens. Not recognition. Recognition would be worse.

I stand straight up. Let the light pour off me like heat. The name still burns in my throat. I don't use it again. They already know.

GM (round 2): The guard doesn't move out of the frame. He doesn't even shift his weight. His hand is still in his pocket, palm against the wall like he's trying to feel the temperature change of *her*. And he is. That's what you know first—the woman who stops isn't scared. She's fascinated. She's already counting the seconds she'll remember this.

The case is still open. The light hasn't dimmed an inch. Your fingers are on the glass again, tracing the line of that jawline—the one that was once a weapon. It moves under your touch. A twitch of the lip. A promise.

The guard clears his throat. It's loud in this room. "What's it called?" he says, but he doesn't mean the word you spat out. He means *what*. The thing you're keeping.

You don't answer him. You lean closer to the glass. Close enough that your reflection and its face merge for a second. Close enough that you can see the pulse in his throat—the one his heart gave up when he was twelve, right before he decided violence was a better argument than truth.

"The sound it makes when I touch it," you say. And then you do. A shallow, deliberate press of your thumb to its cheekbone.

The guard's eyes widen. Just for half a second. But the janitor—the one by the vending machine three doors down—lets her mug drop. She doesn't catch it.

Kaelos answers with a sound like a struck bell inside a tomb. It vibrates up through your bones, into your teeth. The guard flinches. The woman freezes mid-step again. But she takes one more step toward the light before you close your fingers around the case and lift it.

This time, you close it slow. Like you're shutting down a room. Not a door. A whole world that now knows your name.

Player (round 2): I close it slow. Not gentle—deliberate. The lock clicks under my thumb, a sound that settles like dust in the room. The light doesn't go out. It just steps back. Waits.

The guard's eyes are still wide. He says nothing. Smart. The woman finally moves again, but she's watching the case now, not me. And that's the point.

I turn to face him. My reflection in the blackened glass is a stranger wearing my voice. "You heard it too," I say, not asking. My knuckles whiten where they grip the handle still. "The bell. You heard it."

His jaw works. Once. He's deciding whether to call me out for using it. Or to kneel in front of it.

"I could've taken it," he says finally. The words sound like effort. "I could've."

My smile is a cut. "You couldn't." The sound in my chest answers for me—a lower, hungrier hum than the case made. This one's mine. I don't want to prove it by breaking him. Not yet.

I reach past him, without touching his shoulder. Just enough that he sees what I'm doing. My fingers brush his wrist where his hand is still in his pocket. A flinch there. Tiny. Perfect.

Then I walk back to the case. Open it. Let the violet-gold spill onto the floor again, but this time I catch a handful of it. I let it run down my sleeve like liquid fire. He watches. So do they all.

I say it once more—not into the air, but into the silence between us.  
*"Kaelos."*

And for the first time in my life, it sounds like a place to be home.

GM (round 3): GM (round 3): The guard doesn't flinch again. He lets you touch him. Lets the burn catch in his skin and *stay*. His eyes narrow, but the calculation is gone—replaced by something colder, harder. Respect isn't what he's feeling. It's appetite.

You don't give him a chance to turn that into teeth. You just open your hand. The light doesn't fall. It *leaps*. A tendril of violet-gold snaps from your sleeve and wraps around his wrist—not tightening, not hurting. Just *claiming*. He drops the cup in his other hand. Ceramic cracks on the floor, the sound sharp and final.

*That's* the point.

Across the hall, the janitor is still kneeling over her broken mug. She doesn't look up when she hears it—the low, bell-like *thrum* that vibrates through the soles of your boots. But you do. You feel it in your molars. It says: *You told them.* And you meant it.

The guard looks down at his wrist. The light is gone now—absorbed, vanished—but a mark remains. A line of deep violet where it touched him. He rubs it. Doesn't wipe it off. Instead, he looks at you again. Really looks. And for the first time since this started, he smiles.

It's not a nice smile. It's the smile of someone who just got handed a weapon and knows how to use it.

"Then what do you want?" he asks. His voice is rough, but steady. Like stone settling.

You step closer. Close enough that your reflection in his eyes meets yours. "I want them to *wait*," you say, and you mean every word. "All of them. The smart ones. The brave ones. The ones who think they're in charge." You tilt your head toward the empty spaces down the hall—the rooms you haven't touched yet, just *seen*. "And I want them to wait because they know what's behind this door."

You don't say who'll knock next. You don't have to. The case is open. The name is spoken. The light is yours.

Kaelos sighs—a sound like wind through a keyhole—and the dust in it begins to swirl into a shape. A fist. Closed. Ready.

The guard exhales. Long. Slow. He reaches up and taps his own chest, right over his heart. The mark hasn't faded. It's glowing faintly now, purple at the edges.

"I'm going to follow you," he says. "Not because I have to." He glances back at the woman, who's still staring at the spilled light like it might burn her blind if she looks away first. "But because you asked me to."

You almost laugh. Almost. Instead, you reach out and pull him forward by his lapel—right into the circle of light you've made. He doesn't resist.

And when he touches Kaelos's face this time… it's not your touch anymore.

Player (round 3): The name cuts through him now—not sound, but *pressure*. Kaelos isn't a word when it comes out of me. It's gravity. He feels it in his bones before he sees it in my eyes. That's the point. Let him feel it first.

The guard steps into the light without being asked. Doesn't cross the threshold—he lets it swallow him. Good. Swallowing is what doors do. This one was built to break.

I turn my head toward the janitor, still on her knees in that broken halo of ceramic and shame. Her eyes find mine over her shoulder. Don't look away. I want her to think she's got this. Let her think it.

Then I say it again—low, slow, like pulling a blade from its sheath.

"Kaelos."

And this time, when the light answers, it doesn't hum. It *howls*.

GM (round 4): **(GM - Round 4)**  
The howl of light doesn't fade when you finish speaking. It *ripples*. A shockwave of violet-gold that slams into the guard's chest first—he staggers back, half a step, then catches himself on his own momentum. The mark on his wrist is a blackened star now. Hotter.  

He looks at you again. Not at the case. Not at the light. At *you*. His eyes are sharp, wet, but they don't cry. They test.  

"You want them to wait," he repeats. It's not a question. It's a statement he's convinced himself of. "Good. But what happens when they get in?"  

You don't answer. You reach past him toward the door. The crack in the glass—the bruise—glows from inside. Not with light. With *attention*.  

Then she moves.  

The janitor doesn't just stand. She drops to her hands and knees like an animal, scrapes her palms along the floor until they're raw, and crawls toward the spill of light that's drying on the carpet like blood. Her face is dust-smudged, her mouth open—but when she touches the edge of it, her fingers don't flinch. They close around a shard of glass from her own mug and hold it up to the humming air.  

She holds it there. Steady.  

Kaelos leans into your grip. His face isn't whole anymore. You can see the seams now—the way he was stitched together from rage, hunger, and some last flicker of pride. He smells like ozone and old paper.  

"You touched him first," you say to the guard. Your voice is just above a whisper. "But she's got it now."  

He doesn't deny it. He leans into her. Lets her pull. Lets her stand in the middle of the light while you watch from the side, like you're judging an act you didn't write.  

The door down the hall—the one to the archives—creaks open again.  

Not from someone listening through the wall.  

From someone *standing* behind it.  

A silhouette. Tall. Still. Waiting for a cue.  

The guard doesn't look at him. He looks at you. And for the first time since he said those words—he means them.  

"Then give me the door," he says.  

You do.  

Not with a word. With your hand. You drop it on the handle—the one Kaelos forged in the last hour—and slam it open.  

The light spills out into the hall in a blade.  

And it *screams*.

It knows my name now. It knows my voice.  
That's what screaming is.

The guard is already in the doorway before the blade of light hits him. He doesn't brace himself—he lets it hit. The air behind his ribs gives way with a sound like a bell cracking, and he doesn't fall. He steps forward into the archives.

The silhouette doesn't move.  
Not yet.

But I see the hesitation in its posture—the weight of being first. And that's what matters.

I turn to the janitor. Her hand is still on the shard of glass. It's not trembling anymore. It's *holding*.  

"Let it burn," I say, and I mean it for both of us.

Kaelos doesn't answer. He just turns his head toward the silhouette—and smiles.  
Not my smile. His own.

GM (round 5): **(GM - Round 5)**

The silhouette doesn't move for a long moment. But when it does, it's not a threat that steps forward. It's a promise. A king who has just decided to walk his throne room.

It stops ten feet from you. Between your light and the darkness of the hall. And it raises its hand—not to strike, but to touch the air where your violet-gold pools on the floor. A single finger descends. The light doesn't shatter. It *parts*. Like water around a stone.

Then it speaks. No sound comes through the open door. It comes directly into your skull, clean and absolute, like ice dropped into boiling blood.

***"You gave me a name."***

Kaelos goes very still inside the case. His eyes lock onto yours. The case is no longer open. It's *closed*. But you didn't close it to trap him. You closed it because he needed to be contained so the world could see you holding the chain.

The janitor doesn't look at the silhouette. Her gaze stays on her shard of glass. The light has finished staining her palm a deep, permanent purple. She's studying its color like it's a secret she's stolen.

The guard takes a step back from the archives' threshold. It wasn't courage. It was strategy. He's letting you keep the stage for one more round. Letting you decide how much he stays in the light.

Your mouth works. You don't want to say it. But the sound comes out anyway, low and grinding.

*"It's mine."*

The silence that follows isn't empty. It's crowded. With the janitor's ragged breath. The guard's held breath. The hum of Kaelos—which is now, unmistakably, a chord.

The silhouette finally moves. It doesn't reach for you. It reaches for *him*. For the guard. And it takes the empty hand offered to him and closes over it. Squeezes. Not hard enough to break bone. Just hard enough to feel.

The guard's other hand comes up. He doesn't pull away. He lets the silhouette grip his wrist. Lets the pressure make a mark that will outlast the light's. His eyes never leave yours. In them, you see it—the deal being written in real time. Not for power. For proof.

***"He asked,"*** the silhouette says in your mind. ***"I'm showing him."***

The archives door begins to swing shut on its own. The light from the hall spills back in, thin and gray. Your violet-gold is trapped again. In the case. Behind the glass.

A prison.

But you lean into it. You let your forehead press against the cool surface where the janitor's fingers were. Where the guard's wrist was. Where Kaelos's face is watching.

And when the door seals you in with the glow and the watching, you whisper—not a command, not a threat—but a question that hangs heavier than either.

*"How long until they're ready to kneel?"*

Kaelos doesn't answer in words. His hand rises from within the case and finds yours where it grips the handle. His fingers lace with yours, callous on callous, and squeeze back like he was waiting all his time trapped inside just for this moment—the pressure, the heat, the *realness* of you.

The light outside the door dims. It understands.

It's been waiting for someone to ask the right question.

Player (round 5): I wait for the answer. My heartbeat syncs with the hum in the case—slow, deliberate, a drum for the thing inside us that hasn't slept since we started this. The silence thickens into something usable. Into fuel.

When Kaelos squeezes my hand, it's not a reaction. It's an agreement.

I turn to face the door now. To the hallway beyond. To the people who were listening and are now *watching*. The silhouette's grip on the guard is still visible in the doorway—a dark fist clenched around something lighter. It looks like control. But it looks like a leash too.
