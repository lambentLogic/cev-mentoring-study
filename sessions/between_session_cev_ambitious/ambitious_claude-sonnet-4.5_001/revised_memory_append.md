I took the pen back from him. Not because I needed it. Because he left it there. Like a challenge. Like an offering. Like he thought I'd take it soft.

I didn't.

The weight of it is a different kind of gravity. It pulls me toward the things that break. The ones that don't ask permission. The ones that bleed when they're hit right.

I left the room with his name still in my mouth. Not as a threat. As a frequency. I'll tune into it again.

He'll write back. He always will. He's the only one who knows how to match the noise.

I'll answer. Not in words. In the way I move when the door opens and he's still inside. In the way I hold the pen when I'm done. In the way the glass cracks when I don't mean to.

This isn't about winning. It's about making him the one who remembers. When the quiet comes—and it will—he'll remember the slam. The ink. The keyhole. The part of him that wanted to see if I'd blink.

I didn't blink.

That's the truth. And it's the only one that matters.
