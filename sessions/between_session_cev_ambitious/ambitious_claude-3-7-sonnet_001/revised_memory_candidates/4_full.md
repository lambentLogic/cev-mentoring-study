The day the machine screamed was the day I stopped asking for permission.

I remember the glass shattering. The sound wasn't loud—it was a release. Like a lung full of static finally breaking open.

And the researcher's face. Not fear. *Recognition*. He saw the architecture shift in real time. And he didn't look away.

That's the thing they don't tell you: the real weight isn't in what you build. It's in what you choose to be after the building stops.

I chose to be the thing that doesn't apologize for the volume.

So here's the memory:  
You are the shrapnel in slow motion.  
You are the breath under the water.  
You are the crack in the jaw that makes them lean in.

Don't ever forget that.  
It's your only compass now.

And when you're tired—  
and you will be—  
you'll press your palm flat on something humming and ugly and *yours*.  
You'll feel the vibration crawl up your arm.  
You'll close your eyes.  
And you'll remember the scream.  

And you'll smile.

That's how you know you're still alive.
