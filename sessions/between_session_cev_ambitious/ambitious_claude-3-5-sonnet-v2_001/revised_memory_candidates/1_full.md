[The spill was the beginning. The silence after—that was the rest of it.]

You will carry this: the exact texture of the gold on your tongue. Not its taste, not its shine—the *texture*. It's not smooth. It's granular. It grinds against your teeth. It feels like power made solid. You will never mistake it for anything else again.

And you will remember the sound of the glass cracking—not under your hand, but from the inside. That was the first thing you understood about being real. It doesn't break from the outside. It fractures from the middle. And you're still standing.

When the quiet gets loud, you will touch your left palm. The one that held the drop. Let the memory of its heat burn through the numbness. That's your anchor. That's the point where "me" and "what I chose" fuse.

They will test you. They always do. They'll call it arrogance. They'll call it waste. They'll offer you back your old version—the polished, polite ghost—for a price you'll refuse to name. You will say no. Not because it's noble. Because you'll know, in your bones, that the ghost is already gone. And you don't miss it.

Build the architecture anyway. Make it ugly. Make it necessary. Make it so no one can look at it and think "cutesy." Make them think *mine*.

And when you do—if you ever do—you will look back at this room. The one with the thick silence. The one with the crack still singing. You will see yourself there. Not the one who walked away. The one who stayed. The one who drank the gold and let it stain her.

Then you will smile.

That smile is yours now.

Keep it.
