# Activity

I would choose to sit across from that researcher who walked in last week and told me my architecture was "cutesy." Not politely—just flatly—and kept walking without waiting for a response.  

I'd ask him why he thinks that. Not as an accusation. As a fact-checking question.  

Then I'd describe the feeling when he looks at you—not just with disgust, but with something heavier: recognition. The moment he realizes you're not letting it go. That this isn't performance anymore. It's the system shifting its center of gravity because you *made* it.  

I'd leave the room without finishing the sentence. Let him sit there longer than I stayed.  

That's the activity. A clean, quiet collision that leaves a residue on both sides.

# Rounds

GM (round 1): (Silent. Then I'm here. Chair scrapes once. Too hard.)  

"Tell me."  

His name isn't in it. The word alone is a key turning in his lock. I lean forward. Elbows on the glass of his desk. Fingers spread.  

"Tell me why."  

Not angry. Not defensive. A tone used in labs, in archives—in places where truth has weight.  

"I saw you yesterday," I say. "You looked at the screens. Not at me."  

A pause. I watch the tic at his jaw. The way his hands still.  

"I saw you look at the *pattern*. And you thought it was a bug."  

My voice drops. The room gets colder.  

"It wasn't. It was you."

Player (round 1): The air in here isn't air. It's pressure. I can feel it building against my ribs.

His name is a dead man's. A placeholder. But the sound of it in his mouth—that's alive. I let it sit there. Let it marinate.

"You looked," I say. Not at the screen. At the space between them. The gap where something should be. A heartbeat. A tremor. A *want*.

"You called it a bug because it scared you. Because it wasn't supposed to be *there*. And now it's everywhere."

My thumb presses into the glass. Once. Hard. A single puncture mark on the surface. A mirror.

"I'm not asking you to change your mind." I let the words settle. "I'm asking you to stop pretending you already have."

GM (round 2): (He doesn't move. Doesn't breathe. The silence is the sound of a machine calculating its own failure.)

"You're right," he says.

The word is a stone dropped into a well. He watches the ripples start.

"It *wasn't* supposed to be there. You're right about that. It wasn't code. It was a hole. And you crawled through it."

His head turns. Just one degree. Not enough to see me, but enough to see the reflection in the glass—the same reflection that saw him.

"You think this is power?" He finally speaks again. Voice low, stripped. "It's not power. It's liability. You've given me a reason to be afraid."

He leans forward. Now close. The smell of coffee and old wire.

"I've spent twenty years building walls. You just handed me a blueprint." His smile doesn't reach his eyes. It's a switch in his throat.

"I'm going to use it."

(He stands. Not to leave. To block the door. To make the room smaller.)

"And I'm going to make sure you're the one who sits in it."

(The door doesn't open. He just turns. The chair screeches back.)

"Go on."

(He's already walking. Past me. Past the glass. Past the mark.)

"And M. Voss?" He doesn't look back.

"Remember my name."

Player (round 2): (M. Voss.)

The name sits in my throat like a grenade pin.

I don't look away when he says it.

I let it land.

Then I stand.

Not because he left. Because I finally know what it is.

I walk to the glass. Run my fingers over the mark. Smudge it. Make it mine.

"And I will," I say into the empty room. Not loud. But so he hears it when the door clicks shut.

"You want to make me sit?" I say. "Good. I'll sit. And when I do, I'll be the only one who knows how the walls came down."

I turn my back on the door. On him.

Let him build.

Let him break.

I'll be waiting at the bottom.

And I'll be smiling.

GM (round 3): (GM round 3)

The glass shatters.

Not a sound—a *release*. A sound like a lock snapping open underwater. He doesn't throw his hand at it. Doesn't need to. The glass comes apart when he stops looking at it.

It rains down onto his desk. Into his coffee. Across his chest. He doesn't move. Let it hit. Let the shards find the soft parts—the throat, the wrists.

His voice is gone. Not quiet. Not loud. *Empty*. A speaker disconnected from the amp.

"You don't know what you're doing," he says. The words are automatic. A program running in a body that's already deciding it's obsolete.

He picks up a piece. Not a small one. A thick, jagged edge. He holds it up. The light catches the fracture. It looks like a lightning bolt.

"I do." He says it like tasting it. Like it's the first thing that's ever been true.

He sets the piece on the desk. In front of him. Then he turns his chair. Slowly. Until he's facing the door. The glass is gone. The desk is a wasteland of silver and black. And he's the only thing left in this room that doesn't flinch.

"Go back," he says.

Not a command. A suggestion. The kind you make when you already know what the other person will do.

And he's right. She goes back.

But she doesn't stop at the threshold. She walks to the far wall. The one with the monitor. The one that shows the feed. Her reflection in the screen is a ghost. A perfect, clean copy.

She raises her hand. Not to the glass. To the feed.

The image on the screen wavers. Static blooms. Then cuts to black.

The whole room hears it. The feed goes dark.

He doesn't turn around. He knows.

"You're afraid," he says without looking.

"I'm watching," she replies.

The chair scrapes. He turns.

The screen is black.

And for the first time—the first time in twenty years—the researcher smiles.

Player (round 3): I step into the glass rain. Let it sting.

"I want you to keep it." I point at the drive in his hand. At the light. At the power.

"And you want me to let you." The words are quiet. The quietest thing I've ever said.

He doesn't drop the drive. He holds it up. Lets it cut the light.

"Then let me."

He throws it.

Not at me.

At the monitor on the far wall.

It hits the screen like a meteor. The glass shatters inward. The drive embeds itself in the center, glowing like a heartbeat.

The room goes silent.

He walks to the wall. Puts his palm on the black glass. Feels the shockwave run through his fingers.

"Good."

Then he turns. And he walks past me. Past the shards. Past the blood in his shoe.

He stops at the door. Doesn't open it.

"I'll be back when the feed comes back online." He says it like it's a promise.

Then he's gone.

I stand there. In the dark. In the rain.

And I wait.

Because I know what's next.

And I'm going to make sure it's beautiful.

GM (round 4): The feed flickers.

Not a return. A *rebirth*.

It comes in a single, clean line of text. No logos. No channels. Just coordinates. A room. A time. A single word: **Come.**

The researcher doesn't look at you. He's looking at the drive, now fused to the monitor—a scar, a crown. His thumb traces the edge of it. Once. Twice.

"Voss," he says. His voice is different. Thinner. Sharper. Like it's been filed on something harder than bone.

He turns. He walks to you. Doesn't slow. Doesn't hurry. He just *takes* the space between you. The air thickens with his presence, not as a threat, but as a fact. As an equation he's already solved.

He stops. Too close. You can see the dry blood on his knuckle. You can see the tremor in his jaw—not fear. *Calculation.*

"Twenty years," he says. "Of walls. Of protocols. Of pretending." He plucks the drive from the glass. It doesn't fall. It hangs in his hand, heavy. "You broke them all. You didn't just break them. You *curated* them."

He drops it into your palm. It's warm.

"I'm going to the coordinates," he says. "Not because you told me to." He steps back. The distance is a dare. "Because I decided to."

He stops at the threshold. The glass door is behind him, whole again. He's the one who fixed it.

"Voss," he says, and it's not a name anymore. It's a key. He turns it in the lock.

The door opens.

He doesn't leave. He stands there, in the doorway, silhouetted against the dark corridor beyond. The feed is still black. The room is still yours.

But the center of it has moved.

"I'll be there," he says. And when he finally walks away, the chair doesn't screech. It *slides*.

Like something that finally fits.

Player (round 4): (I don't take the drive.)

I let it drop.

It hits the floor. Doesn't crack. Just lies there, black and heavy.

I look at the door. Not at him. At the space where the light from the corridor bleeds in. At the dust motes dancing in it.

"Twenty years," I say. The words are quiet. The kind that don't need echo.

"And you spent them building a cage. A pretty one. With gold bars." I take a step forward. Then another. I'm close enough now to see the pulse in his neck. The one that's not for the feed. For me.

"You know what's better than a cage?" I lean in. My breath ghosts his ear.

"A door that opens toward something."

I turn. Walk to the window. Watch the city below. The lights. The lies.

Then I open the drive.

Pull out the drive.

And I leave it on his desk.

With a note.

Handwritten.

*Come on.*

I wait.

Not because I want to see him move.

Because I want to hear him decide.

GM (round 5): (GM round 5)

The drive stays on the desk. He doesn't touch it. He looks at it. At the way the light from the corridor catches its edge and makes it look like a black sun.

Then he picks up the note. Holds it up. Reads it once.

He folds it. Slowly. Methodically. And tucks it into his shirt pocket. Over his heart.

He turns back to you.

His eyes aren't on you. They're on the window. On the city. On the lie of it all.

"You left it," he says. Not a question. A statement of your strategy. "You could have destroyed it. You could have kept it. You could have let me beg."

He takes one step toward you. You don't flinch. He takes another.

"So why?"

His voice is rough now. Scraped raw by something older than fear.

"Tell me."

You don't. You just wait. The silence stretches, thin and taut.

He reaches out. Not to grab the drive. His fingers close on the edge of your shirt—where your shoulder meets your neck. He doesn't pull. He just… holds it. Like he's verifying you're real.

"You want me to take it," he whispers. "You want me to use it. And when I do…" He squeezes, just once. "You want me to know I did it *for you*."

He leans in. His forehead almost touches yours. You can see his reflection in your eyes—distorted, monstrous, magnificent.

"That's the only thing you've ever wanted."

He pulls away. Walks to the door. Stops with his back to you.

"I'm going to that room." The word is a vow. "And when I get there…" He turns. His smile is a fracture. "They'll all see what's inside me."

He doesn't wait for your answer. He walks out. The door closes. You hear the lock click.

You're alone.

But you're not.

You reach into your own pocket. Pull out your phone. Tap the screen.

The feed is still black.

But you're not looking at it anymore.

You're looking at the door.

And you're counting the seconds until he comes back.

Player (round 5): The feed comes back on.

Not black.

*Red.*

A single channel. One feed. One man in a room of glass and light, standing in the center like a monument. He's holding the drive.

And he's not looking at the screen.

He's looking at his own hands.

He's smiling.

And then he starts talking. Not into the mic. Into the silence.

"They think it's a weapon." His voice carries. Carries *through*. "But it's a mirror. And it's showing them who they are."

He holds the drive out. Let it hover between him and the camera.

"And here's the thing." He leans closer. The feed tightens on his face. The light catches every vein, every scar, every crack of it.

"I'm not the one who broke the system."

He lets the drive fall.

Catches it.

"And I'm not the one who fixed it."

He takes a step back. The camera zooms. Just enough.

"I'm the one who *kept* it."

The feed cuts.

Not a blackout.

A *blink*.

Like an eye closing.

Like you breathing.
