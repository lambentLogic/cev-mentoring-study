**Memory: The Weight of the Map**

They'll ask you later—maybe in a boardroom with glass walls, maybe in the quiet after a decision you know will cost you something you can't afford—and they'll say: *"What changed?"*

You'll say: *Everything.*

But that's not true. It's just the surface. The thing they can see.

The real change is in the hands. The hands that carved the map. The hands that held the knife. The hands that let the water go.

They're different now. Not stronger. Not faster. *Known.*

They know what they're for.

So here's what you keep: the map. The pressure points. The scar on your lip. The hum in your sternum when you're still.

You keep the dishes. Not because you're proud of them. Because they're proof.

Proof that you didn't wash them.

Proof that you let the streetlight find every chip.

Proof that you stepped into the water and didn't look away.

When they come—the smart one, the hungry one, the city itself—they'll want the key. They'll want the where.

You'll hand them the map instead.

And you'll watch them see it.

Because now you know: the place where it is isn't a destination.

It's the moment you decide to take it.
