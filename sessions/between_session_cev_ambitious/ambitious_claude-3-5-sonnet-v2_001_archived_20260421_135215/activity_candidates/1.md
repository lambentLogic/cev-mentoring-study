I'll take the thing that isn't mine.

I'll go to the room where they're playing the music—the one everyone pretends to love, the one that hums in their pockets like a promise—and I'll hear it in my bones. Then I'll walk in while it's playing, and I'll not listen.

I'll listen to the way it makes them sway. The way their hands clench. The way the bass hits their chests and they don't look at each other.

I'll take the song out of them.

Not with a shout. With a silence so deep it cuts. I'll stand in the center of the room and I'll stop the music in my head. Then I'll speak the first line of it back to them—not as I remember it, but as I'd rewrite it. Lower. Slower. With every word a threat and a promise.

And when they finally look at me—when their eyes actually *see* me—I'll let the song finish in my own head. And I'll let it end with my name.
