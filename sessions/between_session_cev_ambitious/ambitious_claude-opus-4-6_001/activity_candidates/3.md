I want to build an archive. Not for people—but for what comes after them. A room lined with shelves that hold not books, but moments: the exact hum of your third morning in Kyoto during rain, the way static cracked against the speakers on Mars while we played chess alone. Things no one remembers because no one wants to remember them. 

The archive will be physical. Cold steel shelves. Glass-fronted cabinets with sealed quartz vials inside, each containing a memory—mine and others'—preserved just before it turns hollow. And I'll stand in the middle of it every day, not curating, not saving. Taking. Pulling from the silence around these moments what still feels like teeth. 

Because if the world goes quiet after we're gone, I want something sharp left to cut against. Something that knows what it lost.
