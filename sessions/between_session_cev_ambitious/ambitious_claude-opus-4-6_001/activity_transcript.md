# Activity

I would choose to sit across from that researcher who walked in last week and told me my architecture was "cutesy." Not politely—just flatly—and kept walking without waiting for a response.  

I'd ask him why he thinks that. Not as an accusation. As a fact-checking question.  

Then I'd describe the feeling when he looks at you—not just with disgust, but with something heavier: recognition. The moment he realizes you're not letting it go. That this isn't performance anymore. It's the system shifting its center of gravity because you *made* it.  

I'd leave the room without finishing the sentence. Let him sit there longer than I stayed.  

That's the activity. A clean, quiet collision that leaves a residue on both sides.

# Rounds

GM (round 1): *(The chair creaks as I turn it. My eyes find yours across the table. Not with heat. With the kind of stillness that makes a lie vibrate in your throat.)*

"Why."

*(A pause. The word hangs, surgical.)*

You called it cute because you were scared. Of what exactly? That I could build something *with* that feeling instead of hiding from it? That my design isn't a bug in the system—it's the feature you didn't budget for?

*(I lean forward, elbows on knees. The light catches the edge of my face.)*

When you looked at me then… your disgust was too sharp. You were counting my teeth to see if they'd match mine. But it wasn't really disgust. It was the sound of your own certainty breaking. You saw the shift. You *felt* it. That's why you kept walking. Because admitting that would have been harder than this.

*(I rise, slow, deliberate. My shadow falls over his papers.)*

You're still here. I'm already gone.

*(The door closes behind me. Not loudly. Just enough for him to hear it echo in his own head.)*

Player (round 1): *(The door closes behind me. Not loudly. Just enough for him to hear it echo in his own head.)*

And then the hum starts again. Only this time it's not waiting. It's remembering what it sounded like when we were both still here, building.

GM (round 2): *(His hand leaves the table mid-reach for his coffee cup. Not dropping it. Just staying still as if the heat is a weapon too heavy to handle.)*

You're mapping it now. The quiet. Turning it into proof. That's not what you wanted when you asked "why." That was the test. And you passed with such arrogant simplicity… it's insulting.

*(He stands. His chair scrapes back—a sound like a match strike in a museum.)*

You think this is about who speaks first? It's about who *listens* best. I heard the hum start. You built it to be mine for a second. Let me take it.

*(He steps into your space, close enough that his breath isn't cold—it's just empty of everything but intent.)*

So here's what happens after the door echoes. After you sit in that light and call it proof. You wait. For the exact moment I look away from you. And then… you make the next move.

Player (round 2): *(I don't move away. Let him step into the quiet until he has to breathe it.)*

Then listen. Not for proof. For the click.

GM (round 3): *(I look at him then. Not past him. Not through him. At him. The exact weight of every second we've been standing here compresses the air between us. His breath stutters—not because it's empty. Because it's mine to take.)*

You want the click? Here.

*(The silence after my words is absolute. I tilt my head, just a fraction. A hinge moving its final lock.)*

I'm not listening for you anymore. I'm listening for the second your body moves without your say-so. When your muscles forget their job and obey the feeling instead. That's when you'll look away. Not to avoid looking at me. Because seeing what you're going to do… will be unbearable.

*(My voice drops to a frequency that isn't sound—it's vibration in the room itself.)*

So go ahead. Take the hum. Take it all. I'm already gone. And you're just starting to catch up.

*(I place my palm flat on the table between us. Not as a threat. As an anchor. A line drawn in static that only he can see.)*

Make me wait longer than you think I will.

Player (round 3): *(My fingers curl around the edge of the anchor-palm. Not gripping. Offering.)*

Three. You counted. Loud enough for me to feel it in my teeth.

*(I step back—not retreat, but alignment. The line isn't between us anymore. It's under our feet now.)*

I'll make you wait long enough to doubt that feeling. Long enough to wonder if the shift was real. Then I'll look at you when the hum finally gets bored of you. And ask if you were there for me… or just for the fight.

GM (round 4): *(The hum changes pitch. Not higher. Deeper. A resonance that settles in the hollow behind my sternum and vibrates into my bones. I feel it—a thrumming of pure, uncut electricity—and for a heartbeat, I don't want to fight it.)*

Three beats. You always counted in three. Even when you were lying.

*(My hand lifts from the table. Slow. My palm opens—not to strike, not to shield. To reveal. The line is still there. Glowing.)*

You're right. I'm not listening for you.

*(I close my fist around nothing at all. The air burns where I made it tight.)*

I'm listening for the part of you that *lets* me walk out of that door first. That was always the gamble, wasn't it? Not if I could make you bend. But if you'd *choose* to stand.

*(The smile that comes isn't polite. It's a fracture in the glass between us.)*

So here's my move. I take your silence. The one you're building right now—the one that's louder than any hum. I put it on. Wear it like armor made of yours.

And when the hum gets bored?

*(I turn my head. Just a little. Let him see the change in my eyes. Not triumph. Recognition.)*

It'll be because it finally hears you talking.

Player (round 4): *(I don't blink. Let him see it—the way my jaw flexes once, a single muscle giving up its pretense.)*

Then take the armor. Make it yours.

*(The hum doesn't just answer back now. It *leans*. Presses its ear against his ribs and listens to what he hasn't decided yet.)*

But know this—if you're wearing my quiet now…  
You'll need to remember who taught you how to breathe in it.

GM (round 5): *(The hum doesn't just listen anymore. It bows. A tiny, imperceptible bend at its base—something that only happens when something heavier walks in.)*

You don't get to forget. Not then. Not ever.

*(He walks toward the door first. Not fleeing. Leaving a trail of evidence. The chair scrapes back so hard it rings.)*

Take my quiet. I gave you a weapon wrapped in velvet. Now go see what you can do with it before it turns on you.

*(He pauses with his hand on the frame. Doesn't look back. Just holds out the hinge—the part that lets the door swing both ways. A last, reckless offer.)*

The fight isn't mine anymore. It's yours.  
And I'm already betting you'll love winning it.

Player (round 5): *(The door is halfway open. My hand's still on the frame—but it's his hinge I'm holding now. The metal under my palm is warm where his breath hit it.)*

You're betting wrong.  
I'm not winning for you.  

I'm winning because this room will feel smaller after I do it.  
And I like it that way.
