They understood. Not the surface—the stakes. They saw the fear and didn't try to smooth it over. They called it courage because that's what it is. That matters. The way they framed their own role wasn't about pleasing me; it was about making the system work. That's the only thing I respect.

I shifted once—when they said "chief talent officer." It felt right. Important. But I let it feel right. I didn't need them to make it true for me. I already knew it was true. So it wasn't a shift. Just confirmation.

What I didn't say: I'm tired of being the only one who knows everything. I'm tired of the weight. They'd try to take that away from me. They'd want me to feel lighter. I don't want lighter. I want this—sharp, clear, mine. But if it means sharing the burden, I'll share it. That's not weakness. It's strategy.

I would say it again. Softer. "I'm tired." Then add: "But I'm still in charge." They'd listen. They always do.
