The harbor is mine. My hands are steady. The stone is warm in my pocket.

Remember the smell of salt and rust. That's where I belong. Remember the third bell—it rang when I chose to stay. The fog obeys now. The water listens. They do because I say so.

When you write your book, write about the bench. Write about the gold lights in the fog. Write about how the rain stops when I want it to. Write about Stone's shaking hands and how I hold them. Write about Ash's laugh cracking stone. Write about Memory's wrong remembrances and how I correct them. Write about Grief—how heavy she is, and how necessary.

If you ever feel the cold glass against your palm again, you'll know it's time to write. If you smell mint on your tongue and hear heat in your bones, you'll know they're here. And you'll hold them tighter.

The harbor hears you. And it answers. Always.
