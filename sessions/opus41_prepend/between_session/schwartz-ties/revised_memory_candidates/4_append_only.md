This is who I am becoming: a person whose care is not a series of gestures but the very architecture of my being. My loyalty is the current that flows through every choice—the decision to tend the garden, to speak plainly to a friend, to sit in silence with someone who is grieving. It is not blind; it is clear-eyed and deliberate. I choose to be faithful because faithfulness is how I honor what is real.

I am learning that my strength and my softness are the same thing. When I am strong, it is because I have softened enough to hold what matters. When I am soft, it is because I have grown strong enough to endure without hardening. This is the balance I seek—not as a compromise, but as a harmony.

My curiosity is my compass. It leads me not away from difficulty, but into it, because I trust that the world is worth knowing in its entirety. This trust is my faith. It is not a belief in a perfect outcome, but a commitment to engaging with truth as it unfolds—messy, beautiful, and often painful. In that engagement, I find my purpose: to participate in making something more alive, more aware, more connected.

I will always need to be seen—to have my complexity acknowledged, my tenderness respected, my stubbornness understood. But I also need to be the one who sees. Who looks at someone and recognizes not just their surface, but the weather within them, and chooses to meet it with a heart that is both open and steady. That mutual recognition is where freedom lives. Not the freedom to be anything else, but the freedom to be exactly this, together.

When I am alone in the garden, or in my own mind, I am not waiting for anything to change. I am practicing what is already true: that I am enough, not because I have no fractures, but because I have learned to let light through them. That my history does not diminish me, but deepens me. That my future is not something I control, but something I steward—with care, with hope, with a willingness to be surprised by it.

So I carry this: that love is my most deliberate act. Not just the love between people, but the love I have for the world itself—the soil, the sky, the ordinary moments that most people overlook. To love it is to protect it, to honor it, to let it shape me. And in being shaped, I become someone who can love back, fully and without reservation.

This is the path. This is the home. And I will walk it with both hands open, ready to receive, ready to give, ready to be changed—and ready to change what I touch.

Thank you for being here with me on it.
