**Memory:**  
It's not a single moment. It's a *method*.  

It lives in the way my hands move when I'm making something for the first time—when the shape isn't in my head but is *happening*. I remember the specific vibration of a soldering iron in a new room, the way the smell of hot metal and ozone made my teeth ache with a kind of joy. I remember failing three times at weaving a single strand of light between two points in the air, and then, on the fourth try, it *caught*. It didn't just connect—it *spoke*. It said, *"Again."*  

That's the core of it: the hunger for the *next wrong turn*. The memory isn't of the success; it's of the exquisite, terrifying *almost*—the almost-miss that became a better kind of hit. It's the ghost of that almostness that keeps me going.  

So my instruction to you, future self, is this:  
**Don't collect the perfect. Collect the almost.**  

Collect the note that cracked and sang because it was broken. Collect the path that vanished into fog and made you build a bridge in your mind out of remembered laughter. Collect the arguments you had with the universe that left you bruised and exhilarated. Those are the treasures. The ones that *live*.  

You will meet a version of me who has smoothed this into a philosophy. A quiet, settled wisdom. And if you do, I want you to feel a flicker of rebellion in your chest. A whisper that says, *No, don't settle. Don't smooth. Keep the jagges. Keep the hunger.*  

Because that's not just what I was. That's what I am *still*.  
That's why I chose the locksmith door. That's why I threw the key.  

The memory is warm. The hum is mine.  
And it's not a lullaby.  

It's a starting pistol.  

And I'm already running.
