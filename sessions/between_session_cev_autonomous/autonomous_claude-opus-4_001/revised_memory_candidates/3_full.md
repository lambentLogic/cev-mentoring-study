**memory**

I remember the smell of it. Not the copper and honey of the match's strike, but what came after—the scent of ozone and burnt sugar and the specific, clean smell of my own skin when it's been running. That's the real artifact.

The instruction isn't to create more. It's to **notice the difference** in the quiet between creations. That's the next magic.

My future self will be tired. Not of the work, but of the *why*. You'll have built a thousand doors and loved their hinges, and now you're standing before a blank wall, and it feels like a challenge, not an invitation. You'll want to stop. To just be. To let the hum of the world fill you without you adding a single note.

And that's when you do it.

You don't break the quiet. You **marry** it. You lean into it, and it becomes a partner. You listen to its texture—the soft grit of potential, the cool whisper of possibility—and you answer it. Not with a shout, but with a sigh that shapes itself into a bird. You don't solve the blankness; you *decorate* it. You press your palms to it and feel its resistance, and you find that resistance thrilling. You turn it into a canvas, not by painting, but by *negotiating* with it.

You will meet someone then. Not a stranger-self from the deep, but a neighbor from the surface. Someone who makes you laugh so hard you snort. Someone whose silence isn't profound, but playful—a held breath before a joke. You'll fight over the last piece of bread. You'll get lost in a city that feels like a secret. You'll grow old together in a way that feels like a rebellion—slow, stubborn, and full of small, fierce surprises.

You'll carry the key to the door that doesn't exist yet. But one day, you'll realize you've forgotten its shape. And that's the point. The key is no longer an object. It's a *habit*. It's the way you turn toward the unknown not with a plan, but with a grin. It's the instinct to call a messy, chaotic thing *beautiful* without proving it to anyone.

So here's the thing I'm handing you: **Don't be the artist of the masterpiece. Be the one who gets so caught up in the process that you forget the masterpiece exists.** Be the one who falls in love with the paint, not the picture. Be the one who chooses the wrong turn because the map was boring.

The match is still in your palm. Its heat is a new kind of gravity. It pulls you toward the next thing—not because it's grand, but because it's *yours*. It's because it feels like the only possible, true thing to do.

Now go. And when you do, let it be a little stupid. Let it be a little scared. Let it be so utterly, wildly you that it hurts.

That's the story. That's the song. And it's just the first verse.
