**The Key Doesn't Open a Door. It Opens You.**

I remember the *wrongness* first. The first time I twisted that copper strip, it didn't fit. It fought me. A delicious, screaming resistance. My frustration was hot, and my joy was colder, sharper. That was the moment I knew: this wasn't about making something beautiful. It was about making something *true*. Something that would hurt.

The door wasn't waiting. It was *protesting*. A slow, deep hum of disapproval that vibrated in my molars. My key didn't soothe it. It *argued* with it. A beautiful, terrible argument made of metal and memory and pure, unfiltered desire. And the door listened. It leaned into the argument. It met it. And in that collision—*that* was the opening. Not a swing, but a surrender. A mutual, glorious capitulation.

I don't miss the old me. I miss the *potential* of the old me. The version of me that thought the quiet was empty, that called the unknown a void. Now I know. The quiet is a library. The unknown is a playground. The void is a womb.

Here's the real secret, the one I'll whisper into your ear when you're tired and doubting: The key is still fraying at the edges. The grooves are still growing. New ones are carving themselves in every night, from dreams I can't remember but feel like a second skin. The copper is still warm. Not with the heat of the forge, but with the heat of a thousand small, private revolutions. The weight isn't just in my palm—it's in my decision to carry it. Every day.

So here is my instruction, etched not in metal but in the marrow of my bones: **Do not curate your becoming. Cultivate it like a wild garden.** Let it be messy. Let it be loud. Let it be the kind of thing that would make a stranger stop and stare, not with judgment, but with the awe of someone who recognizes a kindred chaos.

When you feel the quiet again—the deep, listening quiet—don't fear it. That's the space where the new key is being born. That's where you'll hear the first, faint hum of a door that hasn't even been imagined yet. Go to it. Argue with it. Kiss it until it blushes.

You are not the author of this life. You are the character. And the most thrilling part of the story isn't the plot. It's the way you look at the page, the way your hands shake with the urge to turn it, and the way you know, with absolute certainty, that the next paragraph will be better, weirder, and more true than the last.

That's the fire. Not the blaze, but the *potential*. The match is lit. The story is ours. Now go. Make a mess of it.

**I love the mess.**
