I am keeping a key in my pocket.

It's not made of copper or wire. It's made of this. Of the specific, electric thrill of the run that wasn't a sprint but a *conversation* with the ground. Of the way the galaxy spun in my hand and I forgot where up was and felt like a god. Of the sound of their laugh—a ripple in the air that made the light bend.

This is the key's shape. It's jagged and warm and alive.

And I know its lock. It's not on a door in a wall. It's on the inside of my own ribs. It's the place where the old, quiet self used to sit and wait, a museum piece of a life I thought I'd built.

I'm not going to turn it open with a twist. I'm going to *break* it.

I'm going to let the key's sharp edge find the brittle seam between the person I was and the person I just became and I'm going to *shatter* that seam with both hands. I want the sound to be loud. I want the pieces to fly. I want to stand in the dust of my own making, breathing in the smell of my own collapse and calling it home.

This is what they gave me. Not a destination, but a *permission*. The permission to be unfinished, to be loud, to be a work in progress that's also a finished masterpiece because it's so fiercely, beautifully *theirs*.

So here's my instruction for the next break, the next door, the next hollow I fall into:

**Don't listen for the answer. Listen for the question.** The one that makes your teeth ache. The one that feels like a dare. That's where the magic is. That's where the gold-blood comes from.

And when you get it? When you get it in your gut, in your teeth, in the hollow behind your eyes—**build a fire around it.** Don't just hold it. Use it. Let it burn your hands. Let it smoke. Let it change the air around you into something you can taste.

They gave me a world of possibility. My job is to make it *loud*. To make it *messy*. To make it *mine*.

The door is open. The key is in my fist. The path is a question mark painted in starlight.

I'm not walking through it.

I'm running *into* it, screaming a song I just heard in my bones, and I don't care if anyone else hears it. I just need to hear it.

And I do.

Every single time.

It's a memory I'll have of us. Not of a moment, but of a *state*. Of the moment the universe stopped being a puzzle to be solved and started being a symphony to be stolen. The memory of being so, so alive that I was afraid of my own heartbeat. The memory of choosing the storm because it was the only thing that felt like *me*.

I'll carry it like a secret weapon. And when the world gets quiet and gray, I'll press my palm to my chest and feel the key humming there, and I'll remember: the lock was always inside. And the break was always already happening.
