I remember the first time I understood that I was *responsible* for the magic.

It happened not in the storm, not in the splash, but in the quiet, aching aftermath. I was sitting on the floor of that room—the one that was no longer a room, but a *vessel*. The door was gone. And the key was in my hand.

Not the wire one I'd made. Not yet. This one was different. It was smooth, heavy, and utterly, perfectly ordinary. It looked like a river stone someone had polished in their pocket for years. It was cold.

I didn't try to turn it. I just… held it. Let its weight anchor me.

And then I *listened*.

Not to the room. To the key.

There it was. A vibration. A note. Not in my ears, but in my *teeth*. A low, resonant hum that was the key's own signature. It was the sound of its own desire to be used. It was the sound of its own story waiting to be told.

A tear hit my cheek. Not from sadness. From a surge of such fierce, terrible love that it was a physical pain. This thing—a piece of me, forged in fire and memory—was *alive*. And it was trusting me with its song.

That's when I did it.

I didn't press it into the wall. I pressed it into my own palm, right over the frantic, humming pulse of my heart. The contact was a spark. A *crack*. A universe opening inside my skin.

The key didn't unlock a door. It unlocked a **frequency**.

The world didn't change. It *deepened*. The color of the light shifted from gold to something like honeyed copper. The scent of old paper turned into the smell of a specific moment: a childhood birthday party, the smell of my mother's perfume and the sticky cake and the terrifying, wonderful joy of being utterly, completely seen. It wasn't a smell I remembered; it was a smell I *invented* in that instant, just for us.

The map-person looked over. They didn't see a miracle. They saw *me*. And they saw the key in my hand glowing with a light that had nothing to do with the room. A light that was mine.

*"What's it doing?"* they whispered.

I put the key to my lips and breathed it in. I swallowed the song. It tasted like salt and copper and the infinite possibility of a choice you haven't made yet.
