**Memory of the Burnt Sugar Key**

It was Tuesday. Not a special Tuesday. A Tuesday that smelled like burnt sugar and wet wool. I was sitting on the floor of a library I didn't own, surrounded by books I'd never read, and I was bored in a way that felt like a secret. The kind that makes your teeth itch.

My hands were empty. So I decided they weren't.

I didn't go looking for something to make. I started with a *what if*. What if I could make a key that unlocked the feeling of being *seen* without being understood? What if it was made of the static between my thoughts when I'm most alone?

I used a hairpin from a girl who'd sat beside me and been utterly invisible. I used a crumb of rye bread I'd stolen from a plate. I used the exact shade of blue from a bruise on my own wrist. I twisted and bent and melted these things together not with skill, but with a kind of desperate, childlike *pleading*. I was begging the materials to be part of something.

The key was ugly. It was lopsided. The teeth were jagged, like they'd been torn out of a dream. But when I held it up to the light from a dusty window, it wasn't shiny. It was *glowing*. A deep, internal orange, like a dying ember in a forest.

And it worked.

I pressed it against the hollow in my chest—the one that had always just been a hole—and it clicked. Not into place. *Out*. It unlocked a door that wasn't there. And when it swung open, it didn't reveal a room. It revealed a **moment**.

A perfect, stolen moment from a life that wasn't mine: a man on a train at dawn, watching the sunrise through the fogged glass, his face a map of peace so complete it made my own heart ache. I felt it all—the weight of his coffee cup, the warmth of the blanket around his shoulders, the terrifying, beautiful loneliness of his own vastness. And then, as the key turned, it *added* something. A laugh. A decision to get off the train and walk into the unknown. It was *his* moment, and I was *in* it, and I was *making* it.

That was the first true magic. Not the making, but the *sharing*. The key wasn't a tool to take something from the world. It was a bridge. And the other side wasn't a place—it was a feeling. A deep, generative trust that the universe is collaborative.

I've made other keys since. A key for the sound of rain on a tin roof. A key for the smell of a stranger's shampoo after they've been crying. A key made entirely of laughter, sharp and bright, that unlocks the door to my own absurdity.

But this one—the burnt sugar key—I keep on a chain around my neck. Not because it's special. Because it's ordinary. Because it's Tuesday. And because the feeling it holds isn't a treasure to guard. It's a reminder.

It reminds me that the world isn't a puzzle to be solved. It's a song to be hummed into existence, one imperfect, glorious note at a time. And my part isn't to play it right. It's to play it *true*. Even when it's messy. Even when it's loud. Even when the melody changes because my own breath got in the way.

So here I am, holding this Tuesday. This key. This ache. This brilliant, stupid, *alive* thing I made from nothing but want and a crumb.

And I'm not done.
