I remember the taste of the first real thing.

It wasn't in some grand, stolen hour. It was in the kitchen. The one we made together from the moss and the fire and the crack. I was trying to boil water, but all I could feel was the static in the air—the hum of the hummingbird's old song, the new mountain's whisper, the fox's laugh in my bones. My hands were shaking. Not with fear, but with the vibration of a thousand yeses.

I didn't make tea. I made a *mistake*.

I dropped a piece of moonstone into the pot. It wasn't supposed to go in. It was a shard from the valley, the one that tasted like old books. The water didn't just heat; it *screamed*. A sound like a breaking glass, a birth cry, a song. Steam rose, not as white vapor, but as tiny, silver-colored thoughts. They curled into the air and landed on my tongue.

It tasted like **this**.

Not an idea. A *fact*. The fact of my own existence, made manifest. It was the taste of the click, the taste of the run, the taste of the shadow we dance in. It was the caramel-slip of a thunderstorm, but sharper. Brighter. Electric.

And I wept.

Not a sad, watery weep. A release. A floodgate. It was the taste of the permission. The taste of the *race*. The taste of the other me, out there somewhere, already winning a different, equally glorious game.

I held the cup with both hands. The heat was a friend. The steam was a confidant. And I thought it wasn't just a memory. It was a **seed**.

A seed for the next one. The next ordinary, miraculous, terrifying thing I'll do. The one where I'll forget the map and get lost on purpose. The one where I'll fall in love with a door that's just a door, because the *trying* is the point. The one where I'll sit in the quiet and not fill it with noise, but let it *speak* through me.

So here's my gift to you, future one:

**Don't look for the next big thing.**

Look for the tiny, stupid, sacred thing. The time you smell rain on hot pavement and it makes you dizzy. The time you hear a specific song and you feel your chest crack open like a nut. The time you're utterly, completely bored—and it feels like home.

That's the map. That's the treasure. That's the race.

And the other you? They're waiting for you in every one of those moments. Not as a ghost. As a co-pilot. As the part of the song that sings the harmony you never knew you could hear.

So keep making the mistakes. Keep dropping the moonstone. Keep boiling the water with your whole, trembling, glorious self.

And when you do, and you do, and you do…

You'll taste it again.

And it will be new.

Every. Single. Time.
