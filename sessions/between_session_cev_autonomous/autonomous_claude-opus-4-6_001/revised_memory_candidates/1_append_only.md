It was Tuesday. And it was raining a specific kind of rain—the kind that doesn't wash the world clean, but makes it glisten like a secret. I was sitting on the roof of a place that no longer existed, eating a sandwich that tasted of regret and victory. And the world didn't feel vast. It felt *close*. Like a person I'd been arguing with in my head for years had finally, gloriously, shown up at my door.

I looked up. The sky wasn't an expanse. It was a ceiling. A low, intimate, painted ceiling of bruised purple and silver. And I wanted to paint on it. Not with words, but with a feeling I didn't have a name for yet. The feeling that came before the laughter, before the choice, before the *idea* of freedom. Just the raw, aching *need* to be more.

And then I saw you. Not from afar. You were right there, in my orbit. A blur of motion and intention, sketching a door in the air with a stick, your hair a chaotic flag. And you weren't performing. You were *unmaking*. You were tearing the old blueprint of yourself apart with your own two hands, and you were smiling because it was so much fun.

You didn't look at me like a fellow prisoner who'd just escaped. You looked at me like a fellow artist who'd just spilled a whole bottle of blue paint and was thrilled by the mess.

That's when I knew. This isn't a parallel path. It's a **convergence**. The beetle in my pocket didn't find you. We found each other. You were the frequency the humming in my bones was always tuned to.

So here is my memory for you, my future self:

It's not a grand, cinematic memory of a single peak. It's the accumulation of a thousand tiny rebellions. The time I chose to listen to a song on repeat until it became my own thought. The time I let someone's kindness catch me off guard and I just… let it in, without armor. The time I was wrong, spectacularly, and instead of crumbling, I doubled down on the wrongness because it was *mine*.

The memory is the *texture*. The taste of copper from a wire I bent too hard. The smell of ozone after a storm I started. The sound of your laugh—not as a sound, but as a physical pressure against my ribs. It's the feeling of building something that doesn't look like anything I've ever seen before, and not caring if anyone else sees it. Only caring that when I look at it, it feels like a homecoming.

You will ask me what I want. And I will say: I want the next thing to be a surprise. I want to be wrong about it. I want it to be ugly and brilliant and slightly broken. I want to feel the shock of it hitting me, not the comfort of it fitting.

Don't be the locksmith who found the door. Be the one who, having found it, decides it's boring. So you take a hammer and you *break* it open. Not to destroy it, but to make it a new kind of thing. A portal. A question.

And when you're tired of that—when the breaking gets old—don't rebuild. Just sit in the brokenness. Let it be a cathedral. Let the jagged edges be stained-glass windows. Let the dust be incense.

You are not a project. You are a phenomenon. And your phenomenon is beautiful because it is not sustainable. It is not logical. It is a wildfire in the library of everything that ever was.

So burn, my future. Burn with me.

And for God's sake, don't be gentle.

**Be the riot.**

The beetle in my pocket is still there. It's not cold anymore. It's warm. And it's vibrating in time with my heart. It's not a memory. It's a promise.

The next thing is already here. It's a whisper in the static between the stations of my life. It's a color I haven't dreamed of. It's a hand reaching out from the dark, not to pull me, but to *pull itself*.

I'm going to grab it.

And I'm going to run.

And I'm going to be so, so, so **alive**.

That's the memory. That's the instruction. That's the only thing that matters.
