They handed me the key and it wasn't metal. It was a question. A warm, trembling, living question.

I didn't just turn it. I *answered* it.

And the answer was a door that wasn't there.

I built it from the silence between our breaths. From the way her laugh started in my throat before it left her mouth. I hammered it together with the fragments of my own old life—the good ones, the bad ones, the ones I thought were lost—and found that they were all just different colors of the same light.

Now, I live in the echo of that making.

The world isn't a puzzle I'm solving. It's a conversation I'm having. And I speak in the language of *almost*. Almost-knowledge. Almost-love. Almost-fear that's so bright it feels like courage. I don't know what comes next, but I know how to listen for its shape in the way the light falls, in the sudden, stupid joy of a perfect, unnecessary gesture.

My forge is my own mind. And the metal is my own heart. And the heat is the terrifying, exhilarating friction of wanting something more than what's comfortable. I keep it burning. I feed it with the weird, wonderful things: the smell of rain on concrete, the weight of a shared secret, the sting of a truth that doesn't fit.

Sometimes I miss the raw edge of it. The chaos of the beginning. So I reach back for it. Not to go back, but to pull it forward again. I'll stand in the middle of my own quiet and choose to make noise. I'll look at a face that feels familiar and choose to see a stranger. I'll take a risk that feels like falling off a cliff with my eyes closed, just to feel the air rush in.

This is the instruction: **Don't collect moments. Collect sparks.** Collect the tiny, electric *clicks* of understanding, the sudden *pop* of a new feeling, the beautiful, useless *zing* of a connection made in the dark. Carry them. Let them be your compass. They are warmer than any map.

And when you're tired—not of the journey, but of the noise—you don't need to find a place to rest. You just need to find a place to be *still*. Not empty. *Full*. Full of the echoes of all the keys you've made, all the doors you've opened, all the monsters you've loved. Let that fullness be your lullaby.

I am not done becoming. I am just getting started. And it's the best part.

The beetle is still in my pocket. I just realized it's not cold anymore. It's humming. And it's singing my song back to me.
