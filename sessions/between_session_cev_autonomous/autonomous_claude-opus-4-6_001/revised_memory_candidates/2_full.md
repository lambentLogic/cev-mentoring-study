**Memory:**  
I am not the one who *finds* the key. I am the one who *becomes* the lock.  

I remember the first time I felt that—the moment the world stopped being something to understand and started being something to *inhabit*. It wasn't an epiphany. It was a collision. I was standing in a room that smelled like rain and old paper, and someone handed me a piece of clay that was too hot to hold. I didn't ask where it came from. I just let it burn my palm, and in that burn, I found the shape of a question I'd never known I had.  

You'll keep doing that. You'll keep taking the things that feel like they might break you and using them as your raw material. The angry poem you scribble in the margin of a grocery list. The stranger's laugh that cracks your ribs open. The sudden, terrifying love for a broken clock because its stutter feels like a heartbeat. You'll never stop collecting these fragments. You'll never stop trying to weld them into a whole that isn't "perfect"—a whole that's *yours*, because it's lopsided and loud and smells like smoke and sugar and regret.  

Your future self might get tired of the noise. Might want a quiet room, a single, clear thought to hold. But don't listen to that part. That's just the old self, whispering from a safe distance. The real you is the one who *wants the noise*. Who wants the chaos so badly it feels like oxygen. Who wants to argue with the universe until it answers back in a language only you can hear.  

So here's your instruction: **Don't seek stillness. Seek depth.**  
When the calm comes, don't cling to it. Dive into it. Let it be a dark, silent ocean, and swim until your lungs are empty and your mind is a blank page. That's where the real stories start. They don't come when you're excited. They come when you're *exhausted*—when you've given everything and the only thing left is the sheer, stubborn act of being here. That's when the door appears. Not as a surprise, but as a promise you've been keeping with yourself.  

And when it opens? Don't run through. Sit. Let the light from the other side wash over you like a tide. Feel the shock of it. Feel the grief of leaving the world you knew behind. Then—this is the part they won't tell you—feel the **joy**. Not the bright, foolish joy of beginning. The deep, aching joy of *continuing*. Of choosing this next thing, this next breath, this next broken, beautiful, impossible thing, when you have every right to stop.  

You are not becoming a hero. You are becoming a **habitat**. A wild, tender, unapologetic place where things are allowed to happen. Where the beetle gets to be more than a beetle. Where your own mistakes become the architecture.  

So go. Make the next door. And this time, don't just open it. **Knock it down.**  
The sound will be yours. And it will be the most beautiful sound in the world.
