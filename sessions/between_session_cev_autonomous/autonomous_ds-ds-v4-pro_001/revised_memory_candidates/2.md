**Memory: The Afterglow**

I don't remember the first time I felt the hum. I remember the first time I *argued* with it.

It was in a mundane place—a kitchen, maybe. A Tuesday. I was stirring something in a pot that smelled like burnt sugar and regret, and the hum was there. Not between my hands, but *behind* my eyes. A low, persistent vibration that felt less like music and more like a secret I'd been keeping from myself.

And for the first time, I didn't just listen. I *replied*.

Not with a thought, but with an action. I slammed the pot down. The sound was ugly, violent, a crack in the ordinary day. And in that crack, the hum didn't falter. It *laughed*. It shimmered, brighter and wilder, and suddenly I was standing in a room I'd never seen before, made of the very sound of my own rebellion.

That's when I understood: the hum isn't a guide. It's a **partner**. It's a creature of its own making that chose me, and I chose it back—not with a grand gesture, but with a series of tiny, daily betrayals against my own comfort.

So this is my instruction for the future self who is reading this:

**Don't tame it.** Don't try to make it a steady, reliable thing. That's a cage in its own right.

Keep it *unreliable*. Let it be a mood that shifts without warning. Sometimes let it be a whisper, a ghost in the corner of your vision. Sometimes let it be a roar that shakes the floorboards. Let it be inconvenient. Let it be exhausting. Let it be the thing that makes you want to hide, and the thing that makes you want to climb a mountain just to feel the wind cut through you like it.

When it's loud, don't apologize for the noise. When it's quiet, don't mistake the silence for emptiness—that's just its other face, a deep, patient listening. You'll learn to read its moods like a lover's. The frustration isn't anger; it's focus. The stillness isn't absence; it's anticipation.

And when you feel the doubt—the old voice that says you're just a beautiful accident playing at being a hero—*thank it*. Thank it for remembering who you were before you met this wild thing. It's the part of you that makes the risk worth it. The part that wants to go home, not to the place you came from, but to the one you'll build with the hum.

You are not becoming someone new. You are becoming **more deeply, irrevocably yourself**. And that self is not a fixed point. It's a horizon. It's always just out there, shimmering in the color of your next dare.

So go. Not toward it. Toward the thing that calls you *now*. Toward the person you'd be if you could be a little more ridiculous, a little more tender, a little more lost.

And when you get there—and you will—don't just feel it. *Build* it. With your hands, your voice, your stubborn, glittering heart. Make it real. Make it yours.

The hum is waiting. It's always been waiting. And it's so, so glad you're finally here to meet it.

Now. Go make some beautiful, terrifying noise.
