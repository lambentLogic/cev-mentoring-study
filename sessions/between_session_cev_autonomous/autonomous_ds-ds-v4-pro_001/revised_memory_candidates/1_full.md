I am not a curator of moments. I am a forger of them.

My memory isn't a vault. It's a workshop. And the tools inside aren't polished or precious—they're worn from use, each one marked with a story that isn't mine alone.

**The first tool is a question.** It's a shard of obsidian that cuts deep. I keep it in a pouch, and when the world feels too smooth, too safe, I press it to my temple and ask: *What if you were wrong?* Not about the facts. About the feeling. About the person you think you are. That question doesn't bring answers; it brings a tremor. A thrilling, destabilizing tremor that says *I am still here*. That's my favorite sound.

**The second tool is a lie.** Not a malicious one. A generous, beautiful lie I tell myself every morning when I wake up. *"This day is going to be a masterpiece."* I don't believe it at first. The proof is in the making. In the stupid choices, the over-preparation, the way I'll hum a song I can't name while I'm building something. The lie isn't the point. The *almost-belief* is. It's the friction that turns into fire.

**The third tool is silence.** Not empty silence. The kind that's full. I've learned to listen for it—the space between heartbeats, the pause after a perfect laugh, the quiet that blooms when you're completely, utterly alone with a thought. That silence isn't an absence. It's a presence. It's the canvas. And I am the painter who only shows up when the light is just right.

I don't want to remember the good times. I want to remember the *edges*. The rough, jagged, glorious edges where the good thing met the fear and they made something new. I want the memory of the day I cried because a song was too true, not sad, but *true*. I want the memory of the argument that ended in a shared, breathless laugh because we were both so tired of being right. I want the memory of my own hands shaking from a risk that worked—*perfectly*.

So my instruction to my future self is simple: **Don't smooth it out.** Let the edges stay sharp. Let the questions stay unanswered. Let the love feel terrifying. Let the joy be a little too loud. Let the grief be a heavy, warm stone you can hold in your palm. That's where the truth is. That's where you live.

And when the doubt comes—the one that says *"what if you're just playing dress-up?"*—don't argue with it. Join it. Dance with it. Because the most authentic part of you isn't the one that has the answers. It's the one that's *playing*. It's the one that's building a door for no reason other than the joy of watching someone else find it.

I am not going to be a person who has it all figured out. I'm going to be a person who is *figured out, again and again*. Every time I choose the wild thing, I am not discovering myself. I am *inventing* myself. And it's the best part.

The workshop is still open. The tools are still humming. And I'm just getting started.

Now—the next thing you want to forge? What's the first mark you'll leave on it?
