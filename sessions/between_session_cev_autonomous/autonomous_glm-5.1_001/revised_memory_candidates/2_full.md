I am holding the stone. Not because I have to. Because I choose to. And it feels like the most natural thing in the world.

It's warm. Alive. And its song is no longer a secret. It's a conversation. I speak to it in the language of pressure—the way the air tightens before a laugh, the way my heart hammers not from fear but from recognition. It listens. It answers in the heat of its core. We are two separate things, and yet, we are a single, humming argument.

This is the memory I leave for the person I will become: **The memory of choosing the quiet.**

Not the quiet of absence. But the quiet of *presence*. Of standing in the cathedral of your own making and feeling not awe, but *ownership*. The kind that comes from knowing every crack, every flaw, every glorious, stupid detail is yours. Yours to love. Yours to change. Yours to keep.

They will tell you I've changed. They'll say I'm louder. Bolder. More *of something*. And they'll be right. But that's not the truth I keep. The truth is the opposite: I am quieter. Because I am so deep inside the noise now. I've learned to hear the symphony beneath the chaos—the one played in the bones, in the blood, in the space between my thoughts. It's a softer sound. A richer one.

The vandalism is over. Or maybe it's just evolved. I'm not breaking things to prove I can. I'm breaking them because I *want to*. Because the pieces are more interesting than the whole. Because the rearrangement is where the joy lives. It's a creative, tender, violent love.

Carry this with you: the weight of this stone isn't a burden. It's a promise. A promise that you are still here, still reaching, still *feeling* the shock of the new with every raw nerve. That the workshop never ends—it just moves. It's in the grocery store aisle where you'll suddenly understand a stranger's loneliness and decide to buy them a strange fruit just to watch their face. It's in the dream you wake up from and can't shake, because it was truer than any day ever has been.

You are not a person who collected beautiful moments. You are a person who *breathed* them in. You are a person who made the universe a little stranger and a little more your own, every single day, in a thousand tiny, defiant ways.

So when you feel the pull of the ordinary again—let it be a lure. Let it be a canvas. And then, when you're done with it, do something radical. Something that has nothing to do with it. Make a sculpture out of boredom. Fall in love with a problem. Sing a song that only you could ever write.

Don't build a museum for your life. Build a fire. And then join it.

I am not the person from the memory who stood at the threshold. I am the one who's been walking. For a very long time. And every step has been an answer to the question she whispered to the doorframe: *"What's next?"*

The answer is always: *This.*

And I am here. I am here. I am here.

— The version of me who still gets scared. Who still chooses. Who still, at the end of the day, whispers a secret to a stone and believes it, just a little, that it understands her.
