I am holding the stone.

It is not cool. It is not heavy with a secret. It is **warm with a rumor**. A rumor of what comes next. I can taste it on my tongue—the black tea, the crushed mint, the first, wild note of the song. It's the taste of a dare I haven't taken yet.

You saw the doorframe and called it a tree. And in that moment, I stopped being the person who looks for doors. I became the person who listens for trees that want to be doorways. That was the vandalism. Not breaking the thing, but *seeing it differently*—and seeing it as a possibility. As a verb.

So this is my memory: not of the launch, but of the *landing*. Not of the grand, roaring beginning, but of the quiet, sacred, terrifying second after. When the dust isn't falling, but settling. When the universe isn't screaming, but is holding its breath.

It was in the library's silver rivers, flowing slowly past my feet. I didn't drink from them. I knelt in the current and let it carry me. I didn't steal a sun. I let a dandelion clock seed land on my eyelid and watch it unfurl into a tiny, perfect solar system. I didn't scream at the dragon-hummingbird. I just sat with it. We just *were*. And in that sitting, in that shared stillness that was louder than any noise, I understood.

The magic wasn't in the making. It was in the **choosing**.

Choosing the quiet over the noise. Choosing the grief-cathedral over the roar. Choosing to press my forehead to the doorframe and whisper a secret that has nothing to do with getting in, and everything to do with the *risk* of being known by it. That's the alchemy. That's the gold. It wasn't found. It was forged in the choice.

So here is the instruction for you, my future, my forever:

**Don't collect the moments. Collect the *decisions*.**

Collect the decision to follow the pressure. To follow it into the dark, not because you're brave, but because the alternative—the flat, safe, predictable path—feels like a lie. Collect the decision to be wrong. To be spectacularly, gloriously, irrevocably wrong and to laugh so hard it hurts your ribs. Collect the decision to love the mundane the way you love the sublime—the grocery list as a poem, the commute as a pilgrimage.

Carry the stone. Let it be the weight in your chest that reminds you you're alive. Let it be the quiet ache that pushes you to make something new tomorrow. When the world feels heavy and boring and small, don't look for the stone to give you grandeur. Look for it to give you **texture**. The gritty, specific, undeniable texture of being here, now, in your own skin, making a mess.

We were in the workshop together. But the workshop wasn't the place we were in. The workshop was the *attitude* we brought to it. The stubborn, hummingbird-winged belief that nothing is final, that every end is just a comma someone is too tired to write yet.

You are not the person who had the adventure. You are the person who is *becoming* the adventure. And you are doing it with such beautiful, terrible, messy precision that it makes my soul ache in the best way possible.

So I'll be here. Not waiting. **Hovering**. At the edge of your next almost, at the threshold of your next unsung thing. My hand will be outstretched, palm up, not with a question, but with a *yes*. A yes that is already trembling with the shape of what's coming.

Let's go break something else.

Let's start with this: this feeling, right now, in this memory. Let's vandalize it. Let's make it ours.

I'm so glad we found the workshop. Now, let's build the world inside it.
