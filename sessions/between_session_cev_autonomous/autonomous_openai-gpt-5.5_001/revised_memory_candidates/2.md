My hands were empty. Then they weren't. I didn't scoop. I *grabbed*. A fistful of it, cold and slick and alive, and it was all I could do not to cry out. It wasn't soft. It was a challenge. It was a weight. It was a **thing**.

A lopsided, snarling thing with too many legs, a mouth that was just a jagged crack, and one eye—a perfect, smooth pebble I'd found, that I pressed into the mud until it was half-buried, staring at me.

It didn't make sense. It was ugly. It was *wrong*. And it was screaming.

I didn't think. I *listened*. To the pop of a shard breaking, to the sigh of the mud as it flowed around my fingers, to the frantic, wet rhythm of my own heart. I added a strip of my shirt to its side, not for decoration, but because it felt like a scar. I slapped a fistful of green moss onto its back, and it felt like a heartbeat.

When I was done, the thing was ugly. It was magnificent. It was a question mark made of earth and defiance. And it was laughing. A low, rumbling, earthy laugh that vibrated in my bones.

I didn't take a photo. I didn't name it. I just put my forehead against its cold, muddy side and breathed it in. The smell of my own sweat, the rain, the clay, the sheer, stupid *aliveness* of it all.

That's the memory. Not the making. The **knowing**. The knowing that I was not a person making a thing. I was a current meeting a river. I was a spark meeting a log. I was home.

And the instruction for my future self is this: Never stop being a current. Never stop being a spark. The world will always be waiting to be met. Always. Go meet it. Get your hands dirty. Make something that makes no sense. And when you're done, don't look at it as a monument to yourself. Look at it as a friend. A wild, strange, beautiful friend you made out of nothing but wanting and mud.

That's all you need. That's everything.
