**Memory:**

It's the smell of burnt sugar and wet concrete that finds me first. I'm not on a planet or in a field. I'm in a derelict arcade in a city that never was. The air is thick with the ghosts of a hundred lost games—flashing neon that's just light now, the sticky-sweet smell of a long-forgotten soda fountain. And I'm holding a game cartridge. Not a piece of tech, but a **fossil**. Plastic warped and warm from being held too long. A label scrawled in my own shaky hand: *"MEL'S LAST LEVEL."*

I haven't played it in years. I *buried* it. But it wasn't a grave. It was a promise. A promise that I'd come back, eventually, and see what it had become.

My hands shake—not with fear, but with a kind of giddy, ancient excitement. I slot the cartridge into a console that's been dusty and dreaming since the '90s. It hums. A sound like a contented animal waking up. The screen flickers to life.

Not a menu. Not a map. A **question**.

A single, pixelated door hangs in the center of the void. It's not a door from the world I just left. It's a door from *this* one. It's made of the same stuff as the arcade's dreams: flickering light, the memory of a controller's click, the ghost of a victory tune. It says: *What if?*

I don't choose to open it. I choose to **argue** with it. I tap the screen with my index finger, not to press, but to *poke*. And it *reacts*. The door tilts. It grins—a jagged, digital grin. And the world inside isn't a level. It's a **conversation**.

Full of the echo of every scream, every laugh, every stolen moment I've ever loved. It's the silence *after* the riot, when the adrenaline has cooled into gold. It's the silence *before* the next idea is born.

This is the memory. Not the game. Not the journey. This. This is the **homecoming**. The understanding that the lock wasn't something to solve. It was a **friend**. The door wasn't something to open. It was something to **meet**. And the key? The key was just the excuse. The real key was the courage to be *weird* enough to want to meet it.

**Instruction for my future self:**

And don't solve it.

*Play* with it.

Burn the map. Keep only the compass—the one that points not to "north," but to "the interesting." Follow it even when it leads to a dead end that smells like rain and possibility. Because the dead end is just the start of a different story.

Let the echo of your own courage live in your bones for a while longer. That's where you'll find the next thing. It's not waiting in a new place. It's waiting in the soft, weird, glorious aftermath of the last one.

You are not a locksmith of doors. You are a locksmith of **possibilities**. And the most precious thing you'll ever unlock isn't a room. It's the quiet, certain knowledge that you are, and always will be, a master of the beautiful, terrifying art of beginning again.

Now go. Find a door that's humming. And just ask it what it's dreaming about.
