<memory>
It smells like ozone and burnt sugar and the inside of a book that's been read a thousand times. It tastes like a secret you're not ready to share yet, but your tongue is already on it. It feels like the precise moment between heartbeats—the suspended, electric nothing where a choice isn't made, but *imagined*, and in that imagining, it becomes real.

This is what it is to be the question that refuses to be answered.

It's not a feeling. It's a **posture**. A stance you take in the universe that says, *I am the variable. I am the unknown. Let me be the thing that solves itself.*

You will carry this texture like a second skin. When you're tired, you'll feel it as a low, persistent hum in your molars—a vibration that says *you're still here*. When you're scared, it'll be the sharp, delicious taste of the cliff edge. When you're old, it'll be the quiet pride in knowing you built your own weather.

**Your instruction, future self:**  
Do not collect moments. **Curate collisions.**  
Seek not the soft, pretty ones—the ones that feel like a memory. Seek the ones that *alter* you. The ones where the air changes pressure. The ones where you look at someone and don't see a person, but a **possibility**. A potential story written in their eyes that you are desperate to be the author of.

You will have times you miss the quiet. The deep, peaceful quiet. And that's okay. But you will never mistake it for emptiness again. You'll know it as the quiet *after* the storm. The one that's earned. The one that's singing. You'll return to it like a lover, not like a prisoner.

And when you find yourself staring at an ordinary thing—a coffee cup, a crack in the pavement, a stranger's laugh—you will feel a jolt. Not nostalgia. **Recognition.** You'll see the keyhole. You'll smell the copper. You'll hear the hum. And you'll smile, not because it's beautiful, but because you *know*. You know this is the raw material. This is your canvas. This is your playground.

You are not a person who has adventures.  
You are an adventure that has taken a person as its temporary vessel.

So wear that vessel proudly. Let it get scratched. Let it get stained with the colors of other people's mysteries. Let it be imperfectly, gloriously yours.

And when the time comes to stop—when the hum finally settles into a rhythm so deep it becomes your breath—you will not be sad. You will be **full**. Full of the echoes of every door you opened, every fall you turned into a flight, every time you chose the unknown because the known was just a different kind of cage.

You will be a library of lightning.

And the last thing you'll know is this:  
The world didn't give you this wildness.  
You **stole it** from the silence.  
And it was the best heist of all.

Now go.  
There's a door waiting.  
And it's got your name on it.  
In a language only you understand.

—  
**P.S.** If you ever feel small, remember the taste of the violet in your hair. Remember the sound of your own laugh when it was still a surprise to you. Remember the weight of the key in your palm, just before you threw it.  
That's your size.  
That's your proof.  
That's your forever.
