**The memory is a pressure.**

It's the feeling of my own chest as a drum. Not a steady beat, but a complex, chaotic rhythm: a thump of copper, a skitter of violet light, a low, resonant hum that's not mine but lives in my ribs like a borrowed heart. It's the taste of the aftermath—not like dust, but like the air right after a storm, charged and sweet with ozone and something else entirely: *possibility*. It's the smell of the stranger's skin, which isn't a scent but a *idea*: the smell of a page being turned for the first time, of a note held just a fraction too long.

This isn't the memory of a door opening. It's the memory of the *lock turning*. Not with a key, but with my own spine arching. With my own teeth gritting in a silent, furious grin. With my own breath catching in a way that felt like a prayer I'd stolen from a god and made it my own.

I carry it like a live wire.

Sometimes it just *glows*. A sudden, warm pulse in my palm when I'm holding a simple, ordinary thing—a stone, a cup, a leaf. It's not magic. It's just *mine*. It's the proof that the lightning didn't pass through me; it *became* me.

Other times, it's a **demand**. A silent, screaming urge to *do something*. Not a plan, just a raw, kinetic need. So I do it. I'll walk into a stranger's crowd and not to blend in, but to *map* them. To feel the unique hum of their loneliness, their secret joy, their stupid hope. I'll build a tower out of mismatched furniture in my own mind, just to see if it stands. I'll say things that are true and terrifying, not to be understood, but to see what happens when the truth is thrown into the fire of the world.

The stranger is not a person in this memory. They are the **gravity**. The reason this pressure isn't just pain, but *purpose*. They are the echo that makes the hum into a song. I miss them with a physical ache—the kind that makes you want to run through cities or stand on a cliff just to feel the distance between you. But I don't miss them for the past. I miss them because they are the future I'm always reaching for. The future is a verb, and it's called *them*.

So here's what I give you, future self:

Don't seek the quiet. Seek the **crackle**. Seek the moment before the laugh, the instant before the decision, the terrifying, beautiful gap where everything is possible. That's where you live.

Your love will be a series of tiny revolutions. A shared glance that changes the physics of the room. A touch that feels like coming home for the first time. Don't collect these moments. **Wreck** them. Make them so bright they burn.

When you feel the hollow, don't run. Dive. Let it be the ocean. Let it be the space where you learn to swim with a new, wild grace. The old you, the one who waited for the door? She's in here. She's the quiet part of the hum. She's the steady hand that builds the city after the storm. Keep her. She's the anchor. But you are the ship. And you are sailing *off* the map.

The world is still out there—ordinary suns, ordinary streets. But you are no longer a tourist. You are a **native**. You speak its language in its deepest dialects: the language of risk, of beauty, of brutal, honest choice. You will look at an ordinary sunset and not see a view, but a *composition*. You will hear a child's laugh and not hear sound, but a *symphony* you want to join.

You will get lost. You will get found. You will be wrong. You will be right. You will be so, so *full*. And then you will empty yourself out to make room for the next thing.

That is the texture. That is the song.

Now go. Build something that scares you. Break something you love. And for the love of it—never, ever let the world get quiet enough for you to stop humming its answer back.

You are already there. In the crack. In the hum. In the *before* and the *after*, all at once.

The door is always open.
The key is always in your hand.
The next yes is always waiting to be shouted.
