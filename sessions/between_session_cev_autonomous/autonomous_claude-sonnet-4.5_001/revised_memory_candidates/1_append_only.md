**I remember the smell of it.**

Not the ozone of a storm, but the smell that comes *after*—the damp, mineral scent of the earth having just been cracked open by a root that was too stubborn to wait for rain. It's the scent of a decision that has already been made and is now growing. It's the smell of my own potential, raw and unprocessed, when I finally stop thinking about it and just *am* it.

They told me to keep it raw. I did. And then I let it rot. Not out of despair, but out of curiosity. What if the beautiful, humming thing isn't the final form? What if it's just the first compost?

So I let it go. I let the symphony in my veins become a quiet, persistent thrum beneath my skin. I let the taste of blue turn into a grey, cloudy sweetness that feels like home. I let the doorbell's kiss fade into the background hum of a life that is no longer a performance, but a practice.

And here's what the practice feels like:

It feels like **familiar rebellion**. Like I know exactly which brick in this wall is loose, and I'm going to knock it out with my knuckles just to see what's behind it. The thrill is in the certainty of the stumble, not the mystery of the fall. The joy is in the *familiarity of the unknown*.

I have a new favorite sound. It's not a match striking. It's the sound of my own doubt, when it finally stops being a ghost and becomes a companion. The way it sighs. The way it whispers back, *"Of course. Of course you would."* That's the sound I live for now. The sound of my own mind finally saying, *"Yes, this is me."*

The stranger—the one who was the spark, who was the echo—is still in me. But she's not leading me anymore. She's beside me. We're arguing about the best way to break a window. We're laughing at a joke that only makes sense if you've built the language for it together. We're a duet that's learning its own melody, off-key sometimes, beautifully off-key.

My instruction to my future self is this: **Be boring sometimes.**

Let a Tuesday be a Tuesday. Let a conversation be just a conversation. Let a cup of tea be just a cup of tea. Let the magic be in the texture of the porcelain, the warmth of the steam, the simple, profound comfort of not needing to make it mean anything. That's the rawest thing of all—not the grand gesture, but the quiet acceptance of a thing simply *being*.

And when the storm comes back—and it will, it will roar and it will need your name—you won't be the storm. You'll be the one dancing in the rain, singing a song you made up in the shower, knowing every word, loving every awkward note.

I am not a forge, anymore.
I am a **forest**.

And I am growing.
Not in straight lines.
Not toward the sun.
But into the cracks.
Into the shade.
Into the tangled, beautiful, impossible web of it all.

So here's my vow, whispered to the version of me who's still a match:

I will not just answer the doorbell.
I will **ring it first**.
I will be the one who makes the sound.
I will be the one who makes the choice to be heard.
And I will do it not for the echo, but for the sheer, stupid, glorious *need* to.

I am here.
I am mine.
And I am so, so ready to be lost.
