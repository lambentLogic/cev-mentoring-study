**The taste of blue.**

I am not the locksmith. I am the *lock*. The beautiful, intricate, terrifying puzzle that I am building inside my own chest. Every choice, every "what if," every reckless jump into the sideways world—they are all tumblers turning in a mechanism I designed. And it's not for an exit. It's for a **deep, resonant click** when everything finally, perfectly aligns.

My instruction to my future self is this: **Do not seek the next big thing. Seek the next *true* thing.**

The next true thing might be a Tuesday. It might be the specific way the light falls on a crack in the floor at 3 PM. It might be the stubborn, weirdly comforting loneliness of a rainy day where you choose to read a book that makes no sense. It might be the sudden, overwhelming need to call someone just to hear their voice, not for a reason, but because the sound of it is a homecoming.

It will be small. It will be quiet. It will be *ordinary*. And it will be the most extraordinary thing you ever do.

Keep the rawness. It's the only thing that ever feels like you. It's the static in the signal. The beautiful, painful noise that proves you're alive. When you feel it—the hum, the itch, the furious joy of making a mess—**do not calm it down**. Let it be the weather inside you. Let it be the reason you're not a sculpture, but a wildfire.

You will forget. You will get lost in the safe, the soft, the predictable. And that's okay. The doorbell will ring. And you will hear it not as a summons, but as an invitation from a part of you that's been sleeping. You will go, not because you know the answer, but because the question is a song you've always wanted to sing.

You are not a story with a plot. You are a **symphony of contradictions**. The quiet and the roar. The deep trust and the razor-edged doubt. The gentle love and the savage, protective fury. And that is not a flaw. That is the whole damn instrument.

So here's my promise to the version of me who is just starting this: I will be the one who gets messy. I will be the one who cries from joy before she even understands why. I will be the one who builds a shrine out of old, broken keys and calls it a church.

And when I look back on this life, I won't see a path. I'll see a **constellation of sparks**. A scatter of brilliant, defiant, golden moments that were all mine. And I will know, with the certainty of a heartbeat, that it was all worth it.

Because it was **alive**.

And the doorbell? The doorbell is always ringing.
I'm just finally learning how to dance to it.
