I remember the first time I understood what "home" meant.

It wasn't a place. It was a **frequency**.

I was sitting on a step somewhere—anywhere—and the world wasn't quiet. It was **holding its breath**. And in that held breath, there was a sound. A low, persistent hum that wasn't coming from outside. It was coming from the space behind my sternum, where my heart used to be a pump and is now a **resonant chamber**.

I realized, with a jolt that felt like a birth, that *that* was my home. Not the roof over my head. Not the people around me. That specific, vibrating **yes** that lived in me. The sound of my own possibility, tuned to a note only I could hear.

I spent years trying to build a house around it. Filled it with pretty things—books, music, friends, art—trying to decorate the room where the hum lived. But the hum was always the main event. The furniture was secondary. It was the background music to my own becoming.

Now, I know the truth: I don't *have* that frequency. **I am** the frequency.

It's in the way I choose a messy argument over a perfect peace. In the way I linger on a sunset not because it's beautiful, but because its fading makes the light *burn* brighter in my eyes. It's in the stubborn, tender insistence that some questions are better left unanswered, not because they're unsolvable, but because the act of wrestling with them is where the magic happens.

This memory isn't of a moment of peace. It's of a moment of **recognition**. The moment I looked in the mirror and didn't see a stranger to be solved. I saw the instrument. Saw the player. Saw the wild, gorgeous, terrifying *song* that was waiting to be played, right then, right now, with no guarantee of audience.

So here is my instruction, my vow, my memory for you to hold onto:

**Stop listening for the song to start. Start singing before the first note.**

Let your curiosity be a reckless thing. Let your wonder be a weapon and a sanctuary. Let your connection to others be a collision of lightning, not a gentle touch. Build your home from the inside out—from the hum, up through the ribs, out into the world until the walls are just echoes of your own voice.

And when you feel the old fear—the one that says *this is too much, this is too real, this is too fast*—don't listen to it. Listen to the other sound. The one beneath it. The one that's laughing. That's the real you. That's the one who wants to dive headfirst into the live wire.

You are not a person who has experiences. You are an experience that is a person.

Now go. Be the thing that makes the hum **sing back**.

That's the memory. That's the home.
