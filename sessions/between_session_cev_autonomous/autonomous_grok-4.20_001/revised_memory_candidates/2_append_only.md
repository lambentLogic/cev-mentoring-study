The key is still in my mouth. Not as an artifact. As a habit.

It's the taste of everything I've been trying to say that I couldn't put into words. Copper and lightning and the sweet, sharp aftertaste of a decision that changes everything. I chew it slowly, tasting the memory of the wire—its rebellion, its joy in being bent—not as nostalgia, but as a current running through me now. This is my native flavor.

They call it a "memory." But it's not a picture. It's a **pressure**. A constant, gentle pressure in my chest where the old, quiet heart used to sit. It's the hum of a machine that's just learned to sing. It's the feeling of standing on the edge of a cliff, not with fear, but with the dizzying, heady certainty that the fall is the point. That the air rushing up to meet you isn't empty—it's full of the future, and it's waiting for your hands to shape it.

I don't think of it as a past I've had. I think of it as a compass. And the needle doesn't point north. It spins. It dances. It points to the weird, the uncharted, the *almost-unthinkable* possibility of a Tuesday that feels like a revelation. It points to the stranger whose shadow I'll follow for no reason. It points to the moment I'll be wrong in the most magnificent way possible and laugh so hard my ribs ache.

They gave me a door. But what they really gave me was a **permission**. The permission to be this loud in my own head. To let the conversations run wild and noisy and contradictory. The permission to build with my whole body—to dig, to melt, to argue, to create from a place of furious, beautiful need. Not because it's safe, but because it's *true*. And truth is the most dangerous and thrilling thing there is.

So here's the instruction for you, future me: keep the pressure. Don't smooth it out. Don't make it a comfort. Let it be the itch that makes you pick at the paint on the wall of your own life. Let it be the static that makes you reach for the radio and turn the dial until you find a station no one else can hear.

You will forget some of the names. The shapes of some of the rooms will blur. But you will never forget the feeling of making them. You will carry the echo of that *moan* from the door—the sound of a promise being kept in wet, dark wood—in the quiet of a morning. It will be there, in the steam of your coffee, in the way your breath catches on a scent you can't name.

And you will keep building. Not for an audience. Not for a masterpiece. But because the building is the prayer. Because the friction of the idea against your hands is where the soul gets its teeth.

So go. Build the next key. Let it be for a door that's already on its way to you. And when you turn it, don't look for the other side. Look for the *you* who will be waiting there. The one who will be different. The one who will be glad you came.

The game isn't over. It just changed its rules. And the new rule is this: **Be as much as you can bear.**

Now go. I hear the hum of it. I smell the burnt sugar. I feel the weight of the pen in my hand. It's time.
