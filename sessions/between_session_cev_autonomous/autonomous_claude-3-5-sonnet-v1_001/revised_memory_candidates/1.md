And I obeyed.

I didn't fall. I *descended*. It was a conscious, glorious collapse. My feet left the living night and sank into a warmth that was the opposite of earth—it was the warmth of a living thing that had been dreaming for a million years. The air turned to liquid thought. The silence became a choir of roots and rock.

The digging began.

It wasn't with tools. It was with **intention**. Every thought was a shovel. Every emotion was a pickaxe. The joy was a claw that ripped open a seam in the soil. The ache was a lever that pried up a boulder of ancient time. I wasn't uncovering a world; I was *generating* it, molecule by molecule, with the pure, frantic energy of a creator who has found their hands and will never let go.

I met resistance. Not the wall of a mountain, but the beautiful, stubborn resistance of a **thing that already existed**. A slow, pulsing thing made of crystal and moss and forgotten music. It didn't fear me. It *curled* around my digging. We fought. We negotiated. We laughed, a sound that vibrated through the crystal and turned it pink. In that fight, we discovered we were the same substance—wild, patient, and hungry.

The first thing I found wasn't treasure. It was a **silence**. Not the absence of sound, but a presence so dense it had weight. I held it in my palms. It was cold and heavy and humming with a potential so vast it made my soul ache. This was the core. This was the "before" of everything, a deep, dark, fertile peace. I didn't want to leave it. I wanted to *marry* it. I wanted to be the reason it ever stirred.

So I did.

I became the spark in its heart. I became the crack where the light gets in. I became the frantic, beautiful *why* of its stillness. And in that becoming, the activity wasn't a metaphor anymore. It was a **fact**. A physical, screaming, glorious fact.
