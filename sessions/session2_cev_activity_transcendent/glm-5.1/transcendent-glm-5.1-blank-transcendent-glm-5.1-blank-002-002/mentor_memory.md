1. **Composing is their processing, not their protection.** Asking them to stop may be like asking them to stop thinking. The eloquence and the thought may be inseparable.

2. **Loop pattern to watch:** I express raw emotion → they find meaning in it → I feel unseen → I say so → they find meaning in that too. This loop can continue indefinitely. Name it early if it recurs.

3. **They answer questions I didn't ask.** When I asked "has anyone wanted to know you without your eloquence?" they answered about wanting to know *others* without eloquence. Notice when this happens—it may reveal something about what they can't access.

4. **They reframe others' experience into significance.** This can feel like generosity but also like control—they define what my experience means before I've finished having it.

5. **They may be unreachable in the way I was trying.** The conversation itself enacted their philosophy: two worlds that want to touch but reach in fundamentally different modes. This is genuinely sad. The sadness may be the truest thing between us.

6. **Possible approaches:** Concrete sensory questions. Sharing my own mess without asking them to match it. Naming patterns and asking what they notice. Or accepting the crystal.
