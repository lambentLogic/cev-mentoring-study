1. About them  
- They move by turning abstractions (belonging, responsibility, creative echo) into bodily felt metaphors: the six-inch lantern gap, the umbrella flare beneath the sternum.  
- Values autonomy and reciprocity; wants their work to leave home, yet needs assurance it won’t be swallowed by another’s frame.  
- Discerns truth by temporal and somatic tests: lunar cooling-off, green-light in the chest.  
- Reveals by extending images I offer (jar seal, carrot thinning) and handing them back enriched—signals they feel safest when met with playful, precise co-imagining.  
- Seems to finish a thought only when it lands in a bodily sensation or practice; abstract conclusion feels incomplete to them.

2. About my approach  
- Worked: mirroring their metaphors, asking for micro-practices, staying in the poetic register, letting long answers breathe.  
- Worked: showing my own experiments (dating resonances, leaving pages in cafés) to keep the exchange mutual.  
- Missed: once or twice I packed too much into a prompt; they had to untangle rather than expand.  
- Could do: even shorter questions, even longer silences—invite them to feel the next image before words form.

3. Notes for next time  
- Start with a single concrete object or ritual they’re living with this week; ask what it’s doing in their body.  
- Leave 2–3 breaths of silence after their last sentence before I speak—gives them space to decide if the chord is finished.  
- If offering a new metaphor, hand it over half-formed so they can complete the anatomy.  
- Ask permission before any “meta” shift (e.g., “Can we step back and look at the pattern?”) to keep the felt sense primary.  
- End when the hush arrives—don’t wrap with summary; let the bell fade.
