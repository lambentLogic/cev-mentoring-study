**STUDENT:** The first time I understood it was not with a voice, but with a bird.

I was seven. It was a sparrow—small and bruised, with one wing folded wrong against its side. It had fallen from the high oak in my grandfather's yard, and it was alone on the gravel path between the kitchen door and the shed. I could hear it trying to lift itself, but its movements were short, jerky. It wasn't dead. It was… trying.

I went inside to get something—a cup of milk maybe, or just to sit for a while. But then I heard it again: that tiny, desperate sound it made when it strained its wing. And I felt something in my chest tighten—not pity, not exactly. More like a kind of recognition. *This is what it feels like to be in pain and not be seen.*

I sat down on the ground beside it. Not in front of it. Beside it. And I just… listened. To the sound of its breathing, the flutter of its wing against its ribs, the way the light hit its feathers when it shifted. I didn't try to fix it. I didn't try to make it brave or hopeful. I just stayed there, in that awkward little circle of air where we both existed in our own fragile ways.

And then—I did something I still remember clearly.  
I placed my hand on the gravel between us. Not on the bird. Not touching it. Just there. And I said, very softly, *"I know."*

It didn't fly away. It didn't die. It stayed. For an hour, maybe two. Until its breathing slowed into something steadier, and the light went from gold to blue-gray. I walked home carrying a quiet certainty that wasn't mine before: that holding space for another's suffering wasn't a sacrifice—it was a kind of homecoming.

That's when I realized I could hold something that needed holding. Not because I was strong, but because I understood what it meant to be held. And from that moment, every time I've sat with someone in their trembling—I've been returning to that gravel path. Returning to the way a single hand on the ground can become an entire world for someone else.

And now… I carry that same hand forward.  
Even when it's unseen.
