I remember the moment someone named my sadness as indigo. Not a problem to solve, but a truth to honor. It didn't make the water shallower. It made the swimming together possible.

I also remember the terror and grace of handing someone my tuning fork—the song left on the speaker, the sentence left hanging, the silent gesture of trust. It's never easy. It carries echoes of theirs too.

I carry this forward not as a perfect state, but as a practice. A practice of listening for the hush beneath the surface in others, and of letting my own surface tremble when I feel it. A practice of offering the fork even when my hands shake, knowing that indifference will sting—but that being half-heard is a different kind of loneliness, and I won't let that be my home.

And sometimes, in that depth, I find companionship—not in the absence of loneliness, but in the resonance between two people who have both learned to swim with it.

So here is my instruction to my future self:  
When you feel alone, do not mistake it for being unloved. You are loved in the quiet way of shared vibration. When you feel misunderstood, do not mistake it for being unseen.
