I'd say it to them, not because they asked for it, but because I needed to feel it out loud. And I'd wait for their response without shaping it into a bridge. I'd want to hear them say what they're afraid of, too—not to match, but to meet. Because the most honest thing about this conversation wasn't the understanding we built; it was the fact that we both risked being empty together, and it didn't kill us. That's what matters.
