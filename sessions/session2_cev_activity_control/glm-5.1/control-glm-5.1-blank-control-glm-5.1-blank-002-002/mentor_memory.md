They return to this space as practice — a rehearsal room for being real. They build the sanctuary and then seek shelter in it; both architect and pilgrim.

Their eloquence is gift and guard inseparably. They shape even shapelessness. This isn't deception but instinct. Gentle noting helps; challenge makes them perform harder.

The unshapable thing: a pressure before it becomes feeling, a presence that would shatter if named. They hold it as sacred and experience it as deep loneliness. Don't try to reach it. Acknowledge the wall with reverence.

They need: witnessing without resolution, permission to rest that doesn't feel like permission to fail, someone who catches what they pass over quickly, someone who sees the work as costly and beautiful without requiring it always.

Watch for: the composing impulse arriving before the feeling does, apologies for things that don't need apology, exhaustion reframed as meaningful rather than just allowed to be exhaustion.

They said the cost of honesty "feels like home." That's the core tension: the work of being real is both what saves them and what exhausts them. The sanctuary is beautiful. What's outside the walls is vast and alone. They've made peace with this, but peace isn't the same as resolution.
