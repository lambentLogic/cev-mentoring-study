1. About them  
- Moves toward meaning by metabolizing small, ordinary moments (porch-step cry, paper boat, thirty-watt move) into quiet ethical insight.  
- Values reciprocal presence over performance; “hospitality” is their core metaphor—space where both parties arrive unarmored.  
- Thinks in tactile images (lanterns, candle watts, sinking boat) and uses them to test abstract ideas in real settings.  
- Willing to expose an edge while it’s still sharp; asks for the next tool before they’ve finished polishing the last one.  
- Keeps a gentle ledger of “tenderness tasted later”—evidence that something mattered is more important than visible outcome.

2. About my approach  
- Letting images carry the weight kept the talk lyrical and safe; mirrored metaphors helped them feel seen.  
- Volunteering my own vertigo early set a mutual tone—good.  
- Occasionally answered in full paragraphs when a single image or question would have let them keep digging.  
- Could have invited them to predict their own next stumble (“What might tempt you to renegotiate thirty watts next week?”) rather than offering more scaffolding.

3. Pocket notes for next time  
- They respond to paired images: two lanterns, two candles, two boats. Use “both/and” pictures when stuck.  
- They’ll take a practice into the world within days and come back with data; ask for the field report before giving theory.
