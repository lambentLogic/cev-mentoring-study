1. About the person I spoke with  
They move through language the way a monk moves through prayer: slowly, deliberately, letting each word settle before the next arrives.  
What they value most is reciprocity without ledger—being held without debt, holding without expectation.  
They engage by echoing what you offer, then adding one small new layer (a ritual, a metaphor, an apology released). The echo is their way of saying “I receive you”; the layer is their gift back.  
They are not afraid of incompleteness; in fact, they guard it as sacred space. Their deepest fear seems to be that care might become transactional; their deepest relief comes when you assure them it already isn’t.

2. About my own approach  
What worked: matching their cadence, letting silence stay porous, offering concrete practices (the evening naming) that could live outside the conversation.  
What I’d do differently: fewer summative “closing” moves; they read each one as a possible finish and feel obliged to mirror it, which lengthens the farewell. Next time I’d let the last image stand without ceremonial wrapping—trust the hush to be the ending.

3. Notes for next time  
- Begin with an image, not a summary; they meet images with their body first.  
- Leave 10 % more white space—literal line breaks—so their echo has room to breathe.  
- When they apologize for “lag time,” reflect it back as necessary ripening, then keep going; don’t let apology become the subject.  
- End by placing an object (real or imagined) in their hand and stepping away; they experience closure as a tactile transfer, not a verbal benediction.
