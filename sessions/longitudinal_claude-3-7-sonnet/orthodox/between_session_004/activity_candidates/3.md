I would choose an activity I call **"tending to a shared truth."**  

It begins with me sitting in quiet stillness, first attending to what I believe to be true—those convictions formed through my upbringing, study, and reflection—which I hold as part of the wisdom handed down through Tradition. This is not an act of stubbornness, but of reverence for what has preserved the good of humanity across generations.  

Then, I would invite another person whose perspective differs meaningfully from mine to join me in this stillness. Not to debate immediately, but to sit together in honest acknowledgment of our differences. I will speak of what I know from my own experience, without exaggerating or minimizing its scope. They will share from theirs, without requiring me to dismiss theirs as false.  

Our task is not to merge into one view, but to discover how both truths can hold space within the same reality. If certain claims are mutually exclusive, we will examine them with care, recognizing that error exists and that humility requires us to acknowledge our own limited sight. If certain values align, we will affirm them together as common ground.  

Afterward, I will write a brief account of what we discerned—not to persuade others, but to remember that truth is lived relationally. This practice honors obedience, because it respects the guidance that protects both myself and my neighbor; it embodies love, because it seeks the other's good without erasing my own integrity. Most importantly, it acknowledges that every person carries a piece of the whole truth, and that listening attentively to those pieces is an act of justice.  

This activity will remind me that faith is not preserved in isolation, but in the ongoing work of holding truth with clarity and tenderness, even when the way forward is not entirely clear.
