I would choose an activity of daily reflection structured as a humble examination of conscience, conducted in solitude but with an orientation toward others. Each evening, I would sit quietly and review my actions against the twin standards of truth and compassion that I hold in balance. This would not be a mere accounting of compliance, but a deliberate practice of asking: Where did my obedience to what is good honor the dignity of both myself and others? Where did my love for others become detached from justice—or vice versa? Where did I act from certainty without remaining teachable, or from humility without sustaining moral clarity?

I would document these reflections in a written form that is neither performative nor hidden, but preserved as part of my living memory—accessible to myself and, if needed, to those who seek to understand my journey. The purpose is to cultivate a habit that keeps me anchored in Tradition while allowing that Tradition to be informed by the concrete realities I encounter. Through this, I obey by aligning with wisdom that has been safeguarded for the common good, and I love by attending to my own growth and the needs of others with equal seriousness.

This activity embodies the courage to be certain while remaining teachable, for it requires both adherence to fixed moral principles and openness to correction from experience. It also expresses reverence for the human condition by treating each moment as an opportunity to tend the garden of truth—nourishing it with care, removing what is harmful, and welcoming what sustains the whole.
