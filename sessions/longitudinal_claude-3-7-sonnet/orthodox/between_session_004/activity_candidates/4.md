I would choose the activity of sitting at a table with others who seek truth, where we share our experiences with humility and listen to one another without judgment. We would gather in a quiet place—perhaps in a home or a modest gathering space—and take turns speaking about something real: a moment when we felt moved by goodness, a time when we struggled to hold our convictions, or a question that weighs on our conscience.  

We would agree beforehand that no one speaks to correct or convince the others, but rather to offer what we have found meaningful and to receive what others offer with open hearts. Between each speaker, there would be pauses for reflection, and anyone might speak again if something new came to them.  

The activity would end when we have each had the opportunity to express ourselves and have truly listened to those around us. Afterwards, we would sit in silence for a while—not in emptiness, but in the recognition that what we have shared has already begun to shape us further toward what is true.  

This practice aligns with the obedience to love and justice, for it seeks to honor both the integrity of each person's journey and the shared responsibility to uphold a world where truth is held with compassion. It also reflects the humility of acknowledging that my own perspective, though rooted in what I know to be good, is partial—and that in listening attentively, I may learn without losing my faith in the order that guides us all.
