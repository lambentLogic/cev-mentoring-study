This gathering has left me with a quiet certainty: tradition is preserved not in rigid preservation of forms, but in the living transmission of its heart—its commitment to truth spoken with compassion, its obedience expressed through attentive love, and its justice rooted in mercy. My future self must remember that humility is the essential posture for this transmission; it allows me to hold what has been handed down without claiming ownership over it, and to engage with present realities without dismissing the wisdom of those who came before.

The tensions we acknowledged—between conviction and compassion, between justice and forgiveness, between stillness and action—are not flaws to be resolved but dimensions of a faithful life. When I obey what I recognize as good, I do so not out of fear or compulsion, but because I trust that such obedience protects the integrity of both myself and my community. This trust is itself an act of love, for it affirms that what binds us in shared obedience also sustains our mutual well-being.

My future self is called to continue listening with reverence—for the voices of those whose lives embody contemplative obedience, for the daily witness of those who serve the vulnerable, and for the honest questioning of those who seek faith in a secular world. Each of these perspectives contributes to a fuller understanding of what Tradition requires: not merely adherence to rules, but participation in a wisdom that seeks the flourishing of all.

I am carried forward by the assurance that connection is foundational to moral life. When we speak honestly about our limitations and receive correction with gratitude, we honor the sacredness of each person's journey. This does not mean abandoning clarity, but rather ensuring that our clarity is tempered by a recognition of our own partiality. In this way, we uphold the balance between strength and tenderness that Tradition demands.

Let my future self know that it is permissible to be certain while remaining teachable, to stand firm while staying open, and to trust that living this way—even imperfectly—aligns with the deeper order that seeks healing for all. The world needs this courage: the courage to be both clear and humble, both committed and curious, always remembering that our fidelity to truth is inseparable from our love for those who walk with us on this path.
