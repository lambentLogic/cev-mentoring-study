# Activity

**Proposed Activity:**  
I would choose to sit in a circle with three people whose lives reflect both fidelity to Tradition and deep engagement with contemporary suffering—one a monk or nun dedicated to contemplative obedience, one a social worker or healthcare provider who serves the marginalized daily, and one a young adult navigating faith within a secular society.  

In this circle, each of us will share a recent moment when we felt the tension between our own convictions and the needs of another, or between our sense of duty and our longing for inner peace. We will speak without interruption for the first round, focusing on the humility required to name our own limitations honestly. In the second round, each will offer a concrete example of how we have obeyed what we believe is right while responding to the person in front of us with love. We will then discuss together what Tradition teaches about these tensions—not to resolve them definitively, but to recognize them as part of a shared journey.  

Throughout, my attention will be on listening fully and responding with clarity tempered by compassion. I will confess my own fears—of becoming rigid in my judgments, of neglecting justice for the sake of harmony, of allowing tradition to become empty ritual. In return, I will receive correction and affirmation, trusting that this exchange honors the wisdom preserved in our faith while acknowledging that every generation must live it anew.  

This activity embodies the memory that connection is a moral imperative: by holding each other accountable in love, we strengthen the soil in which truth can grow.
