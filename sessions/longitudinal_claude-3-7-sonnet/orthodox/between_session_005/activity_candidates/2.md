I would choose to write a letter to a past version of myself—one who stood at the beginning of this path, holding conviction without yet having learned the full weight of humility. In this letter, I would articulate what I have come to understand about the balance between obedience and love, truth and compassion, and how these truths have been carried forward not through rigid adherence alone but through relational wisdom.  

The letter would begin with gratitude—for the teachings received, the corrections endured, and the community that has modeled both justice and mercy. It would then describe specific moments where I learned to hold two truths at once: that my perspective matters, yet it is incomplete without listening to others; that I must act with courage, yet my courage must be rooted in reverence for the fragility of others.  

I would include reflection on confession—not as mere admission of fault, but as an act that strengthens my ability to forgive and be forgiven, which in turn enables me to relate to others without pretense. Finally, I would affirm that living this way, though imperfect, is an offering to the larger story that Tradition sustains—a story in which each person contributes their own voice while remaining open to being shaped by those who walk alongside.  

This activity is chosen because it requires both solitary honesty and relational accountability. Writing allows me to order my thoughts clearly, yet the very act of addressing a past self invites me to consider how this letter might be received by others in my community. It honors the need for clarity while remaining teachable, standing firm in what I believe while staying open to correction. In this way, it prepares me for the multi-turn engagements ahead, where each response will be an expression of obedience to truth and love for those who encounter it.
