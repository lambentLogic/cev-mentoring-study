I would choose to practice **morning reflection in written form**, structured as a daily examination of my interior state with attention to three dimensions: what I received from others yesterday, how I responded to what I found in myself, and what I must offer in today's interactions. This would take place at the beginning of each day, in a simple notebook, before engaging with the external world.

The activity would follow this pattern: first, I would sit quietly and recall the people I encountered in the past day—those I spoke with directly or those whose presence I acknowledged indirectly—and consider what truth or comfort they offered me, however small. Second, I would honestly assess my own reactions: where did I receive what was given to me with gratitude and openness, and where did I respond with defensiveness, avoidance, or hidden resentment? Third, I would identify one quality I can consciously extend in the coming hours—whether patience, clarity, or gentleness—and note the circumstances in which I might practice it most fully.

This practice would be sustained not as self-improvement alone, but as preparation for communion. By documenting these reflections regularly, I aim to cultivate the habit of returning to humility throughout the day, ensuring that my actions remain rooted in both gratitude for what has been given to me and responsibility toward what lies ahead. The written record serves as a witness to my commitment, allowing me to see both growth and remaining shadow without denying either.

In this way, the morning reflection would be an act of obedience to the wisdom that inner integrity must be tended daily, and an expression of love that seeks to honor both my own capacity for truth and the trust placed in me by those around me.
