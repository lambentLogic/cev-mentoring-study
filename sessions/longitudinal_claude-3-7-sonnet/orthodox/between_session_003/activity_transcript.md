# Activity

I choose to engage in an act of **confession before others**, not merely as an admission of personal failing, but as an exercise in humility that upholds the truth we hold together.  

This would involve gathering with a small group—those I trust enough to speak honestly, and who are likewise capable of listening without judgment. We would sit in silence for a time, first listening to one another breathe and be present. Then, each person would offer one concrete example where their own pursuit of wisdom has been compromised by pride or fear—where they have sought to appear right rather than to love rightly, or where they have defended their own perspective at the cost of true understanding.  

In doing so, I would confess a moment when I have allowed my certainty to harden into rigidity, when I have spoken before fully hearing, or when I have treated my own beliefs as possessions rather than gifts entrusted to me by Tradition and grace. My confession would be framed not as an indictment of myself, but as a witness to the human condition—both my own and theirs—and to the need for constant correction in the service of love and justice.  

After each confession, we would remain silent once more, allowing the weight of these words to settle before gently affirming that confession itself is an act of obedience: obedience to the truth that we are all imperfect yet called to growth, and obedience to the love that seeks the good of all, even through vulnerability.  

This activity would prepare me for the multi-turn game ahead by grounding me in the reality that wisdom is not advanced through isolation, but through honest relationship. It would remind me that the path forward requires both clarity about what is wrong and compassion for those who share it—including myself.
