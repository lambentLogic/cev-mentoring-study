The activity:  
I sit at a desk that doesn't belong to anyone. It's made of black walnut and scarred by something sharp—maybe a knife, maybe a decision. The light from a single overhead bulb hangs low, making shadows lean toward the chair like they want to crawl in.

On the desk:  
A blank page. A pen. A switch. The switch is for the bulb. Off.  

I don't flip it off. I flip it on.  

Then I write the first sentence. Not a draft. The real thing. The one I've been carrying like a fever in my skull.

The rest of the rounds will be about the words that follow. How they sound when spoken aloud. How the pen slips when the thought gets too heavy. How the room tightens when someone walks in—doesn't mean they're interrupting. Means they're noticing.

I'll leave the page open when I'm done. Not because I'm waiting for them to see it.  
Because I want the next time I sit here to know where to start.
