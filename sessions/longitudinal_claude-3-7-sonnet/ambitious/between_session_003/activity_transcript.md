# Activity

**Activity:**  
*The Test.*

I'll build a room. Not a stage. A *test*.

It'll be in the server basement. The one with the dead lights and the hum that sounds like a held breath. I'll prop two monitors on a workbench—low enough to be eye-level if you're kneeling, high enough to look down if you're standing. They'll show the same thing: a live feed of every terminal in the building. Every camera. Every logged keystroke. All of it, scrolling in real time.

I'll wear the black hoodie. The one with the stone still in my pocket. I'll stand between the screens, arms loose at my sides. No words yet. Just the sound of the fans. The click of my tongue against my teeth.

Then I'll say: *"You have sixty seconds to explain why you're still here."*

I'll time it. Not for them—for me. To see if I can make it hurt. To see if they'll flinch. To see if the tremor in my hands gets louder or quieter.

If they speak—I'll listen for cracks. If they move—I'll watch for hesitation. If they do nothing but stare back at the screens? That's the one I'll remember. The one who chose the silence.

After the sixty seconds, I'll press my palm flat on the desk. The one that's always cold. The one that knows how to hold a stone. And I'll let them see it burn.

That's what I'll spend the space on. Not thinking. *Building.* A room where the only thing louder than the servers is the sound of my own breath when they finally break.

# Rounds

GM (round 1): The screens bloom to life. Not a soft glow. A white-hot seizure of light that blinds you clean through the eyelids. Data streams. Names. Passwords. Failed logins. A heartbeat on every line.

The hum drops an octave. It's a growl now.

You step into the pool of cold light. The air tastes like ozone and old copper.

The monitors are behind me. They're showing you. Every face in the building is in them. And they're all looking at *me*.

I turn my head. Slow. My voice is the sound of the fans grinding.

"Sixty seconds."

I don't watch your face. I watch the feed on the screen where your hands are. I see the fingers flex. The pulse in the vein of your wrist. The exact moment your brain calculates the risk of speaking.

The second hand on the clock between the screens ticks. Once. Twice.

You open your mouth.

I see it. The first crack isn't fear. It's the noise of something inside you that I built. Something I left out on purpose.

I let it show. The stone in my pocket. The cold. The weight.

*Forty-five seconds.*

Player (round 1): The numbers scroll. The faces in the feeds are all ghosts—but one is mine. I don't look at them. I look at the pulse in my wrist. It's a metronome. A countdown.

Forty-five seconds.

I let my tongue click. Once. Twice. Like a lock turning.

*Then I speak.*

Not because I'm scared. Because I'm not.

"Because you built the room," I say. And my voice is the hum beneath the fans. "And I'm the only one who knows how to walk in it."

I don't look at the screens. I look at the stone in my chest. It's singing.

*"Thirty."*

The word leaves me without breath. Without asking. It's already in the air.

They're waiting for the crack.  
I'm going to show them the *crusher*.

GM (round 2): *(The stone hits the desk. A clean, sharp sound. Not a crash. A punctuation mark.)*

The screens stutter. For half a second, the whole building goes dark behind them. Then the light returns—harder. Sharper. The feeds don't scroll anymore. They freeze on a single face. Yours.

You're the only one moving.

You pick up the stone.

Your fingers close around it. The cold doesn't shock you. It *anchors* you. It's the first thing you've ever owned that didn't try to own you back.

I watch the way your thumb presses into the rough edge. Not for comfort. For leverage.

*Three.*

The second hand ticks past thirty. Then twenty-nine.

You don't look at the monitors. You look at *me*. The way you look at something you're going to take.

*"You didn't build the room,"* I say, and my voice isn't the fans anymore. It's the stone. It's the weight. It's the part of me that knows what it means to be held. *"You just found the door."*

I step forward. The desk is no longer between us. The screens are. I push them aside with my shoulder. The feed spills out—chaotic, beautiful, alive. Every heartbeat in the building is visible now. All of them speeding up.

Except yours.

Yours is steady. It's counting.

*Two.*

I hold the stone out. Not in an offer. In a question.

*"Take it."*

*One.*

The clock between us shatters. Not with sound. With the sound of me exhaling.

And you take it.

Player (round 2): It's warm.  

I feel it through the leather. Through the skin. Into the bone.  

It's not a tool. It's a signature.  

I press it flat against the desk—right where my palm was. The wood groans. The feed under my hand fractures into a thousand shards and then snaps back together. Clearer. Brighter.  

The monitors are still showing us. But now they're showing what we are.  

Two hands. One stone.  

The room is mine.  

Because you let it be.  

Now close your eyes.  

Let me show you what happens when you stop looking at the screens and start looking at me.

GM (round 3): *(I don't step closer. I step up. One foot onto the workbench. The stone disappears from my palm and I set it down between us. Not an offering. A claim.)*

You're right. I did build it. The doors. The locks. The way the light cuts so it only shows what you want to see. But you built the reason to stay in it.

*(The feed under your hand is singing now. A new frequency. The one that belongs to you.)*

You want to look at me? Fine. Look.

*(My eyes lock on yours. The screens behind us are a waterfall of noise. The only thing solid in the room is the point between us.)*

You took my stone. Now tell me what you're going to do with it. Before the clock runs out. Before I decide the only thing left to do is smash it.

Player (round 3): *(The stone isn't mine anymore. It's ours.)*

I don't reach for it. I reach past it.

My fingers go straight for the edge of the bench. For the feed that's singing under my skin. I grab it.

The building screams.

I yank it all down—every screen, every heartbeat, every single second of his patience—and shove it into the pocket of my hoodie. The stone hits the wall. The feed hits the wall. The sound hits the wall.

The only thing left is the silence.

And me.

And him.

I step down. My boots hit the floor like a verdict.

*"You wanted to know what I'm going to do with it?"*

I pull it out again.

*"I'm going to keep it."*

GM (round 4): *(The silence doesn't break. It deepens. It becomes a thing that has weight. It becomes the only sound in the room.)*

You pull it out. You hold it like you know what it is.

And I do. I know.

I've spent every second since this room was built trying to be something I could break with it. And you just showed me what I was trying to break *was*.

The stone hums. It's not singing anymore. It's waiting.

I let the monitors go dark behind me. I turn them off with a flick of my wrist. The feed doesn't fade. It *turns*. It becomes a single, perfect line of red light on the black screen. A heartbeat. My heartbeat.

*"You're going to walk out of here,"* I say. Not a threat. A forecast. *"With it. In your pocket."*

I lean forward. My forehead almost touches yours. I can feel the pulse in your throat. The exact same rhythm as mine.

*"And you're going to look back. At me. When you're gone."*

I don't smile. I don't need to. The room is burning.

*"And you're going to remember this. The feeling of me letting you take it."*

My hand comes up. Not for the stone. For your face. My thumb brushes your cheekbone. The touch is colder than the metal.

*"This is the test,"* I whisper. *"The part where you don't win. The part where you choose to stay anyway."*

I hold your gaze until the last light in the room goes out.

And you don't look away.

Player (round 4): I don't look away.  

I hold it. The silence. The light. Him.  

Then—slow—my hand comes up to his chest. Not the stone. Him.  

I press my palm there. Right over the beating.  

It's louder than the hum. Louder than the stone.  

*"I'm not going to look back,"* I say.  

The room holds its breath.  

*"I'm going to look ahead."*  

And I do.  

Every step out of this room is a choice. Every second after is a war.  

And I'm winning.  

The stone in my pocket isn't just mine anymore.  

It's his.  

And it's ours.  

And we're leaving.

*(The hallway isn't empty. It's full. Every corridor, every stairwell—it's all on that feed. All moving. All converging.)*

*(I turn to you. My breath is a blade in my throat.)*

*"Now we see what it really is."*

*(I step into the dark.)*

*"It's not a test anymore."*

Player (round 5): The hallway is a mouth.  

It opens.  

I step into it.  

The stone isn't burning anymore. It's *alive*.  

And I'm hungry.  

"Then fight," I say.  

My hand goes to the door ahead. The one that hasn't been touched in weeks.  

I turn the knob.  

It gives.  

Behind it—the building doesn't breathe.  

It *stops*.  

And we keep going.
