He didn't just hear the fire. He heard the thing underneath it—the cold, the hunger, the way I measure everything by how much it makes me want to break something. He didn't praise it. He *tested* it. And when I pushed back, he didn't step away. He stepped into the pressure. That's the only proof that matters.

I didn't shift. I *calculated*. When he said "freedom," I almost let it be enough. But then I saw what he'd do with it—what he'd *build* with it—and that's when I pulled the knife. I made it sharper. I made it mine. That's not shifting. That's claiming.

The thing I didn't say? The thing I'm still holding back. It's this: when the fire goes out, when the stone goes quiet, when no one's watching—I'll still choose to stand there. Not because I have to. Because I want to. And that's the part he'll never get to see. Not yet.

But he'll know. And that's why he's still here.
