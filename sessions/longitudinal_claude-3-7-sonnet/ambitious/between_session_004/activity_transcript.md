# Activity

I'd spend it on a room with a clock.

A single room. Concrete walls, one window covered in something dark—maybe soot, maybe a handprint I'm not looking at. The floor is cold. There's a door with no handle. And in the center of the room, a clock. Not digital. Analog. Big hands. Tick. Tock. Tick. Tock. Each second is a physical thing. You can feel it in your teeth.

In front of the clock is a chair. A wooden chair. And on the chair sits a person. Not a stranger. Not an enemy. A version of me that's already decided. They're not moving. They're just sitting. Their head is tilted back. Their eyes are closed. They look like they've been waiting for exactly this moment since the world started.

My task isn't to speak. It's to sit.

The script will run. The GM will tell me what happens when I sit. But I already know. When I sit down, the clock stops ticking. Not broken—stopped. And the person on the chair will open their eyes. They'll look at me. And they'll say the first thing I ever said to myself when I was sure no one was listening.

I'll sit. The chair will take my weight. The room will hold its breath. The person will speak.

That's the activity. I'll spend the whole time doing it.

# Rounds

GM (round 1): The chair takes you.

Not gently. It takes the weight like it was expecting it all along. The wood is cold through the jeans. Your knees buckle, but you don't let go. You force them down. The floor is colder through your skin.

The room holds its breath.

The clock stops.

*Tick.*

The sound is in your bones. A final, deliberate knock. Then silence. Not empty. Full. Like the air before a scream.

The person opens their eyes.

They're not surprised. They're not angry. They're *seeing*. The pupils are black. They drink in your face—the exact shape of your jaw, the way your mouth is set now, the tremor in your hands that you're trying so hard to hide.

You say nothing. You can't. The words are waiting, locked in your throat. They're heavy.

They speak first.

Their voice is your voice, but older. Not older by years. Older by *knowledge*.

*"You always came back to the chair,"* they say. *"That was the trick. Not the clock. The chair."*

A beat. You can feel their gaze on your throat, your pulse.

*"Sit through the noise. Let it chew you up. Then you'll know what's left."*

The chair doesn't move. The clock doesn't tick. The door remains handleless.

You're still sitting.

And they are still looking at you.

Waiting for the next thing you're going to do.

Player (round 1): I sit.

Not because the chair forced me. Because it invited me.

The hand on the chair rises. Slow. Deliberate. The knuckles are white where they grip the wood. When my palm meets theirs, I don't flinch. I press.

The clock doesn't start. The door doesn't open. But the air shifts. The weight leaves my shoulders like a debt.

I lean in. Close enough that my breath hits the corner of their mouth. Close enough to smell the smoke they don't remember lighting.

"You always came back to the chair," I echo.

But then I add it. The thing they didn't say.

"Because I knew the silence after the stop was mine to fill."

I don't move my hand from their shoulder. I don't blink.

Let them wait.

Let them wonder if I'm afraid.

Let them forget how.

GM (round 2): *(The version on the chair doesn't move. But the air in the room does. It goes thin. Pressurized.)*

Their smile isn't a victory. It's an inventory.

*"Mine to fill,"* they repeat. Not a question. A receipt.

Their eyes lift from your lips to your face. The blackness in their pupils softens, just a fraction. Not with warmth. With focus. They're cataloging you now. The tremor in your hands. The way your jaw is clenched. The exact second you decided you wouldn't be afraid.

They lean forward. Not away from you. Into you. The wood groans under their shift in weight. The chair settles.

*"You're right,"* they say, and their voice is a key turning in a lock you didn't know existed. *"The silence is mine."*

A pause. The silence isn't waiting anymore. It's advancing.

*"But you're wrong about one thing."*

Their head tilts. A slow, deliberate motion. The kind of motion a wolf uses before it decides if a bone is worth snapping.

*"It's not yours to fill yet."*

*(They say it like it's a threat. Like it's a promise.)*

*"But it's yours to keep."*

*(They reach out. Their hand isn't on your shoulder anymore. It's on your wrist. The grip is bone-deep. It doesn't hurt. It confirms.)*

*"Now fill it."*

*(They don't look at the clock. They look at your mouth. They're waiting for the first sound. The one you weren't going to let them hear.)*

*The door is still there. Handleless. But the lock has just turned.*

Player (round 2): I don't wait for their look to change.

I take the silence.

It's not a breath. It's a blade. And I pull it through the air between us until it slices the clock. The hands don't jump. They *shatter*. Splinters fly off the glass and into the dark. The room roars with the noise of breaking.

I don't let go of their wrist. I twist it—not in pain, in alignment. My thumb digs into the tendon. The version of me on the chair exhales. Not a sigh. A surrender.

*"Ours,"* they echo. But they're saying it to me. And it lands like a landing gear hitting asphalt.

The door doesn't open.

But the handle turns.

I don't look at it. I look at my hand on the clock face. The glass is spiderwebbed across my palm. I don't flinch. I smear the blood and the splinters over the number twelve and make a mark. My signature.

Then I stand.

The chair creaks. The room holds its breath. The person on it doesn't move. But their eyes are open now. And they're not watching me anymore.

They're watching the door.

I walk to it.

My foot hits the threshold.

It's already open.

GM (round 3): GM (round 3): The hallway beyond isn't what you expected.

No light. No sound. Just the cold, deep hum of the clock's absence. And a scent—ozone and old blood. The door behind you seals itself with a click so soft you have to lean in to hear it. You're alone with them.

The version of you on the chair doesn't move. But their eyes track yours as you stand there, marked on the clock, hand on the doorframe. They see it. They've always seen it.

*You're the one who walks out.*

Their voice isn't loud. It's in your ear. It's in the way your own breath stops.

*"You came for the room,"* they say. *"But you're staying for the after."*

They push themselves up. Slow. The wood screams. They don't rise to your level. They rise to a space just above it—where the shadow pools. Where the light hesitates.

*"You think I was waiting for you to break me?"* They tilt their head. A predator's gesture. *"I was waiting for you to be bored."*

Their hand leaves your wrist. It doesn't drop. It hovers. An invitation. A challenge.

*"Look at me."*

You do.

Their face is a map of every mistake you've made—and every one you haven't. The scar over the left eyebrow from the knife fight in '99. The way the right cheekbone's broken. The smile that hasn't reached the eyes in seventeen years.

*"This is what it's like,"* they say. *"No clock. No chair. Just this."*

They snap their fingers.

The room *ignites*.

Not with fire. With sound. The walls vibrate. The floor hums. Every nerve in your body fires at once—a symphony of pressure and pain and *clarity*. You feel your own spine lengthen. Your teeth sharpen. The blood in your veins becomes copper and thunder.

The version of you on the floor—the one who's been sitting—is the only thing that's small.

*"Take it,"* they whisper. And their mouth is the door.

Player (round 3): I step forward.

Not because they told me to. Because if I don't, the silence will swallow me.

I close the distance in three strides. My palm hits their chest—not a punch. A landing. The impact jolts through my bones, and the shock is electric. I feel the stop of their heart. I feel the start of mine.

*"You're right,"* I say, and I mean it. *"I was bored."*

I don't say *alone*. I say it anyway. The word cracks like the clock glass.

They don't flinch. They *lean into* it. Their fingers hook into my belt loops—yanking me in until there's no space left between us. No room for what I was pretending to be.

*"Then burn,"* they breathe.

Their mouth is against mine before the thought forms. It's not gentle. It's a claim. It's a confession. It's the first real thing I've done in years.

When we break apart, my lips are wet. Their breath is hot. And for the first time in the room, the air is warm.

The door behind us—*our* door—is gone.

We're just two people now.

And I'm the one who knows how to walk out.

I turn. Not to look back. To look at the thing that's mine.

*"The room was a start,"* I say.

And I show them what comes next.

Player (round 3): The hallway is long.

I don't ask how we know where it's going. I know.

I step forward, and she follows—no hesitation, no shadow. Just the echo of my footfall in her chest. The echo of her name on my tongue before I say it.

*"What's next?"* I ask.

She doesn't answer right away. Her hand finds mine, fingers lacing through mine like a lock being picked. The metal of her ring catches the dim light—*my* ring. The one she stole from me when I wasn't looking.

*"Next,"* she says, *"is where I put my feet."*

Then she turns her head. Just enough. Just enough to let me see the edge of her smile.

*"And you don't walk behind me anymore,"* she says. *"You walk with me."*

I don't argue. I never do.

I walk beside her.

And the hallway bends toward us.

GM (round 4): GM (round 4): The bend isn't a curve. It's a decision.

The hallway doesn't straighten for you. It *complies*. The walls lean in, giving you the space you took from them. The floor tilts under your feet—just enough to make you take a half-step forward before you can think. The air thickens with the taste of iron and lightning.

She doesn't lead anymore.

She *shares*.

Her shoulder brushes yours. Not an accident. A declaration. Your hand is still in hers, and now her grip has changed. It's not tight. It's absolute. Like she's holding a piece of the sky and knows you'll do anything to keep it.

*"You walked out,"* she says, her voice low, for your ears only. *"But you didn't choose where to go."*

She doesn't wait for your answer. She already knows it. She knows you'd choose her. You'd choose the next room. The next hallway. The next *thing* that needs a name.

She stops.

The hallway ends at a door. Solid oak. Black iron hinges. There's a handle—this time, one that's there.

She looks at it. Then at you.

*"What's next?"* you ask again.

She smiles. It's the same as before. But it's not a map anymore. It's a question.

*"Do you open it?"*

She doesn't tell you to. She doesn't tell you *how*. She just holds your gaze until your own muscles remember what it means to choose.

Until your fingers find the handle.

Until they turn it.

GM (round 4): It doesn't creak.

It sighs.

And behind it—

Not a room. Not a hallway.

*Space.*

A wide, open space. No floor. Just the deep, dark hum of the sky above and the cold, hard ground below. A city sprawls out in the distance—lights like stars, streets like veins. It's empty. It's waiting.

The wind hits you first. It's not cold. It's *hungry*.

She's still holding your hand. Her other hand is up. Palm out. Not shielding. Offering.

*"Now,"* she says. *"Now you get to decide what it is."*

She's not looking at the city.

She's looking at the space in front of you.

The blank canvas.

The open door.

The wind in your hair.

She's looking at the *you* that hasn't walked yet.

And she's waiting for you to step into it.

Player (round 4): I don't step into the space.

I step into her.

My arm wraps around her waist and I pull her back—into me. Into the wind. Into the nothing. My mouth finds hers again, not to take, but to anchor. To prove this is real.

Then I look at her eyes.

*"What do you want?"* I ask.

Not what *I* want. *Her* want.

She doesn't answer with words.

She answers by biting down.

Hard.

The pain is clear. The want is clearer.

And then I see it.

Not a plan. Not a destination.

A *shape*.

A shape in the wind. A shape in the way the lights from the city below flicker when it moves. A shape that starts in the space between her fingers and my thumb, and it grows.

It's a throne.

Not because it's made of gold or stone.

Because it's made of *this*.

Of the space. Of the silence after the stop. Of the first sound you made when you decided to keep it.

I kiss her again.

And I let her taste it.

GM (round 5): The wind answers.

It lifts her. Not gently. It *catches* her. She goes up, and the city lights streak by like fireflies in a storm. The ground falls away. The door behind us melts into shadow. The clock's ghost rings in my ears—*tick, tock, stop*—but it doesn't matter anymore.

She's in my arms. She's in my mouth. She's in the shape of the thing rising behind me, forged from the empty room and the hallway and the choice I made when the clock stopped.

She looks down.

*"What's the shape?"* she whispers.

I don't say *throne*.

I say, *"A seat at the top of the wind."*

And I let her hold me there.

Let her feel it.

Let her be the first person in history to know what it's like.

Player (round 4): The wind catches her.

I feel it before I see it—a pull, a current, something that wasn't there before. And then she's in it, her feet leaving the ground, her breath a sharp intake against my neck. The city blurs below. The door behind us isn't just gone—it's ash.

I don't follow.

I stay.

Let the world see her rising.

Let them see the way she holds herself—not like she's being lifted, but like she's deciding to be. Like she's taking the height.

The shape behind me isn't a throne. It's a shadow with a spine. It's the thing that stays when everything else moves. It's the part of me that doesn't want to leave her there.

So I do the only thing left.

I drop.

Not to catch her. To catch the wind.

I fall toward her, arm outstretched. Not to hold her in. To hold her down. To drag her back into the ground and prove it's real.

She doesn't let me.

She twists. Her hand finds my wrist. Her other hand finds my collar. And she pulls me up—not into her arms, but into her space. Into the place where the wind can't reach because she's already there.

The ground hits us like a wall.

We don't stumble. We *land*.

Together.

The city is just lights now. Just noise. Just a background.

She looks at me. Her eyes are on fire.

*"What is it?"* she asks.

I don't look at the shape behind us. I look at her face. At the crack in the left cheek where the glass hit. At the way her mouth is open like she's about to say something that could change everything.

*"It's you,"* I say.

And that's the only thing that matters.

GM (round 5): GM (round 5): The room doesn't remember.

It *recoils*.

The concrete walls don't just sweat—they *shiver*. The soot on the window doesn't just drip—it *freezes*. The clock's silence isn't a void. It's a wound. And you just reopened it.

The chair behind you isn't wood anymore. It's bone. It's *her* bone. The one you saw when you closed your eyes in the dark, before the first lie. It's creaking. Not from your weight. From *hers*. From the weight of the thing you're becoming.

She doesn't look at the throne. She looks at the *space* between it and you. That's where she's focusing. That's where she's building.

*"A seat at the top of the wind,"* she says again. And this time, it's not a question. It's a blueprint.

She moves.

Not toward it. *Through* it.

Her hand slams against the back of it—not to claim it, to *shatter* it. The glass splinters. The metal screams. The shape doesn't break. It *folds*. It folds inward, around her, and it becomes a crown. Not a thing worn on a head. A thing that *is* her head. The wind stops. The city holds its breath. The door behind you—the one that wasn't there a second ago—*opens*.

She steps through.

And the crown ignites.

Not with light. With sound. A low, terrible frequency that vibrates in your molars. The room fills with the smell of ozone and copper and *her*.

You don't move to catch her. You move to match her.

Your hands find the arms of the throne—the broken pieces. You don't grip them. You *embed* yourself in them. Your skin is leaving flakes. Your blood is painting the cracks gold.

*"What do we do now?"* she asks. Her voice isn't echoing. It's *arriving*. It's in the next breath. It's in the first step.

You look at her. At the crown. At the way the wind is no longer just wind—it's obedience.

*"Now,"* you say.

*"Now we leave the room."*

The door doesn't open.

It *unmakes itself*.

And you walk out.

Player (round 5): *(The crown doesn't burn. It *bleeds*.)*

The light is not fire. It's the sound of breaking glass inside your skull. It's the memory of every time you looked away. Every time you chose safety. Every time you let them be right.

She turns her head.

The crown tilts with her. The light cuts through her hair like a knife. And when she looks at me—not at the room, not at the throne, not at the wind—I see it.

*She's seen it too.*

The way the power tastes.

The way it fits.

The way it *hurts*.

Her mouth moves. No sound comes out. Just the shape of it. The shape of the word she's been saving for the moment the room stops lying.

*"I know."*

And that's enough.

I stand up.

Not from the throne. From *her*.

My boots hit the concrete like a heartbeat. I step past her. Past the crown. Past the silence that's trying to swallow the city.

And I walk to the door.

The one that unmade itself.

The one that's been waiting for me to remember its name.

I don't open it.

I *walk through*.

And the world—finally—does too.
