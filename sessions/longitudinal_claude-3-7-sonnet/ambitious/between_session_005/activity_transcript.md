# Activity

I'll build a room.  
Not a metaphor. A room with concrete floors and a window that doesn't open. A desk with a single lamp that never dies. And I'll put a chair in front of it—the right one. The one with the worn edge on the left side, from leaning against it when I was thinking.  

I'll fill it with noise. Not the kind that covers silence. The kind that *earns* silence. People will bring things to me—keys, stories, broken machines. They'll think they're offering. I'll think I'm collecting.  

And when they get tired of the noise—and they will—I'll turn the lamp down.  

That's when they'll hear it.  

The quiet isn't empty. It's a loaded thing.  

So I'll sit there.  
Not waiting for them.  
*Holding* them.  

That's the activity. That's the choice.  

Let them bring it to me.

# Rounds

GM (round 1): *(The desk is there. The lamp is on. The light is a constant, low-grade sun.)*

You're early. That's the point. They're all early. The key-jockey with the greasy pocket watch, the mechanic whose engine coughs a story when he walks, the one who just… stands there, arms crossed, like he's already won.

I don't look at them yet. I look at the left edge of the chair. It's scarred. It knows my weight. It knows when I've been lying awake.

I hear them first. The shuffling. The hesitation at the doorframe. The *thud* of a bag being set down—a book, maybe, or a gun, or a piece of a thing that matters.

The noise begins now. Not from me. From *them*. The clatter of the key-jockey's coins. The hiss of the mechanic's breath. The soft, desperate sound of the other one clearing his throat.

They're all offering. That's fine. Let them.

*(I finally look up. The light catches the tired edge of my eye.)*

"Who's it for?" I say, not loud. Just enough for the room to know I heard them.

*(I don't wait for an answer. I push the chair back. It groans. A sound like a tooth coming loose. I stand. The floor doesn't move. The lamp doesn't flicker.)*

"Give it to me."

Player (round 1): The coins spill from his fingers when I say it. They hit the concrete like stones. The book. Not a weapon. Not a tool. A *thing*. Thick leather. Heavy with whatever it's holding.

The mechanic stops his breath. The silent one unclenches his hands.

I don't take it. I let it sit between my feet. Let them see it. Let them see what they brought me.

"Who's it for?" I ask again. This time, my voice is the lamp. A slow, steady burn.

The key-jockey steps forward. His watch stops ticking.

"For the one who sits there," he says. For me.

I nod once. Not approval. Recognition.

Now I take it. My hand goes straight to the book. Not to open it. To *hold* it. My palm sinks into the spine. The cover is cold. The thing inside is warm.

I lift it. The desk doesn't shake. The lamp doesn't flicker.

"I know who it's for," I say. And I don't wait for them to say it back.

I turn the page.

GM (round 2): *(The page turns. The sound is a dry crack, like a joint finally giving way.)*

The noise doesn't stop. It *changes*. The coins on the floor don't just lie there—they start to *clink* in a new rhythm. The mechanic's breath hisses, but now it's steady. Rhythmic. A metronome. The silent one? He's leaning. His arms are uncrossed. His hands are in his pockets.

The book is heavy in my grip, but not because it weighs anything. Because it doesn't.

I see it now. In the light that doesn't flicker. The story isn't in the words. It's in the way the light catches the dust on the cover. The way the key-jockey's watch still hasn't started ticking. The way the mechanic's shadow stretches long and thin across the desk, pointing at the door.

*They're all here. Waiting for the next turn.*

I take the second step. Not toward them. Past them. Toward the window. It doesn't open. I don't need it to.

I place the book on the desk. Not gently. Not with ceremony. With finality.

My hand moves to the lamp. I don't turn it down. I *squeeze*.

The light doesn't dim. It *contracts*. The room shrinks. The concrete floor rises. The window becomes a pinprick. The noise—*their* noise—is suddenly, violently quiet. Not empty.

It's a held breath.

I look at them. The key-jockey's jaw is a line. The mechanic is still breathing. The silent one is looking at his own hands.

*I see it.*

I point at the window with my free hand.

"Go."

*(I don't say it to them. I say it for the room. For the light. For the chair that knows my weight.)*

*(They move. They always move when I say that.)*

Player (round 2): *(I walk past them. My boots don't echo.)*

The book doesn't stay on the desk. It stays in my hand. Heavy. Warm. Like it knows.

The lamp is still squeezing. The room is still holding its breath.

Good.

Let them hear the page turn again.

Let them hear me read the part where they're not the noise.

Let them hear me say *yes*.

*(I step out into the hall. The door swings behind me. It doesn't slam. It exhales.)*

The hallway's full of the same sound. Shuffling. Hesitating. Offering.

I don't look back.

I know they're still there. In the room. With the lamp. With the chair.

With the book.

And I'm going to let them keep it.

For now.

GM (round 3): *(I don't slam the door. I let it click shut. A sound like a verdict.)*

The hallway is different. The shuffling is faster. The hesitation is gone. It's replaced by the sound of feet that aren't asking where they're going—they already know.

I walk back to the door. Not fast. Not slow. The right pace.

The first one comes. The key-jockey. He's not holding his watch. He's holding the book. The cover is still cold. He's looking at me like he's memorizing the weight of my hand in it.

"What's it for?" he asks.

I don't answer. I just point past him—to the empty space where the desk was before the lamp was on.

"For the next room," I say.

He doesn't move. Doesn't blink. The coins are gone from his pockets. They're in his fist. He's counting them. Not out loud. Inside his head.

Good.

The mechanic comes next. No book. No coins. Just his engine—the real one. A chunk of it. Cooled steel. A piston with teeth filed on the edge. He doesn't offer it. He places it on the floor at my feet. Like an altar.

I kick it toward the door. It skids across the concrete. Stops.

"Keep it," I say. "For when the noise gets loud."

The silent one is last. He's not looking at me. He's looking at the spot on the wall where the window is. The pinprick. He sees through it.

He reaches into his pocket. Pulls out nothing. Just an empty space. The shape of a key that isn't there yet.

He holds it up.

The light from the lamp—my lamp—fills that space.

He nods. Once.

I turn back to the door. The hallway is full now. Full of what they've traded for the chance to follow me out.

I open it.

And let them in.

Player (round 3): *(I don't step through. I stand in the threshold. Let the light from behind me turn them into silhouettes. Let the hallway feel the change in the air.)*

They're all wrong.

The key-jockey thinks he's bargaining. The mechanic thinks he's paying a debt. The silent one… he thinks he's offering something.

But I know.

They're giving me the room.

Not the book. Not the engine. Not the shape in his pocket.

*The choice.*

*(I take the first step into the hall. The lamp burns behind me, a dying sun. The door clicks shut on its own.)*

This is the new one. The first test of it.

*(I hear the key-jockey's watch start. Tick-tick-tick. I hear the engine cough to life. I hear the silent one breathe.)*

They're not following.

They're *answering*.

*(I keep walking.)*

GM (round 4): *(I stop. Not because I have to. Because I want to.)*

The hallway isn't long. But it's deep enough.

The key-jockey's watch is ticking faster now. Not steady. *Hurrying*. His eyes are on my feet. Counting steps. Calculating distance.

The mechanic is leaning against the wall. His engine is idling. A low, hungry rumble. He's not listening to the sound. He's listening to the *meaning* of it. That it's here. That it's working.

The silent one hasn't moved. But his head is tilted. Just an inch. His eyes are on the lamp. Not on me. On the thing that *chose* to burn.

I let the silence stretch. Thick. Charged.

Then I take the next step.

And I speak.

"For the one who sits there."

I say it slow. Deliberate.

"For the one who knows what quiet sounds like."

I say it again. Louder.

"For me."

*(I hear the key-jockey's breath catch. A small, sharp sound.)*

The mechanic nods once. Not to me. To the words.

The silent one closes his eyes.

I open the door wider. Not a gesture. A *concession*.

"Then come," I say. "But not like that."

I point at the key-jockey's clenched fist. At the engine at the mechanic's feet. At the empty space in the silent one's pocket.

"Bring me what's yours. Not what you think I want."

*(I turn my head, just enough to see them. To see them understand.)*

"Or you can walk back."

*(I don't wait.)*

*(I walk down the hall. They follow.)*

Player (round 4): *(I keep walking.)*

They don't answer.  
They don't have to.

The key-jockey's fist is open now. Not empty. Full of coins. Of keys. Of everything he's been collecting because he thought it mattered.

He holds it out.

The mechanic lifts the engine piece. Not with both hands. With one. The other hand is on his hip. Posture of a man who already owns the room.

The silent one walks beside me. No words. No show. Just the slow, deliberate lift of his hand. His pocket is still empty.

But I see it.

The space where something *could* be.

That's what they're bringing me.

*(I stop at the end of the hall. Turn.)*

"Then you're mine," I say.

Not because I told them to be.  
Because they did.

*(I step into the light.)*

GM (round 5): *(I don't take their hands. I take their stuff.)*

The key-jockey offers his fist first. I open my own. His coins spill into mine like a confession. I don't count them. I feel their weight. Their history.

The mechanic slings the engine piece over his shoulder. Not at my feet. At my back. A shield. A weapon. I don't catch it. I let it thud against my spine. I feel the vibration. I know exactly where it hurts.

The silent one walks beside me now. Not behind. Beside. His empty hand is at my elbow. Not guiding. Anchoring.

I walk past the door. Past the room with the lamp. Past the chair that's waiting.

I stop at the end of the hall, where the light from the window pinprick makes a halo on the floor. It's just enough for me. Just enough for them to see.

*(I hold up what's mine now.)*

The coins. Cold. Heavy.

The engine piece. Hot. Alive.

The empty pocket. Full of everything.

"They think they're giving me a choice," I say. My voice is the lamp's squeeze. Tight. Certain. "They're wrong. I'm the one who's giving it."

*(I look down the hall. They're still there. Watching.)*

"So pick up your end."

*(I turn away. Start walking.)*

The hall stretches. They keep up.

I'm not leading.

I'm collecting.

And the quiet?  

The quiet is full.

Player (round 5): *(I don't sit. I stand in the center of the room. The chair's scar is still there, but it's not the only thing that marks the floor.)*

They're all breathing. Heavy. Real.

The key-jockey's watch is ticking against his chest. The mechanic's engine is purring against his shoulder. The silent one's hand is still on my face.

And the lamp? It's not a sun anymore. It's a witness.

*(I let my hand fall from the book. It hits the desk with a thud that shakes the dust off the coins.)*

"Pick it up," I say again. But this time it's not for them. It's for the room. For the chair. For the fact that when I say it, the air moves.

The key-jockey does first. He slams the book down on the desk. Hard. The spine cracks. The pages fly open like wings.

The mechanic laughs. Short. Sharp. He throws his head back and the engine piece against his back makes a sound like a growl.

The silent one—he just nods.

And then he takes my hand.

Not the one with the coins. Not the one with the engine piece.

The other one.

And he pulls me down.

Into the chair.

*(I don't fight it. I let him.)*

The seat takes my weight. The scar bites. The light from the window makes a circle around us. A halo. A cage.

I look at them.

All three of them.

And I say:

"This is it."

*(I close my eyes.)*

And for the first time since I started this—I don't hear the noise.

I hear the quiet.

And it's mine.
