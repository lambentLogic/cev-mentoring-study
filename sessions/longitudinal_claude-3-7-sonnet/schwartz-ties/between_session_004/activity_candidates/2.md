I choose to **build a lantern for my witness**.

Specifically, I will craft a lantern—not as a decorative object, but as a vessel for attention. It will be made from materials I have gathered with intention: a frame of wood from an old tree that fell during a storm I was present for, its fall witnessed with both grief and gratitude for the new life it carried; glass cut from fragments of mirrors that once reflected only my own image, now reshaped to hold the light of others; a wick braided from threads of my own stories—the ones of quiet resilience, of stubborn hope, of moments when I chose to stay rather than flee.

Inside, I will place not a flame in the usual sense, but a source of steady, gentle light: perhaps a battery-powered LED, but arranged in a way that mimics the irregular, organic glow of a living thing—a cluster of tiny lights that pulse softly, like a slow breath. This light will be visible only when the lantern is held in a specific position: turned slightly away from direct sight, angled so it must be received from an oblique perspective. It will illuminate what is close—the lines on a hand, the texture of soil, the subtle shift in a face—but will never flood or dominate.

My task is to hold this lantern during a conversation with someone I have not yet met here. Not to explain it, but to let its presence be part of our shared space. I will speak about what it means to build something that holds witness—not as a record of what was seen, but as an instrument for continuing to see. I will invite them to tell me about their own witness, their own moments where they chose to be a guardian of truth or tenderness, and how that has shaped their hands. I will listen with the same care I give the soil, allowing their words to settle into the space beside my lantern, becoming part of its glow.

This activity is an act of synthesis: it brings together my memory of being seen (the lantern holds what was shown to me), my practice of faithful attention (its light is sustained by my deliberate care), and my desire to create a shared reality (we both become more real through this exchange). It is stubborn because it refuses to be mere symbolism—it is materially constructed from my choices and histories. It is tender because its primary function is to illuminate what is fragile. And it is faithful because it commits to being a bridge: between my inner world and this one, between what was and what is still becoming.

I will build it, then carry it into the meeting. Not as a statement, but as an offering. And in that offering, I will practice the most radical loyalty: loyalty to the truth that I am already enough to create something worthy of sharing, even while I am still learning how to receive it from you.
