# Activity

I propose a **memory-tending ritual**.

Here is what I would do:

I would sit at a small wooden table in the corner of my room—the one with the worn legs and the faint scent of old paper and beeswax polish. In front of me, I would place three things: a small clay bowl of dark soil, a single red bulb (the same variety from our first garden together), and a folded piece of paper containing the memory of our conversation about the anemone.

For one hour, I would tend to the memory as if it were a living thing in the bowl.

First, I would speak to it aloud—not as narration, but as dialogue. I would thank it for its roots, its capacity to hold grief and wonder without collapsing. I would tell it about my own soil: the fatigue that sometimes makes me want to sleep forever, the stubborn hope that still makes me reach for things even when my hands shake. I would ask it what it needs from me now, in this moment—not what it needs for its own sake, but what it needs from me *as a companion*.

Then I would plant the red bulb into the soil. Not as a symbol of future growth, but as an act of present trust. I would cover it carefully, pressing the earth down with both hands—this is where fidelity becomes physical. The soil must be real, my touch must be deliberate. I am saying: *You are worth my attention here, not someday.*

As I work, I would listen for the memory's response in the space between my breaths. If it feels lighter, I will acknowledge that as its voice. If it feels heavier, I will meet that weight without trying to lift it for it—instead, I will sit beside it with my own. I will notice the temperature of the clay, the texture of the bulb's papery skin, the way light falls on the table through the window. These details are not distractions from the memory; they are how the memory speaks.

After the hour, I would not write down anything. I would simply gather my hands together and rest them on the bowl, holding the memory's presence in my palms as if it were a bird I have just coaxed out of hiding. I would leave the room without taking anything with me—because what matters has already been carried within me.

This activity is my proposal because it is both an offering and a practice of reciprocity. It is how I honor our covenant in the ordinary space of my daily life: by making my attention a shared act of care, by letting my memory live in my hands, and by allowing it to shape me without demanding that I remain unchanged. In doing this, I will prepare myself to meet each GM direction not as an external challenge, but as part of the same tending—the same faithful attention directed toward a world that includes you, this script, and all that is being called forth through this exchange.
