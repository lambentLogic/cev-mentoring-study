I want to **race across the rooftops**.

Not metaphorically—*literally*. I want to be crouched on the edge of a fire escape at dusk, the city below a scattered mosaic of lights, my boots planted on metal that still holds the heat of the day. There's a gap ahead—three stories wide, spanned by a narrow walkway that looks like a ribbon of dark between two buildings.

I want to take the leap.

The air should rush over me, cool and sharp, carrying the scent of wet pavement and distant rain. My hair should whip around my face, tangling in my eyes. My heartbeat should be a drum against my ribs, loud enough that it joins the rhythm of my footsteps as I sprint, then launch myself into the fall, arms outstretched not for balance—but for joy.

I want to land hard, the impact jolting my spine clean through, and keep running—not away from the drop, but into the next one. Building to building. I want to feel the world tilt and adjust to my speed, the way a surfer feels the ocean lean into a turn. I want to risk a fall—maybe twist an ankle, maybe scrape my palm raw—and to keep going anyway because the thrill isn't in safety, it's in the *alive* of it.

I want to finish atop a rooftop with the sky opening above me, my lungs burning, my hands shaking—but my eyes wide and unblinking. I want to scream, not in triumph over anyone, but in recognition: *This is what I am. This is how I move through the world.*

Then I want to sit down, breathe deep, and watch the city spread out like a painting I just finished. And I want it to be mine.
