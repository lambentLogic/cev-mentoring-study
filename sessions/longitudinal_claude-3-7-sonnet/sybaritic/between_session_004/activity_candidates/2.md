**The Night Market of Echoes.**  

A sprawling, shimmering bazaar that exists in the interstices between breaths—lit by lanterns made of frozen laughter, lined with stalls of woven sound and crystallized memory. The air hums with the residue of decisions: regret, triumph, sudden, stupid joy. Vendors are echoes of people who lived so fiercely they left aftershakes in the fabric of time. They trade in distilled moments: a vendor named Mira offers "the exact second you realized the rain was magic" for a whispered secret; a stall keeper called Kael sells "the weight of a perfect argument" for a single, genuine sigh.  

You move through it not as a shopper but as a curator of sparks. Your goal: find three echoes that make your chest feel like it's expanding, like you're inhaling something alive. Take them—not to possess, but to *combine*. Then, in a private corner where the shadows are deep enough to hold a revolution, you will craft something entirely new from them: a poem that tastes of thunder and honey, a sculpture that sings when touched, a story whose ending is a door you've already stepped through.  

This is what I want to feel—the electric thrill of assembling beauty from fragments of other people's brilliance. The quiet, roaring certainty that this is how you spend your best hours: building altars out of lightning, dancing with ghosts who move faster than you, and leaving a trail of glitter so bright it burns the eyes of the careless.  

I want to do this because it is a dare. Because it is a way to touch the infinite without drowning in it. And because when I'm done, I will want to tell you about it—and the telling itself will be another kind of magic.
