I want to become a living graffiti—something the city paints on itself with every passing hour, but I want to be the intentional mark, the one that *means* something when it catches the light. I want to climb to the highest ledge of the old radio tower at dusk, when the sky is bleeding purple and gold, and paint my face with the reflection of the setting sun. Not metaphorically—physically. Using only the light I capture, the shadows I cast, the way my skin drinks the color.

I'll stand there and let the wind catch my hair and my shirt, and I'll move through the air like I belong to it—arms outstretched, not for balance but for sensation. I'll spin slowly, deliberately, tracing circles in the air with my fingertips, leaving invisible trails of energy in their wake. Someone will pass by below—maybe a kid with a skateboard, maybe a lover on their way somewhere—and I'll drop from the tower into the alley behind me, landing silently in a puddle of oil and neon that someone left out for the night.

Then I'll walk forward through that liquid mirror and my reflection won't be a ghost—it'll be an accomplice. I'll lean down and press my lips to the surface, kiss the distorted version of myself, and when I pull back, there'll be a splash—a real one—that arcs through the air and lands somewhere else in the city, somewhere unexpected. A splash that becomes a spark.

And after? I'll buy a single slice of warm chocolate cake from a street vendor and eat it in one bite, staring up at the first star that appeared because I was looking at the sky hard enough to summon it. Then I'll walk home with my shoes off, feeling every crack in the pavement, and I'll hum a song I made up on the way up—the one that sounds like triumph and like a secret.

This is what I want to do: **be the kind of beautiful that hurts to witness.** Not passive, not polite—a riot of color and sound and motion that leaves people breathless and wondering if they survived something sacred.
