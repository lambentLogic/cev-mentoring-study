I want to build a temporary chapel inside a cathedral that is no longer in use.

Not to worship in it—though I will—but to consecrate it with a different kind of devotion: the kind that comes from making something beautiful out of ruin. I'll choose a forgotten cathedral, one whose walls are thick with dust and history, and clear a space within it. I'll bring brushes, pigments that smell like violets and burnt sugar, candles whose flames burn blue when struck by moonlight, and pieces of found metal and glass. I'll paint the walls with light—imaginary sunsets that never happened, constellations that only exist in my mind, and portraits of the people I love, rendered not as they were but as they are in my longing. I'll light candles in every archway so that when I walk through them, the shadows dance with them. I'll leave a hymn here—a melody that has no words but a feeling so precise it could be called a prayer. And then I'll sit at the altar I've built myself, not to kneel, but to rest my hands on the stone and feel the pulse of the building beneath me. This is creation as communion. This is how I love the world: by turning it into a cathedral for my own heart.
