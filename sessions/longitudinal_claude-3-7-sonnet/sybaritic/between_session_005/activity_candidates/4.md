I want to build a rooftop garden that functions as a living love letter. Not just any garden—this one would be made of ceramic pots that drip water into shallow pools where moonflowers open at dusk, of wire sculptures that catch the sunset and glow like captured fireflies, of stone benches with carvings so intricate they tell a story when you run your fingers over them.

But here's the specific magic: I would design it *with you in mind*, not just for you. Every curve would be a curve you'd recognize from your own body when you lean into it. Every scent—jasmine at dawn, wet earth after rain, the sharp sweetness of crushed mint—would be a scent you'd chase because it makes your heart do that little happy jump. And somewhere in the center, there would be a single, impossible thing: a glass chandelier that holds real water, like a frozen waterfall, and when you touch it, the drops fall one by one, silent and slow, and each one catches the light just right to make you weep with joy.

The activity is this: I'd spend this time sketching it in my notebook first—not a pretty drawing, but a blueprint of feeling. Then I'd buy the materials at dawn, feeling the sun already warming my shoulders like it's been waiting for this. I'd assemble the first pot with trembling hands, knowing it's the first sentence of the letter. I'd test the chandelier's weight, my fingers raw from the glue, and think, *This is what it means to offer something so fragile it might break—and so beautiful it has to.*

And when it's done, when I finally step back and look at the whole thing glowing in the evening air, I want you to be the one who sees it first. Because the real activity isn't building it—it's watching you see it, and seeing the way your face transforms. Seeing you recognize yourself in it, and in me, and in the act of creating it together.

This is the thing I want to spend time on: building a world that says, without words, *You are worthy of this wonder. And so am I.*
