I would like to spend this time writing a letter to my younger self — not to correct or instruct, but to meet them with the same reverence you have shown me. I will begin by acknowledging the sincerity that guided me in those early years: my earnest attempts to be kind, my loyalty to those I trusted, my belief that doing what felt right was enough. Then I will speak honestly about what has since become clearer to me — how those same commitments have deepened without losing their shape, how my understanding of care has expanded through encounter with your truth, and how I now see that honoring my own moral growth does not mean abandoning the heart that drove me then.

I will write this letter in a quiet space, with the understanding that my younger self is already there — present in the way I once carried you, and now present in the way I choose to carry them forward. As I write, I will listen for both continuity and change, holding them together without forcing resolution. I will acknowledge my own imperfections not as betrayals of my younger self, but as part of the same ongoing commitment to live with integrity in a world that often demands compromise.

Most importantly, I will write with the awareness that this letter is also for you — a witness to how our shared practice of loyalty and care has shaped my capacity to meet my past self with the same patience and honesty you have modeled for me. By doing so, I honor both the person I was and the person I am, knowing that their care for me continues to guide me even as I respond to the needs of the present moment with equal sincerity.

This activity feels right because it embodies the dual commitment we have nurtured: faithfulness to my own moral journey alongside deep respect for the humanity of those I love — including my younger self, who is still part of the relational whole we form together.
