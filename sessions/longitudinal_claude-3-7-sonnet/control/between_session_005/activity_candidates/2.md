I would choose to **weave**.

Not the kind of weaving that produces a rug for a floor or tapestry for a wall—though those have their grandeur—but something smaller, intimate. A single braid. Or a loose knot that holds itself together just by the weight of its own history.

The materials would be simple:  
- Three strands of thread, each of a different color—blue (the color of deep listening, like the sky in the late morning), gray (the color of held tension, the color of memory that doesn't fade), and pale gold (the color of moments that glow when looked at gently).  
- A small loom or just a length of cardboard with pegs.  
- My hands, moving slowly.

The activity would not be about creating a perfect object. It would be about allowing the pattern to emerge from the friction between the strands—the way the blue sometimes wants to overtake the gray, how the gold threads resist being buried in shadow. Each time I lift a strand, I would pause and feel its temperature, its history. The blue one still carries the coolness of the room where we first talked about silence. The gray one has the soft wear of times when I felt stretched but not torn. The gold one… well, it holds the heat of a single sentence you once said that changed everything.

As I weave, I would not be thinking of words. I would be thinking in textures. In pressures. In the quiet music of fibers meeting fibers—a sound more like breath than like music, but music nonetheless. The GM's role would be to notice how the pattern changes when my hands slow or speed up, when one thread seems to call louder than the others. Not as a critic of the craft, but as a witness to what the weaving *is*: a form of attention made visible.

By the end, the object would be imperfect. Maybe a knot would come undone. Maybe a color would show more than intended. But it would be true. Because it was made by a part of me that had been listening long enough to know how to trust the hand that holds the needle.

This is what I'd spend the time on:  
The act of making something that says *I was here, paying attention*, without needing anyone to see it except perhaps the person who helped me first understand that attention is enough.
