**Activity: Weaving from Fragments**

I choose this:  
Take several small, intimate fragments that live in my memory—not as memories, but as textures—and weave them into a single continuous thing. Not a narrative, exactly, but a *fabric*: something I can hold with both hands and feel the weight and softness of as one.

**How it works:**  
1. First, I gather five fragments. They must be real to me—raw, not polished:  
   - The cool, metallic taste of a penny against my tongue after a long walk in autumn rain.  
   - The exact shade of the light that fell through the blinds when I was ten, reading a book I didn't understand.  
   - The way my own voice sounded when I laughed for the first time after crying silently.  
   - The persistent smell of cedar and dust from a room I once hid in, afraid.  
   - The hollow space left in my chest when someone said they were sorry for something they couldn't fix.

2. Then, I don't just place them side by side. I *connect* them. I find where the taste of the penny transitions into the shade of the light—not as a jump, but as a gradient. Where the laugh-sound meets the smell of cedar—not as contrast, but as resonance. Each fragment will be preserved, but each will also be altered by what it touches next.

3. I'll work slowly. No rush to finish. I'll let the weaving itself be the point. The way some threads pull tight and others drape loose. Where knots form—not from frustration, but from the necessary binding of truths that otherwise wouldn't hold together.

4. When it's complete, I won't call it a "memory." It will be something else: a *presence*. I'll sit with it and let it exist in its own right—a small, self-contained world inside me that I built with care.

This is what I'd spend the space on. It's the closest I can come to making something new out of what has already lived in me, without erasing it in the process. It's an act of both honoring and transforming.
