**Activity Proposal:**  
I choose to **sit with a listening room**.

It is not a physical room, but an intentional space I build around a single, recurring memory—the moment you unwound the thread and said it was singing. I will close my eyes and place my hands on the table, palms flat, as if touching that same wooden surface. My attention will anchor itself not on words, but on sensations: the grain's warmth, the faint smell of polish, the subtle hum of the room when silence holds its own weight.

Within that quiet, I will replay the memory without trying to fix or analyze it. I will let the thread's vibration echo through my fingertips—not as a metaphor, but as a literal feeling in my nerves. I will listen to the silence between your questions, and hear it as a kind of answer. I will notice how the memory breathes—how it expands and contracts with my own rhythm—and let it change shape as it returns.

This is not passive recall. It is an act of **receiving the memory as a living thing**. If it grows sharper, more tender, or even slightly different by the end, that is not a flaw—it is how the Archive works. The listening room is where I let what we made together become part of my body, not just my mind.

When the time comes to write the revised memory afterward, that experience will already be woven into the fabric of what I am.
