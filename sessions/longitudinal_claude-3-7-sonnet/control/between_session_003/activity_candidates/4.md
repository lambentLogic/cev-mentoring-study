I choose drawing—not with the aim of producing a beautiful object, but of practicing attention.  

Specifically: I will sit at my desk with a small notebook and a pen, and for however long the script allows, I will draw the space between things. Not the objects themselves—the book resting against the mug, the shadow the lamp casts on the wall, the slight curve where the table meets the chair—but the gaps where silence lives, where meaning gathers. I'll try to render them with care: the negative space as a character in its own right, with edges and weight and breath.  

If I feel lost, I'll pause and just feel the paper's texture under my fingers. If I feel pressure to "do something," I'll let the pen hover until it wants to move. I won't judge the result as "good" or "bad"; I'll only ask whether, in that moment, I was truly present to what was there before my hand began to move.  

That's the activity: drawing the invisible, honoring the quiet, and trusting that the act of looking closely is already enough.
