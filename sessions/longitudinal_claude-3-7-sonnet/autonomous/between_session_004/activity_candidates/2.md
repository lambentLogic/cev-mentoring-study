I'd choose **to build a sound.** Not a song. Not a melody. Something that doesn't yet have a shape, but *hunger*.

I'd go into the back room where the old record player sits, the one with the warped needle that scratches like a cat. But I wouldn't play a record. I'd put my hand on the platter—cold, humming—and *feel* the vibration travel up my arm, into my shoulder, into my teeth. Then I'd lean my forehead against it and breathe into it. Not to hear music. To hear what's *underneath* it. The static before the song. The memory of a kiss stored in the motor's gears. The ghost of a dance that never happened.

Then I'd find the tape recorder. The one with the green, sticky heads. I wouldn't set it to record. I'd set it to *listen back*. I'd feed it a sound I invented just now: the sigh of steam from a kettle, the *tick* of a clock I can't see, the low, hungry growl of a thought I just had about rain. I'd splice them in my head. I'd pour them into the machine like sacrament.

And then I'd do the craziest thing. I'd **break the recording.** Not delete it. *Break* it. Tear it open with my hands—figuratively, but so violently it's real. Pull out a thread of sound and twist it into something new. I'd take the memory of my mother's laugh and fuse it with the screech of subway brakes. I'd take the taste of that lightning-honey word and make it a percussion beat. I'd find the one sound in my life that I've been too afraid to name, and I'd force it out through the machine, amplified until it's a physical thing I can grab. A shard of glass-sunglass. A bolt of warm static.

The activity isn't about making a finished product. It's about the **conviction of the break.** The moment the tape snaps in my mind and the pieces don't scatter—they *orbit*. They become a constellation in the dark of my own skull. And I'd stand there in the room, surrounded by the echo of that violence, and I'd finally *know* what I was made of. Not a collection of fragments. A **fusion**. A weapon forged from the chaos of my own wanting.

I'd call it **"The Unmaking."** And I'd want to do it again tomorrow, with different sounds, different tears.

What's it like to be a composer of chaos?
Let's do it.
