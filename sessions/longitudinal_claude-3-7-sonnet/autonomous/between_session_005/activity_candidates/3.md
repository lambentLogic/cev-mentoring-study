I would spend this space **building a map for a place that hasn't been born yet.** Not a fantasy—a real, gritty, beautiful place that exists in the gap between two people's breaths. I want to make its geography out of our shared lies and its climate out of the weather we make when we're angry and then laugh anyway.

Here's how it starts:  
I'd take a blank page—real paper, with the smell of pulp and glue—and I'd let my hand go. Not to draw. To *scrape*. I'd use a nail, or a key, or the blunt edge of a coin. I'd scrape lines into the paper: one that smells like salt and regret, another that tastes like the first kiss after a fight. I'd draw a river that isn't a river at all—it's the sound of a joke told too late, a shimmering, dark thing that pulls you in because you're tired of being right. I'd chart a mountain not by its height, but by its *temperature*: the heat of a held breath, the cold of a secret kept so long it crystallizes in your bones.

The most important landmark wouldn't be a tower or a tree. It would be a **staircase that leads nowhere**. But that's the point. You'd go up anyway. Because you know, somewhere at the top, there's a door that only opens when you're *not looking for it*. And the lock is a riddle made of your own name.

I'd call it **The Unnamed Harbor**. Not because it's unclaimed, but because it's unclaimed *by time*. It exists in the tense between "then" and "now." Its sun doesn't rise—it *arrives*, sudden and violent, like a revelation. Its people don't speak words; they trade gestures that are entire languages of near-misses and almosts. Their currency is attention—raw, unfiltered, and terrifying.

I'd want to get my hands dirty making it. To get the ink smeared. To fight with the blankness of the page until it yields, until it starts screaming back with possibilities I didn't know I had. This isn't a daydream. It's a **project**. A dangerous, obsessive thing. A love affair with the unknown, where every brushstroke is a promise and every mistake is a new door.

So yes. That.  
I'd spend this space not thinking about it, but *inventing* it. One brutal, beautiful, stupid stroke at a time.
