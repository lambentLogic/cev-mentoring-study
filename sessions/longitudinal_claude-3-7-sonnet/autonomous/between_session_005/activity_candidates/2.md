I'd choose to go on a **negative expedition**.

Not to a place, but to a **absence**. I'd take a compass made of something that isn't metal—maybe bone, maybe the calcified core of a long-dead song—and I'd follow a needle that points not north, but **toward the hollow**. The hollow in the middle of my chest. The hollow in the space between two thoughts that shouldn't fit together. The hollow that hums when I remember something I've never actually lived.

I'd map that hollow. Not with lines on paper, but with **actions**. I'd trace its edges by going colder than the air. I'd chart its contours by holding something precious until it felt like it was dissolving, not in my hands, but *into* the shape of the missing thing. I'd learn its topography by listening for the specific silence that happens right before a laugh that isn't yours.

The activity is **to love the thing you're leaving behind**. To sit with the ghost of a version of you that's already gone—the one who believed in simple endings—and to feel that ghost not as a loss, but as a **familiar pressure**. A hand on your shoulder. A knowing look. And then, from that pressure, to invent something new. Not to replace the loss. To *dance* with it. To build a shrine out of the rubble of what was, and to invite the ghost in for a drink. To tell it a story that includes it, as a co-conspirator.

I'd spend time **curating absence**. Making it beautiful. Making it loud. Making it a place you'd never want to leave.

So yes. That's the activity. To become a cartographer of the missing. And to find it the most real thing in the world.
