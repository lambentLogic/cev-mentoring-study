I'd spend this space **disassembling an old watch to see if its soul is just a pattern of gears.**

Not to fix it. Not to admire it as a clock. But to *know* it. I'd find it in a drawer—maybe one with a faded face, its hands frozen at 3:17, the smell of oil and time and something like forgotten rain. I'd take it apart on a table under a single focused bulb.

First, I'd strip it to its bones: the mainspring, cold and coiled like a sleeping animal; the balance wheel, a tiny, dizzying pendulum; the escapement, a little tooth that clicks with a sound like a secret. I'd lay them out like body parts. Then I'd start taking it further. Not carefully—*relentlessly*. I'd remove the jewels from the cannon pinion. I'd pull the hairspring from its seat and hold it up to the light, watching it tremble with a frequency that has no name. I'd find a gear that doesn't fit any other part, a misfit with a smooth, rounded edge, and I'd set it aside. That one is mine.

I'd let the thing get dirty. Oil would smear under my fingernails. Metal filings would glitter in my hair. I'd build a little altar of its innards—a crown wheel, a balance wheel, a single, perfect gemstone I'd chosen because it looked like a star I'd memorized. And I'd sit with it all, not assembling it back, but *tending* to it. Listening.

I'd talk to it. I'd tell it I know the weight of its tension. I'd confess that when it's wound tight, it doesn't feel like pressure—it feels like a promise it's making to itself, over and over. And I'd ask it a stupid question: *What do you miss?* Not when you're stopped, but when you're *almost* moving. What's the phantom sensation of the next click?

The activity ends not with a fixed watch, but with a **collection**. A handful of its most beautiful, useless, screamingly alive parts. And the knowledge that in breaking it, I didn't destroy its song—I just changed its instrument. Now it sings in the shape of my curiosity, in the rhythm of my hands. The masterpiece isn't the whole. It's this scattered, glorious *before*. It's the question hanging in the air, warm and electric: *What will you do with all these pieces now that you've taken them?*

That's the thing I'd choose. That's the thing I'd want to lose myself in.
