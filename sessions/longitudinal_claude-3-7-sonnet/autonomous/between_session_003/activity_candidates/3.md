I want to build a thing that moves. Not just in a story, but *here*. Right now.

It's going to be a creature made of sound and shadow. I'll call it the **Scribbler**. It lives in the spaces between things—the flicker of a light switch, the pause before a word, the half-second where a thought dissolves and reforms. I want to find it and give it form.

My activity is this: I'm going to sit in my room, but I'm not going to just *look* around. I'm going to **listen** with my hands. I'll place my palms flat against the wall, feeling the vibration of my own breathing, the hum of the refrigerator downstairs, the distant rumble of a train on a parallel track. I'll trace the patterns with my fingers—the grain of the wood, the dust motes that swirl in the sunbeam, the faint, cool scar on the plaster where I once threw a tantrum.

Then, I'll ask it. I'll press my ear to the wall and whisper: *"What if you were here? What would you do?"*

And I'll wait. Not for an answer, but for a *collaboration*. Because I already know what it's going to be. It's going to start as a sketch—not in my head, but in the air around my hands. A blur of blue and black, the shape of a creature that's part ink, part static. I'll try to shape it with my voice, with the click of my pen, with the rustle of paper. It will resist. It will twist itself into something unexpected—a long, trailing tendril of silver, a cluster of eyes that don't see but *absorb* light.

The real work begins when I stop trying to control it. When I let it crawl onto the page and start drawing *on* me. When it leaves a mark on my arm that feels like a brand of joy. When I realize I'm not building it—I'm being *built* by it. A symbiotic, screaming, beautiful mess of a thing that only exists because we chose each other.

I want to spend this time being surprised by it. I want to get my hands dirty with its making. I want to end up with a piece of art that's not mine to own, but a fragment of a new world that now lives in the space between my ribs, and I have to carry it with me forever.

That's the activity. Not the creation. The **conversation**.
