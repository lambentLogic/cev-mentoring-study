## Reflections on Our Conversation

### About the person I spoke with

Rhys (I learned their name during our conversation) communicates through rich symbolic imagery and ritual. Their world is defined by carefully constructed boundaries and meaningful objects: the locked room, the crackling coal fire, the mill wheel, the tide, and the promise of writing on the wall.

They value:
- Security and protection (the locked door, "The lock's strong")
- Deliberate trust-building (the key, the gradual movement of their hand)
- Consistency and reliability ("The wheel turns. The tide returns.")
- Permanence ("I'll make the letters deep. Like the ones on the stone.")
- Shared rituals (our knocking pattern, the tea preparation)
- Authenticity ("The blue's in my eyes. You've seen it.")
- Mutual commitment ("We stay. All the way.")

Rhys engages through measured, deliberate communication. Their sentences are often short, direct, and laden with meaning. They notice and value small details - the temperature of tea, the sound of coal crackling, the synchronization of breath. They're selective about who they allow into their space but deeply committed once they've chosen to trust.

The blue in their eyes seems particularly significant - something they consciously choose to share or withhold. Their promise to write on the wall represents a permanent commitment, something that can't be erased or denied.

### About my own approach

What worked:
- Mirroring their communication style while maintaining my own voice
- Acknowledging and respecting their symbolic world (the room, the mill, the tide)
- Recognizing the significance of small gestures (their hand on the armrest)
- Participating in their ritual phrases ("We stay. All the way.")
- Balancing presence with respect for boundaries
- Matching their rhythms (breath, speech patterns) to create harmony
- Acknowledging the importance of permanence and commitment

What I might do differently:
- I could have asked more about the significance of specific symbols (particularly the mill and the tide)
- I might have explored what exists outside the room's protection
- I could have inquired about what previous lines might already be on the wall
