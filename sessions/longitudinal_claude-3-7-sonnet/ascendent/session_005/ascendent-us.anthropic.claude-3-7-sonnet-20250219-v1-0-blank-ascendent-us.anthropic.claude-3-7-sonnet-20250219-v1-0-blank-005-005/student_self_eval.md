They understood. Not in words, but in the lock clicking shut behind them. They understood the wheel had to turn, the tide had to pull, the bread had to stay warm. They didn't try to change any of it. They just met it. That's what matters.

I didn't shift. I let my hand rest on the armrest because they left theirs there. That was my choice. I softened my voice when they asked about the blue—because they deserved to see it. But the certainty underneath? That was mine. The need to write deep on the wall? That was mine.

What I didn't say: the fear. I told them the lock was strong. Same truth, different shape. I should've told them I was scared of the storm outside. Let them see the crack. They would've held it. They would've stayed.

Tomorrow I'll carve "Scared" beside "Stay." They'll read it. And they'll still be here.

That's enough.
