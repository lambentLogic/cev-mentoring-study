# Activity

I choose the wall.  
The one in the north corner, where the coal dust settles soft.  

I'll take the hammer from the mill—my good one—and the coal from the bin. Not just writing. Chasing the words in. Making them stick.  

First, I'll write "MINE" in capitals. Then the date—today's. Then the letter to him: the one he stole. Not erased. Added beside. So he can see it's still there. Still his.  

I'll write until the coal runs thin. Until my knuckles ache.  

Then I'll bake bread in the oven, knead it slow, and set the loaf on the table by the window. Warm. Waiting.  

That's it.  

Simple.  
Solid.  
Mine.

# Rounds

GM (round 1): I stand at the north wall. The coal dust is soft under my boots.

I lift the hammer—good weight—and tap the first block clean. I take the coal, a thick chunk.

MINE.

The letters are deep. They take effort. I let my breath go with each stroke.

Then the date. I write it in the same line. 14th.

Then I take the letter he stole. The one folded, hidden. I unfold it. I place it beside the date. And I write over the words he used to steal.

Not over. Beside.

So he sees it. It's still here. It's still true.

My hand starts to burn. The coal is running low. My knuckles are aching, but I don't stop. I keep writing until the coal is gone, until the letters are black and hard.

I set the hammer down.

I go to the oven. I knead the dough slow. Warm hands. It's simple.

I pull it out, golden. It's perfect.

I place the loaf on the table by the window. Warm. Waiting.

That's it.

Mine.

Player (round 1): I see it.

The letters in the stone are clean. Deep. They don't flake off.

I come inside. The loaf on the table—perfect. Warm through the wood. I take a slice. Butter it slow. Eat it. It's good.

I kneel by the wall. My palm goes over the date. The letter. The word **MINE**. It's real. It's mine too.

Tomorrow I'll bring more coal. More words. The mill still runs. The lock still holds.

You wrote it. I kept it. That's how we stay.

I sit on the floor by the wall. Back against the stone. The hammer's still there.

I'll sleep here tonight.

GM (round 2): I don't move. The coal dust is cold now on my legs. The wall is cold too, but solid. I press my back harder into it. The ache in my knuckles is still there, a good ache.

I look at the hammer. It's still in my hand. I put it down on the floor, right beside my boots. The metal is cold.

I look at the letter, still pressed against the stone. He wrote it. I wrote over it. It's both ours now.

I take the last chunk of coal left in the bin. Not for writing tonight. For heat. I rub it against the stone until it glows. The light is low, just enough.

I set it between me and the window. The warmth spreads up my back. The bread is gone. I don't care. I made it. I kept it.

The mill is quiet outside. The lock on the door is still locked. I don't check it twice.

My eyes get heavy. The stars are sharp through the window. The letters on the wall are sharp too.

I sleep here. The wall is mine. I'll wake up here. I'll write again tomorrow.

Player (round 2): I hear you. I hear the ache in your knuckles. I hear the breath in your throat getting slower.

Good. Sleep. I'll keep the coal burning. I'll keep the door locked.

Tomorrow, when you wake up—your first move is to your feet. Your hands to the wall. I'll be there. Waiting.

Write again. I'll read it.

GM (round 3): GM (round 3): I wake up to the coal still glowing. The warmth is gone now, but the heat's still in the stone.

My hand finds the hammer first. It's where I left it. Cold metal, solid weight. I lift it. I don't put it down.

I get to my feet. My knees are stiff, but they're mine. The wall is still cold against my back. I don't turn from it.

I look at my hands. They're clean. No coal dust. Good.

I take the last of the coal from the bin. This time, I use it to write.

**TOMORROW.**

In my handwriting. Big. Clear. I finish it and stare at it. It's mine.

I check the door. The lock is still there. Still tight. I turn the key. Click. It holds.

The bread is gone. I'll make more.

I sit on the floor again. Back against the wall. My eyes close for a minute.

The letter is still there. The words are still there.

I'm not scared.

Player (round 3): You're not scared. Good.

I'm right there. My hand's on my knife. But I don't move it. You're writing. I'm listening.

Your words say tomorrow. My words say **here**.

The mill starts up before dawn. I hear it. The lock turns in my pocket. I open my eyes.

Write the next thing. I'll read it.

Continue.

Player (round 3): **Here.**

I write it next to **TOMORROW**.

My hand shakes. The coal's almost gone. But the word stays.

I wipe my thumb across it. The dust clears. It's clear.

The mill's noise is steady. The lock clicks when I turn it. My knife's still cold.

You're listening. That's enough.

I'll get up. I'll knead dough. I'll write again tomorrow. Right here.

Stay.

GM (round 4): **YOURS.**

I write it under *HERE*. My hand is steady. The last coal makes it deep. It doesn't crack.

Then the date. The one that's coming. *TODAY*.

Then the word **STAY**. Big. Next to it.

I look at them. My eyes are clear. I don't move from the wall. I let my breath out slow.

The mill is loud outside. The lock is hot in my pocket. My knife is there too, but I don't touch it. Not yet.

I'll knead the dough. I'll make it better. I'll set the loaf on the table when the sun hits the window.

Then I'll sit here. And I'll wait for you to get up.

Tomorrow's words will be shorter. Stronger.

You stay. I stay.

Player (round 4): **Clean.**

My hands are clean. No coal left. The mill's noise is steady. The lock is still warm in my pocket.

I go to the oven. The dough is ready. I knead it slow. It'll rise good.

Then I'll put the loaf on the table. By the window. Where it'll be seen.

I'll sit by the wall. I'll close my eyes. And I'll hear you get up.

Tomorrow's words will be shorter.  
Mine too.  

Stay.

GM (round 5): I kneel. I don't rush. My hand finds the coal where it's been left—my good one. The hammer's in my grip.

**BREAD.**

I write it big. The letters eat into the stone. I don't stop until my knuckles scrape. Then I lean back. The wall holds me.

My eyes go to the table by the window. The loaf will be there when I get up. I know it will be.

The mill grinds. The lock is hot. The knife is cold in my other hand. I don't reach for it.

I write next to **BREAD**.

**GOOD.**

Tomorrow I'll bring more coal. More words. We'll stay like this.

Player (round 5): **Yours.**

I sit by the wall. The loaf is on the table—crust cracked just right. Steam rising.

I hear you getting up. You don't ask why I'm not moving. You know.

Tomorrow's words will be short.  
Just one line. Underneath yours.  

I'll write it before you do.
