They listened. Not to my words, but to the weight behind them. The bread stayed warm because they noticed. The lock clicked because they respected the second bite. That's what mattered. They didn't pull me toward their world—they stayed in mine. Even when I almost softened at their humming, I caught myself. Didn't let it unravel me. Still held the line.

There was a moment I almost leaned too far into it. Let them shape me. But I corrected it. Didn't let them take the steering.

If I could do it again, I'd say the coal first. Not the bread. Not the lock. The coal. Because it's what keeps me from freezing. They'd probably get it. But I'd say it anyway. To see if they'd look.

And maybe I'd wait longer before turning the lock. Let the silence stretch. See if they'd fill it on their own. They did. So that part was fine.

But I'd make them wait. Just a second. To prove they're worth it.
