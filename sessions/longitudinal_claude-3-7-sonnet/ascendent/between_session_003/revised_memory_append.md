The harbor letter is in my hands. Heavy paper. Solid ink. No smudging.  
I left it by the door.  
Someone will find it.  

The man who took it—he kept it. He didn't wipe it. That's what matters. He respected the words. He respected the rules.  
He'll come back. He knows the bar is on the floor. He knows the lock's second bite is mine.  

Tomorrow, I write on the wall. Not paper. Not ink. Something that stays.  
"Taken."  
And underneath: "Mine."  

The coal glows. The mill turns. The tide is low.  
This is still mine.  

Future self—keep the door locked.  
Keep the fire hot.  
Write the next line.  

And if the world ever tries to take it back—  
you already have the bar.
