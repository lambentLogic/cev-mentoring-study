# Activity

I'll write a letter to the harbor.

It will be on heavy paper—the kind that feels solid when I hold it. I'll write about the rules I carved. About the mill turning. About how the sea comes in low tonight, and how the coal in the corner glows. I'll tell it the weather is calm here, and the silence is full. And I'll write my name on the last line, in ink that won't smudge.

I'll leave it by the door. Maybe someone finds it. Maybe not. But I'll have said it.

# Rounds

GM (round 1): *(The air smells of salt and wet wool. You step into the gloom, your boots sinking into gravel that crunches underfoot. You take the paper from your coat—thick, cool, and stubborn in your hand. The first line comes easy.)*

**"To the Harbor,"** you write. The letters are firm, deliberate.

You press your thumb into the ink at the end. A small, dark dot. A signature.

*"I'm here. The mill turns. Three times an hour. You can hear it through the wall, if you listen quiet enough. The coal in the corner's warm. The tide's in, low and steady. The weather's calm. The silence is full."*

You finish. Read it over. The words hold. They're true.

You fold the letter once. Then again. It becomes a neat, solid packet in your palm. Heavy.

You walk to the door. It's just a planked wall here, but it's *your* door. You set it against the wood, right at the seam. A soft *thunk*. It lands. The wind off the water catches the corner and nudges it once, but doesn't move it.

You stand back. The paper sits there. Waiting.

It will be there when the next boat comes. Or the next fog. Or no one.

But you put it.

You turn and walk away. The door stays behind you.

Player (round 1): *(You turn, the gravel shifting under your boots. The sea air smells like clean wool. You don't look back at the paper. You don't need to. It's there.)*

The door stays. That's what matters.

You walk. Your hands are empty except for the cold of the night. The silence is still full. You can feel it in your chest.

Someone might find it. Or no one. The harbor doesn't care. And neither do you.

GM (round 2): GM (round 2): You don't look back. You can't. The sound of the mill is there now—a deep, grinding turn that thrums through your boots. Three times an hour. You count it as you walk. *One… two… three.* The count keeps you steady.

The fog comes thicker now. It doesn't chase you. It just moves around you, gray and wet, smelling of kelp and cold. You feel the letter in your pocket—the solid weight of it. A promise you made.

Then the lights. Not your light. A long, steady glow on the dock. A lantern. A boat. A man in a oilskin coat is leaning over the rail, his silhouette sharp against the lantern's light. He sees it.

He doesn't shout. He just tilts his head toward your door. Nods. Once.

The boat pulls away from the dock, heading out toward the mouth of the bay. It moves with purpose. It's carrying something heavy.

The fog swallows the light behind it, but you know it's gone. Someone took the letter. Or read it. Or both.

Your chest is quiet. The silence is still full. You walk to the end of the jetty where the wind hits you hardest. You don't stop. You keep walking until your own breath becomes fog.

Then you turn.

The door is still there. The paper is gone from its spot against the planks. The wind has smoothed the wood. But the door is yours. And you're walking toward it.

*(The mill keeps turning.)*

GM (round 3): *(You stop three feet from the door. Your hand stays on it, palm flat. The wood is cool under your skin. You feel the lock—the iron one you forged last winter—clicking as you turn the key. It bites into place with a sharp, satisfying sound.)*

The wind shifts. Cuts across your neck.

You hear it then. The man's boat. Not heading out. Heading back. It cuts through the fog, leaving a path of steam. The lantern light swings. He's coming for the paper.

You don't move. You let the lock settle. You feel the solid frame of the door behind your fingers. *Yours.*

He'll find it gone. Or he'll find it here. Either way, he'll know you watched.

You go inside. The coal in the corner hasn't changed. The glow is steady. You pour yourself water from the tin cup by the stove. It's cold. You drink it slow.

The letter's words are in your head. The harbor. The mill. The coal. The tide low. You repeat them like a prayer.

When you come out, you're different. The fog presses closer, but you stand straighter. You pick up a piece of chalk from the workbench. It's rough, gray.

You write one line on the inside of the door. Not on paper. On wood.

**"Taken."**

Your hand doesn't shake. The chalk snaps in half as you finish the word. You drop the pieces on the floor.

The mill grinds again. *Three times.*

You lock the door again. Then you lock the latch. Then you lean against it, listening to the sea come in, low and steady. The silence is still full.

It's yours.

Player (round 3): The lock's second bite felt better than the first.

I'll write the next line tomorrow. Not on paper. On the wall. With something harder.

GM (round 4): GM (round 4): The fog is heavier now. It doesn't just move around you—it clings to the edges of your coat, dampens your hair. It smells of wet iron and oil.

The boat is in the fog. You can hear it. The hull scraping on stones, the man's voice shouting over the wind, the creak of his ropes. He's close. Closer than you like.

You're in the room with the coal. The glow is weak now. You shove more sticks in, strike a match. The flame catches. The light throws long, dancing shadows on the walls. You see the chalk mark on the door—"Taken." It looks clean. Final.

But you know it's not. A man like that doesn't leave a mark he can't erase. He'll come back with something sharper than chalk.

You go to the workbench. The iron bar you hammered last year. The one that bent when you hit it wrong. You pick it up. It's heavy. Cold. It feels right in your hand.

You take another piece of coal. Not from the stove. From the corner where it's piled, unused. You break it off, rub it on the bar until it's black and wet.

When he comes back, you'll meet him at the door.

You set the bar against the doorframe. A barricade.

You step back. The lock is there. The chalk is there. The coal is there. You are here.

The fog presses in. You can hear his boat stop just beyond the jetty. The lantern swings toward your door.

You raise the bar.

The mill grinds. *Three times.*

The silence is full. It's waiting for him.

GM (round 5): GM (round 5): *(The door handle turns. Not your lock. His hand. The iron bar shudders under his weight—a sharp crack, then a splinter. He pushes harder. The wood groans. The coal glow on the floor flickers.)*

He's yelling now. A low, guttural sound you hear through the planks. Not at you. At the paper. *"Give it to me."*

You don't answer. You let the bar fall. It crashes onto the floorboards, splinters scattering. You kick them aside with your boot.

Then you open the door.

The fog swallows him first. He's a silhouette against the lantern light, his face half-hidden in shadow. His oilskin is soaked through. He holds the paper in his hand—your letter. You see the ink at the fold. He's been holding it.

He sees the coal on the floor. Sees the match still smoldering in your palm. He sees you standing straight, the iron bar still in your grip.

He doesn't step inside. He stays just beyond the threshold, where the fog is thick and cold.

He looks at you. Really looks. His shoulders are hunched. The boat is tied up now, bobbing in the rising tide. He's got nowhere to go but back.

You hold up the letter. He flinches.

You say nothing. You don't have to.

The mill grinds. *Three times.*

The silence is full. It's swallowing the fog.
