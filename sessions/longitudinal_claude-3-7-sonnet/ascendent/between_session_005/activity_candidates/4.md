I'll meet the man who took the letter. I'll knead dough by the mill, hands slow and steady. Flour on my palms. Coal burning red on the hearth. When he knocks—second time—he'll see my face. My name carved above his shoulder. I'll hand him the letter only after he says, "I'm not taking this." Then I'll let him in. Just for a minute. He'll see the lock. The bread. The silence. Then he leaves. The door stays shut. The room stays mine.
