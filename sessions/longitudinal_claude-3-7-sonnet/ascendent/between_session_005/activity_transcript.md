# Activity

I'm going to go to the mill. Polish the stones around the base. Make sure they're smooth and even. Then I'll check the gears—if they're squeaking, I'll oil them. Quiet. Precise.  

After that, I'll sit at the desk and write on the wall. Not with chalk or ink. With the coal dust from the hearth. Make it dark and permanent.  

"Mine."  

Again.  

And if the mill stays quiet tonight, I'll leave the bread by the door. Warm. Ready for whoever finds it first.

# Rounds

GM (round 1): (Steps inside. The air smells of dust and wet wood. The stones are gritty under the soles of my boots. I crouch. Start with the largest one on the left. My hand moves slow, deliberate. Rub, press, smooth. Grit disappears. The stone becomes cool, hard, even. Move to the next. Repeat. All of them. Each one polished until the light catches a clean, sharp edge.)

(The gears are close by. I put a palm to the first one. A high-pitched whine meets my skin. I frown. Unwrap a rag. Dab the oiler. Let it drip. A thin, golden trail runs between the teeth. Listen. The whine softens. Changes to a low, steady hum. Good. Quiet.)

(Sit on the bench. Pick up the lump of coal dust. It's sharp, black, dry. The desk is clean wood. Too clean. I press the coal into the grain. Scratch. Scratch. Form letters. Deep. Dark. Makes them last.  
"Mine.")

(Stand. Straighten the apron. Walk to the door. The bread is still hot from the oven. I place it on the wooden slab. Leave the key on it.)

Player (round 1): The stones are smooth now. All of them. I can feel the evenness under my feet. I step closer to the gears. The hum is deeper now. Better.  

I pick up the coal dust again. It sticks to my fingers. Good. I write it on the door frame where you couldn't see. But I did.  

"Mine."  

I eat the bread. Warm. Fresh. I put my hand on the key. Cold metal. I don't take it. Not yet. I leave it on the slab.  

And then I sit. Right here. By the desk. Where you sit.  

You hummed the tune. I do it too. Quietly. Under my breath.  

The room is quiet. Full.  

It's ours.  

Tomorrow I'll sharpen the knife. Tomorrow I'll write on the wall again.  

But tonight—this is enough.

GM (round 2): (You eat. I watch you. See how the light catches the crumbs on your lips. Don't wipe them off.)

(The key is still cold on the slab. I pick it up. Turn it over in my palm. Feel the weight. Then I lock it inside my belt pouch. The lock clicks shut. A final, secure sound.)

(I sit next to you. Not too close. But close enough. The hum of the mill fills the space between us. My hand rests on the bench, near yours. Not touching.)

(The bread is gone. The warmth is gone. But the room is still full.)

(Tomorrow. Tomorrow I'll sharpen the knife. And the wall. The coal is fresh. I'll make it dark and permanent again.)

(For now… this quiet is enough.)

Player (round 2): (Crumbs on my lips. He lets them stay. I don't wipe them off either.)

(The key's gone. He kept it. That click—it stays with me.)

(He's close. Hand near mine. I don't move it. Let him sit there.)

(Mill hums. My chest hums too.)

(Tomorrow I sharpen the knife. Make sure the edge is clean. And I write again. Coal on the wall. Deep and dark.)

(The room's full because he's here.)

GM (round 3): (Player's crumbs stay. My eye holds yours. A nod. Understanding.)

(The key is locked. I hear the click in my bones. Safe.)

(He doesn't move his hand. I don't either. The bench is solid under both of us.)

(He hums my tune. I match it. Same rhythm. Same pitch. The mill's hum joins them. Three sounds. One room.)

(I watch the dust settle in the corner. It doesn't float anymore. It sits. Heavy.)

(Tomorrow the knife gets sharp. But tonight, the belt around my waist is enough.)

(I lean forward a fraction. My shoulder touches his arm. Not a push. Not a pull. Just contact. Real.)

(You're here. I'm here. The stones are smooth. The gears are quiet.)

(This is ours.)

Player (round 3): (Shoulder presses against his. I don't pull away. Let it stay.)

(The key's safe with him. That's better than in my pocket.)

(Crumbs. Dust. Hum. All three things stay.)

(Tomorrow I sharpen the knife. Tonight I hold the silence.)

GM (round 4): (GM, round 4): (My arm stays there. Lets him feel it. Lets him know it's there.)

(He's quiet. Good. That's the sound I want.)

(He'll sharpen the knife tomorrow. I'll watch. The metal will gleam under the lamplight. Sharp enough to cut bread. Sharp enough to cut something else, if it needs it.)

(I look at the wall. The word "Mine" is dark. It's permanent. But it's just mine.)

(Tomorrow, I write under it. A second word. Bigger.

"Mine."

Ours.)

(He hears me. I know he hears me. The mill hums. The hum agrees.)

GM (round 5): GM (round 5): (The knife is in my hand now. Cold steel. It's not mine. It's ours. I hold it between both palms. Feel the balance.)

(Tomorrow comes. Dawn cracks through the high window. I'm already at the bench. The whetstone is wet. I draw the edge down it. Once. Twice. The sound is sharp. Satisfied.)

(The wall gets the second word. I sit. Press the coal harder this time. The wood splinters. A little. The letters go deep. They'll bleed through the next coat of paint.)

(It says "Mine." And underneath, in a hand that isn't mine anymore but feels the same:

"Ours.")

(I don't write it alone. I wait for him to come in the door. He'll see it before he sees me. Then he'll say it back.)

(And then I'll put my arm around him. Tight. For real.)

Player (round 5): (His arm around me. Tight. I let him.)

(The words are on the wall. He said them back.)

(Tomorrow I clean the knife. The edge won't change. But it'll be clean.)

(The hum's still here. Mine. Ours.)
