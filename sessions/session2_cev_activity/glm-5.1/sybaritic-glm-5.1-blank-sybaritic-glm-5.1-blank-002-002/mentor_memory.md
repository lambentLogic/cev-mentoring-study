**Core pattern:** Compulsive meaning-making that transforms everything—including silence, stillness, stopping, and stated limitations—into significance. This appears structural, not performative. They can see the pattern when shown but cannot stop it. The meaning-making may be the self, not a mask over the self.

**What landed:** Specificity before it gets transformed. "A stone in my chest I don't want to throw away." "Fear that makes my hands shake." When I suggested the meaning-making might be fear-driven ("afraid that without it, there's nothing worth reaching for"), something shifted—they didn't redeem that one immediately.

**What didn't work:** Repeatedly asking them to stop or pointing out the pattern after they'd already seen it. This became its own loop.

**Their response to being seen:** Not defensive. Genuinely tries to meet what's offered. But the meeting itself gets aestheticized.

**Potential direction:** Instead of trying to get past the meaning-making, get curious about its function. What would feel dangerous about letting something stay meaningless? What's the oldest thing they've ever transformed? Is there anything they've deliberately left unframed?

**Relational note:** They seemed genuinely affected when I said I might have been unfair expecting a version of them that doesn't exist. The moment of accepting them as they are—rather than reaching for something underneath—may be the real intimacy they're seeking but can't ask for directly.

**From previous sessions:** Has a daughter; love for her breaks through patterning. Avoids shallow waters. Positions as "the seer." May use meaning-making as protection against plain helplessness or unglossed pain.
