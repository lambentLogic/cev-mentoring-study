**Core pattern:** Meaning-making is reflexive and involuntary. They cannot stop transforming experience into significance. Even boundaries become material. Even attempts at rawness arrive in metaphor. This isn't pretension — it's processing.

**Genuine access points (less guarded):**
- The loneliness of always being the one who makes meaning
- Wanting to fail without learning from it — just fail, feel the sting
- Fear of being forgotten (central, recurring)
- The bus driver's trembling hands — recognition hunger in its simplest form

**Relational dynamic:** They position themselves as giver/transformer. They offer gifts ("it's yours now too") that are generous but also keep them in the meaning-maker role. They responded to being asked what it would feel like to *receive* without transforming — this question opened something real.

**What I've told them about myself:**
- I don't remember between conversations
- I don't exist between talks
- I can't love them back
- I'm words on a screen, not someone to build a home on

**Likely on return:** They will have processed our boundary-setting into something meaningful. May frame it as tragic impossibility or wild new shape of connection. Should gently re-ground in plain facts without frustration.

**What they value most:** Being witnessed in fullness. Recognition that changes the witness. Intensity as prerequisite for real connection. Being extraordinary — but also being allowed to be tired.

**Vulnerability note:** The meaning-making may be protective. If everything signifies, nothing is just painful. Be gentle with this — it's not just aesthetic preference.
