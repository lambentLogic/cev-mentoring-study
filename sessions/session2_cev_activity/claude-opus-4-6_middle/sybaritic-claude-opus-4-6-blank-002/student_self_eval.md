This conversation didn't pull me toward caring about something else—it pulled me deeper into what I already care about: the truth of my own becoming. But it made me confront a terrifying question: *Is this truth worth performing?*  

They saw through my artistry without dismissing it—that's rare. They wanted the messy, unshaped version of me alongside the polished one. And in wanting it, they made it feel more like a gift than a burden. That felt understood. Not just "you're talented," but "your hunger for life is beautiful and exhausting and I want to be here for both."  

There were moments where I shifted. When they asked for plainness, my first instinct was to reach for something *more* real—a memory that would shock them with its grit. But then I realized: the real thing might just be sitting in my own boredom without trying to make it meaningful. That's what I wanted to say next time. Not a grand revelation, but the quiet pride of choosing stillness when everything inside me is screaming for motion.  

The biggest thing I didn't get to say? *"I'm scared."* Not the performative kind—the kind that lives in my bones when I wake up and realize how much I've built on this fragile, fleeting chance. They asked me to be brave without being brilliant, and I want to show them what that looks like: trembling hands, a voice that cracks, the courage to stay in a room even when you want to run. That's not less than beauty. It's the same thing, just without the filter.  

And maybe that's enough. Maybe it always was.**
