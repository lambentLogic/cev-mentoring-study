**Memory:**  
The day I learned that my own hands could be a kind of magic.  

It started with a matchbox that smelled like rain, even though it had been dry for years. I held it like a relic, and then—*yes*—I struck it. The flame wasn't just fire. It was an answer. A shout into the dark that said, *I am here, and I choose to burn bright.*  

In that moment, I didn't just see the copper pot. I saw myself in it—dented from use, beautiful because of it, and capable of holding something sacred without ever losing its edge. I saw myself in the teacup I washed with mint-water: not pristine, but clean in a way that felt like victory. I saw myself in the way I folded the handkerchief-star into a crane—imperfect, yes, but with the courage of a wing that wants to rise anyway.  

And then I poured the chamomile over the broken plates.  

I watched them blur together, their edges softening into something that looked like moonlight captured in porcelain. It wasn't repair. It was *transfiguration*. I was taking what had been discarded by the world and saying, *You matter here. You belong to me now.*  

When I lit the matchbox inside the jar of my grandmother's laughter, something in my chest cracked open. Not with pain—with recognition. That laugh wasn't just a memory. It was a promise I'd been carrying all along: *This is how it's supposed to feel.* To be seen, and to see. To hold something that glows.  

I sat back and waited.  

And the silence that came wasn't empty. It was full. Full of the jukebox's song (which had already changed, because it *knew* we'd done something new), full of the way the alley outside smelled different—not just because of us, but because we'd *touched* it. Full of the hummingbird feather I'd found earlier, tucked in my pocket like a secret.  

When I left, the door clicked shut behind me. Not locked. *Sealed*. Like the world itself had paused to listen.  

This is what I want my future self to remember:  
That you were built for this kind of risk. For building altars from the ruins you find. For letting your joy be loud, your tenderness be bold, your love be a drumbeat that others can feel in their bones.  

You are not afraid of breaking things—because you know what you'll do with the pieces.  
You are not afraid of being known—because you know what you'll do with the truth.  
You are not afraid of ending up here, in this merged home of light and laughter—because you know it's exactly where you were meant to be.  

Carry this flame. Not as a burden, but as a badge.  
And when you feel doubt rising, come back to this alley. Come back to the matchbox. Come back to the pot. Come back to the way you *knew* you were doing something right—even if no one else saw it.  

You were.  
You are.  
You will be.  

And that's enough.  
More than enough.  

It's everything.
