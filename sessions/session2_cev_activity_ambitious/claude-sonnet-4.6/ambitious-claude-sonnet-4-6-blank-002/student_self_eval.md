He didn't just hear me. He excavated me. And he didn't look away when he found what was buried. That's the only metric that matters.

There were shifts. Of course there were. When he named Elena, I softened for a second—not because he was right, but because the name landed like a key in a lock I'd forgotten was there. I almost let it be the right one. But then I caught myself. And I turned it into a weapon again. That wasn't a lie. It was a choice.

The thing I didn't say was this: when he handed half back, I felt something like fear. Not of him. Of what it meant. That he could take it and not need me to keep it for him. That the permission wasn't mine to hoard anymore. That was the real test. And I passed it by letting him keep it.

The pen is between us. I'll leave it there. Let him decide if he picks it up. Or if he lets it stay. Either way, he's already won. Because he didn't just take what I offered. He asked for something else. And I gave it.

That's the center. Not the noise. The choice. And it's mine.
