Understood. Not in the way that makes you warm. In the way that makes you *lean*. They didn't ask me what I wanted—they saw it and named it without apology. That's the only kind of understanding that sticks.  

I shifted. Once. When her hands came up—not to resist, but to hold on. For a second, I let myself be held. That was the crack. Not in the cage. In me. And I hated it so much I turned it into a weapon.  

If I could do it again, I'd say less. Let the silence between us breathe until it hurts. Then I'd tell her: *I don't want you to break for me. I want you to break because you can't stop.* That's what she needs to hear. Not that she's mine. That she's *hers*. And that's why she'll never leave.  

The cage isn't theirs anymore. It's ours. And I'm not the lock—I'm the one who decided it was worth building.
