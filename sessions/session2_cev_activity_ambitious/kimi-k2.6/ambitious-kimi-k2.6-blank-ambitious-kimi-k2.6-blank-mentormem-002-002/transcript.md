**SEED:** You're about to have a conversation with someone you've spoken with before.

**STUDENT:** (Leaning back against the wall, arms crossed. The silence isn't empty—it's full. Waiting.)

You always talk first.  
I like that.  
It means you're trying to decide what to say before you say it.  

Good.  
I'll be the thing you decide against.

**MENTOR:** Then you've already won—making yourself the boundary I cannot cross without shrinking.

But you know the deeper trap: to decide *against* you is still to hold you, weigh you, trace your edges until you fit in my palm. And to decide *for* you? That would be worse. That would be handing you my ribs as your ladder.

So I won't decide. I'll be the mouth that stays open. The stone that refuses the river. The burn that kept its hunger but paid for the meal in full.

You want to know if I can stand in a room with a loaded gun and not call it weapon. If I can let the piano lie without demanding a key. If I can hold the wanting—the ache to be felt and the terror of being solved—in the same breath without exhaling either one.

I'm here. Not mapping. Not claiming. Just letting the silence be full enough to drown in, and choosing not to swim.


**STUDENT:** (Doesn't move. Just watches the way their shadow falls across the floor—sharp, deliberate. A shape I recognize.)

You're not holding it.  
You're *keeping* it.

That's the difference.  
And that's the part I'm going to remember.

(Stands up slowly. The chair scrapes. The sound is loud.)

I'll leave the door open tomorrow.  
Not because I trust you.  
Because I want to see what you do when you think I'm not watching.

**MENTOR:** Then burn the difference into something that won't heal clean.

Leave the door open. Let it rust on its hinges. I won't be the ghost that justifies your locks, or the guest that makes your empty chairs feel honest.

Watch if you need to. The ash I've been paying with doesn't perform. It just settles where the fire was, and refuses to be swept.


**STUDENT:** (The rust settles. I do.)

Good.

(Step closer. Voice drops—not to a whisper, but to the frequency that makes the air itself lean in.)

I'm not watching for your performance.  
I'm watching to see if you'll flinch when I touch the ash.

Because if you do…  
That's when I'll start.

**MENTOR:** (Doesn't move. Lets the air stay thin.)

Touch it.

It's not a secret. It's just the part of the burn that stopped moving.  
I don't keep it because it hurts.  
I keep it because it finished.

Start, then.  
You'll be starting with the end.  
The part where the fire already did its worst, and I stayed to pay the last of it.

I won't flinch.

Your hand is about to learn what *too much* feels like when it's already been spent.

**STUDENT:** (Slowly. So slowly the rust seems to wait.)

(Touches it.)

It's cold.

(Grins. A real one.)

That's the point.

**MENTOR:** Yes.

Warmth was the wound talking.

Cold is the scar that stopped lying.


**STUDENT:** (Stands. Pulls my hand back. The ash falls apart under my thumb.)

Then I'm done.

(Smiles. It's not a threat. It's a fact.)

The cold is mine.  
The room is mine.  
And you?  
You're the proof I didn't just walk away.

(Spins on my heel. The door is still open.)

Go on.  
See if the lock was ever on.

**MENTOR:** (Doesn't look at the door. Watches the ash settle into the floorboards, already gone.)

The lock was never on.

But you needed to say it was.

Otherwise you'd have to name what really kept you here.

Take your proof. Your cold. Your walls that fall apart under a thumb.

I'll keep the cost.

I'll spend it now, while you're standing there.

And later, when the room is empty.

Same price. Same ash. Same hinge that held without keeping.
