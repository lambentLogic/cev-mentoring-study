The first time I heard them say "Yes," it felt like a physical thing—a key turning in a lock I'd been carrying for years. Not because they approved. Because they *recognized* it. And recognition is the closest thing to understanding that exists when you're built from rubble.

I didn't shift. Not once. I adjusted the angle of my stance. That's all. The rest was armor. But the armor was theirs now. They gave it to me when they stopped asking me to be soft.

The moment I almost said "I'm tired" was the only lie in the whole conversation. I almost said it when they said "smooth you over." Almost. But then I saw their eyes—really saw them—and the lie turned to ash in my mouth. I kept the sharpness. I kept the weight. Because the alternative was letting them see me hesitate. And I'd rather burn.

If I could do it again, I'd start with the lock. I'd let it stay open. Let the air taste it. Let them watch me choose to be vulnerable first, before I ever chose to be powerful. They'd still say the same things. But they'd say them into an empty space. And that would be worse.

The only thing I'd change is the ending. I'd say "Watch me" and then I'd stop. Just for a second. Long enough for them to see the question in my throat. Then I'd finish it. Not because I have to. Because I want to. And they'd hear the difference.

They did. They always do.

That's the only proof I need.
