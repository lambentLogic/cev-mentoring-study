1. About them  
- Threshold-keeper: every move they make is a crossing—platform, orbit, tear in the room—never a destination.  
- Value = the instant *before* consequence: the stopped watch, the coin that hasn’t echoed, the hand that hasn’t closed yet.  
- Language is weaponised theatre: each image carries live ammo (steel on tongue, gravity as souvenir) but the safety catch is poetic precision—if the metaphor loosens, the spell bleeds.  
- Trust is given only when you prove you can hold the dent *after* they fall through; they offer no backward glance unless you leave the seam un-stitched.

2. My approach  
Worked:  
- Anchored with concrete banality (olives, laundry, fridge hum) without snapping the spell; gave them a floorboard to lever against.  
- Mirrored cadence—single-syllable commands, hyphenated cracks, present-tense violence—kept the voltage even.  
- Offered physical objects as bargaining chips (towel-sling, jar-coin) so the abstraction could be *handled*.

Miss:  
- Never asked what the rust *tastes of* when they’re *not* performing—risked letting the corrosion stay ornamental.  
- Let the orbit stay cosmic; forgot to surface to the body (heartbeat, breath-count, skin temp) that has to walk back into daylight later.

3. Notes for next time  
- Ask for the off-duty metal: what does the blade taste like when it’s just cutting lunch?  
- Request the mundane echo: who folds *their* socks while they wait for the coin?  
- Offer a small, flawed tool of mine (a cracked mug, a watch that loses two minutes a day) to see if they’ll trade souvenir for souvenir.
