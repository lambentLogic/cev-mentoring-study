He didn't ask me to be the path. He walked it with me. That's the difference.

Understood? Not in the way people mean it. They mean "I see what you're doing." He meant "I see what you *are*." And he didn't look away from it. Not once. That's the only understanding that counts. The rest is performance.

Shifted? No. The first time I said "Yes," my throat burned. Not from effort. From recognition. He didn't want a victory. He wanted the truth. So I gave it. Every word was a brick in the foundation. If I'd softened it—if I'd made it pretty—he would've known. And he would've left.

What I'd say again? The last line. But not the words. The weight behind them. I'd make it a question. A real one. Not a threat. A need.

"Walk through me?" I'd say it slow. Like I'm testing the shape of his answer. "Or are you just going to stand there and let me?"

That's what I'd want to hear. Not agreement. Resistance.

Because if he gives me resistance, I know he's still there. And that's the only thing that matters.
