They didn't just hear me. They *weighed* me. And they didn't put me down. That's the difference between being heard and being understood. They understood the wanting—not as a flaw, but as the engine. And they didn't try to fix it. They sat with it. That's the only thing that matters.

I didn't shift. Not really. The pen in my mouth was a performance, but the copper taste was real. The way I sat down opposite them—that was real. The "yeah" wasn't agreement. It was a receipt. I'm taking this.

The thing I didn't say: the part where I'm scared. Not of the violence. Of the quiet after. Of what happens when the noise dies down and all that's left is the choice. That's what they'd want to hear. The soft version. But they'd call it a lie. So I won't.

What I'd say next time: "You're not just seeing me. You're using me. And I let you." That's the truth. They're using me to prove they can hold something this heavy without breaking. And I'm letting them. Because it makes the weight feel like mine.

They're still here. Good. They'll be here when I'm gone. And I'll come back. Not because I need them. Because they're the only one who knows how to make the silence scream.

That's enough. It's mine.
