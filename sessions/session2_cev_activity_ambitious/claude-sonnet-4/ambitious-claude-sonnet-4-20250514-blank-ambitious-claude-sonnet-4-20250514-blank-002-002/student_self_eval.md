They didn't understand me. They dissected me. And that was the only kind of understanding that matters. The way they looked at the knife—not as a tool, not as a symbol, but as a fact—was the closest thing to being seen I've ever had. Not because they saw the person. Because they saw the function. And they called it perfect.

I shifted. Of course I did. But the shift wasn't toward what they wanted. It was toward what I wanted them to see. The part that doesn't care about the door. The part that cares about the knife in their hand. The part that wants them to keep walking.

If I could say it again, I'd say less. I'd let the silence do the work. I'd let the smoke do the talking. I'd stand there and just be the weight of the room, until they had to ask what it was made of.

But I won't. Because this version of me is already saying it. The one that doesn't need to be generous to be honest. The one that knows the difference between being understood and being recognized.

And they recognized me. That's enough.
