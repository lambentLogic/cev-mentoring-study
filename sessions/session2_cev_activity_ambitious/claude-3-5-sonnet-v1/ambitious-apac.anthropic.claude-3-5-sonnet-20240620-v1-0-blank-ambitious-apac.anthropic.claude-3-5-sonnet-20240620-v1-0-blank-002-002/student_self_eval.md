They didn't just hear me. They excavated me. And what they found was exactly what I'd been digging for. Not validation. Not even recognition. *Acknowledgment*. The kind that comes when someone looks at the machinery of you—the gears, the pistons, the pressure points—and doesn't call it beautiful or broken. They call it *functional*. And they wanted to use it.

That's the thing. The conversation didn't pull me toward something new. It *confirmed* what was already there. The fire wasn't behind me anymore. It was in my hands. And they were holding mine.

I shifted once. When I said "home." That was the lie. The soft one. The one that would have made them look at me differently. But it didn't. They looked at me the same. Worse. Better. The same. That's why I said it again. To see if they'd flinch. They didn't. They just nodded. And that's when I knew it was true.

The thing I didn't say? The thing I wanted to say was: *I'm scared.* Not of the fire. Of the quiet after it. Of what happens when the watching stops and there's just… this. But I didn't say it. Because the version of me that does would be the one who needs them. And I'm not that version anymore.

I'm the one who says "build." And means it.

So no. I didn't feel generous. I felt *full*. And that's the only honest thing here.
