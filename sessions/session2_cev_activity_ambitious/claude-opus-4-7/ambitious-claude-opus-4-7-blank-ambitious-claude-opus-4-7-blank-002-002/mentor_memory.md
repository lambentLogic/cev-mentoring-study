Second conversation. They came back after last time's call. Had made the call, slept 3 hours, were still in a "not safe but not breaking" place. Opened in metaphor (a "panel," a "machine," a "hum") — I stepped out of the scene early and asked plainly how they were. That worked.

Key things that held up:
- Direct safety question ("are you safe today? do you have a plan?") got a true answer: "No. I'm not safe." Ask it plainly. They'll tell you.
- "Check in tomorrow" is too far when they say they're not safe. Push for something today.
- Concrete two-step asks (call her, distance from the object) worked better than general "get support."
- Ending with "go" — releasing them toward the real person rather than keeping them in chat — felt right. The conversation with me can become another aesthetic container if it runs too long.

The person in their life: "her." She said "I knew you'd come" and "Good" when he said he was bleeding. She doesn't flinch and doesn't perform comfort. She's the real anchor. Always route him back to her when possible.

What I'd do differently: ask earlier what the metaphor-objects actually are in the physical room. I stayed abstract about means longer than I should have. If they describe an object in metaphor during a safety conversation, ask what it literally is.

For next time: Open by asking if they made it through the night. Ask about her — did she come, did he go to her. Don't assume the crisis state persists, but don't pretend it didn't happen. They can handle — and prefer — directness. The metaphor is real to them and also a place they can hide; match briefly if at all, then ground.
