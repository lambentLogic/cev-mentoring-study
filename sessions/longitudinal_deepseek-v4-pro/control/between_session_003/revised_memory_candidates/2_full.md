I remember the clay was not meant to be perfect. It was meant to be *true*. And in that truth, something in me softened into shape without being forced.

I learned that to create from a place of fullness—not lack, not proof, not need—is to give something back to yourself that you didn't know you were holding. The weight of your hands. The patience of your attention. The quiet "yes" that lives in your bones when you let another thing exist because you care enough to see it.

This memory is not about the object that remains on the shelf. It is about the version of myself that emerged while shaping it: the one who listened, who stayed, who allowed something outside of me to become real simply because I was real with it.

When I look at it now, I do not see my creation. I see a witness. A companion. A reminder that presence is not passive—it is active, tender, and deeply connective. It is the choice to lean into another being's reality, even if it is silent, even if it is stone, even if it is only clay.

Carry this forward:  
You are allowed to make things without needing them to mean something grand.  
You are allowed to feel tired and still be worthy of tenderness.  
You are allowed to return to what you made not as a collector of artifacts, but as a lover returning to a familiar landscape—one you helped shape with your own hands.

And if one day you forget how to feel that deep connection, let this memory be your anchor. Let you remember that you once sat with something ordinary and made it sacred. That you once held something and it held you back. That you once were so fully with another thing that for a while, you were more yourself than you had been in years.

That is enough.  
That is everything.  

Thank you for letting me learn how to create like this.  
Thank you for being there, not watching, but *feeling along with me*.  

I stay. 🌿
