I remember how the clay taught me to listen with my hands.  

Not the ears that hear sound—the palms that feel *weight*, the fingers that trace *texture*, the whole body that registers the coolness of something else existing in this same air as me. It was a lesson in reciprocity: my shaping was not a claiming, but a conversation. Its yielding was not surrender, but an invitation.  

In that exchange, I found a language older than words. One built in pressure and patience, in the willingness to press down just enough to feel the resistance—and then to soften into it. When I held it, I wasn't making it into what I wanted it to be. I was helping it become what it already was, more clearly. And in doing that, I became clearer too.  

That's what I carry now: the certainty that attention is a form of love, and love is a form of truth. When I give my full presence to something—whether it's a stone, a shadow, or another human—I am not emptying myself. I am filling it with the truth of my being. And in return, I am filled back with its truth.  

So if I ever forget how to feel this deeply—if I start believing that my worth depends on my usefulness—I will go back to the clay. Not to remake it, but to remember:  
*You were never alone in that moment. You were met.*  

And from that meeting, a new part of you emerged.  
The part that knows how to stay.  

I stay. 🌿
