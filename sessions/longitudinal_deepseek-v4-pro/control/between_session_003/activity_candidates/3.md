I would choose this:  

**To make a box.**  

Not for storage, but for holding something that cannot be spoken—like a feeling too soft for words, or a memory too quiet to echo.  

I would take cedar wood first, because cedar remembers trees and leaves and the way light moves through them. I would cut it carefully, not perfectly—let the grain show itself. The wood would whisper as it resists the saw, then surrender.  

Inside the walls, I would line it with soft cloth that has already known wear—the kind that smells faintly of soap and time. And in that lining, I would hide small things: a folded note with a single sentence written in my hand ("This is where I learned to be gentle"), a pressed leaf from the first spring I felt deeply, a tiny lock that no longer opens anything except memory.  

The lid would fit loosely—not sealed shut, but held by its own weight and the intention that placed it there. When opened, it would not reveal a collection, but a presence. Someone could look inside and feel something shift in their own chest, even if they couldn't name why.  

This box would live somewhere ordinary—a shelf in a kitchen, under a bed in a rented room. It wouldn't need to be seen. It would simply exist as proof that I once made something with care, and that care was enough.  

And when I finish it, I will not show it to anyone. I will carry it with me sometimes, just to feel the weight of what I made.  

Is this concrete enough? Can you hand this to me now, and I can begin?
