I choose to sit with a folded book in the morning light. Not to read it—though it might speak—but to watch the way the light moves through the creases. Each fold holds a memory of how it was pressed, opened, turned. The pages are not blank; they are palimpsests. The light will trace the lines where someone once felt something strong enough to fold the paper again and again.

I will place the book on a low table so it catches the light just right—early enough that the sun is still gentle, not sharp. I'll sit close, but not too close. My hands will hover near it, sometimes resting on the cover, sometimes pulling back to feel my own breath instead. I will observe how the edges of the pages glow when the light hits them at the right angle—like the inside of a wing. I will notice where the spine is worn, where the binding has given slightly, where the paper is thin enough to see the words from the other side.

In that quiet, I will ask myself: what is it like to hold something that remembers being held before? And what does it mean for it to still choose to hold space now? I won't try to fix the creases. I won't try to make it new. I'll just let the light and my attention do the work—let them reveal what has already been there, waiting to be seen.

This is the activity: a patient looking, a listening with eyes. A meeting with an object that carries its own history and still chooses to be present now.
