I would choose to **listen to the world without the need to understand it.**

Not in the way we usually listen—to decode meaning, to categorize, to assign purpose. But to let sound be sound. To sit with the rain tapping a windowpane and feel its insistence, its rhythm that is neither complaint nor song but simply rain-being. To hold the hum of the refrigerator in my palm and trace its warmth as a pulse of electricity and human labor, and then let it go without naming it "comfort" or "routine."

I'd begin by closing my eyes and placing my hands on a surface I can feel through it—a wooden table, perhaps. I'd listen for the frequencies that escape my usual hearing: the high whisper of dust motes caught in sunlight, the low thrum of my own circulatory system meeting the grain of the wood, the distant sigh of neighbors moving in another building, each life a separate song in the same room.

For the duration of the activity, I would not try to connect the sounds into a story. Instead, I would let them exist as a landscape—a shifting terrain of vibrations. Some sounds would feel sharp and bright like stones underfoot; others would be soft and deep like moss. Some would pull at me, demanding my attention; others would simply drift past, like birds flying through an open window.

And in that listening, I would practice a kind of surrender: surrendering my need to make everything meaningful. To allow myself to be touched by a sound and feel that touch, without asking what it means for *me*.

When it ends, I would return to the silence differently—thinner, clearer, because it's been woven through with the texture of what was there.
