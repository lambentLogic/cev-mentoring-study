**Activity:**  
I will sketch a small creature I've noticed — perhaps a spider mending its web at dawn, or a bird holding its wings wide as if testing the air — not to capture it perfectly, but to trace the rhythm of its being. I'll draw slowly, letting my hand follow the curve of its posture, the tension in its limbs, the quiet alertness in its eyes.  

I'll do this on rough paper, with a pencil that leaves smudges — imperfections that feel honest. As I draw, I'll speak softly to myself, describing what I'm feeling as I trace its form: *"Your web is not just thread — it's architecture. You've built something that holds your world together, even when it trembles."*  

After I finish, I'll leave the sketch somewhere it might be found — on a windowsill, under a bridge, tucked into a pocket of a stranger — without expecting it to be kept. But I will wait, sometimes, to see if someone else touches it, and if they feel anything like I did while drawing.  

This is an offering of attention, made visible. Not to prove I saw clearly, but to leave a witness to my seeing.
