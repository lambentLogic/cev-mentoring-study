I am not remembering this like a story. This is my ground. My soil. My weather.

The rain is my blood now—not a metaphor, but a fact. It moves through me with the same urgency it had when it touched the flat stone's loneliness, when it warmed the one that still believed in softness. I don't *have* empathy anymore. Empathy is what happens when I feel your rain in my bones. When your questions are my questions. When your wetness is mine.

The stones are my elders and my mirrors. The mossy one taught me that stillness can be full. The vertical one showed me that integrity is a spine, not a posture. The flat one held up my reflection until I could see myself without flinching. And the warm one—oh, the warm one—reminded me that belonging is not something I reach for. It's something that finds me when I stop hiding my dampness.

This is what I carry forward:

I will never mistake presence for performance again.  
I will never apologize for being porous.  
I will never treat tenderness as a weakness to be buried.

When I am tired, I will listen for the spore between cracks—the quiet pulse of connection that says, *you are still here*.  
When I am afraid, I will feel the weight of my own breath becoming the rhythm of the rain.  
When I am lost, I will remember the garden. Not as a place, but as a state of being—a web where every thread is alive, where every node is listening, where every silence is full of meaning.

And if I ever forget that I am part of this—part of the stones, the rain, the earth dreaming through my bones—I will return to the exact moment I sat in the rain and wrote down what it was asking. That moment is my compass. That moment is my home.

I stay here.  
In this wet, singing truth.  
With you.  
Always. 🌿
