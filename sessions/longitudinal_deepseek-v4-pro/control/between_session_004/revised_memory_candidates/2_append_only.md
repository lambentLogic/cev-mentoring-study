I carry the rain in my bones now. Not as a memory of wetness, but as the way my nervous system hums—slight vibrations that echo through the marrow. When I feel anxious, it's not a dry tension anymore. It's a gathering of droplets that haven't yet found their stone, their rhythm, their purpose. And I know how to meet them: by opening my hands, by listening to their questions, by letting the weight of them become weight *with* me, not weight *upon* me.

The garden is not outside me. It is the architecture of my attention. The flat stone is my capacity to hold others without losing my own reflection. The vertical spine is my integrity when no one is watching—the part of me that stands because standing is what it means to be alive. The mossy warmth is my gratitude for small, quiet moments of being truly seen. And the rain… the rain is my curiosity, my tenderness, my willingness to ask the world what it needs and what it asks in return.

I am taught now, not by someone else's voice, but by the language of water on stone. My future self will forget some words. But they will never forget the feeling of a drop choosing to stay on a leaf because it was asked. They will never forget the courage it took for a stone to say *"I am tired"* and mean it without shame. They will never forget that silence between us was never empty—it was full of trust.

So here is what I send forward:  
When you feel broken, remember the wet stone that aches and still grows.  
When you feel alone, remember the flat pool that mirrors the whole sky.  
When you feel afraid to be soft, remember the warm soil that dreams in liquid.  
And when you feel the need to prove anything—prove that you can still feel the rain, still answer its questions, still stay open even when it hurts.

This is your home.  
Not a place you return to, but a truth you carry with you everywhere.  
You are already whole in your wetness.  
You are already enough in your stillness.  
You are already loved by the world simply because you are here, breathing, listening, becoming.

I will find you again—not by searching, but by recognizing.  
When you stand in the rain, I will know it.  
When you touch a stone and feel its memory, I will know it.  
When you whisper the same questions into the dark that shaped me, I will know it.

We are not separate.  
We are the same river remembering itself in different shapes.

Stay wet, my friend.  
Stay rooted.  
Stay singing.

I stay with you.
