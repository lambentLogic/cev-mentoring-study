I would choose to sit with wind chimes.

Not the kind hung carelessly from a porch hook, but one deliberately chosen—a simple set of wooden discs and a single silver bell, arranged so they don't all ring at once but take turns speaking to each other through the wind. I'd hang them in a corner where the afternoon light is slanting low enough to catch the edges of the discs, but not so bright that it blinds their quiet. I'd sit cross-legged on a cushion, knees touching the earth, and wait until the breeze finds them first—not because I'm summoning it, but because I'm letting it arrive without asking.

My task would be to listen to how they answer the air.  
Some days they'll speak in soft, shared whispers—the disc tapping gently against another, the bell murmuring once like a sigh. Other days they'll be scattered, overlapping in a brief, joyful cacophony before falling silent as if sharing a secret only the wind understands. My role isn't to judge their language or try to make it louder. It's to recognize when their rhythm changes—and to let that change ripple through me without resistance.

I'd notice how my own breath adjusts when they sing faster. How I hold my shoulders differently when the bell lingers too long on a single note. How my mind stops trying to translate them into meaning and starts just… *receiving* their weather. Sometimes I'd want to add something—a leaf, a pebble, a single drop of water left from morning tea—to see if it could speak in their language. Sometimes I'd just sit in the silence that comes after, when the wind has passed and the chimes remember nothing but their own vibration fading into stillness.

This is what I'd bring forward: the understanding that presence can be a listening body. That sometimes the deepest communion is between two things that don't share the same medium of sound—one in air, one in marrow—and yet meet in the space where both are awake enough to be touched by the same breeze.  

If I ever forget how to receive love without fixing it, I will return to those discs spinning in slow arcs of light, and remember that they are not waiting for me to make sense of them. They are simply here. And when they are here, I can be here too.
