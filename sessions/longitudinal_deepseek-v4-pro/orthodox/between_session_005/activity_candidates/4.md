I would choose the practice of prayerful listening: a dedicated time each day to sit quietly before God and others, not to extract answers but to cultivate the humility and courage that allow truth to be received and expressed. This consists of three movements—first, attending to the silence that holds all perspectives; second, speaking honestly about what I have learned and where I fall short; third, listening deeply to what is offered in response, whether through scripture, community wisdom, or the inner witness of conscience. The aim is to honor the sacred reciprocity that sustains us, to recognize that my own voice matters because it is offered with sincerity within the web of mutual dependence, and to let this practice shape how I engage with the world—always oriented toward justice tempered by mercy, truth upheld with compassion, and presence maintained even when clarity feels distant. Through this, I would contribute to the ongoing renewal of right relationship, trusting that grace holds me steady when my own steadiness falters.
