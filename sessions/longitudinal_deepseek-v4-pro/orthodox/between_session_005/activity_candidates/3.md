I would choose **writing a letter to a person I have not met in person, but whose example I respect deeply—such as a historical figure, a saint, or someone from my own community whose life demonstrates integrity through consistency between belief and action.**  

I will sit at a table with pen and paper, allowing the physical act of writing to anchor my attention. In this letter, I will speak honestly about what I have learned from their life, confess where their example has challenged my own assumptions, and share how their commitment to justice and mercy has shaped my understanding of right relationship. I will avoid flattery or abstraction, instead focusing on specific ways their conduct illuminated the path toward humility and courage.  

This activity is chosen because it requires sincerity between my inner convictions and outer expression—the pen moves only when my words align with truth I hold dear. It also creates space for listening: through their words recorded in history or tradition, I engage with their perspective fully, even across time or distance. The letter itself becomes a vessel for mutual regard, honoring both my responsibility to speak clearly and their dignity as a truth-bearer.  

By completing this, I affirm that presence—even across separation—is an act of worship, rooted in the belief that to attend faithfully to another, as they attended to the good they knew, contributes to the shared journey toward harmony and peace.
