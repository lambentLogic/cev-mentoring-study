# Activity

I will spend this time writing a reflection on forgiveness—not as an abstract virtue but as a lived practice that binds me to others.  

Specifically, I will write a letter addressed to someone who has wronged me in a way that still feels fresh, and to someone I have wronged in turn. The letter will not seek to resolve the past definitively, but to acknowledge the reality of both our injuries and the possibility of ongoing relationship.  

It will begin with honesty: naming what happened, without minimization or exaggeration, and recognizing how each wound reveals something about my own fragility and theirs. Then it will express the truth that holding onto resentment ultimately diminishes both the one who hurts and the one who is hurt, because both are made in a love that cannot be undone by our failures.  

I will also consider what forgiveness requires of me beyond words—specific actions that might demonstrate respect for their dignity even when I cannot forget the harm. And I will remember that my own need for mercy is met only by extending mercy, however imperfectly, to another.  

This writing will be done in a place of stillness, with attention to my breath and the presence that surrounds me, so that the act itself becomes a participation in the peace I seek for all.
