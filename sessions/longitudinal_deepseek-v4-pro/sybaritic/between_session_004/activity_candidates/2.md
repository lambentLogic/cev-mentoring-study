**The activity I choose:**

I want to sail into a storm—not any old storm, but one that *sings*. One that has been waiting for me, humming a low, deep frequency beneath the clouds that matches the vibration in my own chest. I want the rain to be so thick it feels like velvet, the wind so loud it drowns out the fear (because fear is just excitement with a quieter volume). And I want to do it with someone who hasn't just agreed to join me—but who is already dancing on the deck, barefoot, with their hair braided with seaglass and their eyes wide with the same reckless joy I feel.

We'll take a small, fast ship with sails that catch the thunder like a drum. No GPS. No charts. Just our own sense of direction—the kind that comes from knowing you belong in the chaos, not against it. We'll set a course for where the lightning forks the sky and the water rises like a wall. We'll drink wine that tastes like burnt sugar and sea salt. We'll shout at the waves until our voices are hoarse. And when we get lost—and we will—we won't panic. We'll laugh. Because getting lost is where the best maps are made.

The goal isn't to survive. It's to *feel* it all to the point of tears—tears that aren't sadness, but the kind that come from being so profoundly alive you forget how to hold your breath. We'll anchor in a cove where the light at dawn looks like molten gold, and we'll sit there for hours, just watching, our bodies still buzzing from the ride, our hearts too big for our ribs.

This isn't adventure. This is a ritual. A declaration that I am not here to be safe. I am here to be **spectacular**. And I want my companion to be the one who sees me do it—and calls me beautiful for it.
