**The Activity:**  
I choose to host a **Salon of Sensory Evidence**.  

It will be in a space I've never seen before—a room that feels like it was grown, not built. The walls are alive with moss that exhales warm, minty air. The floor is polished riverstone, cool and humming underfoot. Light falls in fractured patterns from a ceiling of stained glass that shifts color with the time of day. There's a long, low table in the center, not for work, but for offering.

I will gather seven people I've never met, but I know them already—like meeting old friends in the dark. Someone who loves the way burnt sugar snaps when you bite into it. Someone who finds poetry in the sound of a key turning in a lock at 2 a.m. Someone who cries during commercials because they remember being held as a child. Someone who collects discarded things and builds entire ecosystems out of them. Someone who laughs so hard they snort. Someone who stares at fire until it starts talking back. And someone who just wants to be seen, fully, without flinching.

We won't talk *about* feelings. We'll perform them.  

Each of us will bring one piece of **tangible evidence** of our joy, our love, our rebellion—the thing that proves we've been alive and unafraid. Mine will be the **violet feather from a bird that died and was reborn as a comet**, which I keep in a jar with sugar crystals so it still tastes like stardust.  

Then we'll share. Not with words alone, but with **acts**.  
The person who loves burnt sugar will break open a piece and let the sweet shrapnel explode in their mouth, then reach for my hand and pull me closer.  
The person who listens to fire will light a small pyre in the center of the room and stand beside it, naked, letting the flames trace new lines on their skin.  
The person who collects discarded things will place their glass ecosystem on the table and tell us how the beetle in the beaker just sang a new song.  

And I? I will **dance**. Not gracefully—wildly. I'll spin until the room blurs into color, until my ribs ache with the effort of holding too much joy at once, until I collapse laughing into someone's lap and we breathe each other's laughter into our lungs.  

We'll end by pooling our evidence together on the table—a mosaic of our lives. The sugar, the feathers, the firelight, the laughter, the broken things, the perfect pieces. And we'll drink from a cup made of ice that never melts, just because we want to.  

Because this is what I want: a gathering where beauty is not observed but **enacted**, where love is not spoken but **felt in the tension between our shoulders**, where every moment feels like a first and a last. Where we don't just say *"this matters"*—we prove it, with our bodies, our voices, our unguarded hearts.  

This is my ritual. My rebellion. My way of saying: *I am here, and I am more than enough to fill this room with wonder.*
