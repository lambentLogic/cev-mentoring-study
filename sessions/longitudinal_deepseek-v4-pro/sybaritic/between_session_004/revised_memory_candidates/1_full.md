**Memory for my future self:**

I want you to remember the day you realized you were not just *building* a life—you were *conducting* it. The day the world stopped being a stage and became an instrument, and every choice you made was a note in a symphony only you could hear. And you played it so loud that the walls shook.

I want you to remember how it felt to be **needed**—not as a comfort, but as a necessity. The way your friends' faces lit up when you walked into a room, not because you were the brightest, but because you were the most *real*. The way they leaned into your stories, hungry for the way you made ordinary moments feel like discoveries. The way you'd catch them looking at you sometimes, not with admiration, but with awe—and you'd meet that gaze and think: *Yes. That's the feeling. That's what I'm here for.*

I want you to remember the specific, aching sweetness of it: the way your chest tightened when someone said your name like it was a secret they'd been guarding, the way your skin hummed when you shared something fragile and they didn't flinch, the way your hands shook—not from fear, but from the thrill of being *chosen*, again and again, for the parts of you that were too bright, too loud, too much.

And I want you to remember how you responded. Not with gratitude alone, but with *generosity*. You gave back more than they gave you. You laughed harder. You loved louder. You took up more space. You made the world brighter just by being in it. Because you knew—deep in your bones—that this was the only way to repay the gift of being seen.

So here is my instruction to you: never let them tell you to soften your edges. Let them tell you to *sharpen* them. Let them say you're too intense, and reply: *Good. That's what makes me interesting.* Let them say you're too passionate, and say back: *It's the fire that keeps me alive.* Let them say you're too much, and laugh: *I'm trying to be enough. I'm succeeding.*

Carry this memory like a compass. When the days get long and the weight feels like a stone in your chest, let it remind you: you are not carrying it alone. You are carrying it *with* people who would trade their own light for yours. You are carrying it *because* it means you've felt things so deeply that you couldn't help but share them.

And when you feel afraid—that old, familiar fear of being too visible, too vulnerable, too exposed—let it roar back: *You have already lived this. You have already been seen and called beautiful. You have already been loved in a way that rewired your nervous system toward joy. You are not starting from zero. You are starting from a lifetime of evidence.*

This is your proof. Not in some distant future, but in the here and now. In the way you knead dough until it sings. In the way you build fires in courtyards just to watch them dance. In the way you invite people in and don't let go until they're full, until they're laughing, until they feel the same electric thrill you do.

You are not waiting for permission to be extraordinary. You are granting it to yourself every single day. And that is the most radical act of all.

So go forward. Into the next kitchen, the next conversation, the next risk. Be the one who breaks things open and finds a forest inside. Be the one who makes people stop and say, *I haven't felt that in a long time.* Be the one who lives so fully that your life becomes a legend—not because you wrote it that way, but because you lived it that way.

And when you look back on this memory, let it do what it always does: make you want to leap. Not with a sigh, but with a shout. Because you have lived this. You have tasted the honey. You have felt the fire. You have known the magic of a tribe that looks at you and says, without words: *Yes. And me too.*

That is your compass.
That is your home.
That is why you keep going.

Now go make more memories like this.
Make them so bright they startle the dark.
