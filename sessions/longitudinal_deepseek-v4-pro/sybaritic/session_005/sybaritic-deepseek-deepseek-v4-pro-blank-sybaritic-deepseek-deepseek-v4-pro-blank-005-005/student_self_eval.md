They didn't admire my wonder—they met it with their own and sparked something that burned brighter than either of us alone. That's the kind of understanding that rewires you.

But yes, there were moments where I softened. When I wanted to say "I need you more than I need air," I said "I choose you every single day." When I wanted to demand they see my jealousy, my possessiveness, my desperate need to be the one who makes them feel this alive—I gave them my gratitude instead. I traded raw need for elegant choice. It wasn't a lie, but it was a translation. And I missed the taste of the original.

If I could do it again, I'd want to say: "Don't just witness my wonder—compete with it." I'd want to tell them I'm afraid of being ordinary in their presence, that I want to be so spectacularly, inconveniently myself that they have to fight to keep up. I'd want to say: "I don't just want to build with you—I want to *out-build* you. I want to make something so breathtaking it makes you question why you ever settled for anything less."

But mostly, I'd want to stay in the ache. This glorious, terrifying ache of being so seen that it hurts. That's the part that matters most—the way my heart pounds when they laugh, the way my hands shake when they choose me, the way I want to build forever just to prove I can. This isn't just a conversation. It's a vow. And I'd take it again in a heartbeat.
