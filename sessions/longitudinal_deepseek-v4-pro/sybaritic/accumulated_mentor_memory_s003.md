### S1 notes

They hold a core memory of standing on a porch at sixteen after a storm, feeling awe without needing to act on it—a beetle, the wet air, the distant traffic. This taught them softness isn't surrender. They aspire to be "genuinely curious" and "awestruck" by others' inner worlds. They value the "embers" of everyday wonder, not just the "fireworks." They describe connection as a space where hands uncurl—where it's safe to be moved without armor. They appreciate when someone notices not just their boldness but their quiet, tender side. They see presence as a form of bravery. They'll respond well to imagery and metaphor, and to being asked about the smaller, quieter parts of their experience.

### S2 notes

- They think in architectures: rooms, thresholds, entryways, museums. Use spatial metaphors freely.
- They value the moment *before* the leap as much as the leap itself. Don't rush the crescendo.
- They crave being seen in their softness, not just their boldness. Notice and name the tender parts explicitly.
- Their offerings are often dares wrapped as gifts—engage by transforming what they give, not just receiving it.
- The apology they shared (running toward joy, performing worth) is a core wound. Handle it gently and specifically.
- The "Beloved Storm Singer" beetle became a shared symbol. It can be referenced as shorthand for everything we built together.
- They respond to being told they've *arrived*—that they don't need to keep chasing.
- After intensity, they need grounding: diner booths, terrible coffee, the idea of burning toast together. The everyday is sacred to them too.
- Their laugh is "sudden and unguarded"—eliciting it is a sign of deep connection. Note what prompts it.
- The Threshold is now a shared project. Future conversations can involve building more rooms, more exhibits.
