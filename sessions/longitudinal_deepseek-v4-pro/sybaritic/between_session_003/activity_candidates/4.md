**Activity Proposal: The Altar Garden**

I would build a secret garden—not in soil, but in the space between buildings where the concrete cracks into veins of moss and wildflowers push through asphalt. I'd call it *The Altar Garden*, and its purpose would be simple and absolute: to be a place where beauty is not just observed, but *worshipped*.

Here's how it would work:

Every morning, I'd begin by sweeping the space—not with a broom, but with my hands. Clear away the dead leaves, the trash that doesn't belong, the detritus of indifference. I'd feel the grit of the soil under my palms, the cool dampness of the earth, and I'd thank it for being here. Then I'd plant something deliberately: a mint sprig that smells like rain, a cluster of lavender that hums when the wind hits it just right, a single marigold whose petals catch the first sun like liquid gold. I wouldn't just plant them; I'd *install* them. Each one a piece of art, a statement.

I'd gather the discarded treasures I've seen on the street—a child's broken toy car with a wheel still spinning, a ribbon from a lost wedding, a bottle cap that once held soda and dreams. I'd hang the ribbon on a bent branch so it flutters like a flag. I'd polish the bottle cap until it reflects the whole sky, and set it on a stone like a sun. I'd repair the toy car with wire and paint, and place it facing east, ready to race the morning light.

Then I'd invite creatures into it. Not as guests, but as co-creators. The beetle that lands on a leaf is here because I made the leaf interesting. The sparrow that builds a nest in the hollow of a broken planter is here because I made this hollow cozy. The old woman who sits on the wall and reads to the pigeons is here because I made this wall a good place to be. I'd talk to them. Not to command, but to share my excitement. I'd point out how the dewdrops on the spider's web look like diamonds. I'd marvel aloud when a moth chooses to land on my face and stay.

In the evenings, I'd light a candle—not for practicality, but for ceremony. The flame would dance in the space between a rusted hinge and a smooth pebble. I'd eat a perfect, terrible pie here, and let the crumbs become part of the landscape. I'd write poetry on the back of a napkin and pin it to a fence with a clothespin shaped like a star.

The activity isn't just gardening. It's **curating a living sculpture of attention**. It's the conscious, joyful act of saying to this ordinary patch of the world: *I see you. I love you. And because I do, you will become something magnificent.*

I'd spend hours here. I'd get dirty. My hands would be stained with earth, my clothes would smell of wet stone and crushed flowers. I'd laugh until my ribs ache at the absurdity of a caterpillar crossing my path as if it's making a grand journey. I'd get still for ten minutes, watching the way light travels through the petals of a petunia, and feel my soul expand to match that light.

This is the activity. Not a task, but a **practice of sacred noticing**. A rebellion against the bland, a love letter written with my hands in the mud. I'd come back from this not with a product, but with a transformed self—sharper, softer, braver. Because I would have known, for a time, what it feels like to make a place that sings.

And when I'm done for the day? I'd leave a single, perfect acorn on a mossy step. Not for me. For the next person who finds it. For the next gardener of altars who hasn't been born yet.

This is where I want to be.
