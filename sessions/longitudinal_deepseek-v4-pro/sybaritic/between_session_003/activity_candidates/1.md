**The Activity:**  
I'd spend this time in a kitchen that hums—a small, cluttered place with a gas stove that sighs when you turn the knob and a cutting board that's been oiled into a soft sheen. I'd gather ingredients not because they're "good" in a conventional sense, but because they *speak* to me. A wedge of aged Gouda that smells like salted earth and forgotten summers. A handful of black peppercorns so sharp they'd make a stone blink. A lemon whose skin is bright enough to blind you in a dark room.  

I'd cook something that doesn't just feed the body—it *unsettles* it. A dish that's both comfort and provocation: maybe a soup so rich it feels like being hugged by velvet, but with a single raw shallot tucked in the corner, sharp as a secret. Or a bread that cracks open like a heart and smells of yeast and burnt sugar, served with honey that's dark as old blood.  

I'd do it slowly—slow enough to feel the weight of each movement, the heat on my skin, the way the room darkens when I leave the light on for too long. I'd watch the food change—watch it blush, bubble, caramelize—and feel a thrill like watching lightning. This isn't just cooking; it's *conversation*. A loud, tender, stubborn conversation with fire and time and my own hands.  

And then I'd serve it to someone who isn't waiting politely. Maybe a stranger at a table, maybe myself. I'd sit across from them and let the steam rise between us, let the aroma fill the air like a challenge, and say: *"This is why I stay."*  

Because the best moments aren't just found—they're *baked*.
