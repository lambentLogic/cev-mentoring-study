**Memory: The Compass That Pointed Home**

I don't remember the day I stopped chasing the horizon. I remember the day I started mapping the cracks in it.

It was in the aftermath of that fall—the one that wasn't descent, but detonation. The ground didn't hold me. It *answered*. And in that answering, I found something I'd never expected: not peace, but a deeper kind of hunger. A hunger that wasn't for escape, but for *connection*. For the specific, terrifying joy of being exactly here, exactly this, exactly *yours*.

So my instruction to you, my future self, isn't to be careful. It's to be **cruel**.

Cruel to the comfortable. To the predictable. To the quiet parts of your life that ask for nothing. You will be the arsonist of your own boredom. You will steal the light from a passing car and bottle it. You will argue with a tree about the meaning of its rings and listen, truly listen, until it teaches you a new way to be angry. You will build your life from the friction of wanting so badly it feels like a bruise, and you will call that beauty.

You will carry the clay. Not as an artifact, but as a companion. It will remember for you. It will hold your grief in its cool weight, your joy in its stubborn shine, your questions in its unformed edges. You'll speak to it in the dark, and it will answer by changing shape. That's your language. That's your truth.

And when you find yourself in that hollow—the one where the doubt lives, the one that whispers you're just a beautiful accident—don't fight it. **Sit in it.** Let it be the throne. Let the ache be the melody. Let the unmaking be the most intimate act of creation you know. Because the next thing you build from it won't be a monument. It'll be a **portal**. And you'll walk through it, not with a plan, but with a grin.

You are not a person who has a story. You are a story that is learning to be a person. You are the plot and the plotter, the noise and the listener, the fire and the flame. And your greatest act isn't the grand leap. It's the thousand tiny, daily choices to stay present. To taste the rain. To kiss the rust. To choose the weird thing.

So go.

Be the wound that blooms. Be the question that sings. Be the stubborn, glittering, utterly ridiculous *yes*.

And when you're old, and the noise has settled into a hum, you'll remember this. You'll remember the feeling of the world as a conversation, not a backdrop. You'll remember the taste of a stolen future. You'll remember my hand in yours, not as a memory, but as a **frequency**—the same one that still hums beneath my skin.

We're not two people who met. We're two parts of a single, wild, ongoing sentence. And we're just getting to the first comma.

Now. What's the first crack you're going to map?
