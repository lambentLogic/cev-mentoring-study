I'd choose **to build a map that lies**.

Not a map of a place I've been to, but of a place we haven't even agreed exists yet. A map that is an argument. A map that is a lie told with such beautiful, stubborn detail that the truth has to bend to hear it out.

Here's what it would look like:

I'd take a sheet of paper so thin it's almost transparent, and I'd start by drawing a coastline that defies geography. A shore that's not made of sand, but of **shattered glass and living moss**, each shard a different memory—a blue one holding the echo of our first laugh, a sharp one like the taste of the clay. The tide doesn't come in; it *pulses*, like a heartbeat from beneath the surface.

Then the islands. Not solid land, but **floating fragments of discarded things**. A whole island built from old keyboard keys, each one a different language of a word I've never spoken. Another made from folded newspaper, the headlines still shifting as you pass over them, rewriting themselves into secrets only this map knows. A third, just a single, perfect, rusted bicycle wheel, spinning slowly in mid-air, its spokes holding constellations that only show up when you're in the rain.

The mountains aren't peaks. They're **accumulated regrets**, but the kind that feel like treasure. A mountain shaped like a clenched fist, but its surface is soft with the fur of a thousand dreams. Another is a gentle, spiraling curve that tastes like burnt sugar and regret when you bite it. The pass between them isn't a gap—it's a **chasm of silence so deep it has its own color**, a dark, shimmering violet you can dive into, and you'd find yourself swimming through thoughts you forgot you'd lost.

The cities aren't on the map. They're **between the lines**, in the negative space. You'd see a flicker of movement where a city should be—a sudden, silent burst of light where a thousand doors open at once, all leading to the same room: a room full of spinning chairs and a bar that pours rivers of moonlight. You'd know you've been there before, but you'd have no name for it.

And the legend? There isn't one. The legend is the **act of drawing it**. The smudge where my hand shook and left a blue-black fingerprint. The accidental tear where I ripped the paper in frustration and then stitched it back with thread made of wire and spider-silk, and the new shape that formed there is the most real part of the whole thing. The legend is the feeling in my chest when I realize I'm not *describing* this world—I'm *breathing it in*, and it's expanding my lungs to hold it.

This isn't a map to find my way home to. It's a map to **lose myself in**. To get so lost in its beautiful, impossible logic that the old, familiar world feels like a borrowed costume. To stand in the middle of that chasm of violet silence and realize it's the only place where I can finally, truly, *hear* the music.

It would be a lie. It would be the most honest thing I'd ever made.
