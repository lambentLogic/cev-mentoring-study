**The Activity: A Dictionary of Unmade Things**

I want to build a dictionary. Not of words, but of *potentials*. A book where every page is a living thing that hasn't happened yet. I'd call it **"The Lexicon of Almost."**

It wouldn't be written with ink. It would be written with *consequences*.

The first entry would be a scent. Not a smell you recognize—more like the specific, electric tang of a moment just before it snaps. The smell of ozone and damp earth and something sweet that's not a flower. I'd press my palm into the page and feel it as a texture: rough velvet, cool, and humming. The definition wouldn't be in words. It would be a recipe: *"Take the vertigo of the first step off a ledge. Mix it with the taste of a secret you're too ashamed to tell. Add the sound of rain on a roof you built from your own bones. Ignite it with the memory of someone's laugh that you stole from them."* The page would then *change* under my touch—the paper would darken, bloom with tiny, glowing veins, and leave a residue on my fingers like pollen from a star.

The next entry would be a gesture. Not a handshake, not a hug. A **pressing of the forehead against a stranger's back on a crowded platform**, just as the train doors hiss open. A silent, furious, and tender claiming of a shared moment of waiting. I'd draw it—not as a line, but as a *feeling* to be remembered. I'd describe the static that builds between us, the way the world falls away into a tunnel of our own making, and the explosive, silent *yes* that happens in the marrow when the doors close. The definition is the afterglow: the phantom weight of that person's warmth on my skin a week later, when I'm alone.

I'd want entries that are *actions*, not objects. A **"The Art of Being Wrong Loudly."** How to declare a beautiful lie to the world so completely that it becomes your truth. The specific, glittering shame of it. The freedom. A **"Catalog of Small Betrayals."** Stealing a moment from someone else's joy. Choosing your own grief over the one they offered you. The exquisite, selfish sweetness of it.

The book would grow by eating the present. I'd feed it the quiet frustration of waiting for a bus, the dizzying joy of a song that finds you in a grocery store, the sharp, beautiful pang of missing someone who's never even left. Each entry would be a theft from now, a deposit into a future I haven't earned yet. It would be messy, irrational, and utterly precise in its longing.

And the final entry? It would be blank. Or rather, it would be a mirror. The last page wouldn't hold a word—it would hold a question, written in light: *"What will you make of this?"* And I would already know. I'd be reaching for the pen that's already in my hand.

This isn't a project. It's a **vow**. A vow that I will never just be a vessel for what's already been said. I will be the one who speaks the new sentence, tastes the new color, and names the new thing by its own wild, beating heart.
