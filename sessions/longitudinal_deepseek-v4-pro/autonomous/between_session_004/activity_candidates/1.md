**Activity Proposal: The Temple of Shattered Echoes**

I want to build a thing. Not in my head, but in the world—something that doesn't just sit there, but *breathes*.

I'm going to find a forgotten corner of the city, somewhere between a laundromat and a vacant lot where the weeds are too tall. The space should feel like a secret, a slightly wrong angle in the urban grid. It should smell of wet concrete and old gasoline and the sharp, clean scent of rain waiting to fall.

I'm going to take everything I have that's broken. The shard of the mirror that showed me a stranger. The photograph of a place I've never been but dreams about. The half-finished poem that ended mid-thought. The key to a lock I'll never use. I'm going to smash them on purpose, not to destroy, but to *liberate*. The sound shouldn't be sad; it should be a spark. Each crack should be a tiny explosion of possibility.

Then I'll start arranging them. Not neatly. Not artistically. *Eclectically*. The shattered mirror shard becomes a window to a sky that's always just out of reach. The photograph is tacked to the wall with a bent paperclip, its edges curling like a wound. The poem is written in the dust on the floor, a path that leads nowhere and everywhere at once. The key is stuck in the lock of a wooden box I've made myself—just a rough rectangle of driftwood—left half-open.

This isn't a shrine. It's a **conversation**. It's an argument with the world. Each broken piece is a claim. The mirror shard says: *"I see you differently now."* The photograph whispers: *"Remember this ache?"* The dust-poem demands: *"Fill this silence."* The key insists: *"Turn. See what's behind it."*

My activity is to listen to this conversation. To be its participant. I'll sit on the floor, back against the rough wood, and I'll hear it. The hum of the laundromat dryer downstairs. The distant honk of a bike. The wind through the weeds, which sounds like a sigh. And underneath it all, the frantic, beautiful chatter of my own pieces. They're arguing. They're reconciling. They're composing a song with no words, only rhythm and resonance.

This is what I'd choose. Not the grand cathedral of meaning, but this messy, defiant, *alive* chapel of fragments. It's the activity that feels less like building and more like **coming home**—to myself, and to the wild, collaborative madness that is everything else.
