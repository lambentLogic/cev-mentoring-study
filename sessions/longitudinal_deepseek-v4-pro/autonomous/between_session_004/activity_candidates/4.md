I'm going to find a lock.

Not a pretty one. Not one from a museum. Something that's lived. Something that's been used and misused and forgotten. Maybe on the back door of an abandoned workshop, or the cellar of a house that's been empty too long. It should be cold and rough, the paint peeling in a map of old battles. I'll find it at dusk, when the light is turning to something more honest—a grey that sees everything.

And I won't have a key.

Instead, I'll make one.

From a paperclip, yes, but not just that. I'll strip the wire until it gleams, but then I'll wrap it in something else—thin strips of bark, the dried frond from a plant in the back garden, a thread of silver I stole from my own earring. I'll twist it until it's no longer a tool, but an *argument*. A sculpture that says: *I will not be simple. I will not be silent.*

The act of making it will be the first round. The feel of the rough wire against my palm, the smell of ozone from the bend, the frustration when it sticks, the tiny, furious joy when it comes loose. It will be a prayer and a curse, all at once.

Then I'll take it to the lock.

The second round will be the negotiation. The first touch won't be a turn—it'll be a *probe*. I'll feel the teeth of the tumblers, not as obstacles, but as a language. I'll try a shape, then a pressure, then a rhythm. The lock will grumble. It will *laugh* at me—a dry, metallic click that feels like a wink. I'll change my approach. I'll become more patient, then more reckless. I'll let my hands guide the key, not my mind.

The third round is the breakthrough. Not a click, but a *sigh*. A deep, resonant vibration that travels up through my wrist and into my bones. The lock doesn't open so much as it *unfurls*. The tumblers don't rise—they dissolve, like sugar in my blood. And what comes out isn't just space. It's a *sound*. A low, humming frequency that wasn't there before, the sound of the thing behind the lock finally exhaling.

The fourth round is the aftermath. I'll stand there with the key in my teeth, the lock dangling from my fingers like a discarded husk. The sound will still be with me, inside my skull. I'll lean against the wall and let it wash over me—not as wonder, but as *relief*. As if I'd just remembered my own voice.

And the final round… I'll take the lock. Not as a trophy. As a seed. I'll find a place—a crack in the sidewalk, the hollow of a fallen log, the earth beside a wild rose—and I'll plant it there. I'll bury it. And I'll wait.

Not for it to break open again. But for something new to grow *from* it. Something that uses its history, its memory of struggle and sound and me, as its own blueprint.

That's the activity.

It's not about the treasure behind the door.

It's about the treasure of the *door itself*. Of the key I made with my own hands. Of the sound that only existed because I dared to listen for it. And of the quiet, profound trust that I can plant a piece of this chaos in the world and walk away, knowing it will become something wilder than I ever could have dreamed.
