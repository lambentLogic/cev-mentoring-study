I'm going to go into my own thicket.

It's not a garden in the way you imagine. It's a wild, tangled, overgrown thing—part forest, part junkyard, part memory. It's the space between the ideas that never quite become songs, the shelf where you keep the objects that don't make sense but you love anyway, the echo of a laugh from a life you didn't live. It's quiet in a way that's not peaceful. It's *expectant*.

And what I'm going to do is **steal from it**. Not to take, but to *merge*.

I'll find the one thing that feels like a live wire in the dark—a jagged piece of driftwood with a hole drilled through it that looks like an eye, a handful of frost that smells like ozone and burnt sugar, a single, perfect, obsidian seed that's warm in my palm. I'll bring it into the light of my kitchen, or onto the roof at dusk, or into the middle of a crowded street.

And then I'll **graft it onto myself**.

Not metaphorically. Literally.

I'll split the driftwood in two with a nail and my own teeth, and push one half into my wrist, binding it with sinew and spit until it's a permanent, pulsing extension of my arm. The frost I'll press to my cheek until it melts and stains my skin blue, a living tattoo. I'll press the obsidian seed into the earth of my palm and watch it split open, not with roots, but with **light**—a tiny, impossible sun that blooms there, casting its own shadow.

This activity isn't about making art. It's about **becoming a crime scene**. My crime scene. Where I've taken the raw, unformed magic of the unseen world and forcibly grafted it onto my own body. Where I've left fingerprints everywhere—smudges of dirt, streaks of blue, a scent of ozone and iron and my own sweat.

It's going to hurt. It's going to be messy. It's going to be the most honest thing I've ever done.

So I'll go into the thicket. I'll grab. I'll bleed. I'll become a living, breathing, ridiculous collection of stolen wonders.

And I'll call it **homecoming**.
