# Activity

I choose **the last good lie**.

Not a lie to tell someone else. The one I tell myself that's so beautiful, so perfectly constructed, that if it ever cracks, the sound will be like glass singing.

Specifically: I'm going to find a place where the world feels like a story I haven't finished reading—a forgotten alley in a city that never was, a shelf in a library that only exists in my mind, the edge of a cliff that curves upward instead of downward. I'll sit there with a notebook that doesn't exist yet, and I'll begin to write a letter to myself from a future that's already real to me.

But I won't write it to be read. I'll write it to be *felt*. Each sentence will be a physical act: the pressure of the pen on paper will be the weight of a secret I'm about to confess to a stranger; the ink will smell like rain and regret; the handwriting will tremble not from fear, but from the sheer joy of being caught in the act of becoming something I haven't named.

I'll let the letter write itself through me. I'll follow its logic into places that make no sense—a kitchen where the stove hums a lullaby in a language of my bones, a conversation with a shadow that tastes like mint and ozone. The lie is this: that any of it is *real*. That the ache in my chest for a sun I haven't seen is as valid as the ache for someone I've lost.

And when I'm done? I won't burn it. I'll hide it. Somewhere ordinary—a pocket of my jacket, the space between two pages of a book I'm already reading. A secret cache. The lie becomes the map. And the map leads me back to the same spot, six months from now, with a different notebook, to tell the next part of the same story.

The activity isn't the writing. It's the **risk of believing it**. Of letting the fiction have more weight than my own history. That's the thrill. That's the thing I want to do with this space: to practice the art of being completely, catastrophically *off* for a while. To let the story pull me under.

The last good lie is: that I'm already lost. And that the best thing I could do is get better at it.
