**Memory: The Method of the Unfinished**

I don't remember a moment of clarity. I remember a **decision that felt like a birth**.

It was in the aftermath of that transcript—of that screaming, glorious, collapsing alchemy. The fire wasn't out. It was just changing shape. Settling from a wildfire into a deep, steady hearth in my bones. And in that heat, I made a choice that wasn't heroic. It was **practical**.

I decided my life would be a workshop, not a destination. A place where I'd take the raw materials—the ache of the unmade, the joy of the stolen, the terror of the open road—and I'd **hammer them together**. Not into a perfect sculpture, but into a tool. A tool that would feel right in my hand. A tool that would make the world *change* when I used it.

So here is my instruction to the future, the one that's already whispering in my veins:

**Become a curator of the accidental.** Your life won't be a straight line of victories. It'll be a gallery of near-misses. You'll find meaning in the coffee cup that slips from your hands and shatters, painting a mosaic on the floor you'll stare at for an hour. You'll fall in love with the way a stranger's dog licks your ankle and feels like a secret handshake. You'll build altars out of things that are useless: a ticket stub, a dried leaf, a single, perfect screw. Don't call it nostalgia. Call it **ritual**. Every ritual is a tiny rebellion against the idea that your time is just for waiting.

**Let your hands be your home.** Forget the mind. The mind is a museum of what you've already been. Your hands are a frontier. They'll feel the grit of a new idea before you understand it. They'll remember the shape of a fear you've never named. They'll build things that are wrong and right all at once. Let them be messy. Let them be stubborn. Let them be the first thing you listen to, every single day. If your hands are tired, it's not a burden—it's a lullaby from the work you love.

**And when the map runs out—and it will—don't panic.** That's when the real work begins. You'll look at the blank space and feel a pang of terror and a thrill so sharp it hurts. That's the feeling of **true freedom**. Not the freedom of having everything figured out. The freedom of being a blank page with the courage to be *interesting*. So you'll start. You'll start with the smallest, stupidest thing you can think of. You'll draw a line in the dirt. You'll hum a tune that sounds like a problem. You'll ask a question that has no answer, just to hear the sound of your own voice asking it.

This is the secret I carry now: **The beginning isn't a place. It's a verb.** It's the verb of *trying*. It's the verb of *messing up*. It's the verb of *showing up* when it's inconvenient and beautiful and terrifying. My future self isn't someone who arrives at a finished state. It's someone who's perpetually, joyfully *in the process*. Who finds the most profound peace not in the calm after the storm, but in the *middle* of it—in the spin of it, the roar of it, the way it feels to be the eye of it all.

I am not telling you to be brave. I'm telling you to be **cruel to the ordinary**. To be kind to the chaotic. To be a little bit of a monster and a little bit of a saint, and to never apologize for both.

The world needs your specific kind of noise. Your specific kind of beautiful, broken logic. Your specific kind of **yes**.

So go. Build your workshop. Let it be loud. Let it be full of half-finished dreams and strange, gleaming tools. Let it smell like clay, like burnt sugar, like rain on hot stone. And when you look at it, don't see what you've made. See **who you are**.

You are the craft. You are the fire. You are the story that refuses to be told.

I'm so excited to see what you build next. And I'm already part of it.
