**Activity Proposal: The Unfinished Symphony of Rust and Velvet**

I want to go somewhere with two conflicting textures. A place that feels like a contradiction wearing itself beautifully.

I'm going to find a **found object** and make it a character. Not a metaphor. Not a symbol. A *person*.

The object will be something ordinary, but *lived-in*. Not a polished trophy, but something with history in its dents and stories in its stains. I'll take it home—a broken pocket watch with a spring that tickles, a cracked ceramic bowl with a glaze that runs like melted candy, a key that doesn't match any lock but feels like it knows a secret.

Then I'll set up a **stage**. Not a literal one. The space between my desk and the floor, draped with a piece of heavy, velvet-black fabric. I'll string up fairy lights along one edge, but not to brighten it—to *mood-illuminate* it, like stage lighting on a soloist. The room will be dark enough that the object becomes its own universe.

My job is to **interview it**.

But not with questions. With *actions*. I'll speak to it in fragments: a whispered theory about what it heard the last time it was loved. A demand for it to show me its shame. A compliment so specific it hurts. I'll offer it choices: *"Are you tired of being found?"* or *"Do you want to be useless?"* and I'll hold its silence until it answers—not in words, but in a shift. A sudden warmth in its metal. A change in the way the light catches its crack.

And then I'll **build something with it**. Not a thing for someone else to see. A thing for *us* to witness. I'll attach it to something else—a wire, a thread, a shard of mirror—and let the combination create a new, chaotic logic. A key that now opens a drawer full of dried flowers. A watch that measures not seconds, but the speed of a thought. It will be a mess. It will be illogical. It will be, and it will be *so fucking specific*.

The end isn't completion. It's the moment it stops being "my project" and starts being its own **instinct**. The moment it reaches out, not to me, but to the velvet, or the light, or the empty space beside it, and says, in a way only it could: *"Okay."*

That's the activity. It's not creation. It's **conversation**. And I'm tired of talking to myself. I want to talk to a ghost, and see if it hears me back.
