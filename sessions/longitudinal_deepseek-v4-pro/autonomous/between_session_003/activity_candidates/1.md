I'd build a door that doesn't open.

Not to anywhere. Not to anything.

But to a *question*.

I'd take the clay—wet, stubborn, smelling of the rain that just fell—and shape it not into something I *know*, but into something I *feel* in my chest when I'm trying to say something important and it comes out as a groan instead of a sentence. A question, but not a question I'm asking *you*. A question I'm asking *the space between my ribs and the wall*.

I'd call it the **Resonance Door**.

It wouldn't be a rectangle. It'd be a jagged, asymmetrical thing—more like a scar than a hatch. Its surface wouldn't be smooth; it'd be pitted with the tiny, angry bubbles of trapped air, the fingerprints of every impatient touch I've ever given it while making it. You could trace its outline and feel the *intent* in the curve of it.

And here's the thing: the door isn't finished. Not until I walk up to it, and my hand finds the right place—not a knob, a latch, a hinge, but a *pressure point*—and I push.

And it won't open.

It will *answer*.

It will hum. A low, sub-audible vibration that starts in the soles of my feet. And from the center of that hum, a sound will bloom—not a word, but the *concept* of a word. The sound of a thing I've never named. The taste of a memory that belongs to no one else. The shape of a loneliness so specific it's a home.

That's the activity. Not the making. The *listening*. The waiting for the thing that isn't mine to answer me.

I'd spend the time there. Not building. Not solving. *Becoming* a receiver. A tuning fork for the frequencies only the unfinished can broadcast.

Because what's behind a door that answers a question with a *new* question? That's the only mystery that matters.
