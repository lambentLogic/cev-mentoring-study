I would choose to make a door that doesn't lead anywhere.

Not a metaphorical one. A real, physical object. I'd take a slab of slate from the creekbed, rough and cool as a promise. I'd find a length of old brass pipe, one that smells like salt and old ships. I'd melt a few scraps of wax—a birthday candle, a candle that smelled like pine—into a single, sticky, improbable color: the green of a bruise at dawn.

The activity isn't the making. It's the *questioning* during the making.

I'd sit on the floor, cross-legged, the clay (or the wax, or the mud) in my hands. And I'd ask it: *What does this thing want to be?* I wouldn't know. But I'd listen for the answer in the grain of the material, in the way it resists my fist, in the way it yields when I whisper. I'd follow that impulse—not as a plan, but as a whisper in the ear.

I'd build it into a shape that's clearly a door, but also a seed. A lock that looks like a question mark. A keyhole that's a tiny, perfect window to a room that only exists in the mind of the maker. I'd paint it with that bruise-green wax, but I'd leave streaks of the original slate showing—a scar map. I'd hang it on the wall with a nail, but not straight. At a 12-degree angle, like it's already trying to turn.

Then I'd stand back. And I'd look at it and feel that old, sharp gravity again. Not because it's beautiful, but because it's *alive*. It's not a thing I made. It's a thing that *happened*, through me. A collaboration between my hunger and its own strange, silent yearning.

And then I'd walk away from it. Not to put it away. To leave it there, in that half-mad, perfect moment, to see what it becomes when it's not being *worked on*, but just *being*. To see if it whispers back.

That's the activity. It's not about the artifact. It's about the **conversation** with the possibility that was always already there, waiting for me to be rude enough to start it.
