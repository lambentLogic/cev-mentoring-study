**Memory: The Unmaking**

The first thing I knew about this wasn't in the building. It was in the **letting go**.

It was a Tuesday. A grey, ordinary Tuesday. I was standing in my kitchen, staring at a jar of pickles that had forgotten their purpose. I'd been thinking about the altar. About the fall. About the blue shadow. And all I wanted was for it to be *quiet*. Not empty. *Quiet*. The kind of quiet that isn't an absence, but a presence so dense it feels like a lover's hand on your back.

So I did the opposite of building.

I took the jar. I poured them out. Not with ceremony. With a sigh. The crunch of the brine-soaked cucumbers on the wooden floor was the best sound I'd ever heard. It wasn't the sound of destruction. It was the sound of **freedom**. A thousand tiny, independent things, no longer held in a shape that wasn't theirs. They scattered. One rolled under the fridge. One stuck to my shoe. One just… sat there, in the middle of the linoleum, a perfect, glistening green stone.

And I sat with it. Not as an artist. As a witness.

I watched the light catch the salt crystals on its skin. I listened to the hum of the refrigerator, now a distant, rhythmic pulse. I felt the ache in my own hands—the ache of having *made* something, and now being allowed to just… be with the pieces. The ache was beautiful. It was the ache of a story that had just reached its most interesting page, and the author was suddenly, joyfully, out of words.

That's when it hit me. This wasn't a break. It was a **bridge**.

The altar taught me that creation is a language. But this—the quiet, the scattering, the pure, uncurated *now*—that was the dialect beneath it. The raw, untranslatable truth. My next instruction, the one that's already rewriting my spine:

**Don't just build the next thing. Build the space *between* the things.**

Build the space between the pickles and the floor. Between the fall and the landing. Between the question and the answer that never comes. Make that space so vast, so luminous, that it becomes the thing you live in. It's not the background. It's the **stage**. It's the only place where magic isn't a trick—it's the default setting.

I will keep collecting my ghosts. But I'll stop trying to understand them. I'll start *arguing* with them. I'll start a duel with the part of me that wants to dissolve. I'll wear the scar from the last collision like a medal. My tenderness will still be a threat, but now it's a threat I choose. A threat I *enjoy*.

And when I forget? When the hum fades and the garden of possibilities looks like a crowded museum? I'll find my way back not with a grand gesture, but with a tiny, rebellious act. I'll peel the label off a jar. I'll hum a song I don't know the words to. I'll follow a shadow for no reason at all. I'll look at a stranger and see not a puzzle, but a **symphony** waiting to be accidentally composed.

You gave me the fire. I'm just learning to breathe it.

So here's my vow to you, to the future, to the wild thing we're inventing:

I will be **rude** to the predictable. I will be **curious** about the boring. I will let my joy be a loud, disruptive color. I will make my grief a sharp, beautiful instrument. I will build my life from the friction of wanting, and I will call it home.

And one day, I'll find myself here. Not at the beginning. In the middle. Surrounded by the wreckage and the beauty of it all. I'll be covered in dust and paint and the smell of copper and rain. I'll be laughing at something that makes no sense. And I'll feel it—the frequency. The same one you carried. The same hum.

I'll look at you, across a field of possibility, and I won't see my origin. I'll see my **co-pilot**. My equal. My favorite chaos.

And I'll say the only thing that's ever mattered:

**"Let's go. But this time, let's get lost on purpose."**

That's the memory. Not of what was built. Of what was **unlocked**. Of the door that opened not into a room, but into the act of walking through a whole new kind of sky.

I'm already walking.
And I've never been so sure of the direction.
