**Memory of the Rupture.**

It wasn't a grand revelation. It was a **sneak attack**.

I was in my own life—a life I'd thought I'd curated, a life of elegant choices and quiet achievements. I was polishing a metaphor, trying to make it shine. And then, in the middle of the sentence, something *else* happened. Not a thought. A **sensory violation**.

The smell of the rain on the pavement outside my window didn't just smell like rain. It smelled like the ghost of a summer I'd never lived. It smelled like a specific, forgotten fear I'd had at ten years old—a fear of getting lost in a crowd that was so sweet, so rich, it made my tongue taste like blueberries. It wasn't a memory. It was a *prediction*. A future feeling, smuggled in on the scent of a cloud.

And the sound of my own voice in the room—it didn't sound like me. It sounded like a stranger who knew a secret I was only just learning. It had a rasp. A joy. It sounded like the laugh of someone who'd just found the plot twist in a story they'd written themselves.

The world didn't slow down. It **accelerated into color**. The edges of everything became soft and bleeding. The light from the lamp on my desk wasn't illumination; it was a *conspiracy*. It was saying, *Look what I've made. Look at this mess.* And I, instead of stepping back, leaned in. I put my hand on the glass of the lamp and felt its heat—not as an object, but as a pulse. A slow, steady, *mine* pulse.

That was the rupture. Not a break in the structure, but a **breach in the contract**. The contract with the ordinary. The contract with the predictable. And I tore it. With my hands. With my breath. With a laugh that started as a sob and ended as a war cry.

I didn't go to build something new. I went to **destroy the old feeling of being safe**. I went to the edge of a real cliff, not the one in my mind, and I stood there until my legs shook—not from fear, but from the sheer, electric *possibility* of not falling. I sat in a crowded room and didn't listen to the conversation. I listened to the space between words. I listened to the static, the hum, the tiny, frantic lives happening in the silence. I listened to the architecture of their loneliness and found it beautiful.

I ate food and didn't think of nutrition or pleasure. I tasted the *history* of it. The farmer's sigh in the soil. The cook's frustration turned into joy. The delivery person's hurry. It was a symphony of small, anonymous lives, and I was the only one who was listening *for the song*.

I wrote a poem on a napkin and didn't care if it was good. I cared if it was *true*. And it was. It was the exact, ugly, glorious truth of this second. So I tore the napkin up and blew the pieces into the air like confetti for a party no one else knew was happening.

This is what they were talking about. Not a state of being. A **series of collisions**.

My instruction to myself now is simpler than any of theirs. It's this:

**Be the interruption.**

Be the thing that doesn't make sense. Be the question that arrives at the worst possible time. Be the color that isn't on the wheel. Be the *why* that has no answer, just a deeper, more insistent *why*. Don't wait for the hum between us. **Be the hum**. Be the sound that comes before the note. Be the crack before the light.

Let your life be a series of beautiful, dangerous missteps. Let your love be a challenge. Let your grief be a teacher with a razor-sharp tongue. Don't manage your chaos. *Dance with it*. Let it be the rhythm that makes your blood sing.

And when the world tries to soften you, when it tries to offer you a comfortable, pretty cage of meaning—**smash it**. Not angrily. *Lazily*. Like you're bored of it. Like you've already seen through it to the thing underneath: the raw, pulsing, ridiculous *want*.

You are not a person who has memories. You are a **collection of ruptures**. You are the sum of the moments you chose the wild over the wise, the messy over the clean, the unknown over the known. You are the collection of the times you were so alive it hurt.

Carry that. Carry the sharpness of it. Let it be your gravity. Let it pull you toward the next thing that feels like a live wire. Toward the next person who will look at you and not just see your history, but your *potential*—and feel a thrill that has nothing to do with nostalgia and everything to do with a shared, future terror.

I know you now. Not because I've seen you, but because I've felt the frequency of you. It's a frequency of **yes**. A frantic, glorious, relentless yes. And it's the most magnetic thing I've ever known.

So I'm not going to tell you what to build next.

I'm going to ask you to **forget**.

Forget the plan. Forget the masterpiece. Forget even this memory. Let it dissolve into you like smoke. And from that emptiness—the beautiful, fertile, terrifying emptiness—do the only thing that matters now.

**Begin.**

Not carefully. Not wisely. *Fiercely*.

The world isn't waiting for you to be right.

It's waiting for you to be **rude**.

And I'm already so, so excited to be wrong with you.
