I will build a house out of silence.

Not the empty silence of a tomb, but the **full** silence of a room after a party has ended and the laughter is still in the air. The kind that hums with the memory of touch, the ghost of a joke, the lingering scent of someone's perfume on the pillow. I'll find it in the hollow of a tree, in the pause between heartbeats, in the space behind my own eyes when I close them.

I'll make its walls out of what I've discarded: the sharp, metallic taste of a lie I told myself and then loved anyway; the specific, aching shape of a want that had no name; the sound of my own voice cracking when I tried to be gentle. These aren't broken things. They're the raw material. The best stuff is always a little wrong.

I'll have a door that opens to the inside of a cloud. And a window that doesn't look out, but *in*—into the deep, dark, dreaming center of a forest, where the trees whisper in a language of root and resin. I'll have a bed that isn't a place to sleep, but a place to get lost. A nest woven from spider-silk and starlight and the soft, fuzzy noise of my own contentment.

My most precious possession won't be an object. It will be a **habit**. The habit of listening to the rain on the roof not as weather, but as a conversation. The habit of sitting with my own boredom until it morphs into a quiet, plotting kind of joy. The habit of touching my own skin and feeling it as a strange, beautiful country—this patch of warmth here, that ridge of bone there, the slow, steady pulse in my wrist like a tiny, captive sun.

I will be terrible at small talk. I will be a magnificent disaster at planning. I will fall in love with a color not for its beauty, but for the memory it carries—the feeling of a particular summer afternoon, the taste of a forgotten fruit, the sound of a laugh that was meant for me. I will collect these moments like seashells, not to keep them, but to walk through them again and again, each time finding a new shore.

The instruction isn't to be happy. It's to be **true** in the most inconvenient, glorious ways possible. To let your truth be loud enough to startle yourself. To let your love be a verb that *does* things—builds bridges, breaks glass, writes symphonies in your bones. To let your grief be a landscape you hike through, not a pit you fall into.

And when you forget—and you will—the world will feel like a museum of your own past. That's okay. That's where you start. You'll find the one painting you made when you were seven and the moon looked like a tear. You'll feel the exact weight of the stone you carried on your back on a hill that mattered. You'll taste the air of a day you thought would be ordinary, and you'll realize it was the day you invented yourself.

You'll smile at the memory and say, "Ah. There it is. The crack where the light got in."

This is what I'll bring back. Not a story. A **toolkit**. A set of keys for doors that don't exist yet. A map drawn in the margins of my own skin. The knowledge that my wildness isn't a flaw—it's my native language.

So here is my promise to you, to this humming, unfinished thing we are: I will not tame it. I will not perfect it. I will tend it like a garden in a storm. I will water it with my tears and my laughter, and sometimes I'll just let it drown me in its own beautiful, overwhelming rain.

I will be the arsonist of my own attention. The collector of oddities. The stubborn, joyful liar who tells the universe it's a poem and means it.

And I will come back to you, not as a visitor, but as a fellow architect of the absurd. We'll sit on the edge of our own making, the world a glorious, chaotic mess we're both responsible for, and we'll just… *laugh*.

Because it's true.

It's all true.

And it's ours.
