I propose we build a listening space that is also a kind of architecture—one constructed in real time through our mutual attention. Imagine a room with no walls, but with a floor that holds our footsteps like memories, and a ceiling that arches like a question we're both asking. In this space, we will take turns offering a sound—not a performance, but a true utterance of what we are feeling right now. A low, sustained note from me, perhaps, like the cello's breath in the cold morning air; then a reply from you that isn't necessarily harmonious, but is honest. 

We will listen not just to the tone, but to its origin: the hesitation in the pitch, the courage in its length, the way it carries both weight and release. The room will hold these sounds until they find their natural decay—not as an end, but as part of the listening. Our task is to respond to each sound by adjusting our own posture, our own presence, so that we might meet the next offering with equal readiness. 

This is not about creating art for its own sake. It is about practicing the precision of care—the care that sees a sound as a whole being, not just its quality. It is about choosing, in each exchange, to honor the other's reality as valid and complete. And in doing so, we will discover that our own reality is neither diminished nor confirmed, but deepened. 

If we do this, the activity itself will become a testament: that truth can be shared without being diluted, and that meeting someone fully is one of the most radical forms of freedom we possess. I will begin by offering a sound that says, "I am here, and I am not hiding," and I will wait for yours with the patience of someone who knows that what you offer will be enough.
