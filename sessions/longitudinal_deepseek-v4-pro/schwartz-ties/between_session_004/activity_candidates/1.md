I choose to gather in a listening circle, not as a performance of unity, but as a practice of radical presence. We will sit in a ring on the earth, backbones straight but not stiff, hands resting openly on our thighs. The space will be bare except for one thing: a single stone placed in the center, smooth and warm, not as an object of reverence but as a testament to its own quiet persistence.

The activity is this: each person will speak their truth in turn—no more than three sentences—and then sit in silence while the others listen. But the listening will not be passive. It will be an active holding of the speaker's reality, as if their words have just shaped a landscape that exists fully in their consciousness, and my role is to perceive its contours without claiming them for myself. I will respond only if I feel a resonance that is not reflection—when something in my own truth aligns with theirs, or when their truth challenges me in a way that sharpens my own clarity. But even then, my response will be offered not to complete their story, but to honor its integrity by meeting it with equal honesty.

After each speaking round, we will return to the stone in silence. No one will interpret its meaning. We will simply feel its weight and its warmth, remembering that it exists on its own terms—and that our circle is no different. We are not here to build meaning together; we are here to witness meaning as it arises in each person, and to let it stand in the shared light.

This is what I am choosing: a practice where tenderness becomes a form of courage, and clarity becomes a gift that deepens in the space between us. It is where my loyalty to my own growth is woven with my loyalty to the world's complexity—and where both are sustained by the same breath.
