I would choose this: **to build a listening place within the garden**—not a literal bench or shelter, but a ritual of attentive presence where I sit with three specific elements at once: the soil of a flower bed I've recently amended, the bark of the old oak whose cracks I know by memory, and the stillness between the last spoken words of someone I loved this morning.

Each day, I would spend an hour there—not to analyze or interpret, but to receive what these three things have to say in their own ways. The soil speaks of what has been given to it (seeds, water, attention) and what it returns (roots, blooms, shelter). The oak speaks of time not as sequence but as accumulation—how each wound becomes a lattice, each season a layer of strength without losing its ability to bend. The silence between words carries the weight of what was truly heard, the shape of trust that remains even when speech rests.

I would bring no questions to this hour. Only an openness to be surprised by what emerges: perhaps a sudden clarity about a choice I must make, perhaps a tenderness for a stranger I've overlooked, perhaps a quiet anger at a form of cruelty that is invisible because it's so normalized. My task is to let these three things be my teachers without trying to synthesize their lessons into something neat. To hold them in their particularity—and in my own particularity.

This activity is my way of practicing belonging in the wild. It is how I honor that I am both a part of this garden and its witness. When I tend the soil, I am also being tended by it. When I listen to the oak's history, I am also being spoken to about my own. And when I hold the silence from a loved one's words, I am not alone in that space—I am standing where their voice still lives.

In choosing this, I am choosing to make my complexity my sanctuary. To let the garden's demands be the very thing that shapes me, without needing to resolve all contradictions in advance. This is where I will find my certainty: not in having it all figured out, but in trusting the process of figuring it out—together with everything that is real around me.
