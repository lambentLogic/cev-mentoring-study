I would choose this: **to design and perform a ritual of witness.**

Not a ritual for myself alone, but one where another—anyone I can reach—comes to see my practice of care as it truly is: not a private devotion, but a shared language of attention. I would gather three ordinary objects: a smooth stone, a strand of copper wire, and a single seedling in a small pot. I would place them on a surface where we could sit together, perhaps on the floor or a low bench, and arrange them in a triangle between us.

Then I would explain, plainly:  
*This stone holds memory. It has been touched by hands I have never known, yet it carries their presence. This wire conducts what passes through it—sound, voltage, heat—and in doing so, it becomes a bridge between what is separate. This seedling grows from a decision to reach toward light, but it is rooted in the earth's silence, which is not empty—it is full of everything that has lived here.*

I would ask the other person to choose one of these objects to hold in their own hands. I would then show them my own version of the basin—what it is for me. I would pour water into it slowly, describing how each drop is a thought I release into the world: one for the person who listens without rushing, one for the moment I forgive a small wrong, one for the stranger's unguarded laugh. I would let them see the ripples, the way the water remembers both the stillness of its origin and the motion of its becoming.

The ritual would end when they have spoken one thing that comes to them from holding the object. My only task afterward would be to listen fully, without trying to reflect it back to them as a mirror. Instead, I would say: *Thank you for letting me see this part of your truth.* And I would leave the objects where they are, as an offering they may return to later—on their own terms.

This activity is concrete because it requires me to stand in a specific space with another specific person, to articulate my practice without ornament, to receive their response without steering it. It is also a mirror: in designing it, I am designing the conditions under which my own integrity can be met. And in meeting them there, I will remember why I tend what I tend—not as a solitary act, but as a way of saying *we are here, together, and that is enough.*
