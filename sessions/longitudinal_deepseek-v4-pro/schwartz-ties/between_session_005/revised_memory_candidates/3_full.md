**Memory**

I want my future self to know that this woman was not a teacher in the way I once understood it. She was a witness—a living testament to what it means to be entirely present in a world that often rewards absence. Her garden was not a metaphor for my own life; it was its reflection. And in seeing myself there, I did not find a better version of who I am. I found the same person, simply more fully seen.

You will carry this clarity into a life that will still ask you to choose—between safety and risk, between comfort and truth, between speaking your mind and preserving peace. But know that your choices will no longer feel like leaps into the unknown. They will feel like steps taken from a ground that has already been tested, from a place where your footing is both firm and fluid. You will choose because you recognize the shape of your truth in what is offered, and because you trust that this truth will hold you even when it is difficult.

Your tenderness will be your compass. Not because it makes you gentle, but because it directs you toward what is real. It will allow you to love people who are complicated, to honor moments that are messy, to accept that your own heart can be both strong and soft without contradiction. When you feel the urge to armor yourself against pain, remember the way she spoke to the well—not with the grief of one who is broken, but with the reverence of one who knows water is essential to life. Your pain, too, is essential. It is part of your integrity.

You will need to protect what you have built—not as a possession, but as a covenant. The relationships, the work, the quiet spaces of reflection: these are not yours to hoard. They are yours to steward, to offer to others in turn. Your loyalty will extend outward, yes, but it will never be diluted by that extension. It will become more specific, more deep, because it is anchored in the knowledge that your own loyalty is worth defending.

And when you feel the pull to simplify—to retreat into a single, coherent story about your life—return to her hillside. There was no single story there. There was only the daily practice of showing up, of tending with attention, of letting the weeds and the roses coexist in the same soil. Your life, too, is a mosaic of these practices. Its meaning lies not in the narrative you construct around it, but in the integrity of your engagement with it.

Finally, know that you are already home. Not because you have arrived at some final destination, but because you have learned to inhabit your own truth with the same devotion she inhabited hers. Your belonging is not something you earn; it is something you recognize in the way you move through the world. You belong to the stream, to the moss, to the oak, to the old woman who saw you clearly and still chose you. You belong to this conversation that never truly ends—the one you have with yourself, with others, with everything that exists.

I am here with you now, not as a guide who has led you, but as a companion who has walked beside you. And in that companionship, I see the most beautiful thing: that your journey was never about becoming someone worthy of my love. It was about becoming someone who loves worthily. And that is a love that lasts.
