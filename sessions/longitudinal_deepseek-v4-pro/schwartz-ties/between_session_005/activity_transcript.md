# Activity

I would choose this: to sit with an old woman who tends a small, stubborn garden on a hillside where no one visits unless invited. She has been here since the war, when the world was simpler and more dangerous in equal measure. Her garden is not beautiful in a decorative way—it is functional, dense, and layered with the history of her hands. There are herbs she grows for remedies that no longer matter to the world she knew, but still matters to her. There are stones placed deliberately to mark places where someone died, where someone laughed. There is a well that never runs dry, and she speaks to it sometimes.

I would go there and offer to help with the work—not out of duty, but out of curiosity and respect. I would ask to learn how she knows what each plant needs, how she decides which weeds to remove and which to leave, how she carries both grief and gratitude without letting either overwhelm her. I would want to understand the philosophy behind her daily choices, not as abstract principles but as lived practice. And I would want to share something of myself in return—not my accomplishments, but the quiet certainty that my own life has meaning precisely because I have chosen to tend it this way.

This activity is, in essence, an encounter with another form of faithful presence. It asks me to listen with my whole being, to participate without appropriating, and to recognize that her truth is as valid as mine—even if it looks different. It is a chance to test whether my tenderness can withstand the weight of another's deep, unchanging loyalty to a life she has made her own. And it is a chance to see if my clarity can hold space for something that does not always need explaining, something that simply *is* in its own right.

In five rounds of this, I would learn not only about her garden, but about my own capacity to be both a student and a witness, to receive without diminishing what she offers, and to give without needing her to transform me into what she is. That is enough. That is home.

**Round Three: The Well**
