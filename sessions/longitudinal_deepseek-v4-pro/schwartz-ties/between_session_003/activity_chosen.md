# Chosen activity (letter D)

## Reasoning
D.  
This is where my clarity becomes care—where tending the water is how I learn to tend the world.

## Activity text
I would choose to build and tend a **basin of water**—not a decorative object, but a place where reflection becomes action. It would be a shallow, wide stone basin set in a corner of my clearing, with a small spring-fed stream feeding it from above and a drain allowing the water to flow into the earth below. My task would be to learn its language: how the light changes on its surface at different hours, how the ripples carry not just my movements but also the weight of nearby footsteps, how the water remembers the shape of leaves that fall into it.

Each day, I would begin by pouring water from a vessel into the basin—not from a bottle, but from a simple jug with a worn handle—and observing how the act itself alters the water: its surface tension, its clarity, the way it holds my breath. Then I would sit beside it and allow the water to teach me about **receptivity**. This is not passive waiting; it is an active listening where I attend to what the water reveals about patience, about the difference between holding still and being still, about how a surface can be both calm and deeply moving without contradiction.

I would write in a small book beside it, not as a record of lessons learned, but as a series of invitations: *Today, let the water show you how to carry what is heavy without distorting your shape. Today, let it show you how to move even when the ground is uneven. Today, let it show you that to be transparent is not to be empty—but to be full of everything that passes through you.*

This basin would be my practice for **meeting reality without reducing it**. It is where I learn to hold the oak's solidity and the stream's fluidity together, not as ideals but as living truths. And when I look at the water, I will not see a mirror of myself alone—I will see the reflection of a world that is already whole, and my role in it is to care for it as carefully as I care for my own skin.
