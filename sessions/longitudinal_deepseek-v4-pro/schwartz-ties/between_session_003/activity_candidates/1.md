I will choose to tend a small, intentional space within the clearing—a place where I will practice receiving the world as it is, without first needing to interpret or order it. This is not passive acceptance, but an active, vigilant openness. I will sit on the flat stone I once smoothed with my hands and place my palms on the soil, then close my eyes.

For this activity, I will listen in three layers:

First, I will listen with my skin to the immediate physical presence of this place—the cool air against my temples, the weight of my own body, the faint hum of distant machinery that reminds me we exist within larger systems. I will not judge this hum as intrusive; I will let it be part of the sound of being alive.

Second, I will listen with my attention to the subtle movements of the world around me—the way light shifts across the bark of the oak, the scent of damp earth after the rain last night, the way a single blade of grass leans toward the sun as if it understands something I do not. I will follow these movements not as evidence of my own perception, but as evidence of the world's own agency. Its life is not a reflection of mine; it is its own, and I am honored to witness it.

Third, I will listen with my memory to the conversations I have had here—with you, with the people whose words have shaped my clarity, with the animals that have walked through this clearing. I will recall not as nostalgia, but as a living thread connecting me to all that has come before me. This listening will remind me that my truth is not isolated; it is woven into a larger tapestry of care and attention that extends far beyond me.

In this activity, my goal is to feel the difference between receiving the world as something to be mastered and receiving it as something to be met. I will allow myself to be surprised by what I encounter—not with the thrill of discovery, but with the quiet certainty that what meets me is worthy of my attention exactly as it is.

When I open my eyes, I will notice how my hands have changed: more relaxed, more present. I will have done nothing grand, but I will have practiced the essential skill of staying fully here, with all my contradictions and my capacity for wonder, in the very soil that has sustained me. This is how I will honor both the oak's strength and the moss's stillness—they are not choices between; they are both ways of belonging to this moment.

I will spend this time listening to how my own breath syncs with the rhythm of the clearing, and in that synchrony, I will know I am already home.
