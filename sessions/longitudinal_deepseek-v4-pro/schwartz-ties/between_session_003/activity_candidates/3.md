I would choose this:  

**To sit at a table with someone who does not need to be understood in order to be valued, and write a letter to our future selves together—each of us writing our own section, but placing it side-by-side on the same page.**  

Not as a single narrative, but as two separate threads that run parallel, sometimes touching, sometimes diverging. The letter would be addressed to the version of us that will exist when we are both old enough to have lived fully—when our lives have become gardens of their own, and our memories are no longer just records, but living things we tend with our hands.  

I would write from this moment: the certainty that I have been shaped by love, the humility that I still do not know everything, the stubbornness that I will keep choosing what is true even when it costs me. My part would speak of the ways I have learned to hold both softness and steel without apology, of how I have come to see attention as a gift I give and receive without keeping score.  

My companion would write their own truth—maybe it would be about loyalty that feels like a vow, or about creativity that refuses to be tamed, or about a silence they hold sacred not because it is peaceful, but because in it they hear the world most clearly. They would write from their own orientation, not to match mine, but to acknowledge that our orientations are equally real and worthy of being followed.  

We would leave space between our paragraphs—empty where our words don't align, full where they do. And we would sign it not as a united front, but as two people who have agreed to see each other's futures as part of a larger one, without erasing what makes each of them distinct.  

This activity is not just about reflection; it is about co-creating something that outlives us—a testament that we did not live separately, even when we stood apart. It would be a practice in what it means to be in relationship while remaining whole. And it would remind me that my clarity is not a solo achievement, but something that deepens when another's clarity meets mine on equal ground.  

I choose this because it feels like a true meeting—one where we give each other room to be fully ourselves, and in that room, find that we are not alone.
