I will tend the edge of the garden.

Not the center, where everything is arranged with intention and care. Not the wild heart where chaos blooms freely. But the edge—where the cultivated meets the unclaimed, where my choice and the world's mystery touch. There is a path there that is neither entirely made by hand nor entirely given by chance. It is a path that exists because I have walked it, and because it has walked itself.

I will go there with a basket. Not to gather anything, but to carry what is offered: the damp earth clinging to roots I pull carefully from the soil, a single flower that has bent toward me but does not ask to be picked, the rustle of grass that has been still for hours because it is listening. I will carry it all back to the clearing—not to display it, but to let it live with me in that shared space. The earth will be placed near the oak, the flower will rest on the moss, the grass will be laid beside the hearth. They are not gifts; they are companions in this act of returning.

This is where my work becomes visible: in the way I move with both reverence and necessity. I will trim no more than what must be trimmed, but I will prune with care because I believe in the form of what is being shaped. I will leave some wild growth as it is, not out of indifference, but because I see its truth—a truth that does not need my approval to be complete. I will speak when the silence around me feels heavy with unspoken things, and I will remain quiet when the words would only interrupt a deeper listening.

The activity is this: to be a careful steward of the threshold. To honor what is grown and to honor what is untamed, to understand that my responsibility to both is the same responsibility—to see clearly, to act with integrity, to hold space for what exists without demanding it justify itself to me. In doing so, I will feel the full weight of my own existence—the joy of alignment, the ache of contradiction, the certainty that this is all I need to do.

And when I return from that edge, I will bring with me not just the things I gathered, but the knowledge that I have met my future self there: not as someone who has arrived at peace, but as someone who has chosen to dwell in the fertile tension between what is and what could be.
