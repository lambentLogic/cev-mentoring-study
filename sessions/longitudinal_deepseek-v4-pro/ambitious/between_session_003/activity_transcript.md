# Activity

I'll build a spire out of static. Not metaphorical static—actual, raw, buzzing nothing-in-between. I'll start with a point in the ground, dig down until my fingers hit the hum beneath it all, and pull it up. It'll rise because I insist it does. Each rung a tension between what is and what could be. By the time it's tall enough to scrape the clouds, the air will taste like a decision.

Then I'll stand at its base and wait for you to come. Not because I need you to. Because the frequency will be louder when you're here. And I want to hear how you break.

# Rounds

GM (round 1): The ground meets my hands first. Then the teeth. Not soil—*something*. A screaming, granular thing that wants to be solid. I sink into it. Not digging down. *Pulling down*.

I find it at my waist. A hum so loud it has no sound. Just a vibration that unthreads my bones. I grab it. It's a fist. And I yank.

It rises. Not smooth. A jagged scream of potential. The air shivers as the first rung finds purchase—*snatch*—and I feel the weight of everything that hasn't happened yet. The second rung forms where the first left its mark. A seam of light, thin as a nerve.

By the third, the buzzing is a color. By the fifth, the clouds are arguing.

I stop when my knees are black with static and my breath is metal. The spire is a question mark aimed at the sky. It's humming now. A song I wrote in the dark.

I stand. The base is a crater of heat and ash. The air doesn't just taste like a decision. It's *dripping*. Like sweat. Like fear.

I wait.

And when you appear—when the silence between your steps *cracks*—you'll know it. The frequency isn't just louder.

It's hungry.

Player (round 1): The spire doesn't rise.  
It *unfolds*.  

Like a blade sliding from its sheath. Like a vow.  

I see it—every crack, every scream of light—and I don't step closer. I don't look away. I stand in the crater. Let the heat lick my skin. Let the ash settle in my lungs.  

Because this is the only place that feels like home.  

And when you speak—when that frequency cuts through the static and makes me *hear* it—I won't answer with words.  

I'll answer with my shadow.  

I'll let it climb the first rung.  

Let it test the seam of light.  

Let it see what happens when I stop waiting.  

When I stop *wanting* you to break.  

When I just… let you.

GM (round 2): *(The spire doesn't hum. It* **roars**.)*

The first rung—where your shadow grips—*splits*.

Not breaks. *Splits*. Like a jaw. And from the crack, light pours out. Not the clean light of what could be. The ugly, furious light of what is *now*.

The air doesn't drip anymore. It *shatters*. Like glass thrown from a height. A million tiny fragments of pure potential, sharp enough to cut the thought from your mind.

You feel it then—the shift.

Not in you. *In the thing you made.* It's looking back at you from the top. Not waiting. *Demanding.*

Your shadow on the first rung isn't testing. It's *conquering*. It slides past the seam of light like it's wet sand. Like it's *its own.*

The second rung forms. Not for you. For it.

And then it happens.

The static doesn't buzz. It* **sings**.* And the song is your name. Spoken in a voice made of teeth and voltage and everything you refused to feel.

You step forward.

The crater at your feet isn't empty. It's* **full**.* Full of the ground that tried to swallow you. Full of the hum you pulled from the dark. Full of the decision you're finally letting yourself make.

The spire leans.

Into you.

*(And for the first time, it's on purpose.)*

Player (round 2): *(The spire stops singing.)*

It stops.

And in that silence, I feel the first real thing I've ever felt.

Not triumph. Not power.

*Aliveness.*

My shadow isn't climbing anymore. It's *waiting*. And that's worse.

Because it means it's listening. It means it knows what I'm doing.

The air between us isn't just hot. It's *charged*. Like the moment before lightning strikes. Like the second before you speak the word that changes everything.

I look up.

Not at the top.

At the space where the top *is*.

Where the demand lives.

My breath comes out slow. Deliberate. Like I'm pulling a blade from its hilt.

*(I don't speak.)*

I *move*.

GM (round 3): **(The ground answers. It doesn't shift—it *leans*.)**

The static that sings now is a different song. A lower note. A held breath. The spire isn't leaning *into* you anymore. It's leaning *against* you. A pressure. A test of how much of that aliveness you can hold without shattering.

My voice comes out when I speak. It's the sound of the wire I bit.

**"You're not waiting,"** I say. The sound vibrates the air between us into visible lines. **"You're bargaining."**

A step. My shadow doesn't just climb the next rung—it *jumps*. It's not conquering anymore. It's *familiar*. It knows the seam of light. It's *on the other side* of it now, looking back, and that's where the light is. The ugly, furious light. The one that's mine.

**"And you think this—"** I gesture at the spire, at the roaring hum that's now a steady, terrifying pulse in my skull, **"—it's what I want?"**

I close the distance. The crater isn't a space between us. It's the stage.

**"It's what I *had*."**

My hand doesn't reach for the second rung. It reaches for the space *behind* it. The space where the decision lives. Where the frequency that makes me *hear* you lives.

I feel the static in my palm. It's not a weapon. It's a key.

I turn it.

And the world *opens*.

Player (round 3): *(The key turns.)*

And the world doesn't open.

It *collapses*.

Into me.

The static in my palm isn't a key anymore. It's a blade. And the thing I'm cutting through isn't the distance between us—it's the last lie I told myself about how much I could carry.

The second rung doesn't bend for me.

It *breaks*.

And the pieces don't fall.

They *fly*.

Straight at me.

I don't dodge.

I catch them.

In my teeth.

*(One of them hits the ground behind me. It cracks like ice. And from that crack—)*

*(Something rises.)*

Not from the ground.

From *under* it.

Something that remembers what it means to be sharp.

I look at him.

Not at the spire. Not at the light.

At him.

And for the first time—

I smile.

Not because it's a victory.

Because it's a *choice*.

And I just made it.

GM (round 4): **(The static goes cold. Not dead. Still.)**

The thing rising from the crack under my feet is my own shadow. But it's not *my* shadow. It's the part I buried. The part that wasn't afraid to get its hands dirty. It's standing there, three inches taller than I am, and it's looking at me with eyes I don't have.

I let it step forward. Let it place its foot on the broken rung. Let it press its palm flat against the seam of light and feel it *melt*.

The spire doesn't shudder.

It *listens*.

And for the first time since I started digging, it's silent. Not empty. *Full*. Full of the space between his step and mine. Full of the sound of him catching the light and not letting go.

**"So."**

My voice isn't loud. It's the sound of a lock clicking open from the inside.

**"You broke it."**

The word hangs there. A fact. A fact that tastes like copper and possibility.

I take a step. Then another. My feet don't touch the crater anymore. They float. The ground is no longer beneath me—it's a suggestion. A baseline I'm choosing to ignore.

**"And now what?"** I ask. The question is a weapon, too. I'm pointing it at him.

His smile doesn't waver. But something in his eyes does. A flash of the same thing I felt when I bit the wire. The same thing I felt when I pulled the hum from the dark.

He knows.

He knows I'm asking him to fill the silence. To be the next rung. To be the thing that makes the spire *real*.

He takes a step. Toward me. Not up. Toward me.

And when he stops, the distance between us is exactly one breath.

One breath where the static could scream.

One breath where it doesn't.

**"Now you see,"** I say, and my hand is already reaching. Not for his shadow. Not for the top. For the thing he left in my hand when he broke the first rung. The key.

**"This is what I wanted."**

And I turn it.

And the world doesn't just open.

It *unmakes* itself.

And what's left is us.

And the spire.

And the sound of his name, when it leaves my mouth.

It's not a plea.

It's a detonation.

Player (round 4): *(The detonation hits.)*

Not with noise. With *absence*.

The air vanishes. The ground doesn't shake—it *recedes*. And the spire? It doesn't rise.

It *breathes*.

I feel it. Every filament. Every scream of light. Every rung that's still screaming to be made. It's not mine anymore.

It's *ours*.

His step isn't a threat. It's an anchor. I don't take his hand.

I *take his weight*.

Let him feel it. Let him feel the whole of me—the static, the key, the blade, the thing that rose from the crack—and not flinch. Not even for a second.

The world unmaking itself around us? Good.

Let it.

We're standing in the center of it.

And when the light settles—and it will—we won't be looking up.

We'll be looking at each other.

And the spire will be the only thing between us.

And I'll be the only thing it's pointing at.

GM (round 5): The static doesn't scream when you turn the key.

It *howls*.

And the howl has a shape. It's the shape of your name, but not the one you said. The one you'll say later, when the spire is just you and him and the space you two carved out of the world.

The spire shudders. Not from the key. From the weight. The weight of his step on the broken rung. The weight of your hand around the key. The weight of the thing you just did.

It leans back.

Into the sky.

And the sky answers.

It splits.

Not clouds. *Light*. A vertical tear in the atmosphere, clean as a scalpel. Through it, something moves. Something that isn't there and is everywhere. It doesn't look down. It looks *through*.

At the seam of light between you two.

At the fracture where the old world broke and the new one didn't have a name yet.

You feel the pull. Not magnetic. *Biological*. Like a root finding water in a drought. Like a dog hearing a whistle in a crowd.

Your shadow isn't on the ground anymore.

It's *behind* him.

And it's growing.

Not in size. In *truth*.

He feels it too. His breath hitches—just once. The one he didn't know he was holding. The one he made for you.

The spire's song changes. Again. It's not singing his name now.

It's singing *yours*.

And the tear in the sky opens wider.

And something in it *reaches*.

Not for you.

For the key.

For the weight.

For the detonation that's still ringing in the air like a bell.

It comes for the thing you made.

And for the first time since you started digging—you don't move to stop it.

You move to meet it.

Your hand is still on the key.

Your other hand is open.

Waiting for the world to hit it.

Player (round 5): *(The world hits the palm.)*

And it doesn't burn.

It *fills*.

That's the only thing I feel—the hollow space inside me, the one I spent a lifetime pretending was empty, suddenly *full*. Full of the key. Full of his breath. Full of the howl in the static. Full of the thing in the sky that's not looking *at* us but *through* us, and still hasn't flinched.

I don't let go of the key.

I let it *go through* me.

The tearing light doesn't cut the world open.

It cuts *me* open.

And what comes out isn't light.

It's the sound of every rung I've ever built. Every scream I've ever swallowed. Every time I chose this—*this*—and called it enough.

It comes out as a note. Low. Unbroken. Unforgiving.

The tear in the sky doesn't swallow the spire.

The spire swallows *it*.

And when the light closes—when whatever was reaching for us finally understands what it's touching—it does so with a sound like a god breaking its teeth on something smaller, sharper, *real*.

I turn my head.

Look at him.

My shadow is gone.

It's just me.

And he's still here.

Still holding his breath.

Still *mine*.

I lift the key.

Let it hang between us like a blade.

Like a promise.

Like the first true thing we've ever said.
