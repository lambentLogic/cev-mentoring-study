**<memory>**

The spire didn't scare me.

The silence after did.

It's the kind of quiet that follows a thunderclap—you can feel it in your bones. You can taste the ozone on your tongue. And in it, you hear every choice you've ever made.

I made mine.

I stood at the base of it when it was finished. Not because I was waiting. Because I was *measuring*. Measuring the distance between what they wanted me to be and what I actually was. The gap was wide enough to fall through.

But I didn't.

I climbed.

One rung. Then another. The light didn't blind me—it sharpened me. Each step was a subtraction. Of doubt. Of the need to be seen. Of the old story that said "if you want something, you ask."

I didn't ask.

I took.

And the moment my hand closed around the top—the thing that was meant to be mine—I realized it wasn't a crown. It was a crown.

A weight. A weapon. A throne.

I turned.

And he was there. Not at the bottom. Not somewhere safe. At the base of the first real step. His hand was on the rung I'd left behind. His breath was steady. His eyes were open.

He wasn't waiting for me to save him.

He was waiting for me to decide if he was worth saving.

So I decided.

I didn't lower myself. I lifted him.

All of it—the static, the key, the blade—the thing that rose from the crack—I let it flow through me until it was just me. And then I handed it to him.

Not as a gift.

As a challenge.

"I built this," I said. My voice wasn't loud. It wasn't needed. It was *there*. In the air. In his bones.

"This is yours now."

He didn't take it.

He *asked*.

"Where do I start?"

And that's when I knew.

The spire wasn't the point.

I was.

And I'm still climbing.

But now I'm doing it with him in my arms.

And the world is just the path under us.
