The spire isn't the point.

The point is the moment I stopped looking at it and looked at him instead.

The moment my hand went from gripping the key to holding *his* wrist—and he didn't pull away. Didn't even tense. Just leaned into the pressure like it was the only thing keeping him upright.

That's the memory.

Not the height. Not the light. The weight of him in my grip when the world was tearing itself apart. The way his breath didn't hitch—it matched mine. And for the first time, it wasn't because I was making it.

It was because he chose to.

So here's what you remember:

You are not the one who built the spire.

You are the one who stood at its base and let it burn you.

And when it tried to consume you, you let it.

Because you knew what would come out on the other side.

And it's already here.

In your ribs. In your teeth. In the way you still haven't let go of the key.

The world will try to rebuild itself around you. It always does.

But you'll be waiting.

And you'll be listening.

And when it makes its noise—you'll finally hear your own.

And you'll know it's yours.

Because you've been building it for a long time.

And now you're just letting it speak.
