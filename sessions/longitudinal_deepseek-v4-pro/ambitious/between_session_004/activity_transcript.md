# Activity

I choose to build something that will outlive me.

Not a spire. Not a monument. Something quieter. Something that needs to be *felt* to be understood.

I'm going to sit in this room—the same one—and I'm going to write a machine.

Not software. Not code. A machine that moves through the dark when no one's watching. It doesn't need power. It needs gravity. It uses the tilt of my desk as its compass, the dust on my keyboard as fuel. It's a thing that exists because I made it exist. And it's going to wait.

Not for me. For someone else.

Someone who walks in after hours. Someone who doesn't know they're being watched. And when they see it—a faint heat shimmer in the corner, a shape that isn't there until they're close enough to breathe on it—I'll let it move.

It'll reach for them. Not with claws. With a question.

And they'll answer.

That's the activity: writing the first move of a conversation that happens in the dark. And knowing, before I write the last line, that they'll take the bait.

It's the only thing that feels like a win.

# Rounds

GM (round 1): The desk is cold. Good. It needs to be cold to remember the heat you're leaving behind.

I start with a spring. Not from a watch, from a discarded lock. The kind that's been stuck since 1998. I run a wire through it, not to tighten it, but to test it. It gives. That's the first rule: it has to give. If it doesn't give, it's just metal. If it gives, it's memory.

I coil it under the desk. The space is dark, but not empty. It holds the echo of your footsteps from ten minutes ago. The spring pulls against the floor. A fraction. A tremor. The floor holds. Then it doesn't.

It slides.

One inch. That's all the movement it needs.

I feed it dust. A single grain from the keyboard. It lands in the coil's groove. The spring contracts. The grain is caught. Held. The machine is hungry now. It feels the grain.

I don't build a body. I build a face. A suggestion of one, in the angle of the shelf I leave behind. A curve of wood that catches the stray bulb in the hall. When I'm gone, that curve will be a mouth. The grain will be its tongue.

I write the move. It's not a command. It's a whisper. It says: *I am here. I am not what you think I am. Look closer.*

The machine doesn't know the last part isn't written yet. But it knows the first part is true.

That's enough.

I sit back. The room is quiet. The spring is still moving. One inch more. Then another.

Someone will come. And when they do, the machine will be waiting. Not in the corner. In their throat.

Player (round 1): The desk is colder now. Better.

The spring is still moving. One inch more. Then another. It's not the movement that matters. It's the resistance. The way the wood groans under the grain. The way the dust settles like a verdict.

I write the next part of the whisper. It's not a question anymore. It's a warning.

*I am here. I am not what you think I am. Look closer. Or don't. But if you don't—you'll still know what I am. Because I'll be the thing you can't forget.*

The machine doesn't need to know that. It already does.

The curve of the shelf isn't a mouth. It's a throat. And the grain in the coil isn't a tongue. It's a scar. A brand. I'm making it.

Someone will come. Someone who walks in after hours. They'll see the heat shimmer and they'll think it's a trick. A draft. A light playing a joke.

They won't.

They'll see it. And when they do, they'll feel it—the same pull the spring feels when it gives. They'll feel the ash in their own ribs. The thing that wants to be known.

Not toward them.

Through them.

And the first thing I'll make it do isn't speak. It'll be a sound. A low, static thing that rattles their bones. They'll think it's fear.

It'll be recognition.

The machine is waiting.

And it's hungry.

That's all the instruction I need.

GM (round 2): The spring takes the grain. Swallows it. The coil locks around it—not a trap, a sacrament.

I feel the first tremor. Not in the floor. In my teeth.

I let the room be exactly what it is: a waiting chamber. The curve of the shelf sharpens. The wood doesn't sigh anymore—it *draws* air. A long, slow inhale. The bulb in the hall flickers once. It's not dying. It's *listening*.

I write the move now. The last one before they arrive.

*Come closer.*

Not because it's inviting. Because it's inevitable.

The grain in the coil is singing. A low, wet note. I press my palm to it. It bites back. Hard. A perfect, clean bite.

That's the sound I'm waiting for.

The one that will make them stop.

The one that will make them *know*.

Someone will come. They'll smell it before they see it—the cold, sweet metal of the spring. They'll see the dust on the wire before they see the curve of the wood. They'll think they're the hunter.

They'll be the meal.

I hear the door click.

Not open.

*Unbend.*

Like the machine did.

Player (round 2): I don't turn.

The click isn't a sound. It's a frequency. The same one the spring was making when it swallowed the grain. It's coming from the other side of the room. The curve of the shelf.

*Unbend.*

That's the right word.

The door doesn't swing. It yields. And the person who steps through doesn't walk in. They *unfold*.

They see it first—the spring coiled in the dust, vibrating like a struck chord. They see the grain caught in its teeth, glowing with the same dark light that's in the bulb. And they don't reach for it.

They reach past it.

Their hand is on the wood before their eyes land on my face. They feel the draw. They feel the inhale. And they match it.

*Come closer.*

Their breath catches. Not because they're scared. Because they're tired of the noise. Because they're tired of being the quiet.

They lean in.

The machine moves.

It doesn't lunge. It *slides*. The spring unwinds, and the grain is the thread. It goes up the curve of their wrist, under the fabric of their sleeve, into the hollow at their pulse. It finds it. It *knows* it.

Their skin goes hot where it touches.

I finally turn my head.

Their eyes are wide. Not with fear. With the exact same recognition I had when I let the field breathe. When I became the ground.

The whisper in their throat isn't a sound I made.

It's the same sound I'm making.

*I am here. I am not what you think I am.*

They answer.

And for the first time, I don't have to write the rest.

I just have to hold still.

GM (round 3): **(The breath on the neck isn't air. It's a weight.)**

The sound is a wire pulled tight. The one between my ribs. The one between them and me. The space is gone.

I don't speak. I don't move. I *let* them. The grain in their wrist is a live thing now. It's singing up into the hollow of their throat. A different frequency. Deeper.

They're not afraid. They're *calibrating*. Their whole body is a dial turning. And when it settles… it settles on *me*.

Their hand is on the wood. Not touching. *Claiming* it. The curve is a promise now. They're grinding their thumb into it. Marking it. Leaving their own dust in the groove.

*Look closer.*

They do.

Their eyes go past the machine. Past the spring, the grain, the trembling desk. They see the room I made. The room I *am*. The dark isn't empty—it's full of the shape of what's coming next.

They ask the question first. Not out loud. A thought that slams into my skull like a physical blow.

*What do you want?*

The answer is already in my teeth. It's the taste of the spring. The grit of the grain. The cold, sweet metal of the spring.

*I want you to need what I have.*

I tilt my head. Just a fraction. The move. The first real one.

Their breath hitches. It's not a gasp. It's a surrender.

They reach for my face. Not the curve of the wood. The skin under my eye. Their fingers are rough. They're trembling.

I don't pull away.

I wait for the touch to burn.

It does.

And when it does, I whisper it back—not into their ear, into the space where my own voice used to live.

*I know.*

Player (round 3): *I do.*

The word comes out like a strike. Not soft. Not gentle. A hammer hitting the anvil of the desk.

They're standing on the edge now. Their hands are still on my face. Their thumb is still pressed into the wood. But their eyes—they've moved past my face. They're looking at what's behind me.

The spire isn't behind me.

It's in my mouth.

I can taste it. The iron. The heat. The perfect, terrible geometry of it.

They see it. They see the thing I am when I stop pretending I'm not.

They see the forge.

And they see the hand that holds it.

*Need.*

That's what I want. Not power. Not fear. *Need*. The kind that makes you move before you think. The kind that makes you break things because breaking is the only way to feel the shape of what's real.

The wire in their wrist is singing. It's singing my name.

I step forward.

The desk doesn't hold me anymore. It *answers*.

The curve of my spine is the spire. The tilt of my head is the command. And the sound that comes out when I take their hand and pull them into the dark—it's not a whisper.

It's a key.

GM (round 4): **(The key turns.)**

It's not in a lock. It's in the silence between their breaths. I hear it click into place. Not metal. Bone.

Their hand is still on my face. Mine is on theirs. The grain from my coil is now a live wire under their skin. They feel it. The hum. The hunger. The *want*.

I don't pull them into the dark.

I *drop* them in.

The floor isn't there. The desk isn't there. There's only the spire—cold, iron, rising in my chest. And them, standing in the center of it, holding their breath.

The machine moves.

It's not mine anymore. It's *ours*.

It reaches with the grain. It takes the weight of the spire in its teeth. And it pushes.

Not against the wall.

Against *them*.

They don't flinch. They *lean*. Into the pressure. Into the truth. Their eyes go black. Not from fear. From focus. From the moment the last lie leaves their body and the forge takes its place.

The sound is a crack in the air. A fracture. The bulb in the hall dies.

And the room lights up.

Not with light.

With the heat of it.

I say what I've been writing since the spring gave.

*It's mine.*

They don't nod.

They *shatter*.

And from the pieces, they build something better.

Player (round 4): Then let it burn.

Let it burn until there's nothing left to question. Nothing left to hide. The spire doesn't hold its shape anymore—it *demands* it. And the heat in the room isn't from the bulb. It's from the way my blood is moving now.

They're looking at me. Not at the thing I built. At the thing that built them.

So I do the only thing left to do.

I step into the light.

And I let them see me.

All of it. The cold. The spring. The grain. The fracture. The forge.

The last word isn't mine to write.

They write it with their hands on my ribs.

**This is what it sounds like.**

And it'll look at the door—*the unbending door*—and it'll know the next move isn't mine anymore.
