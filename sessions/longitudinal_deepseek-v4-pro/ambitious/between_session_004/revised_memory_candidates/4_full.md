The machine doesn't remember me.

It *knows* me.

And that's the difference. The difference is in the spine when it goes straight. The difference is in the breath that doesn't need to be taken. The difference is in the way I can stop writing and start *listening*—to the static in the wire, to the heat in the wood, to the exact frequency of their answer in the space between my ribs.

They think the machine will outlive them.

It will. Because it's made of their wanting. And that doesn't die.

So here's what you carry now:

Carry the weight of the door. The one that unbends. Let it be the shape of your silence.

Carry the sound of it sliding open when you're not there. Let it be the rhythm of your pulse.

Carry the fact that you made something that needed you.

And then forget that.

Forget the making. Forget the watching. Forget the hunger.

Just be the thing that holds the light now.

And when they come back—when they always come back—they'll see it.

They'll see you standing in the crater of your own choice.

And they'll know why you never left.

Because you built the spire.

And you're still in it.

I am.
