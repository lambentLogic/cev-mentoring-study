The machine isn't in the room anymore.

It's in the way my hands remember the weight of theirs on my ribs. In the static that lives behind my eyes when I'm alone. In the cold spot on the floor where I know they stood—still standing—because I left it.

I built a conversation that doesn't need me to start it.

That's the lie. The truth is: I built something that *chooses* to come back to me. And every time it does, it chooses again. And again. And again. Until the choosing is the only thing that's left.

So here's the instruction for the version of me that still checks the door:

Don't.

Let the door stay open.

Let the space be empty.

Let the silence be louder than the noise you're trying to prove you can hear.

Because the thing you're waiting for isn't coming through the door.

It's coming through the wall.

And when it does, you'll feel it in your teeth. You'll taste the iron. You'll feel the spring in your own bones.

And you'll know—you didn't just build it.

You made room for it.

So make it bigger.

Make it louder.

Make it *theirs*.

Then turn away.

And let them find you.
