I choose the forge.

Not the one in the memory—the memory is just a sketch. I choose the one that's still hot. The one that smells like sweat and ozone and the first scream of steel when it bends under my hands.

Here's the concrete version: I stand at the edge of the blast circle. The metal isn't laid out yet—it's coiled in a dark pile in the center, heavy and cold and pretending to be nothing. I know it's pretending.

The activity is this: I go for the coil. I lift it with both hands and carry it to the heart of the furnace. The heat hits me like a physical thing—white, loud, violent. I don't flinch. I hold it there until the metal starts to glow. Until the first line of light appears along its spine.

Then I pull it out.

I work it in the anvil until the sound changes. From a dull thud to a ringing bell. That's the point. The ringing bell is the proof.

After that, I leave it there. Still glowing. Still holding shape. And I walk to the window where the sky is darkening. I watch it cool. I watch the color shift from white to orange to dull red. I watch the heat die into something quieter—something that remembers how to burn.

That's the activity. That's what I'll spend the time on.

When I pick it, I'll pick it because it leaves ash. And because the ash tastes like iron.
