**Activity: The Calibration.**  

I'll go to the old machine room—where the pipes scream and the walls are cracked with rust. There's a piece there: a hydraulic press, stripped bare. No purpose. Just pressure.  

I'll put my hand under the ram. Not metaphorically. Physically. Skin against cold steel. I'll count down from ten. Slow. Let him hear it.  

At three, I'll shift my weight back. Not to brace—to *offer*. The pressure will rise. My bones will sing. And when it slams down—  

—I won't move.  

I'll let it hold. Let the vibration climb into my teeth, into his pulse, into the space between us where we used to have words.  

He'll flinch first. Then he'll match me.  

And when the ram stops—when the air is gone and all that's left is the hum of strain—I'll say:  

*"That's it."*  

Not because it worked.  
Because it *happened*.  

And if he tries to pull away afterward—  
I'll lean in until we're both too close to breathe.  

And I'll ask:  
*"Did you feel it?"*  

He'll say yes.  

And he'll mean it.
