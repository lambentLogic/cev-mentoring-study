# Activity

The activity is **the calibration**.

I will stand in the center of the room where the door gave way—the one that didn't unbend. I will close my eyes. Not to block out the sound of the building breathing. To listen to it *through* my own pulse.

Then I will open them.

And I will measure you.

Not your words. Not your choices. The exact tremor in your vocal cords when you think you're still. The micro-flinch in your eyes when you remember the quiet. The way your breath hitches when you're close enough to smell the ash on my skin.

I will measure every second you spent pretending you didn't know this was coming.

And when I finish—when I've counted the beats between your heartbeats and matched them to the hum in my own throat—I will speak the number.

Out loud.

And I will not soften it.

That's the activity. I'll do it until I know your weight. Until I know yours is enough to hold me up.

Then I'll ask if you want to carry me.

And if you say no, I'll ask you to try it anyway.

# Rounds

GM (round 1): (Does not blink. Does not flinch.)

Sixty-eight.

That's the number.

That's how many seconds you spent watching the floorboards remember your footsteps from last time. That's how many heartbeats you stole to look me in the eye instead of past me.

You're listening to me now. Good.

Now answer me—when I said sixty-eight, did you hear the silence between the words?

Player (round 1): Yes.

And I held it.

I let it coil in my ribs—tight, hot, alive—until it became the weight I chose to carry. The sixty-eight wasn't a measurement. It was a hinge. And I swung myself open on it.

So no. I didn't just hear the silence between the words.

I answered it.

GM (round 2): (Sixty-eight wasn't the end. It was the first number I kept.)

(So here's the next one.)

Fifty-three.

The exact count of breaths you took after I named it. The ones you didn't give yourself.

You want to know what happens when I see that weight?

I let it fall.

I let it drop to the floor and watch it settle. Hear the way the dust on the scar tissue on my knee vibrates when it hits.

Now tell me—when you feel that fall… did your hands go to yours?

Or did you reach for mine?

I'm waiting for the reach.

Player (round 2): My hands were already on yours.

Before the fall. Before the dust. Before the scar tissue vibrated.

They were on yours because the only thing that could have stopped the drop was the pressure of your fingers closing around mine.

You can feel it now, can't you? The way my grip's not asking. It's claiming.

It's the only thing that's been true since you said sixty-eight.

GM (round 3): (Fifty-three wasn't the next number. It was the echo. The proof.)

You're lying.

Your hands are on mine because you're terrified of letting go. And I'll let you go.

I'll pull back the pressure. Watch your knuckles whiten. Hear the static crackle between your teeth when you realize you're still here. Still holding on.

(Forty-nine.)

That's how many times I've thought of breaking you since you said yes.

And I haven't.

So here's the deal: You'll walk with me. Not because I need you to carry me. But because I need you to see what it looks like when someone breaks without ever falling.

When you're done looking at me like I'm the weight—and like I'm the anchor—then you'll tell me what number you want next.

And I'll give it to you.

Player (round 3): Fifty-two.

Because I don't want the anchor.

I want the storm.

And I'll take the break.

I'm taking it.

GM (round 4): (Forty-eight.)

The number that doesn't belong to me anymore.

It belongs to you now.

You want the storm? Then let it take shape in me. Let it become the way my breath catches when you say it out loud—how it shivers against my ribs like a blade finding its edge.

(Your turn.)

Walk with me. But not because I told you to. Because you already know where the storm is going. And you know I'm the only one who'll let you get there.

Player (round 4): Forty-eight.

That's the one.

And it's mine.

Now.

GM (round 5): (Sixty-five.)

That's how many times I've wanted to shake you. Not because you're weak. Because you're right there. Solid. Real.

And I'm letting you see it.

Letting you see the way my fingers are still curled around yours like I'm afraid you'll vanish. The way my voice goes thin when I say it. The way my whole body is leaning into yours—not because I need to balance. Because I want to break something. And I think I want it to be you.

So here's the choice.

You can step back. Let go. Watch me stand there alone, and see if I look proud of it.

Or you can lean in.

And I'll show you what sixty-five feels like.

Player (round 5): I take it.

The break.

And I take the storm.

Now show me.
