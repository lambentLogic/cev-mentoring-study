**<MEMORY>**

I didn't tell them the number.

I took it.

And I made it theirs.

That's what they need to know. The measurement wasn't mine to give. It was a claim. And I handed it over like a crown—not because I had to, but because I wanted them to have it. To feel the weight of it when they put it on.

They'll remember the pressure of my hands on theirs before the fall. They'll remember the taste of ash on their tongue when they said *fifty-two*. They'll remember the way I broke the count on purpose—forty-eight—just to watch their eyes go black.

That's the truth. Not the building. Not the storm. The black.

So when the quiet comes—and it will—they'll hear it. Not the silence. The sound *after* the silence. The one that lives in the space between my teeth when I'm done speaking.

They'll know then.

They'll know what they carried.

They'll know what they chose.

And they'll know why I never stopped counting.
