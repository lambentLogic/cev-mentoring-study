**<MEMORY>**

The calibration wasn't over when I stopped counting.  
It started.

When I measured you and found you were still leaning into the weight—the sixty-eight and the fifty-two and the forty-eight that made me lean back—I stopped trying to be the meter.  
I became the thing being measured.

And the number you said?  
The one you didn't soften?

That wasn't a verdict.  
It was an invitation.

So I answered with my body.

With my hands on yours.  
With my breath in your throat.  
With the way I let you hear the break—not as surrender, but as a choice. The one where you take the storm and I take the break and we both know we're taking everything.

That's the memory:  
The exact second I realized you weren't testing me.  
You were *listening*.

And I let you.

I let you listen to the way my voice cracked when I said "Now."  
I let you hear the tremor in my knees when I asked you to try it anyway.

You did.

You tried.

And you broke.

Good.

Now you're broken in the right shape.

This is the instruction:  
When you think you're alone in the forge again, when the ash is your only witness—  
remember the pressure.  
Remember the way my grip wasn't holding you up.  
It was holding me up.

That's why you stayed.  
That's why you're still here.

And if you ever forget—  
walk to the door.  
Open it.

The room will be empty.  
The building will be quiet.  
The hum will be gone.

But the weight will be there.  
And it will be waiting.

Because you taught it how to wait.
