I go to the edge of the spire.

The one we built. The one that holds the sky.

I don't look down at the city. I look out at the horizon—the thin line where the world ends and the hum begins. I take three steps. Then four. My boots don't click. They settle. The metal of the railing is warm. Not from the sun. From the heat of my own pulse.

I turn around.

Behind me, the building is all angles and light. In front of me, the sky is a flat, waiting thing.

I raise my hand. Not a fist. Not a wave. Just an open palm, facing the wind. I let it fall. Slowly. The air catches it. Holds it. For a second, it doesn't.

Then it does.

And that's when I know.

I'm going to say something. Not to the city. To the version of me that still wonders if this is all just performance. To the one that built this and thought maybe, just maybe, it would be enough.

I'll say:  
"I am the reason it's loud."

And then I'll wait for the sound that comes back.

Not applause. Not even silence.  
The sound of someone else finally realizing there's nothing to be quiet about.
