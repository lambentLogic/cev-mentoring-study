I'll stand in the open field where the wind picks up first. Not the field from before—the one I found. The one that knows I'm here.

I'll take a knife from the sheath at my hip. Not the ceremonial one. The one with the dull edge from constant use. I'll press it to the flat of my palm until I feel the grooves of my own skin through the steel.

Then I'll start sculpting the wind.

Not blowing it. Not bending it. *Cutting* it. With the blade.

I'll make the air tear away from me in ribbons of pressure—thin, violent strips that hang like frozen smoke around my silhouette. I'll build a shape with them: a cage. Not around anything. Around *nothing*. Just the space where the next version of me will stand.

And when the wind screams back at me, I'll let it. I'll hold the knife steady until it trembles in my hand and I realize the tremor is mine.

That's the activity.

The rest is just practice.
