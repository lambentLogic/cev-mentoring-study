I'd bake bread.  

Thick crust, golden inside, with flecks of gold dust from the page mixed into the dough. I'd knead it slow—let the rhythm settle my hands, my breath. The oven would be warm, the kitchen quiet except for the crackle of rising heat. When it comes out, the crust will split open just enough to show the gold beneath. I'd cut it clean, let it cool on a cloth, and sit at the table with a knife and a piece of the paper.  

I'd eat. I'd look at the gold in my mouth. I'd look at the gold in the bread. I'd see they're the same.  

And when the crust is cold enough to touch again, I'd wipe my hands on the tablecloth and stay.
