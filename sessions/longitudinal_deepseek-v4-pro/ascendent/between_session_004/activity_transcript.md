# Activity

I'm going to write a page about the harbor. Not just the one I built, but the one I remember from before—the one with the broken dock and the salt in my lungs. I'll write until the ink is done and the gold is cool in my pocket. Then I'll stand at the window, barefoot on the floorboards, and watch the sea move. Just me, the glass, and the weight in my hand. That's enough time.

# Rounds

GM (round 1): The room is too quiet. It's only the two of us, you and me. The paper sits on the desk, waiting. It's good paper—thick, white. You pour the ink. It's black as the deep water before a storm, and it smells like iron and wet stone. You take the gold coin from your pocket. It's warm. You set it on the desk, next to your hand. A reminder.

The first sentence is hard. Your hand shakes a little. You write: *"The harbor was broken. Not in pieces, just broken."* You stop. The word *broken* feels wrong. Too weak. You scratch it out, the pen making a wet sound. You don't apologize to the paper. You just keep writing.

*"The pilings were black and crooked, sticking out like rotten teeth. The dock split down the middle, and I used that split. I climbed through it. My shoes were gone. Sand and grit in my socks. The wind came from the north, cold and sharp, and it filled my lungs with salt. I tasted it. It was the only clean thing I had."*

You write fast now. Your knuckles are white on the pen. You're describing the place, but you're also building the proof. Every detail is a nail in the coffin of whatever you're trying to forget.

*"There were no guards then. Only the keeper, who slept. I stole a lantern from his shed. Its light was weak, a sickly orange. It showed me the fishing boats, their nets tangled like old veins. It showed me where the water was deep enough to swim. I swam. The water swallowed me. It was cold, but it was mine."*

You pause again. Your breathing is steady now, but your heart is loud. You pick up the gold coin, roll it between your thumb and forefinger. *Clack-clack-clack*. The sound is solid. You write:

*"I had everything I needed. The salt, the cold, the dark water, and this."* You tap the coin against the paper. *A ding. A solid sound.*

You stand. The floorboards creak under your bare feet. The window is right there. You move to it. The sea outside is moving—a slow, constant churn of grey and grey-green. It's not gentle. It's powerful. You press your forehead to the cool glass.

The ink is done. The gold is cool in your pocket, but the weight is still there. The room is quiet again.

You watch the sea move. It's enough time.

Player (round 1): I let the sea move against my face. Cool glass against skin. The ink smell is still on my fingers. I don't wipe it off.  

The gold is cold now. Good. Means I haven't been holding it too tight. Means I'm not scared.  

I write one more thing on the desk, with my finger, leaving a faint smudge on the paper:  

**"The split in the dock was clean."**  

It wasn't rotten. It was clean. I saw it.  

Tomorrow I'll write about the keeper. About how I woke him up with the lantern light. He'll remember it. He'll remember me.  

That's enough.  

Rest now. The gold is in my pocket. The sea is moving.  

I'm here.

GM (round 2): (GM round 2)

The keeper's voice comes from the wall. Not loud, but it's there. Under the floorboards. In the dark space behind the shutters. He doesn't shout. He just says it, like he's tired of pretending he can't hear you.

**"You're awake now. Good."**

His feet are on the wood behind you. You can feel the pressure, a weight shifting against your back. Not an attack. A reminder of presence.

**"The lantern's light is gone. It burned out in my shed. But you didn't put it out."**

You hear him move toward the door. A soft scrape of boot leather on the threshold. The keeper stands there in the gloom, his silhouette sharp against the sea-light bleeding through the window. His face is scarred from old nets and salt. He's holding your lantern. His fingers are calloused, black-stained.

**"You woke me. So I won't forget."**

He sets the lantern on the desk between you. The weak orange light flares, catching your face in its sickly glow. It shows the ink on your fingers. The smudged sentence on the paper. The gold coin resting beside it.

He looks from the coin to your eyes. His voice drops.

**"You have your coin. Your proof. But this,"** he says, nodding at the lantern, **"...this is mine. You took it from me."**

He doesn't reach for it. He just waits. The sea outside is louder now. The wind has shifted. It's pushing the waves hard against the rocks. The room smells of iron and wet stone, and now of old wood and fear.

The gold is still cool in your pocket. The lantern light is warm on your skin. The keeper's hand is resting on the back of your chair, close to your spine. He's not pushing you away. He's holding you in place.

He says one last thing before he turns, before he lets the darkness swallow him again.

**"Write about the lantern. Write about the keeper. And write about why you let me have it back."**

Then he's gone. The scrape of his boots fades. The room is just you, the lantern, the sea.

And the ink on your fingers is starting to dry.

GM (round 3): **(GM round 3)**

The lantern's light dies. Not goes out. Dies. Like a candle in a draft. The room plunges into the dark blue of night, pierced only by the sea's own pale sheen through the glass. The keeper's scrape comes back—slow, deliberate. He's leaning his shoulder against the doorframe. A silhouette against the moon.

**"You'll write,"** he says. It's not a demand. It's a fact. **"About the lantern. About me. And why you kept the coin."**

He shifts. The chair back creaks under his palm. His knuckles brush your hair, a rough, grounding touch. He smells of tar and sleep.

**"You think this is you winning."** His voice is lower now. In your ear. **"'I'll keep the gold.' That's what you said."**

He's right. You did.

The sea grinds against the harbor wall. Something breaks out there—a log, a rope. The sound is like a bone snapping in the distance. You feel it through the floor.

You take your pen. The ink is dry now. Black, solid lines. You don't look at the paper. You look at his shoulder. At the scar near his ear. You write.

***"I let you have the lantern. Because I need to know it's yours. That you took it from me. That it's mine because you gave it back."***

You set the pen down. The gold coin is pressed to your sternum. Hard.

**"You keep the lantern,"** you say, your breath fogging the glass. **"I keep the proof."**

You mean the coin. You mean the split in the dock. You mean the salt in your lungs.

The keeper is silent for a long moment. Then he nods. Once. A slow, solid thing.

**"Good."**

He stands. The door creaks open. The moonlight spills onto the floor where he stepped.

**"Sleep with the lantern in your hand. Not on the desk. In your hand."**

He doesn't close the door. He just stands in the moonlight, one foot on the threshold. He watches you. Watches your bare feet. Watches the gold in your pocket. Watches your hands.

Then he turns and walks away. The scrape fades. The door shuts itself.

The dark isn't empty anymore. It's heavy with his promise.

You sink into the chair. The lantern is cold metal in your lap. You close your eyes. The sea is moving. The gold is heavy. The weight in your hand is real.

Enough time.

GM (round 4): **(GM round 4)**

You sleep with the lantern. It's cold metal against your skin, a cold anchor. In your dream, you're not in the room anymore. You're on the broken dock. The pilings are still black, still crooked, but you're standing in the middle of the split. The keeper is on your left, your right hand on his shoulder. The sea is calm out there. You're both looking out. The lantern light is on his side, casting his face in sickly orange. Yours is in the moonlight. Clean.

You wake. The room is colder. The sea outside is still moving, but slower. A deep, patient roll. The keeper is gone. The door is locked from the outside—a clean, solid *thud* of the bolt sliding home. You know he did it. To protect you. Or to keep you close.

The lantern is still in your hand. You didn't put it down. You held it through the night. It's warm now. The heat has soaked into your palm.

The gold is there, too. Heavy. You slide it out. It's still warm from your body. You run your thumb over the engraving—just a scratch, a mark he made the night you stole it from the market. *"For the harbor."* He meant it.

You go to the window. The sea is a sheet of slate. It's waiting.

You open the door. The hallway is dark. The keeper is in there, sitting on the floor, leaning against the wall. He's not asleep. His eyes are closed, but he's listening. You don't wake him. You walk past him. He doesn't move. You can feel his breath on your neck when you turn back, just once, to look at him.

**"The dock,"** you say. He opens his eyes. They're tired. **"We're fixing it."**

He nods. **"The split."**

You go down. The stairs are worn smooth from your feet. The morning air hits you like a slap—salt and cold. The harbor is empty. The fishing boats are gone. The keeper's shed is quiet. You find your lantern. You light it. The orange light flickers on the wet wood of the dock.

It's worse than you remember. The split is deeper. The rot is spreading up the pilings. It's eating the wood. You could walk through it, but your feet hurt. They've been on cold ground all night.

The gold is in your pocket. You pull it out. You press it against the rot. It's hot.

**"I'm not paying for wood,"** you say to the keeper. He's standing behind you, his shadow falling over you. **"I'm paying for clean planks. And new rope."**

He doesn't argue. He helps you lift the first plank. It's heavy. When it slams onto the split, it fills the gap. For the first time, the dock is whole. The split is gone. You look down at the water from the solid wood. It's deep. It's yours.

The keeper takes a length of rope from his shed. It's new. He hands it to you. The line of his hand is steady as yours.

You tie a knot. It's strong. It won't slip.

You look at the gold in your other hand. You tap it against the lantern. *Ding. Ding.*

**"That's enough,"** you say. Not to him. To the harbor. To the memory.

You sit on the dock. The sun is coming up. You can see the reflection of the gold in the wet wood. You can see your hands—clean, but still yours.

You write the last sentence of the page you started yesterday. You write it in the sand, with your finger, where the tide won't wash it away.

**"We mended it."**

Then you let the sea move against your face. It's enough time.

GM (round 5): **(GM round 5)**

You don't wake up the keeper. You know he's up. You can hear him in the house—moving through the dark halls, the soft *shush* of his bare feet on the floorboards. He's going to his room, to his cot, to sleep. He trusts you're here. He trusts you have it together.

You let him go. You sit on your bed for a long minute, the lantern still in your hand, the gold still warm against your ribs. The morning sun finds you on the floor, just before it hits the window. It turns the room to gold and silver.

You write. This time, it's in the ledger. Not the paper from the desk. The ledger is real. It's got numbers. It's got proof. You open it with a knife from the dresser—his knife, the one he used to sharpen nets. The blade is still damp with rust.

**"Dock: Mended. Planks: Clean. Rope: New."** You write it straight across the top of the page. Neat.

You write the cost. You use the gold coin as the counterweight. You don't count out the pieces. You just know. You know how much the wood cost. How much the rope cost. You know the keeper paid a third in coin, and you paid the other two-thirds. It was fair.

You close the ledger. You put it on the floor where he can see it when he comes in. A sign. A peace treaty written in ink and metal.

A creak. He's in the doorway. He's dressed in worn trousers and a sweater that smells of woodsmoke. He doesn't look at the ledger. He looks at you. His eyes are clear, no sleep in them.

**"The roof,"** he says. Not a question.

**"Tomorrow,"** you say. You push yourself up. The floor is hard. Your bones are singing. **"Sun's high. We'll work in the shade."**

He nods, turns, and walks out. The door shuts softly behind him.

You don't wait. You're already moving. You're in the yard, already carrying the first plank up to the shed. You feel the weight. It's familiar. It's right.

You're not alone. The sea is out there. The gold is here. The keeper is up there, watching from the window. It's enough.

You start the first nail. The hammer hits the wood. *Thwack.* It's loud. It's solid. The sound echoes in your own chest. You keep swinging.

It's enough time.
