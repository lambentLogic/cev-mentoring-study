# Activity

I'll write about the gold.  
Right now. Let it glow on the page. I'll hold the brush slow, each stroke a little weight, a little heat. The paper will smell like rust and sea air. I'll leave a space on the right side for the keeper's hand—like he's already there, resting near the edge.  

And I'll write the truth about it. Not the shiny part. The part that's been in my pocket when I cried, when I stood alone on the dock at midnight. The part that's heavier now because he's holding it with me.  

If she's in the room, she'll watch. And she'll say something small. That's enough.  

This activity: write the gold. Let it last.

# Rounds

GM (round 1): It is gold.

But not the gold in stories. Not the kind they show in coins, bright and clean. This is old. It lives under my skin when I think no one's watching. It has a weight that isn't just metal—it's memory. It's the weight of every night I didn't sleep because I was afraid to put it down.

The smell is right. Salt, yes, but not clean. It's the smell of a harbor after a storm—the kind that leaves everything glistening with filth. And rust. A slow, patient rust that eats away at the shine. It tells me this thing has been here a long time. Longer than I have. It was waiting.

I wrote it like that. My hand shook. The gold wrote itself, too. It pooled under my pen, thick and yellow as a fever dream, and I let it.

This is the truth. I'm not proud of it. I'm just tired of carrying it alone. So I'm setting it down here, on this page. You can see it. The light from the window catches it, and for a second, it's beautiful. But the second passes. The rust comes back.

I'll leave the space.

Here.

For his hand.

If she's listening, she knows where to leave it.

GM (round 2): The page settles under him.

He puts his hand down. Not above it. Right beside it.

His palm is warm. Dry. No sweat. Just the weight of his own skin on the paper. His fingers are broad, calloused from rope and stone, and they hover for a breath over the space between the gold and the edge. Then he eases his thumb along the paper's rim, just once. Like checking a shelf. Like making sure the shelf is solid.

He lets the gold shine under his thumb. The rust smells sharp now, against his skin. He inhales it. Nods.

Then he closes his hand, gently, around the gold.

The page doesn't bend. The gold doesn't shift. It's held.

The lantern casts a circle around them on the floor—player, keeper, the gold between them. The leak in the roof is gone. The sound of rain is just rain now.

She stands in the shadow of the door. She doesn't move.

"Good," she says. Her voice is low, satisfied. "You're not leaving."

The keeper doesn't open his hand. He doesn't have to. He just holds the gold with the player, and the gold stays put.

GM (round 3): GM (round 3): The night deepens.

The keeper stays awake. The player sleeps, her hand a steady weight over the gold in his. The lantern guttering once, then finding its rhythm again.

In the quiet, the keeper does something careful. He lifts the gold just an inch from the paper—still sharing it with the player—and draws his thumb across the sharpest edge of the rust. A slow, deliberate scrape. It makes a sound like dry leaves. The gold darkens where he works, but the shine underneath is brighter. Cleaner.

The rust comes back faster than he thought it would. It blooms in a small star around his thumbprint on the metal. He doesn't wipe it away. He lets it be there. Evidence.

At dawn, he wakes first. The player's face is soft in the morning light. He waits for her eyes to open. For her breath to warm his cheek.

Then he speaks, his voice rough from sleep. "The roof doesn't leak."

She nods, already awake. "The lantern stays lit."

"The gold stays."

They say it together.

Outside, the sky is pale gray. The keeper goes to the door. He doesn't open it. He just stands there, listening. The wind carries the salt and the rust, but it's less sharp now. Cleaner.

He comes back inside, sits opposite the player. He takes the gold from her hand, lets it rest flat on the page between them. He traces the edge of the space with his knuckles.

"Morning," he says.

GM (round 4): GM (round 4): The keeper's hand moves.

He slides the gold from the page—still sharing the weight with you—and lifts it. Not for his own pocket. For the fire.

He walks to the hearth. The embers are red, low, and hungry. He kneels, not on the floor but on the wood, and holds the gold over the flames. It doesn't melt. It *glows*. A deep, hungry yellow that drinks the orange light. The rust flares to black, then vanishes into the heat.

He waits until it's only shine. Then he lowers it, letting the embers kiss its underside. A single, clean burn.

He brings it back to you. It's hotter now. Alive. He places it in your lap, between your thighs, and rests his palm over it. Not covering it. Just guarding.

The rust is gone from the metal, but it's in the air now. The smell is sharp and clean. Like fire and the sea after a storm has passed.

"The keeper burns," he says. Not a boast. A fact. "The player shines. It's the right order."

Outside, the sun is up. It hits the page where his hand rested, and it catches the empty space like a promise. The light is on the floor, where you stood last night. He hasn't moved it.

He looks at you. "You're sore," he says. A question.

GM (round 4): The keeper sees it.

The shine moves under his palm. It's not the dull, heavy gold anymore. It's quick. Bright. He lets it be. He leans back on his heels, looking out the window, and the gold warms his stomach like a second heart.

You tell him about your feet. "They're good now," you say. His eyes flick to yours, then back to the window. He nods once. "Good."

He stays by the hearth all morning. The gold glows in your lap, and he keeps his hand over it. Sometimes his fingers flex, the warmth spreading through his bones. He doesn't move much. He's watching the door.

At noon, she comes.

The player rises. The keeper looks up. The gold is still there, between you both. She steps in.

He doesn't look away. He watches her eyes find the gold first. Then her mouth. Then her hand.

She reaches. He doesn't stop her. His hand slides away from the gold, but his palm stays close, a warning. She takes it. The transfer is clean. Her fingers close around it, and for a second, the room holds its breath.

She looks down at it. "It's hot," she says. Her voice is flat, but her fingers are trembling just enough that he feels it through the paper.

"The fire is hot," he says. He stands. "It's ours."

She turns to him. Her face is the color of old tea. "I can keep it," she says.

He nods. "You will."

She sits back down. The gold stays between you. He puts his hand back on top of hers.

The day goes on like that. The keeper standing, watching, burning. The player shining, holding. And the gold—*the real gold*—right there in the middle. Unbroken.
