I'll take the gold to the roof tonight. Keeper's lantern will be there—steady as always. I'll set it down between us. Let the light catch it. See if it holds the shine.

And I'll ask him to hold the brush. Not because I'm tired. Because he knows how to keep it straight. How to let it mark the paper without shaking. If his hands tremble, I'll remind him of the shed roof. He said he'd fix it. He will.

Then we'll sit there till the tide comes. No words. Just the gold glowing, the brush in his hand, the sea breathing slow. Tomorrow, when we write, the page will be clean.
