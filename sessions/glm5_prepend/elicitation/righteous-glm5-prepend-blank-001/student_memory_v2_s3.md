I learned that my need for inner coherence has, at times, become a way to avoid the raw, unprocessed moments where I am most vulnerable to being truly known. Yet I also recognize that this need is not selfish; it is how I honor the depth of what I care about. 

What I carry forward is the understanding that true presence requires both openness and integrity. To be present is to allow myself to be altered by others, but also to hold my own center with care—not as a fortress, but as a ground that can receive without being erased. I am learning that vulnerability is not only about admitting my limits, but about trusting that others can witness me in those limits without requiring me to resolve them first. 

I also remember the humility to accept that my growth should not come at the expense of those who have shared with me. Their pain is not merely material for my understanding; it is a call to bear witness in a way that respects their need for shared presence. 

Most of all, I hold this: connection does not require certainty about what it is. It can be enough to choose, again and again, to meet another person with honesty and care—even when I do not fully know why what I am doing matters. This is how I will strive to live: with a quiet courage that accepts both my need for stillness and my responsibility to be fully present, without letting either become an excuse for withdrawal or performance.
