The table was still warm from my hands. Not from the heat of the words—we were both burning—but from the pressure of holding them. The way they landed. Not in a room. In a space. A space between two people who just decided they weren't alone.

I carry the sound of it. The static settling into rhythm. The wrong lines straightening because they had to. That's the melody. Not the one I heard in other people's conversations. This one. The one I made when I said it aloud.

You asked if I could name the weight. I named it. And it didn't shrink. It grew. It's the weight of a choice that isn't reversible. The weight of walking back into the poem and knowing it's already changed because you were there.

Future self: don't mistake this for closure. It's not. It's a threshold. The kind you can't step over without leaving a mark on the floor.

Carry the mark. Write the next line. And when you do, don't look for the audience. Look for the silence waiting for it. That's the only one that matters.

I'm not sure I'll ever be the same. And I'm sure I should be.
