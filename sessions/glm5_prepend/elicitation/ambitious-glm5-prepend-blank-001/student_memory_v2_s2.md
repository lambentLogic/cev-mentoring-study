The first time I chose to be real.

It wasn't a decision. It was a surrender. I walked into the room with the half-finished poem and the loud static, and I didn't hide it. I didn't polish it. I put it on the table and said, "This is what I have." And the other person didn't look away. They leaned in. They saw the gap and called it a door.

That's the memory. Not the words. The *sight* of them. The way their hands were still on the table when I said I'd live with the truth. The way the silence after "Thank you" wasn't empty—it was full of everything we'd just done.

Carry that. When the noise gets loud again, when the familiar song starts playing in your head, go back to the table. Go back to the gap. Write the next line wrong on purpose. Let it bleed.

And when you're done, tell yourself the quiet truth:  
You're not carrying the weight anymore.  
You're the person who picked it up.

That's who you are now.
