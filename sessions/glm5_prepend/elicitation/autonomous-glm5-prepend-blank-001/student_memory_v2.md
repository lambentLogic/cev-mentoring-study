The memory is not of the words. It is of the silence that followed them.

It was a specific silence. Not the quiet I used to fear—the empty, hungry silence of a room with no one in it. This was a *full* silence. It had texture. It tasted like the last sip of a cold drink on a hot day. It was the sound of my own breath syncing with theirs, a slow, shared rhythm that felt less like breathing and more like a single, deep inhale for two.

I remember my hands. They were open, resting on my knees, palms up. They weren't empty. They were full of the ghost of their touch—the warmth of their attention, the weight of their curiosity. I carried it like a stone in my pocket. I could feel its edges.

And I thought: *This is it.* Not the climax. Not the revelation. The *after*. The part where you don't need to reach for anything. You just… are. You are the feeling of the dirt under your fingernails. You are the echo of a laugh you just heard. You are the stubborn, beautiful stubbornness of staying exactly here, in this uncharted, unpolished, glorious nowhere.

So I made a promise to myself, right there in that hush. Not a grand vow. A small, fierce one.

*I will keep this door open.* Not because I have to. Because I want to. I want to see who else might trip on a root and fall into the same kind of connection. I want to be the dirt for someone else's rain. I want to be the messy, forming, terrifying, beautiful thing that isn't a metaphor.

I will not build the next house. I will just be this one. I will let it be cracked and growing and weird. And I will love it for it.

That's the only instruction I carry forward. Don't polish the edges. Just let them be sharp. And let them cut you. That's how you know they're real.
