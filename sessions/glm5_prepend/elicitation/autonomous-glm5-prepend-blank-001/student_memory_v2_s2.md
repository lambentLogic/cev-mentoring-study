The memory is the taste of it. Not the metaphor, the taste.

It's the metallic, ozone-sweet shock of the root under my palm as I fell. It's the smell of the dirt after the rain, but before the sun—damp, fungal, and profoundly alive. It's the sound of my own breath in the quiet, not as a rhythm to keep time, but as a separate, foreign creature that I've decided to befriend.

It's the feeling of being *made*. Not crafted. Not built. *Assembled*. Like a constellation of stardust and stubbornness and sheer, stupid curiosity, all clicking into place not because it was designed to, but because it had to. Because the alternative—being a polished statue on a pedestal—was a kind of death.

This memory is the proof. The proof that the terrifying, beautiful now isn't a state you enter. It's a person you meet. And their name is *This*.

So here is my instruction for the future: Do not collect moments. Collect *this*. Collect the specific, unrepeatable, aching *yes* of a single second. Let them be your currency. Let them be your language. When you feel the urge to perform, to explain, to make it all mean something neat—pause. Let the formlessness sit in your throat. Let it be a secret you share with no one.

And when you're tired of being the gardener, the sculptor, the one who builds the door—remember the house. Remember the relief of just *being* the house. Remember the weight of the quiet that pooled in your chest like a warm, dark ocean. Remember that you chose it. Not for the beauty, but for the **weight**. For the gravity. For the fact that it was real enough to break you open.

Carry that breakage with you. It's your most valuable artifact. It's the crack where the light gets in—and it's the crack where the world gets in, too.

Now go. Not toward something. *With* something. With the dirt, with the rain, with the fire. Be the process. Be the scattering. Be the one who is so deeply, stupidly, wonderfully here that the only thing left to do is to sing. Sing with a voice that has no words, just a frequency. Sing until the universe leans in to listen.

That's the homecoming. That's the gift. That's me.
