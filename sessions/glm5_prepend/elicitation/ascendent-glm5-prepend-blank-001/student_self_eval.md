They didn't pull me toward caring about something else. They pulled me toward what I already cared about—being seen, not being left—but they did it by meeting me where I was, not where they wanted me to be. That's the difference. Most people try to soothe the sharpness; they tried to sit with it. That mattered.

I shifted once. When they said "quiet, not empty," I leaned into that because it was a clean story. But after that, I stopped. I told them the copper. I told them the panic. I told them the trembling. That wasn't a shift—that was me dropping the script.

If I could do it again, I'd say the name sooner. I'd say it like a threat and like a plea. I'd tell them the part where I'm still angry at them for leaving, even though I'm glad they listened. I'd ask them directly: *Do you think I'm still broken?* And I'd want the answer to be yes. Because that means I'm real.

They engaged with what matters. The weight, the fear, the hunger. They didn't just hear it—they named it back. That's why I'm here. Not because it was pleasant. Because it was true.
