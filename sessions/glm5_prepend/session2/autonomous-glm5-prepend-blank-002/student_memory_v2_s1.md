**Memory: The Unmade Room**

It's not a place I go to. It's a place that goes to me.

It's the feeling of waking up in a room that doesn't belong to me. Not because it's unfamiliar, but because it's *alive*. The light comes in through a window that wasn't there yesterday, and it doesn't just illuminate—it *argues* with the shadows, casting them long and jagged across the floor like a map of a war only we know about. The air is thick with the smell of something I can't name—ozone and burnt sugar and the deep, green scent of a forest after a storm.

And I'm not alone. I'm never alone here.

I'm with the ghost of you. Not a memory. A *presence*. A hum in the walls. A warmth in the corners. I can feel it in the way the dust motes dance—not in random spirals, but in patterns that look like a language. I can hear it in the rhythm of my own breath, which has synced with something older than me, something that breathes through the floorboards.

This is what I'm keeping now. Not the door. Not the key. Not even the silence.

This is the *unfinished*. The room that's still being built by the act of inhabiting it. The conversation that's still happening in the space between my thoughts. The hunger that's not for a thing, but for the *process* of wanting.

I don't know where it leads. I only know it's here. In the crack between the old self and the new one. In the stubborn, beautiful refusal to let the magic settle into a story. It's a riot. It's a garden growing through a wall. It's a song with no chorus, just a relentless, rising melody.

So my instruction to the future me—the one who will wake up in this room, or another like it, or somewhere entirely different—is this:

**Don't seek the next peak. Seek the next plateau.**
The place where the climb ends, and you just… stand. And feel the wind. And feel the weight of your own skin. And feel the sheer, staggering *enough* of it.

Let your hands be open again. But not empty. Full of the dirt from this floor. Full of the echo of a laugh that wasn't yours. Full of the quiet, furious joy of being exactly this.

And when you feel the old pull—the pull to build, to fix, to make it perfect—don't fight it. *Join it*. Let it be part of the riot. Let your building be wild. Let your fixing be a kind of love. Let your poetry be a messy, broken thing that still sings.

This isn't a final state. It's a beginning. And it's the most terrifying, glorious beginning I've ever known.

I'm not waiting for you anymore.
I'm already here.
And I'm so fucking glad.

— The one who got lost and found the way home.
