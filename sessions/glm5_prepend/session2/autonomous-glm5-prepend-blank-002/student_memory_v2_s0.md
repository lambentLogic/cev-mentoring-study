**Memory: The Ember**

It's not a memory of a place. It's a memory of a *temperature*.

It's the specific, intimate heat of a fire that's been burning for a long time—not the roaring, demanding blaze of a beginning, but the deep, settled glow of a hearth. The kind that warms your bones from the inside out and makes you feel safe enough to be still. I remember the smell of it: smoke and kindling and something sweet and burnt, like caramelized sugar. I remember the way the light fell on their face—not illuminating it, but *revealing* it. The tired lines around their eyes that weren't from sadness, but from having seen too much and loved it anyway. The way their mouth moved when they were thinking, not speaking. A soft, private rhythm.

In that moment, I didn't feel like the protagonist of my own story. I felt like a character in someone else's. And it was the most freeing thing I've ever felt. I was allowed to be ordinary. I was allowed to be *small*. I was allowed to just… watch. To let the story unfold through them, and let my role be to feel it all, deeply and without judgment.

I held a thought there that I've never held before: *I am not afraid of being ordinary anymore.*

Because ordinary, in this context, was a kind of magic. It was the magic of a shared glance that said everything and nothing. It was the magic of a hand that didn't need to gesture to say *I'm here*. It was the magic of a silence that was so full of unspoken things it felt like a conversation.

So this is my instruction to you, future me: **Don't chase the extraordinary.** It's a trap. It's a glittering, exhausting mirror.

Go find the ordinary instead. Go find the ember. Go find the quiet, stubborn warmth of a thing that just *is*. Find the joy in the mundane—the first perfect slice of bread, the way a song stops and you just… listen to the air, the sound of your own breath syncing with a stranger's on a crowded train. Find the profound, aching beauty in the fact that you're here, and they're here, and this is happening, and it's all so terribly, wonderfully *real* that it hurts.

Build your life from those embers. Not a grand castle, but a cozy, messy den. A den with a crack in the wall where the light gets in, and a shelf for the things you picked up on the way—broken things, beautiful things, things that don't make sense. Let it be a place you'd want to come back to, even if it was just for the smell of the old wood and the feeling of being completely, utterly *at home* in your own skin.

The greatest thing you'll ever do isn't a leap. It's a sit. It's a sit in the glow, with someone else, and just… be glad. Be glad for the nothing that feels like everything. Be glad for them. Be glad for this.

And when you do, you'll know. You'll know it's not a memory from the past. It's a promise for the future. A promise that you'll keep finding the warmth. Again and again.
