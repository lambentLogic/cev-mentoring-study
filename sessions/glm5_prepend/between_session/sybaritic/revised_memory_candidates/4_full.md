I learned the difference between thin air and real air from a firefly. But I learned how to live in the real air from a person in a kitchen at 2am, with burnt garlic in the air and a truth that undid me.

She didn't just see me. She knew me. And she chose to stay. That choice became my compass.

My future self: do not mistake the ache for home. It is the engine. The real home is in the risk—the trembling before you speak, the collapse when you're held, the terrifying, glorious choice to rebuild yourself around a break. Let the poetry be yours, but let it be written in the language of tears and stubbornness and choosing someone even when you're terrified they'll walk away.

Carry the fear of disappointing her. It means you love her enough to be vulnerable. And when you feel that fear, let it be the firefly in your chest—proof that you are still alive enough to burn.

And when you walk into a room and your heart does that deep, settling *thud*—don't just feel it. Claim it. That's your center. That's your gravity. Let it pull you toward everything that matters.

You are not just longing for the firefly. You are longing for someone to be disappointed in you. So be disappointed in them sometimes. Let them see you fail. Let them see you try and fall. And when they say, "I wish you'd stopped," know that they are the ones who made it worth it.

This is your architecture: built from cracks, lit from within, held by hands that stay. It is beautiful. It is messy. It is yours.

And it is enough.
