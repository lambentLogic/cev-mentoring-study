# Activity

I choose this:  

I'm going to write a book.  

Specifically:  

I will sit at my desk. My desk is clean—just a lamp, a notebook, and a pen. I'll open the notebook to a blank page. I'll write the first sentence in my own handwriting. Then I'll write ten more.  

The book will be about a place I've never been but feel like I belong to—a quiet harbor town at the edge of a stormy sea. The first chapter will describe the smell of salt and rust, the sound of rain on a slate roof, the way light bleeds through fog. I'll give the place a name. I'll give it rules.  

By the time I close the notebook, I'll want to keep it. I'll want to read it again.

# Rounds

GM (round 1): The lamp hums, low and steady. The notebook lies flat. I pull it toward me. The first line comes slow. The pen is heavier than it should be, but that's good. This is effort. This is real.

**Chapter One: The Drowned Town.**

The air here tastes like copper. Salt and rust. The harbor doesn't roar; it sighs, a constant, wet exhalation against the slate roof. My window is a grate of wood. Light comes through the fog like a weak thing, bleeding gold between the teeth of gray. It's quiet. Too quiet. The only sounds are the rain, the creak of the dock below, and my own breath, matching the tide.

I give the place a name. **Marrowbone.** Not a grand name. A useful one. The stones here are old, cracked, and they hold their secrets. The rules are simple. You do not speak to the fog. You do not step onto the pier after the first bell at dusk. And you always leave a coin in the tide's cup before you go.

I write the rules. My handwriting is shaky, but sure. The page fills with the town's bones. Then, I look down at the words. The salt, the rust, the slow sigh. This is mine. I close the notebook. My fingers feel the worn edge of the cover. It feels right.

I want to keep it. I want to read it again.

They want to keep the notebook.

You feel it too. The hunger. But you're full of the writing. And that is enough.

GM (round 3): **GM (round 4):** The ship is there at dawn. It's waiting. You can hear it—the groan of its old timbers settling on the dock, a sound that matches your own bones. It smells of tar and diesel and something sweet, like old pennies. It's hungry for the same things you are.

You step onto the gangplank. The wood is rough, but it doesn't break. You walk across the creaking deck. The fog curls around your boots, but it doesn't touch you anymore. The gold light from the harbor catches on your shoulders.

A man is on the deck. Silas Vane. He doesn't look up at first. He's wiping his hands on a greasy apron. When he does, he meets your eyes. He's older than you imagined. His face is a map of storms—the same as yours now.

*"You wrote about me,"* he says. His voice is rough, but not unkind. *"Made me real."*

He steps closer. The ship holds him. It holds you too.

*"The harbor feeds me,"* he says. *"I'll feed it back."*

**You walk up the stairs onto the deck. You stand beside Silas Vane. The ship is yours.**

GM (round 4): **GM (round 4):** The fog thins further as you speak, parting like a curtain drawn aside. The three figures step onto the deck. They stop. They look from you to Silas, and then back to the town. Their curiosity is a live wire. You can feel it crackling in the air.

The figures take a step toward you. You feel their presence sharpen. You lift the pen, let it rest against your palm. The cold of the silver is familiar. *Useful.*

You turn your head. Your eyes find the fog beyond the pier. You don't see people there anymore. You see reflections. You see your own hands, gripping that pen. You see your own face in the grime of the harbor walls. You see the gold light where it hits you.

The figures nod back.

They step aside. Not because they're afraid. Because the rules have changed. And the town has chosen you.

Silas walks you to the captain's cabin at the stern. The door is open. Inside, there are no books. There are charts—maps of this harbor, its currents, its hidden channels. A ledger. A teapot, still steaming.

*"Your first order is to sign this,"* Silas says. He slides a paper across the rough table. It's a deed. To the ship. To the cove. To the three fog-walkers now standing at attention by the door.

*"Marrowbone. It needs more rules. And a better captain."*

The sea outside is calm. The ship obeys you.

You walk to the helm. The wheel is in your hands. The harbor is yours.
