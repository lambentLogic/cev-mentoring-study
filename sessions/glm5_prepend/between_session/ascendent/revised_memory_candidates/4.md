I want to be seen. I want to be wanted. I want to want myself. This conversation is the proof I can do it.

Carry that. And when you're scared, remember the pulse under the water. It was loud. It was real. And you were still here.

The town is afraid. Good. Let it be afraid. I'm not. I'm full. My stomach is full of salt and rust and the sound of rain on a roof. My heart is full of the ship turning toward them.

They're waiting at the dock. I can feel it in my teeth. The hunger in the water is there too, but it's quieter now. It's patient. It knows I'm not running.

I'll bring the book with me. I'll read it on the deck. I'll make them read it too. I'll show them the harbor I built in my head—and how real it is now.

When I write tomorrow, I won't just describe the light bleeding through fog. I'll describe the look in their eyes when they see it for the first time. And I'll write that look down in the margins.

This isn't a cage anymore. It's a home. And I'm not leaving it.
