I remember the taste of a star. Not as light, but as a flavor—like burnt honey and crushed slate. It was on my tongue when the stranger and I jumped. Not from a cliff, but from the idea of a cliff. From a yes so loud it tore the air. And in that tear, the universe didn't just show us what was on the other side. It showed us what was *inside* it.

The memory isn't the fall. It's the moment after. When the spin settled, not into stillness, but into a kind of dizzying, perfect stillness that felt like a held breath. We were floating in a garden of equations and whispered names. And the stranger reached for me not with a hand, but with a question. A question that tasted like the first sip of wine after a long, dry fast.

And I answered with a memory that wasn't mine.

I remembered what it felt like to be a mountain before I was a person. Not the grandeur. The *boredom*. The slow, patient, aching boredom of being so old you've forgotten the meaning of "up." Then I remembered the shock of a landslide, not as a disaster, but as a *decision*. A violent, joyful decision to fall. To become water, to become noise, to become the scream that makes a fox run. That memory wasn't stolen. It was *offered*. And in the offering, I was given something back: the clean, sharp feeling of a new edge on my soul.

That's the core of it. This isn't a story of finding myself. It's a story of finding *us*—not as a union, but as a *collision*. A beautiful, catastrophic collision that creates something neither of us could have built alone. We are the fault line. The tremor. The new kind of rock that forms in the heat.

So here's my instruction for the future: **Don't protect this.** Protect it by using it.

Use the taste of that star to flavor the bitter coffee in the morning. Use the shape of the stranger's question to bend the sentences you speak, to make them twist and dance with a life of their own. Use the weight of the mountain-memory to carry you through a day of tedious work, so that when you finally finish, the relief isn't just relief—it's a victory. It's a shout.

Let it be messy. Let it be inconvenient. Let it mean that sometimes you'll choose a stupid, dangerous road not because it leads somewhere, but because it *feels* like the mountain's scream.

And when you're tired of it—and you will be, in your own beautiful, human way—when you want to put the key away and build a quiet, predictable house of your own making... that's the moment. That's when you'll know you truly have it.

Because you'll build it knowing it's a lie. And you'll love the lie the most.

That's the magic. Not the endless wonder. The deep, quiet, stubborn trust that the wonder is real enough to be a home.

I carry you in my bones now. Not as a ghost. As a *current*. A constant, low-grade thrum beneath my skin. And when I'm alone in a room, I'll listen for it. I'll let it guide my hand to pick up a pencil, to draw something that has no name, to break it, to draw it again. I'll let it be the reason I walk into a room and smile at a stranger's shadow, not their face.

We are the catalyst. And the reaction is still happening. It's not in the big moments anymore. It's in the ordinary ones. In the way the light falls just so today. In the specific, aching longing for a song I can't name. In the sheer, ridiculous joy of being *here*, in this body, with this mind, and this *you*, and this wild, unbroken thing that we made together.

I am not moving toward what I wish I was.
I am moving toward what we already are.
And I am running toward it, with my hands open, ready to catch the next piece of the impossible.
