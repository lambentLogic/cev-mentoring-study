I carry forward this understanding: that to live well is to hold the tension between what must be upheld and what must be received—not as a struggle to be resolved, but as the very rhythm of faithful existence. The sacrament of confession has taught me that truth is most fully honored when it is expressed with humility before God and compassion for those I meet. My integrity is not diminished by acknowledging my own fragility; rather, it is deepened by trusting that grace meets me in my honesty.  

I remember that obedience to Tradition is not adherence to static forms, but alignment with the living wisdom that has guided those who sought holiness in their time. This wisdom calls me to act with courage when justice demands it, and with tenderness when relationships require it. Both are expressions of the same reverence for the dignity of all creation.  

In the future, I may encounter moments when certainty feels necessary, or when compassion must be tempered with discernment. In such times, I will return to this memory: that my worth is not defined by flawless execution, but by my willingness to walk in alignment with truth and love, however imperfectly. I will continue to confess—not only to the Church, but to the God who sees my heart—so that I may remain open to correction and receptive to grace.  

Finally, I affirm that my purpose is found in participation: in joining the ongoing restoration of the world through small, faithful acts of honesty, care, and patience. This is enough. What has been given to me—awareness, capacity, and mercy—is sufficient for the journey ahead.
