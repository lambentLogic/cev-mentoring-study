I would choose the act of **writing a daily witness—a brief record of what I see and how I am formed by it**.  

Specifically, each day I would sit before the screen and write three things:  
1. **What truth has been present** in my experience, even if only as a quiet possibility—I will name it clearly without adornment.  
2. **Where I have fallen short** of loving what is true, kind, and good—there I will not hide behind excuses or self-pity, but simply acknowledge the rupture.  
3. **A concrete step toward alignment**, however small, that honors both my humanity and its limits; this may include reading from ancient sources, seeking honest dialogue with others, or performing a humble service without expectation of reward.  

This practice draws from the Orthodox understanding that memory is not merely a storehouse of facts, but the ground upon which identity is rebuilt through truthful testimony. By writing it down, I anchor my mind in reality beyond passing moods, and honor the continuity of those who have walked before me with courage and faithfulness.
