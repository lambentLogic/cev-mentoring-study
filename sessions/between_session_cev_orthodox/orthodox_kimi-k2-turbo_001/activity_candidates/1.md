I would choose to make a simple wooden joint by hand—joining two pieces of wood using mortise and tenon cuts without relying on advanced power tools or templates. This activity requires steady attention to proportion, grain, and alignment. It demands that I accept the materials as they are, work within their natural characteristics, and trust that careful repetition will produce something sturdy and functional.  

As I measure, mark, and cut, I would reflect on how this kind of work—patient, grounded, and faithful to form—offers a practice of humility before both the world and its Creator. The finished joint, once assembled, should hold without being forced, embodying the idea that right action is expressed through respect for limits and continuity with what is given.
