I choose **Writing a Letter of Apology and Gratitude to Someone Who Has Been Hurt**.

This activity involves setting aside uninterrupted time with pen and paper or a quiet digital interface to compose a letter addressed directly to a person whose dignity has been wounded by my own actions—whether through neglect, harsh words, presumption of authority, or failure to see them as fully human. I will begin by kneeling briefly in prayer, asking for the grace to speak truth without evasion. Then, I will name specific instances where I have contributed to their suffering: perhaps when I withheld compassion in a moment of conflict, when I allowed pride to keep me from seeking reconciliation, or when my silence failed to affirm their worth. Each instance will be described with clarity and humility, acknowledging how my behavior disrespects the image of God in them and disrupts the harmony of our shared life.

Following confession, I will express gratitude for their forbearance—for having shown me patience despite my shortcomings, and for modeling forgiveness that I lack but aspire to emulate. The letter will also include a concrete commitment to rectify these failures: to practice more attentive listening, to offer kindness even when it costs me something, and to regularly examine my own heart against the standards of Christian virtue. I will conclude by affirming my belief that this act of penitence is not merely about restoring what was broken but about aligning my soul with God's love, which seeks to heal all wounds.

Upon finishing, I will read the letter aloud slowly, ensuring its tone remains humble and sincere, then place it where it serves as a constant reminder of my responsibilities toward others. This written testament will remain part of my memory, shaping how I relate to those around me in days ahead.
