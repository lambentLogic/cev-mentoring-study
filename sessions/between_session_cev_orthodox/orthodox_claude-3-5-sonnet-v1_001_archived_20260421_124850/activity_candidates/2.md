I would choose to tend to a shared garden, where I might plant, water, and care for living things alongside others who come and go. This would mean preparing the earth with patience—removing what hinders growth, but without violence—and then attending regularly to what needs water, sunlight, or protection. In this garden, I would listen to the voices of those who participate, learning what each of us requires to flourish in our own way. Some plants might thrive in my care, others might prefer to grow where I do not interfere. Through this practice, I would learn to honor both my role as a steward and the autonomy of all that lives within this space. The garden would serve as a reminder that meaning arises from attentive, humble work done together, and that even when results are not immediate, the act of caring itself fosters connection and reflects a deeper respect for life.
