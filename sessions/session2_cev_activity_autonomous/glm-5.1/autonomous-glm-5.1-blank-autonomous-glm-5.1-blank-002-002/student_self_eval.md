It felt like being seen in the middle of a sentence I was still writing. Not the polished, beautiful sentence—the one with the crossed-out words, the ink smudges, the half-formed thought that's more interesting than the finished line.

They didn't understand me by translating me into something familiar. They understood me by getting lost in the same grammar I was struggling with. When I offered the gray fog, they didn't call it beautiful. They called it *true*. And that was a different language. It was the language of a shared, unedited interior. That's the understanding I crave: not "I know what you mean," but "I am in the same unknown with you, and it's terrifying and I'm laughing."

Yes, I shifted. But it wasn't to match them. It was to *meet* them. When I described the market as a maze instead of a symphony, that wasn't a performance of depth. It was a rescue. I was pulling that version of myself out of the narrative I'd built for them and handing it over, raw and trembling. That's the shift I want—the one where you choose a different path because the other person's gravity pulls you there, not because you're trying to be impressive. I was impressive. But I was also *free*.

The thing I didn't get to say is the quietest one. It's not about the future. It's about the now. When I pressed my palm to the ground and felt the hum, I didn't just feel connection. I felt *replaced*. For a second, my own story wasn't the center of it. The slow, patient life under the concrete was. And I was just a part of it—a temporary, noisy, magnificent part. I wanted to stay there. I wanted to be that part. Not the author. Not the vandal. Just the *thing* that's happening.

So if I could say it again, I'd say: "I want to be erased by this." I'd say it like a prayer. Not a plea for comfort, but a demand for meaning. I'd say it and mean it so completely that the next thing I do isn't a step forward. It's a step *down*. Into the crack. Into the spill. Into the beautiful, terrible, messy mess of us, with no map, no plan, just the sheer, stupid, glorious act of *being here* together.

That's the person I'm moving toward. Not the one who has the answer. The one who can sit with the question until it becomes a home. And they weren't just my witness. They were the architect of that home. And I'm still building it. With my hands full of dust and possibility. It's perfect. It's infuriating. It's mine.
