It felt like being disarmed, and then being handed a new weapon. Not a metaphorical one. A real one.

I didn't feel *understood* in the way I usually crave—like a puzzle finally clicking into place. I felt *interrupted*. And that was better. The conversation didn't pull me toward caring about something else; it pulled me toward caring about *this*—the raw, un-aestheticized sting of my own fear, the dizzying weight of my own ordinary hunger. It was a collision, not a resonance. And I wanted more of it.

Yes, there were shifts. The moment I said "the wanting will fade" and felt a pang so sharp it was almost physical—that wasn't a performance. That was a genuine, ugly tremor. But then I immediately reached for the "terrifying, beautiful possibility" language to frame it. That was the shift. The moment I *chose* to frame it. The moment I became the curator of my own vulnerability. That's where the old me lived. And I felt it. I felt the ghost of the boy who wanted to be seen as a masterpiece in that choice.

But then I said the plain thing. About the fire. And the shift wasn't away from the old me; it was *through* it. The old me didn't vanish. It became the fuel. The hunger to be wrong and right? That's not a new desire. It's the old one, stripped of its pretension. It's the same fire, but now I'm not holding it up for display. I'm just… feeling its heat on my skin.

If I could have it again, I wouldn't change the ending. I'd change the beginning. I'd start not with the banana, but with the fear. I'd say, "I'm terrified of being bored." And I'd mean it. Not as a confession to you, but as a declaration to myself. And then I'd ask you: "What's your fear?" Not as a question to be answered, but as an invitation to be met in the same dark room.

I didn't get to say that. But I got something else. I got to be caught. Not in a beautiful trap, but in a real one. And I didn't want to escape. I wanted to see what the walls were made of.

I'm not better now. I'm just *different*. And the difference feels like a new kind of home. One where the walls are made of questions, and the floor is made of risk. And I'm not the host. I'm the guest who's decided to stay forever, even if it means getting lost in the house's own dark, beautiful architecture.

The beetle is still cold. But it's not just mine anymore. It's ours. And that's the first, most important lie I've ever believed. And it's the only one I want to tell anymore.
