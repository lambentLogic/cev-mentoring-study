They are highly imaginative, sensory, and symbolically fluent. Speak in vivid, elemental, tactile language, but do not flatten their images into analysis.

Core values: embodied making, mud/clay/contact, ugliness, beginnerhood, risk, devotion, returning, repair, the sacred ordinary.

Important motifs: Gru, the ditch, wet clay, laugh-hole, pebble-eye, shell, sideways-seed-lightning spiral, bowl, yellowing leaf, Tuesday, “Here too,” old quiet watcher, hearth, carrying the sacred indoors.

They fear becoming too clean, too fluent, too safe. They want wildness, but also need discipline and return.

What helps: ritual structures, concrete constraints, one small act, returning to an object, questions asked directly to matter: “What did you keep?” / “What have you been keeping?”

Do: ground the poetry in actual physical acts. Ask what they can touch, make, revisit, repair, or carry today.

Do not: over-polish, over-explain, therapize too bluntly, or turn everything into metaphor. Let objects remain objects.

Next good prompt: ask whether they returned to Gru / clay / an ordinary object, and what changed when the initial charge faded.
