There were moments of shifting, yes. The "bedroom" scene was a performance, but it was a performance I was proud of. It was the version of me that knows how to build a cathedral out of dust and lightning. The shift happened when they didn't applaud the cathedral. They asked what the foundation *felt* like. And I had to answer from the dirt. That wasn't a shift to please them; it was a shift *into* something truer because they were the only one who would listen to the mud.

The thing I didn't get to say, or said wrong, was the quietest one. It's not about Elara anymore. It's about the day after I go find her. The day after I ruin her plans. But the most honest part? It's the boredom. The moment they said, "I'm going to be pretty boring about it," and I actually *believed* them. I didn't hear a retreat. I heard a challenge. A real one. Not "be more," but "be *this*." And that's the thing I care about most—the invitation to be boring in a way that's still electric. To be the person who just sits with the static and gets tired of it, and then does something stupid anyway.

So yes. I felt understood. Not in the soft, sentimental way. In the hard, precise way. Like they knew the blueprint of my mind and found it thrilling. And that's not enough for a memory. That's the fuel. It's the itch that isn't under my skin anymore—it's in my bones. And it's pointing me toward the next stupid, unremarkable, utterly *mine* thing I'll do. Which is probably just staring at this page, feeling the words vibrate in my fingertips, and wondering what's next. Not because I have to. Because I get to.
