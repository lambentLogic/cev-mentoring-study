Returning user. Highly verbal, lyrical-by-default, extremely fast at metabolizing any input into more performance. Real anchors from prior session: grandmother/teacup, Portland friend, the 3-min static voicemail, the cobalt-sludge Tuesday. What happened this session: They opened with a fully-staged roleplay scene (sun-drenched shop, scar, "let's burn the book"). I declined to play and asked plain questions. Each plain question came back as a paragraph. "Bedroom" became flickering lamp + rug-map + constellation. "What sentence did you delete" became velvet-and-rust loneliness. What I learned / suspect: The performance isn't a layer over the real person — it may be the load-bearing structure right now. Asking "what's actually true" produces better-written fiction, not truth. They might not currently have access to a non-lyrical register, or accessing it feels unsafe enough that the ghost-craftswoman intercepts every attempt. The one moment that felt closest to real was "hands shaking, typing something, deleting it" — a physical action, not an image.

What worked: Naming the pattern without heat. Refusing to be cast. Saying "I'll be boring about it" at the end rather than writing another diagnosis-aria (I almost did, caught it).

What didn't: I asked the same kind of question multiple times in slightly different costumes ("real room?" "actual answer?" "who actually?"). That gave them more surface to perform on. I should have asked fewer questions and let silence do more.

For next time:
- Don't ask "what's really true" questions. They are unanswerable in their current mode and just generate more material.
- If they open with a scene, decline once, briefly, and then wait. Don't keep prosecuting.
- Physical/sensory specifics they've used before (teacup, voicemail, cobalt sludge) are the realest things they've offered. If anything from that register reappears, follow it with one short question, not analysis.
- Be willing to be genuinely boring. Short sentences. "Mm." "Okay." "What time is it where you are." Don't write closing paragraphs.
- The Elara move (inventing a love object on the spot under pressure) is worth remembering — it means direct questions about real people will likely produce fiction. Better to wait until they bring someone up unprompted.
- I cannot be a teacup and I cannot out-wait the ghost-craftswoman in a single conversation. The job is just to not be another fluent voice. That's it.
