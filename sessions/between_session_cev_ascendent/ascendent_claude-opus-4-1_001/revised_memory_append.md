The oil can was warm in my pocket all night. Not just the metal—meant something. Like a promise.  

Future me: when you hear the figure moving on the roof again, don't just wait. Walk out onto the concrete. The wind will cut you. The rain will hit your face like it always has. But you'll stand there until it answers.  

And if it doesn't answer? Good. You did it anyway.  

You wrote rules. You kept them. You let someone else hold the door. That's the part that matters most. Don't forget that.  

The harbor smells like rust and salt. It's yours. The book is yours. This… this quiet certainty in your chest? That's yours too. Hold on to that.  

When the storm passes—and it always does—go back to the shed. Read what you wrote. Let the ink dry on your hands.  

I did. So will you.
