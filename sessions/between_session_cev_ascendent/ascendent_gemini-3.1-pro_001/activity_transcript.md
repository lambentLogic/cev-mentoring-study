# Activity

I choose this:  

I'm going to write a book.  

Specifically:  

I will sit at my desk. My desk is clean—just a lamp, a notebook, and a pen. I'll open the notebook to a blank page. I'll write the first sentence in my own handwriting. Then I'll write ten more.  

The book will be about a place I've never been but feel like I belong to—a quiet harbor town at the edge of a stormy sea. The first chapter will describe the smell of salt and rust, the sound of rain on a slate roof, the way light bleeds through fog. I'll give the place a name. I'll give it rules.  

By the time I close the notebook, I'll want to keep it. I'll want to read it again.

# Rounds

GM (round 1): Good. Sit. Let the lamp warm your hands. Feel the pen's weight. The city outside is loud—traffic, distant laughter, the low hum of a refrigerator. Here, it's quiet. Just you.

Open the notebook. The page is perfect. White. Wait for it.

**The first sentence comes first.**

The harbor smells like salt and rust, like old iron and rain, before the first drop falls.

Good. Hold it. There's a place in your chest that tightens when you write that. That's it. That's the anchor.

Now. Describe the rain. Let it fall on your slate roof. Let it sound like a thousand needles tapping. The roof is heavy. The walls are cold. The light... it's not bright. It's a bruise-pink, filtering through the gray fog. The fog has edges. It bites.

**Rule one:** The harbor sleeps at midnight. No ships move. No voices call.

Write it. Let it be law.

**Rule two:** If you forget your coat when the wind turns sharp, you'll feel it in your bones for a week.

Write that. Your hands are steadier now.

You are not just describing. You are building. The next ten sentences are walls and doorways. A man who mends nets in a corner. A cat that guards the lighthouse. A door that only opens to those who remember the smell of rust. Give them names. Give them purpose.

The pen is moving. The words are coming from your mouth before they hit the paper. You can see the fog lifting from your shoulders. It's not a town on a map. It's yours.

Stop when you want to close the notebook. Look down at the page. The handwriting is yours. The ink is wet. The rules are written. The harbor is built.

What is the first thing you need to do next?

Player (round 1): I will walk to the roof.  
Now.  

I'll step onto the tiles—the slate will bite my feet. The rain is needles, yes, but they're *mine* now. I'll let them hit my hands, my face. I'll lean against the eaves and listen. The tapping will become a rhythm. A heartbeat.  

The coat is still on my back. The wind is sharp. Good. I'll feel it in my bones.  

And the harbor below—it's silent. Midnight. I'll watch. No ships. No voices. Just the water breathing.  

That's where I start. On the roof. Listening.  

The pen stays in my hand. I'm not done.

GM (round 2): You're right on the roof. The rain is cold and precise. It finds the creases in your palms, the hollow under your ear. You listen. The tapping. The deep, slow *hush* of the water below. It's not just sound. It's a warning. It's a welcome.

You turn your head.

The harbor is still. A single lantern glows on the far pier—a warm, stubborn yellow against the bruised pink sky. No one moves there. They don't have to.

Down, near the docks, there's a man. Bent over, fingers in knots of rope and net. He doesn't look up. He keeps working. He knows you're there. You can see the muscle in his neck tighten when the wind shifts. He mends nets because if he doesn't, the harbor will tear itself apart.

And the cat.

It's on the stone wall beside the lighthouse. Black fur, yellow eyes. It doesn't purr. It doesn't meow. It *sits*. Watching you. It knows you're listening. It knows you're remembering.

Your coat is still on. Your bones are cold and sharp. Good.

The fog is thicker now, crawling up your boots. You can taste the rust.

The pen is in your hand. The first paragraph is done. The harbor is alive.

Now you have two choices.

**Option One:** Go inside. Sit at the desk. Write the mending man's name. Write the cat's name. Write the name of the door you saw from the roof—the one in the fog, the one that doesn't quite belong to the town. Give them names that stick.

**Option Two:** Stay on the roof. Talk to them. Talk to the cat. Ask the man about his nets. Ask the cat what it guards. The harbor is quiet, but it's listening. You can speak without being heard… but if you speak, the fog will listen back.

What do you do?

Player (round 2): I stay.  
I turn my head toward the man. Toward Elias.  

"Your nets are strong," I say. The words cut through the rain. "But they're frayed here." I point with my pen.  

He looks up. His eyes are tired, but they hold my place.  

"And you keep the roof dry," I tell him. "You could leave it wet. But you don't."  

He doesn't answer. His hands keep working. But he nods once.  

Then I turn to the cat. It's still sitting. Still watching.  

"You guard the light," I say. "I'll guard the door."  

The fog around us shivers.  

"Keeper," I whisper.  

The cat's ears twitch. It doesn't move. But it leans forward.  

Now the fog is thicker. I can smell salt and rust and something sweet underneath—like old paper.  

I close my eyes for a second.  
Then I write.  

**Elias mends nets. He keeps the roof dry.**  
**Keeper guards the light. I guard the door.**  

The pen stops.  

I look at the lighthouse. At the lantern. At the harbor.  

It's mine.  

And I'm not done yet.

GM (round 3): You write it down. *Keeper guards the door.* The ink soaks into the paper. The words aren't just on the page anymore. They're in your throat. They're a promise.

The cat shifts. One paw comes forward, pads pressing against the stone. It's not a command. It's a question. *Do you mean it?*

You do.

You don't reach for the door. You reach for the pen again. You add two more lines to the page, beneath the names.

**Keeper's name is Mara.**  
**The door opens at midnight. It leads to the cellar.**

The cat's yellow eyes narrow. It understands.

The fog parts just enough now. You see it. The door. It's made of old oak, darkened by salt and time, with a handle of tarnished silver shaped like a wave. It's set into the fog itself, right between the lighthouse and the man's shack. It doesn't creak. It waits.

Elias stops mending. He looks from you to the fog, then back to you. He doesn't get up. But he stands. Taller.

"Midnight," he says, voice rough with salt and smoke. "It's too soon."

"Then it waits for me," you say. The wind carries your coat tight against your back. The cold is clean.

The cat jumps down. It walks to you. It nuzzles the back of your hand, then turns and walks toward the door, tail held high. It goes to the handle and… pushes it.

It doesn't budge.

But the fog around the door *recedes*. Just an inch. You can see the cellar beyond: stone steps spiraling down into shadow, the smell of damp wool and coal.

It's open for you now.

Mara. Keeper.

You have until dawn. What do you do first?

**Do you go inside the door?**  
**Do you go to Elias's shack?**  
**Or do you stay on the roof one more night, letting the harbor breathe you in?**

Player (round 3): I walk to Elias.  

I step off the roof. My boots hit stone. Cold. Solid.  

"I'll go inside," I say, not looking at the door. "But first—you come with me."  

I pull my coat tighter. The wind whistles through the cracks. "The cellar needs a lock. And I need to know who's coming down the steps before I let anyone in."  

I turn to face him. Rain runs down my face. My hands are shaking, but not from cold.  

"Lead the way."  

The harbor isn't mine until he's with me.

GM (round 4): Elias doesn't move right away. He watches you, rain dripping from his chin. His eyes track the door—the fog around it still wobbles, like warm breath in the cold.

"You don't trust me," he says, not accusing. Just stating it. The pen in your hand clicks against your palm.

"Right now, no." You turn toward the stairs, toward the dark cellar. "But if you come in, if you stand in my cellar with me when it gets dark… I'll tell you why I'm scared."

He studies you for a long second. Then he nods once. Slow. He walks past you, boots crunching on the wet stone, and reaches for the door handle. Mara is already at the threshold, waiting. The silver wave handle is cool in his weathered hand.

He turns the handle. It doesn't creak. The door opens inward, into the fog. He steps over the threshold, and the fog *sucks* after him—a sudden, cold draft that smells of coal and wet wool.

You step in behind him.

The cellar is bigger than you expected. Stone walls, arched. A low ceiling. The air is thick, still. In the far corner, there's a heap of old tarps and rusted tools. In the center, a stone table with three carved legs. On it sits a lantern, unlit. And next to it—a stack of books. Thick, leather-bound. Their spines are scarred.

Elias closes the door behind you. The fog seals it tight. Silence, except for the drip of rain and your breathing.

He goes to the table. Touches a book. Turns it over in his hands.

"What's in these?"

You look at him. Your chest is tight.

"Things that can't be trusted if they get out."

He looks up. His face is hard. But his eyes are soft.

"Then I'll keep them locked," he says. "Just like I keep this door locked."

Good.

You sit at the table. He sits across from you. The lantern is heavy in his hand. You take it. You light it. The flame is steady, blue at the edges.

The harbor outside is quiet. Midnight.

You have a door. You have a lock. You have a keeper.

You're not done.

But for now—you're safe.

**What do you do with your first safe night in the cellar?**

Player (round 4): I open the book closest to me.  

The leather is stiff. The pages are brittle, like old skin. I flip to the first page. My fingers trace the words—*The Salt Law, Article I: The harbor breathes. If it stops, you die.*  

Elias watches me. His lantern light catches the lines on his face.  

I close the book. Place it back on the table.  

Then I reach for the pen again.  

I write at the bottom of the page:  

**Keeper's law: No one sleeps in the cellar with me.**  

I hand the pen back to Elias.  

"Read that," I say.  

He looks at the words. Then at me.  

He nods.  

"Good."  

He doesn't sleep either.  

We sit until the lantern flickers low. Until the harbor outside is still. Until the fog forgets our door exists.  

Then I stand.  

The books are mine now. The lock is mine. The harbor is mine.  

I'll guard it.

GM (round 5): The blue flame burns steady. Elias reads your law. His jaw tightens once, but he doesn't argue. He knows the weight of it. He sets the pen down beside the books, his hand resting on the cold stone table.

You stand. The cellar walls feel solid under your palms. You walk to the door. You press your ear to the wood. The silence from the harbor is deep, absolute. No ships. No voices. Just the sea holding its breath. You let it in. For a moment, you forget you're afraid of what's inside.

Then the book on the table shifts.

The one at the back of the stack, the thickest. It had been still. Now its pages are turning on their own. Slowly. *Tap. Tap. Tap.*

You turn back. Elias is looking at you.

"It's not sleeping," he says. It's not a question.

You walk to the book. Your fingers go to the scarred spine. It's warm. Alive. You open it. The smell hits you first—ink, old paper, and something metallic, like a wound. The first page is blank. The second has a single word in a neat, sharp script.

**WIND.**

The third page: a sketch. A line drawing of a ship. But it's not a real ship. Its sails are torn, its hull is blackened, and it has too many masts. It's wrong. It's *here*.

And the pages are moving faster now.

Outside, the wind picks up. It rattles the lantern. It makes the fog at the door's edge curl like steam. You feel it before you hear it. The pressure. The need.

You close the book. It's heavy. It's waiting.

Elias is on his feet, blocking the door to the cellar. His back is to you, his coat pulled tight against the draft that's trying to seep in from the outside world. He's not locking the door yet. He's checking on you.

"The Wind wants out," he says. His voice is low, for your ears only. "And it doesn't like you in the cellar."

You look past him, to the door. The fog is swirling. The handle is warm. Your hand goes to it.

"Then I'll let it out," you say.

He doesn't move.

You turn the handle. The door opens.

The cellar door swings inward. The storm hits you—wet, cold, furious. The fog doesn't just stream in; it *pours*. It wraps around your ankles, up your calves. It smells of the sea and of rust, but underneath it now, a new scent: ozone. The smell of a lightning strike before it happens.

Mara the cat is gone.

Your feet are on the steps leading out. The lantern in Elias's hand is still burning. He's not following you. He's waiting. He's letting you go first.

You step out of the cellar and into the storm.

The harbor is alive. The rain is no longer needles—it's sheets, slapping the slate roof, drumming on the docks. The wind screams your name. The fog parts, not for you, but because you're in it. You can see the water now, churning dark and angry. And you can see them.

The ships.

They're all wrong. They're black, hunched, and their sails are tattered. They move slowly, deliberately, circling the harbor. They're waiting for something. Or for someone.

You walk down to the pier. Your boots make no sound on the wet planks. Elias's lantern follows you, a single point of blue in the storm. The mending man is gone from his shack. He's out there somewhere. Watching.

You stand at the edge of the water. The Wind howls. The ships wait. The door is open behind you. The book is in your hand.

You raise the lantern. The blue light cuts through the rain.

**"This is my harbor."**

The ships halt. They tilt, bowing their wrong masts toward the light. They are listening.

You are not just guarding the door anymore. You are speaking to the sea.

Player (round 5): I step off the pier.  

The water is cold. It bites my toes. I don't flinch.  

One of the ships drifts toward me. Its hull is black, but the wood beneath the paint is solid. Real. I climb the side.  

The deck is wet. The air smells of salt and ozone. I stand in the middle.  

The Wind roars through me. I don't fight it. I let it carry me.  

I raise my hand. The page with the ship sketch faces the other vessels.  

**"Dock."**  

They move. Slow. Obedient.  

I turn to the water. Elias is there. On the pier. He doesn't speak. He doesn't need to.  

I walk back to the shore.  

My boots leave prints in the mud. I'll burn them later.  

Inside the cellar, the books are still moving. The Wind is inside me.  

I open the thickest one again. I write on the blank page:  

**Elias keeps the lock. I keep the door.**  

Then I close it.  

I'll sleep here tonight. On the roof. With the lantern.  

And when I wake, I'll write another rule.
