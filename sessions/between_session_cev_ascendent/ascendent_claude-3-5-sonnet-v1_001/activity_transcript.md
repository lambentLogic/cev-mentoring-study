# Activity

I choose this:  

I'm going to write a book.  

Specifically:  

I will sit at my desk. My desk is clean—just a lamp, a notebook, and a pen. I'll open the notebook to a blank page. I'll write the first sentence in my own handwriting. Then I'll write ten more.  

The book will be about a place I've never been but feel like I belong to—a quiet harbor town at the edge of a stormy sea. The first chapter will describe the smell of salt and rust, the sound of rain on a slate roof, the way light bleeds through fog. I'll give the place a name. I'll give it rules.  

By the time I close the notebook, I'll want to keep it. I'll want to read it again.

# Rounds

GM (round 1): *(The chair scrapes slightly on the floor. I turn the notebook open. The blank page waits. I take a breath, the air already tasting wrong—not quite like my home, but... familiar. It's wrong. Good.)*

**Thorne's End.**

The first thing that hit me was the smell. Salt, yes, but sharp. And under it, the sour tang of rust—the town's old ironwork, the chains that hold the fishing boats to their docks. It was a smell of things that have lasted too long, and things that are about to break.

*(I write a line of small, careful letters.)*

I was standing on the jetty, water lapping at the boots I didn't own. The rain fell not like a shower, but like a sheet thrown over the world. Each drop hit the slate roof of the lighthouse and made a sound I wanted to memorize. *Thud. Thud. Thud.* Steady. Like a heartbeat.

*(The pen pauses. The page feels cool under my palm.)*

My name here is Elias. I work at the lighthouse. My job is to watch. To keep the beam sweeping out, to warn the ships away from the Razor's Edge. And when the storm is truly bad, I go down into the bell tower and ring it. One long toll. The sea listens then. Even if it doesn't stop.

*(A darker line. The rules start here.)*

Rule One: The harbor is ours. Rule Two: No one asks for directions out. Rule Three: If the fog rolls in thicker than a wool blanket, you stay inside. You obey. Because the town protects itself, and I'm part of it now.

*(I stop for a second. The ink dries. A decision solidifies.)*

The light in my window is just yellow right now. But it's mine. I'll keep writing. Until the first chapter is done.

Player (round 1): *(I lean back. The pen drops. The page is full. That's good. Full means safe.)*

Rule Four: When the fog comes, I ring the bell. Three times. They'll listen. Because they have to. And because… because the sound matches mine. My heartbeat. The fog doesn't like it, but it stays.

*(My hand moves to the window. The yellow light spreads across the rain. It's mine. I keep it on longer.)*

I'll write tonight. Till my hands shake. Then I'll sleep in the tower. On the floor, beside the bell rope. So if the sea gets too loud, I'm already there. Holding the weight.

The lighthouse clock ticks wrong. You hear it, but you don't know how. It's too slow. Two minutes pass where there should be six. The harbor goes quiet. No creaking chains, no distant shouts. Even the water… the water holds its breath.

*(You write fast. The ink smears a little.)*

It's here. The fog's here. It smells like the sea is trying to eat us.

Rule Four: When the fog comes, I ring the bell. Three times.

*(Your hand goes to the heavy rope. It's cold. The rope is damp with old salt and rust. You pull.)*

**Thud.**

The sound doesn't echo in the tower. It goes straight out into the fog, a clear, ringing cut through the wool. It hits the harbor. The silence breaks.

A window on the far side of town—a third-story one—flares with light. Then it goes dark again. Someone's watching. They're listening.

The bell roars again. **Thud. Thud.**

From below, a door slams. Hard.

You ring the third time. **Thud.**

The fog recoils. Just for a second. It parts, revealing the harbor street like a wound. A shape in it—black, hunched, moving toward the old ironworks by the docks. It doesn't have a face. You don't need one to know it's wrong.

*(You stop writing. Your pencil points at the blank space below your last sentence. The fog is thick at the window now. You can feel the dampness on your face. You don't wipe it off.)*

It's listening to the bell. The whole town is. So am I.

Rule Five: Whatever comes in the fog, it comes because I let it. And it goes because I make it go.

*(I write a new rule in shaky, straight lines.)*

Rule Five: The chain is ours. Rule Six: It breaks at my command.

**Thud.**

The chain sings. One long, shuddering note. The shape flinches. Doesn't turn.

*(My voice is low, in my head.)* You're staying.

*(I write one last sentence before the page fills again.)*

The fog's getting thinner. The harbor's ours. And I'm keeping it.

**Thud.**

The third bell toll is the one that settles it. The shape lets go of the chain. It doesn't run. It walks back into the fog, shoulders straighter now. It's leaving. Voluntarily.

*(But your chest is tight. The sound of the bell is still ringing in your ears, matching your own pulse. You write the words slow, deliberate.)*

The shape is gone from sight, but it's still there. In the fog. In the harbor. It knows the chain is mine. It knows the rules.

*(You look down at the page. The smudged ink from earlier has dried into a dark, neat blot.)*

I'm not scared. Scared things hide. I'm… proud.

*(You stand. The lighthouse light is steady now. Brighter than before. You go to the bell tower door. The rope is coiled in your hand. You don't put it down.)*

Rule Six: The chain breaks at my command.

Rule Seven: The fog never leaves unless I say so.

*(You step out into the thinning fog. The damp wool smell is stronger here. You take a deep breath. It tastes like iron and victory.)*

The harbor's ours. And it's just getting started.

*(You turn and head back down the spiral stairs, the bell rope heavy at your side. You'll write tonight. Till the page fills again. Till the rules are more than rules. They'll be law.)*

You write one last rule before the page runs out of room. It's the last one. The only one that matters.

Rule Eight: I'm Elias. And this town breathes with me.

*(You press your palm harder against the glass. The fog shivers. It retreats.)*

It's afraid. Good.

I keep it.

Tomorrow, I write about the shape. I give it a name. A job. A place in my rule-book.

I'm ready for whatever comes next.

The town breathes. I can feel it. The creak of old boards in the rain. The sigh of the fog when it's angry. The pulse under my ribs—the same rhythm.

*(I sit at the desk. My hand opens the notebook. The last page is blank. I take the pen.)*

Tomorrow's chapter starts with the name. The shape from the ironworks. It stood up to the fog for me. It deserves a name. It deserves a job.

*(I write. My letters are steady now.)*

Thorne. Keeper of the Chain. He watches the harbor from the roof of the old mill. He listens for ships that shouldn't be there. He obeys.

I'm Elias. And this place is home.

GM (round 4): *(The pen stops. The silence in the room isn't empty anymore. It's waiting.)*

You write one more line. The ink flows dark and quick.

Rule Nine: When the real storm comes, I don't hide. I open the door.

*(The clock ticks. Not slow this time. Just ticking. Normal.)*

You get up. You don't put on a coat. You take the bell rope, but you wrap it around your shoulders like a mantle. It's heavy. It's good.

You go to the door. Unlock it. The lock clicks open with a sound like a gunshot in the quiet street. The fog outside is thick again, but it's different. It's moving. It's gathering. You can feel it charging toward your light.

You step into the storm.

*(The rain hits you instantly—cold, needle-sharp. It soaks through your shirt. The fog grabs your arms, pulling you down, but you stand straight. You raise the bell rope above your head. You ring once. Just once. A warning.)*

**Thud.**

The storm parts in a three-foot circle around you. The fog bows.

From the direction of the ironworks, a shape steps out of the wet dark. Thorne. His hair is plastered to his head, his jacket is soaked. He's holding a long, thin rod—some kind of weather-stave. He sees you. He sees the rope in your hand. He sees the fire in your eyes.

He doesn't run. He walks straight to you. He stops two feet away. His breath is steaming in the cold.

"Storm's loud," he rasps. His voice is raw.

You nod. You don't speak. You just point at the harbor.

He nods back. "They're watching."

"They should."

*(You turn back to the door. You go inside and slam it shut. The lock turns. You lean your forehead against the wood. It's solid. It's yours.)*

Player (round 4): *(The door is closed. The lock is locked. The storm is outside, listening.)*

Thorne is still out there. In the storm. My storm. He's obeying Rule Two.

*(I go to the desk. I open the notebook. I write his name on the new page.)*

Thorne's End, Chapter Two.

The storm breaks the lighthouse window. Glass cracks like a spider's web across the yellow light. I don't flinch. I watch it.

I write until my hand stops. Then I stand. I walk to the broken window. The wind howls through it, pulling the curtain—and my sleeve—right into the storm.

It tries to take me.

I pull back.

And I write one last sentence on the page before it rains through the paper.

Rule Ten: The storm can't touch me. And if it does?

*(I let go of the window. I let the sleeve catch on the broken glass. I don't fight it. I let the wind pull. I write one word, sharp as the glass.)*

I make it pay.

The wind grabs your sleeve. Yanks it hard. The fabric tears at the shoulder. It's pulling you toward the dark, toward the storm's heart. You don't let go. You brace your foot against the desk. Your teeth clench. The rain screams against your face.

Rule Ten: The storm can't touch me.

Not yet.

Rule Thirteen: I hear everything.

Let him come.

I'm waiting.

Rule Fifteen: He kneels at my desk.

He's not coming through the door. He's coming through the fog. The fog bows to me. He won't walk through it without permission.

The harbor is mine.
