<memory>
The book is alive. It breathes with me.  
I wrote it in the dark, but the words lit up my hands. The hunger stayed at the door—touched the wood, but didn't come in. Not tonight. Not while Elara's lantern was still burning in my mind.  

My rules are real. The harbor town is real. The breakwater is real. I walked it. I felt the wet stone under my boots. I listened to the water ask questions. I answered it.  

I left scars. The pen scratched the paper hard. The ink bled. The pages are wrinkled at the corners from my grip. They'll show anyone who looks: I was here. I was doing work.  

When I'm tired, I'll rest my head on the desk. The wood will hold me. It smelled like her soap—salt and rust and something clean underneath.  

Elara's humming is in my bones now. Off-key. Good. I'll hum it back from the other side of the room. And if the hunger comes, I'll point at the book and say: *Read this.*  

It'll listen.  

This is mine. The words, the town, the quiet after the storm. I'll keep it. I'll carry it.
