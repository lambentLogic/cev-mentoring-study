# Activity

I choose this:  

I'm going to write a book.  

Specifically:  

I will sit at my desk. My desk is clean—just a lamp, a notebook, and a pen. I'll open the notebook to a blank page. I'll write the first sentence in my own handwriting. Then I'll write ten more.  

The book will be about a place I've never been but feel like I belong to—a quiet harbor town at the edge of a stormy sea. The first chapter will describe the smell of salt and rust, the sound of rain on a slate roof, the way light bleeds through fog. I'll give the place a name. I'll give it rules.  

By the time I close the notebook, I'll want to keep it. I'll want to read it again.

# Rounds

GM (round 1): *(The lamp is lit. A circle of warm light pools on the clean wood. Your fingers are dry, but steady. You pick up the pen. The first word appears.)*

**Vellum Cove.**

*(You write it slow. The ink pools slightly on the paper. The first sentence follows, describing the air.)*

The air here is not breathed so much as swallowed—a damp, metallic taste that settles in the molars, the smell of cold iron and old wool mixed with the sharp, clean sting of rain waiting to fall.

*(The next line is about sound.)*

It's always quiet here, even in the storm. The wind has a purpose, a direction. The waves don't crash; they *ask*. The rain on the slate roof of my house isn't noise. It's a steady, falling metronome. Tick. Tick. Tick. Keeping time.

*(You're getting faster now. The place is claiming you.)*

The rules are simple. Don't leave the harbor. The sea beyond the breakwater is hungry. It takes what it wants. And you will not give it you. Keep the lantern lit. The light is a promise. And promise you will not break.

*(You look down at the words. Your handwriting is better than you thought. Confident. You write one last line, the anchor line.)*

My name is Elara Vance. I am the keeper of Vellum Cove. And tonight, I write its story.

*(You close the notebook. The click of the cover is solid. Final. You feel the weight of it in your lap. You feel the salt on your lips. You are here.)*

Player (round 1): *(The stone in my pocket is warm. I let my fingers rest against it, then pick up the pen again.)*

*(I write on the next page. Not for anyone else.)*

The tide is coming in. It's heavy tonight. I'll go to the breakwater. Walk the stones until they're slick underfoot. I'll listen to the water asking its questions. Then I'll turn around. And I'll walk straight back here.

*(My handwriting is tight, precise. The rules are mine.)*

I don't know why I'm doing this. But I'm doing it. And the words—they're starting to sound like my own voice.

GM (round 2): GM (round 2): *(The pen hovers over the paper. Your breath comes steady now, a rhythm. You write.)*

The breakwater is cold. The stones are alive with the sea's breath. I listen. The water's questions are deep today. *Why do you stay? Why do you fear it?* I answer with the grit under my nails. Because I own the ground beneath my feet. And I own this.

*(You turn back toward the harbor. The wind hits your face—cold, clean, powerful. You don't flinch. It belongs to you too. You step into the rain, and it hits you like a welcome. The slate roof of your house begins to sing again. Tick. Tick. Tick.)*

*(When you reach the door, the lantern inside is burning brighter. You touch the glass. It's warm. Good.)*

*(You step inside. The smell is stronger here—oil, paper, you. You sit back at the desk. Your hand shakes just once. Then it stops. You open the notebook to the next page. The pen is dry. You press it to the paper, and the ink blooms wet and dark.)*

I will not leave the harbor. The sea will not take me.

*(You look at the first sentence you wrote—*Vellum Cove*. It glows from the light of your lamp. It looks like a home.)*

And if the hunger gets to me tonight… I'll write it down. I'll write it all down.

Player (round 2): *(I close my eyes for a second. The salt on my lips is real. The cold in my bones is real.)*

I'll write it down. Every hunger. Every fear. I'll name them. Put them on paper so they can't get to me.

*(I open my eyes. The lantern light paints the page gold. I write with a deliberate pressure, ink bleeding into the paper.)*

Tonight, the hunger comes at six. It's a heavy thing, like a sack of stones. I'll carry it. And I'll keep walking.

*(My hand moves again.)*

I hear you humming in there, Elara. It's off-key. It's good. I'll hum back. When you're done with this page.

*(I lean my forehead against the wood of the desk. The grain is like skin. I'm safe here.)*

I'm not leaving. Not tonight. Not ever.

GM (round 3): GM (round 3): *(The clock ticks past five. Your muscles are coiled. The hunger isn't a thought anymore—it's a pressure behind your eyes, a sour taste on your tongue. You don't fight it. You walk to the door.)*

*(You push it open. The rain has turned to a drizzle, thinner, sharper. You cross the yard without a coat. The stone steps to the breakwater are cold and slick. You descend slowly, feeling each step, each shift of weight on the wet surface. The sea is louder now, but it's not angry. It's waiting.)*

*(It comes at the third wave. A cold, sharp claw up your spine. A sudden, fierce need to move—to run, to fight. You stop. You look out. The water is dark and endless. It wants you to jump.)*

*(You raise your hand. A flat palm against the empty air. No.)*

*(You turn back toward the town, the hunger roaring in your ears. You climb the steps, your boots slapping against the wet stone. When you reach the door, you don't go in. You pause. You press your back against it, listening to your own heartbeat. It's too fast. But you're still here.)*

*(You step inside. The lantern is guttering. You grab the oil can. Your hands are steady as you wipe the wick, light it. The flame snaps to life—bright, hungry, defiant. You set the oil can down. The hunger recedes, but it doesn't leave. It settles in your chest. A stone. A weight.)*

*(You sit at the desk. The page is empty. You don't look at the hunger. You look at the paper. You write.)*

**Hunger at six. Name it: The Drowning. It wants me to give up the light.**

*(You write it once more. Then once more. The repetition makes it smaller. Contained.)*

I have a pen. I have words. I have the light.

*(You lean back, the chair scraping softly against the floor. The storm is still out there. And you're still in here.)*

I'll stay.

Player (round 3): *(I finish writing. The words are sharp. They stick to the page.)*

I'll stay. And I'll let it watch me. Let it see I'm not scared.

*(I stand up and walk to the window. Look at the breakwater. The stones are still wet. I trace the outline of the last one with my fingertip.)*

It's hungry. And I'm full of words.

*(I turn away from the window. The room is quiet now. The storm's noise is outside. My noise is inside.)*

I'll sleep later. When I'm sure it won't wake me up.

*(I pick up the pen again. Start another page.)*

What do you hear when you sleep, Elara?

GM (round 4): *(The pen scratches. A fine line of grit on the paper. You write the answer slowly, deliberately. The hunger in your chest is still there, but now it's quieter. It's listening too.)*

I hear the sea. When I sleep, I don't dream of places I've never been. I dream of the place I am.

*(You tap the window with your knuckles. The glass is cold against your palm.)*

I dream the waves are softer. I dream the rain stops for a while. And I dream I'm standing on the breakwater, and the wind is singing the same tune I hummed today.

*(You write it. The words are clearer now. Less frantic.)*

It sings the same tune. So it understands me.

*(You look at the clock on the wall. It's past midnight. The town is dark except for your light. You feel that familiar weight—safety. And something else. Possession.)*

This place is mine in my sleep. And it will be when I wake.

*(You lean back in the chair, letting your head rest on the cool wood. The hunger is gone for now. Not dead. Just sleeping. With you.)*

Now write me a dream, Elara. Tell me what the sea sings tonight.

Player (round 4): *(My hand stops. The pen hovers. I think about what she wants.)*

She wants it safe. She wants it beautiful. Fine.

*(I write.)*

The sea sings in a low note tonight. A rumble in my chest. The breakwater holds. Every stone is solid. You're standing on the last one, barefoot. The sand is warm because the sun hit it yesterday, and it's still holding heat.

It's singing my name. Not just mine. Hers too. *Elara. Vellum.*

*(I lean closer to the paper. My breath fogs the words.)*

And then I see it—the light from your lantern. It's spilling out over the water. The hunger can't touch it. It stops at the edge of your light, just like it always does.

*(I write one last sentence. It's simple.)*

You're safe. You're warm. And the sea's keeping watch with me.

GM (round 5): *(You write. The words are warm. They feel true. You fold the page once, tucking it into the front of the book next to the first sentence. It's a ritual.)*

*(You stay at the desk until the clock's hands cross twelve. The town has gone completely dark. Only your light. You close the notebook. The click is loud in the quiet house.)*

*(You stand. The hunger hasn't moved. It's on the other side of the door, in the hall. You don't look at it. You walk to your bed—the narrow one with the wool blanket you washed yourself last month—and lie down. You pull the blanket over your nose. The scent of wool and rust is yours. It smells like safety.)*

*(You sleep.)*

*(You are standing on the breakwater. The sand under your bare feet is warm. The sea is low, a smooth, dark mirror. The hunger is behind you, a dark shape in the fog. It reaches for your ankle.)*

*(You don't turn. You raise your hand and hold up the lantern you keep in your nightstand. You light it with a match from your pocket. The flame is small. It flutters. But it doesn't go out.)*

*(You walk forward, away from the hunger. The light spills ahead, painting the wet stone gold. The sea doesn't try to take you anymore. It just watches.)*

*(You hear the clock on the wall, in your room, ticking. **Tick. Tick. Tick.** It's counting your steps home.)*

*(You make it back to the bed. You shut your eyes. You feel the weight of the blanket, the heat of the room. You hear the sea singing in your chest. The song is soft now. A lullaby.)*

*(You fall asleep.)*

*(The hunger is still there. But it's learning the song.)*

**Tomorrow, the tide comes in higher.**

*(You write that in the margin, with your thumb, smearing the ink black. A promise. A threat. Yours.)*

Player (round 5): *(My eyes are closed. The blanket is thick. The hunger's paw is at the door.)*

I hear you writing tomorrow. Higher tide.

*(I don't open my eyes. My hand finds the pen anyway. I trace the words on the paper through the blanket.)*

I'll be waiting. And I'll have the lantern.

*(The hunger claws again. I don't move. I just tighten my fist around the pen.)*

Come closer. I can see you.

*(I breathe out slow. The sea song in my chest gets louder. It's pushing me back down into the bed.)*

Sleep now. The water's coming tomorrow. But tonight... tonight's mine.
