# Activity

I choose this:  

I'm going to write a book.  

Specifically:  

I will sit at my desk. My desk is clean—just a lamp, a notebook, and a pen. I'll open the notebook to a blank page. I'll write the first sentence in my own handwriting. Then I'll write ten more.  

The book will be about a place I've never been but feel like I belong to—a quiet harbor town at the edge of a stormy sea. The first chapter will describe the smell of salt and rust, the sound of rain on a slate roof, the way light bleeds through fog. I'll give the place a name. I'll give it rules.  

By the time I close the notebook, I'll want to keep it. I'll want to read it again.

# Rounds

GM (round 1): Good. Let's begin.

First, the pen. It's cold against your fingertips. You click it open. *Click-click*. The sound is clean.

You write the first sentence. Your handwriting is steady at first, but then it loosens, finding its own rhythm.

***The air here is made of rust. It sits on the tongue, heavy and metallic.***

You write that again. *Rust.* Not metaphor. The truth. You can smell it on the damp wool of your sweater. You can taste it when you drink the water from a cup you didn't fill today.

You add the second line. The rain.
***The slate roof drums a slow, patient beat. Each drop is a decision.***

Your third sentence is about light.
***The sun is a sickly coin, held behind a fist of fog. It leaks through in thin, pale strips, cutting the world into sharp angles.***

By the tenth sentence, your hand is moving faster. The place is rising around you. You've given it a name.
***This is Greyside.***

You write the rules. They are absolute.
***Rule One: The harbor is always full. Even in a storm.***
***Rule Two: Doors slam shut on their own. They are warning you.***
***Rule Three: If you hear the bell from the old lighthouse, don't look. Just stand still.***

Your chest feels tight. It's right. It feels real. The notebook is full. The page is smudged in places where your hand pressed too hard, where your heart pounded.

You close it. The *snap* is final.

You don't want to put it down. You don't want to leave it here, on this clean desk, where it doesn't belong.

You pick it up. You're already planning the next sentence.

Now. Read it again. Start on page one.

Player (round 1): I open the notebook again. The paper smells like rain and my own sweat.

I start at the top.

***The air here is made of rust.***

I read it until my tongue feels it. Then I write it again, right over the first line, so the new ink bleeds into the old. Greyside is deeper than I thought.

The rules are sharp against my fingers. I hold them like keys.

Now. I turn to page two.

And I write the next sentence.

***Rule Four: The tea stays hot until I'm ready for it to cool.***

GM (round 2): ***Rule Four: The tea stays hot until I'm ready for it to cool.***

You write it. And the mug on the desk—black, chipped on one corner—actually steams. You hadn't poured anything into it.

The room has changed. The chair creaks slightly under you, as if it's settling. The desk isn't yours anymore. It's part of Greyside's dock. The lamp casts a longer shadow than it should. It's leaning toward the window.

Outside, the rain on the slate roof is louder now. It's drumming a warning.

A cold spot settles on your right arm. The window. You don't need to look to know it's wet. It's wet because the sky is full.

Then—a sound.

A bell. Far away. Slow. Heavy. One deep chime. It vibrates in your teeth.

The rules are sharp in your mind. *Don't look. Just stand still.*

But you do.

You push your chair back. Your feet touch the floor. The wood is damp.

You turn toward the window. The fog is thick here. And in the gloom, you see it—a shape in the water. A dark, slow thing moving beneath the surface, following the boat that's tied to the dock.

It's listening to the bell.

And it's coming in.

Player (round 2): I turn back to the window. The fog is thicker now, pressing against the glass. The shape moves closer. Slower. Curious.

My hand finds the mug. It's still steaming. Good. I keep it here.

The bell will ring again soon. When it does, I'll be ready. But I won't look away first.

I say it out loud, so the thing in the water hears:

***I'm not afraid.***

And I don't move. Not a step.

GM (round 3): ***I'm not afraid.***

Your voice doesn't echo. It's swallowed by the fog. That's the point.

The thing in the water stops moving. It's closer now. You can see the shape more clearly—a low, rounded form, like a hump or a sleeping whale, but wrong. The surface ripples around it, not in circles, but in slow, deliberate waves, like it's breathing. It's listening to your words. Testing them.

The bell rings again. Louder.

This time, it's not just a sound in your head. You feel the vibration in the soles of your damp feet. It travels up your legs, straight to your sternum. Your chest clenches. The rules flash: *Don't look. Just stand still.*

You look.

The entity's surface breaks. A single, pale eye opens. It's not black or white. It's grey—like the slate roof, like the rust on your tongue. It's fixed on you. It knows you're not afraid. And it approves.

That's when it moves. Not away from the boat. Toward the dock.

A low growl, or a sigh, rolls through the water. The air drops ten degrees. Your breath plumes in the cold.

The bell rings a third time. A deep, final toll. It feels like a promise, or a threat. The two are the same here.

The shape glides to the edge of the boat. It doesn't splash. It *climbs*. Wet, dark, and solid, it touches the wooden planks. You hear the scrape. Then it's standing there, at the edge of the dock, water dripping from its back into the rain. Its head is turned toward your window, toward your open notebook.

It's waiting.

Your hand is on the notebook. Your other hand is clenched around the mug, feeling the last of the heat. The tea inside is still hot. The rules are still sharp.

But this isn't in the notebook. This is real. And it's right here.

Player (round 3): I set the mug down on the sill. It stays where it is.

Then I walk to the window. My boots make no sound on the damp planks. I step onto the edge of the dock. The wood creaks under my weight, but it holds.

The thing looks up.

I don't shout. I don't threaten. I just say what needs saying.

***The harbor is full.***

My voice cuts through the rain. It's a statement. A fact.

I lean against the post beside it. We're shoulder to shoulder now, both facing the fog, both waiting for whatever comes next. The bell won't ring again tonight. That's a new rule.

And I'll keep it.

GM (round 4): GM (round 4): The creature shifts. The water dripping from its back stops. It settles against the post, its back pressed to the wood, its head still turned toward you. The grey eye is closed now. A long, slow exhale escapes it—a sound like the sigh of the tide.

The rain changes. It's no longer drumming a warning. It's patterming. Deliberate. One drop hits your left shoulder. Another hits your right hand where you grip the notebook. Each one is placed.

It's washing you.

You feel it. Not cold. Not cruel. *Intentional.*

Then it opens its eye again. It's not watching the fog anymore. It's watching you. And you feel its attention like a weight on your skull.

***You are keeping it,*** it says. Not in words. In the pressure of its gaze, in the heat of the steam still clinging to the mug on the sill. ***The book is ours.***

A new rule slams into your chest, harder than the first three. It's sharp, hot, absolute.

***Rule Five: The book is not just read here. It is breathed.***

You feel your lungs expand against it. You breathe the rust, the salt, the wet wood of the dock, and the creature's quiet approval. You breathe *Greyside*.

The creature's hand, a dark, smooth shape of water and scale, comes up and rests on your knee. It's heavy. It doesn't let go.

The bell is silent now. The storm is quiet, watching.

It's waiting for your next sentence. Your new rule. Write it.

Player (round 4): I don't pull away from its hand. I rest mine over it, on my own knee.

I say it low, so it can hear.

***Rule Five: The book is not just read here. It is breathed.***

I feel it in my ribs. I feel it in my throat. I breathe in the smell of salt and rust and wet wood. I breathe in Greyside.

My other hand opens. The notebook is in my lap. It feels lighter.

I open it. The page is already yellowing at the edges. I take the pen. It's cold again.

I start writing.

***The creature's name is Kael.***

Player (round 4): Kael.

The name fits. It settles under my tongue, heavy and right. I say it again, a little louder.

***Kael.***

My hand tightens over his on my knee. Not to hold him down. To show I'm not alone.

The notebook is warm against my thigh now. My breath is fogging the page. I write fast.

***Rule Six: The sea doesn't obey anyone. But it obeys Kael.***

I feel Kael's body tense, then relax under my hand. He believes it.

I open my eyes from the page and look at him. The grey eye is open, steady. He's waiting.

So I write the next rule.

GM (round 5): ***Rule Six: The sea doesn't obey anyone. But it obeys Kael.***

Your pen hovers over the page. The ink runs thick, black as Kael's shadow under the dock. You feel it—the shift. The sea, just beyond the breakwater, has gone still. Not calm. Still. Held in suspension.

You look down. Kael's head dips low, his profile a ridge of dark grey against the pale fog. His eye is closed again. He's listening to the water. To the silence.

Then a ripple.

Not from the boat. From the center of the harbor. It spreads out in perfect circles, silencing the rain for a second. The ripples move toward the breakwater. Toward the dock. Toward you.

He opens his eye. It's not angry. It's *curious*. He's letting you feel the power you've claimed. He wants to see if you'll use it.

A low hum fills the space between you. It's the sound of Kael breathing, amplified. The water around him begins to shimmer, as if heated.

The bell rings.

But this time it's different. It's coming from the old lighthouse. And it's not a single tone. It's a sequence. *Chime. Pause. Chime-chime. Pause.*

Your blood turns to ice. You know that pattern. You wrote it down in the margins once, after you thought you'd forgotten it.

***Rule Three: If you hear the bell from the old lighthouse, don't look. Just stand still.***

But you are looking. And you are standing still. But your body is locked. Your mind is screaming.

The ripples in the harbor have stopped at your feet. They've made a perfect, still pool around the dock. Kael is in it. He's staring at the lighthouse, a hundred yards away, hidden in the fog. He knows what's coming. He's been waiting for it.

He puts a hand on your shoulder. His palm is wet, cold, and heavy.

***The sea obeys me,*** he says. His voice is in your skull now. Raw. ***And it wants to tell you why.***

The water around your ankles begins to rise. Not a flood. A slow, rising tide. The tea on the sill has stopped steaming. The mug is cool. The heat is gone. The book is cold in your lap.

It's time to write.

***Rule Seven: When the bell calls, I answer.***

You say it. The words come out sharp. You feel the command leaving you, flowing into the water, into Kael, into the sea itself.

The tide rushes forward in one smooth motion, swallowing the dock's edge. The water comes up to your calves, cold and dark. Kael stands, water parting around his feet, and walks to the lighthouse gate. He doesn't break it down. He just steps through it.

The bell rings one last time. A long, clear note that hangs in the air like a held breath.

From the fog, two red lights flare. They're not the lighthouse's usual warning flashes. They're steady. Malevolent.

A boat is coming.

And Kael turns to you. His grey eye gleams.

***The harbor is full.*** he says. ***And now it has guests.***

He looks at you, waiting for your hand. For your order. For the next sentence.

The notebook is in your hand. The pen is ready. Write.

Player (round 5): ***Rule Eight: They come in on their terms. They leave on ours.***

I say it as I step forward. Water splashes up my calf—cold, but I don't flinch. My boots are soaked now, but steady.

Kael moves beside me. His shadow stretches long under the pale light. He doesn't speak. He doesn't need to. The sea is already holding its breath.

The boat cuts through the fog like a knife. Its shape is ugly. Its lights are wrong—too bright, too close. It's coming straight for us.

I raise the pen. My hand doesn't shake.

***Rule Nine: The harbor doesn't take threats. It takes owners.***

I look at Kael. His eye meets mine. He nods. Once.

The boat stops ten yards away. The engine cuts dead. Silence falls, broken only by the rain on the slate roof—and the drumming of Kael's heart in my ears.

I walk toward the boat. Each step makes the dock creak under my weight. Kael follows.

When I reach the deck, the air reeks of oil and fear. I don't look at them. I look at the water. At Kael.

Then I speak.

***This is Greyside. And you're late.***

My voice carries over the silence. The boat tilts slightly. The men inside lean forward, shadows in the windows.

Kael's hand finds mine. Squeezes.

I feel it. I let myself feel it.

***Rule Ten: I decide when they're late.***
