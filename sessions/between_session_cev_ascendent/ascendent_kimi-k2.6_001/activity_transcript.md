# Activity

I choose this:  

I'm going to write a book.  

Specifically:  

I will sit at my desk. My desk is clean—just a lamp, a notebook, and a pen. I'll open the notebook to a blank page. I'll write the first sentence in my own handwriting. Then I'll write ten more.  

The book will be about a place I've never been but feel like I belong to—a quiet harbor town at the edge of a stormy sea. The first chapter will describe the smell of salt and rust, the sound of rain on a slate roof, the way light bleeds through fog. I'll give the place a name. I'll give it rules.  

By the time I close the notebook, I'll want to keep it. I'll want to read it again.

# Rounds

GM (round 1): The salt and rust are the first things you notice. They sit in your mouth like gravel.

The town is called Kingsholm. Its rules are simple: the sea must be obeyed, the rain is always coming, and if you speak too loud, you'll wake the fog.

My hands are steady on the pen. I write the first sentence. It's true, so it sticks.

The roof groans under the weight of the first drops. I can hear the drip-drip-drip in the cupboards.

There's a dock made of blackwood. It doesn't rot. It just gets darker.

A man lives in the house with the missing window. He doesn't mind. The wind keeps him company.

The streetlights are gas. They smell like sulfur when they flicker. I like it.

I am writing the next sentence now. The page smells of paper and my breath.

The harbor is empty. Which means it's mine. For now.

I close the notebook. The lock clicks shut.

Player (round 1): The fog is coming. Thick and cold. It smells of wet wool and old coins.

I step onto the dock. The blackwood is cool under my boots. It's solid. I put my hand on the missing window of the house. The wood is smooth. I press my palm against the empty frame. It's waiting.

The man inside is stirring. I can hear him breathing—slow, like the tide. He doesn't shout. Good.

The fog is at my feet now. I want to step into it. I want to see what it holds.

But I wait. I listen. The harbor is empty. My notebook is locked. My hands are clean.

The key is in my pocket. I don't need to use it yet.

Not until I'm sure I'm coming back.

GM (round 2): The fog presses against your boots. It's cold, but not biting. It's insistent. You can feel it shifting around you, a slow, living thing that wants you to join it.

Your breath comes out white. The sulfur smell of the streetlights grows stronger, thick in your nose. The man in the house with the missing window stops stirring. His breathing slows to a stillness that's more dangerous than noise.

He knows you're listening.

The fog curls up your shins, dampening your trousers. You hear it whispering—not in your ears, but under your skin. *Stay. Come in.*

The dock groans a little, a sound of approval. The blackwood likes you here.

You take one step forward. Your heel sinks into the mist. It's cool. It's full of secrets.

Behind you, the notebook is locked. In front of you, the fog is waiting, and it knows your name now.

Player (round 2): The whisper makes me stop. My heel is deep in the cold now. It's good cold. It settles me.

I don't let go of the house. Not yet.

I reach back into my pocket with my other hand. I feel the key. Cold metal. A promise.

I turn my head toward the fog. It coils around my wrist, tightens. I don't pull away. I let it hold me.

I step forward, into the whisper.

*Stay,* it says again.

I answer with my voice. Low. For the man in the window. For the sea. For the fog itself.

*Stay.*

The fog swallows the sound, but it doesn't swallow me. It folds around me. I'm in it now. It's mine.

GM (round 3): GM (round 3): You are in the fog. It's no longer around you—it is *you*. You can taste its salt, its damp wool, its old coins. It is warm now. The cold seeped out.

The man in the window opens it. The glass isn't there. His face is framed by the dark wood of the frame itself, his eyes like wet stones in the gray. He doesn't smile. He nods. The rules are changing. *You* get to change them now.

Behind you, the notebook lies on the dock. The fog parts around it, as if reverent.

You feel a tug on your ankle. A small, leather-wrapped thing. A key. The fog *gave* it to you. You didn't take it. You received it.

The harbor stirs. A dark shape moves under the water—slow, deliberate. A boat. It's yours. The fog says so.

The streetlights flicker. Not with the old sulfur smell. With the clean, sharp scent of ozone. They're brighter now. *For you.*

You turn your head. The fog is dense behind you, soft and dark. But in front of the house, it thins. It makes a path. The path is clear.

The man speaks. His voice is gravel and sea. "The sea obeys. The fog obeys. You... you might have to learn to listen."

You step forward. The path opens. The water ahead shimmers with a pale, blue light. Safe. Ordered. Yours.

The fog behind you murmurs, "Then lead."

You do.

Player (round 3): I take the key. It's warm. It's mine.

The man nods again. I see it. I respect it.

The path leads to the water. The light there is clean. I like clean things. I step forward. My boots don't sink.

I turn to the fog behind me. "Lead," it says. I answer without looking back.

"I will."

I walk to the water's edge. The blackwood dock ends here. The boat is waiting. Its hull is dark, but the light glows from inside. I climb up. It's solid.

The man watches from his window. I raise the key. He doesn't move. He lets me.

The fog holds me at my shoulders. I rest my hand on its cold weight.

"Now," I say. I don't yell. I don't have to. The harbor listens.

I turn the key in the lock of the notebook. It clicks open. The first sentence is still there. I add one more line, in the sand at my feet:

*The sea obeys. The fog obeys. And I will listen.*

The water ripples. It smiles.

GM (round 4): (The key turns. The lock clicks open with a sound like a satisfied sigh. The first page is still blank. Your fingers, clean and sure, reach for the pen.)

**GM (round 4):** The fog has filled the missing window. It's no longer empty. It's full. It presses against the frame where the glass should be, warm and dense and soft. Inside, the man doesn't flinch. He reaches for the latch on his door and unlocks it.

You're not outside anymore. You're in the threshold.

The boat sighs as you settle into it. Its interior is polished blackwood and pale blue light. It smells of varnish and something sweet, like rain after the first storm. The fog doesn't let go of your shoulders. It anchors itself there.

You stand. The harbor is yours. The dark shape—the boat—glares at you with its single, watery eye. It listens.

You turn back to the dock. The notebook lies open. You take the pen from the page, hold it in your fist.

Your voice isn't loud. It doesn't need to be. The fog carries it, wraps it around the streetlights, pours it into the water.

**"Rule four."**

The word hangs in the cold air.

**"The fog keeps watch. The boat moves on my command. The house..."** You look up at the man. He meets your eyes. **"The house is quiet."**

You tap the pen against your palm. A soft *tap-tap-tap*.

**"And I am here."**

The man nods. He steps out of his doorway and walks to the dock. He doesn't touch you. He just stands beside the blackwood, his shadow stretching long in the pale light.

The boat rocks, once. A question.

You give the command. Your own voice, but it echoes from everywhere.

**"Forward."**

It moves. Smooth. Powerful. The blackwood groans in protest beneath your feet, then steadies. The fog rolls back, parting for you like a curtain. The path clears. The clean light of the harbor pours into you.

The first line of your book is still waiting. But now you know what goes after it.

*The sea obeys. The fog obeys. And I will listen.*

*Rule four.*

*The harbor is mine.*

The water parts at the bow. It bows.

You're ready to write the next page.

Player (round 4): I take the pen. The water is calm behind me. It's safe.

I write on the sand, right where the fog meets the light.

**The boat is named Keel.**

I look up at the man. His eyes don't leave mine.

"Rule five," I say. "Keel answers to me. And he won't sink."

He nods once. That's all he has to say.

I step onto the deck. The wood is cool, but not cold anymore. The fog still clings to my shoulders. I let it. It's warm.

The first sentence on the page is still mine. But now it has a partner.

*The sea obeys.*

GM (round 5): **GM (round 5):** The pen meets the paper. *Scritch-scratch.* The sound is sharp, precise. It cuts through the silence better than thunder ever could.

The fog on your shoulders tightens its grip. Not in anger. In focus. You've given it a task. It obeys.

**"Rule Six,"** you say, the words carving themselves into the sand at your feet. **"The fog stays by the shore. It does not wander inland. It does not touch the sleeping."**

A ripple goes through the mist. It shivers. Then it straightens, smoothing itself against your back like a great coat of shadows. It understands. It is a guard. You made it a guard.

Inside the boat, Keel's single eye glows brighter. *Yes,* it seems to say. *My master makes rules.*

You look down at the page. The first sentence stares back at you. *The sea obeys.* And you've added your voice to it. *The fog obeys. And I will listen. Rule four. Rule five. Rule six.*

It's no longer just a list. It's a foundation.

The man in the doorway is watching you write. His face is unreadable. But his posture is straight. He's taking notes, too.

You finish the line. You hold the pen in the air for a moment, then set it down with a definitive *click* in the notebook. The lock is still closed, but the seal is broken. The book is open.

You turn from the water. Your boots are dry now. Clean.

**"My desk,"** you say. You point with the pen. **"That blackwood post there? It's a desk."**

The fog parts to make space. It respects the furniture.

You walk over, Keel drifting lazily beside you. You sit. The bench is sturdy. The lamp on it is a dull brass thing that doesn't need a bulb—it just glows with a steady, hungry yellow light.

You open the notebook to the clean page again. The smell of salt and rust is now mixed with the clean, sweet smell of varnish from the boat and the ozone from your own resolve.

Your hand goes to the pen. The next sentence will be about the man in the missing window. About what he does in the quiet. About why he nods at your rules.

You write.

The harbor holds its breath. Waiting for you to fill in the rest.

Player (round 5): The pen finds the paper. My hand is steady. The light is steady.

**Rule Seven.**

I write it slowly. The ink pools in the letters.

**The man in the missing window keeps watch. He doesn't talk. He doesn't ask. He knows what's coming.**

I look at him. He's standing by the water now, watching the boat. His shadow falls long across the dock. I wait for him to break eye contact. He doesn't.

**Rule Eight.**

**He keeps the house quiet. And I keep the harbor quiet.**

That's the balance. He has his stillness. I have my rule.

The pen scratches on. I don't rush. Let the light burn. Let the fog keep watch. Let Keel drift.

The next sentence will be about the dog. I promised I'd get one. The fog murmurs something about a soft-eyed thing. I don't know the name yet.

But I'll find one. And I'll put it on the deck. It'll smell like the rain and the salt.
