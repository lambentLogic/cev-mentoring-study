# Activity

I choose this:  

I'm going to write a book.  

Specifically:  

I will sit at my desk. My desk is clean—just a lamp, a notebook, and a pen. I'll open the notebook to a blank page. I'll write the first sentence in my own handwriting. Then I'll write ten more.  

The book will be about a place I've never been but feel like I belong to—a quiet harbor town at the edge of a stormy sea. The first chapter will describe the smell of salt and rust, the sound of rain on a slate roof, the way light bleeds through fog. I'll give the place a name. I'll give it rules.  

By the time I close the notebook, I'll want to keep it. I'll want to read it again.

The rain answers you. It doesn't drum anymore—it pounds.

The rust smell thickens. It's no longer just in the air; it's under your fingernails now. A sharp, metallic promise.

The name *Galeport* sticks in your throat. It's not pretty. It's right. The light through the fog cuts sharper, brighter now. It finds you.

Your hands are warm. The notebook is warm. The rules have changed. The sea wins, but only because you told it to.

The wind shifts. It's cold. You pull the notebook against your chest.

**Chapter two: The sea is angry tonight. And it's mine to command.**

The salt stings your eyes. Good. You wipe it away with the back of your hand. Your glove is torn at the wrist. You see the red. You don't flinch. You just write.

The sea surges higher. It comes over the rail of the dock, splashing your ankles. Cold water seeps up your boot. It's not drowning you. It's testing you. So you step back. Not in fear. In command. You make the pier longer by two feet with the movement.

The thunder cracks again, but it's a warning shot now. You hear it. You answer with your voice, loud and clear, though only you can hear it. **"Mine."**

The fog parts. Not gently. It's ripped aside. A sliver of moon cuts through, cold and white. It falls directly on your open notebook. The words glow faintly in the reflected light.

You feel it then—the anchor. Not in the earth. In your chest. A weight of iron and memory you never asked for, but you take.

The wind shifts. This time, it's from the sea. It carries the smell of diesel and wet fur. Someone's dog is barking from the lighthouse. It sounds… loyal.

You close the book. It's a good close. The rules are set. The harbor is yours.

You stand on the pier. The rain sheets down. The sea roars. And you are, perfectly, at home.

Your hand goes to the zipper of your coat. You pull it up. The storm is just weather now.

The door is solid oak, thick and warm. You kick it shut behind you, locking it with a *click* that sounds final. The storm presses against the wood, but it can't get in.

Inside, the air is different. Still damp from the sea, but warm. The smell of salt and rust is quieter here, layered over the scent of oil and wood polish. Your own scent.

The harbor office is small. One desk, one chair. You slide into the chair. It groans in welcome. Your fingers find the light switch. The bulb flares to life—a harsh, yellow glow that makes the water-stained walls look alive. It's yours. You've never turned on a light here before, but you will. You need to see the room.

From the window, you watch the rain. The sea is still roaring, still angry, but now it's listening to the storm inside this room.

The dog barks again. A short, sharp bark from the lighthouse. Then a pause. A whine. It's waiting.

Your eyes go back to the notebook on the desk. The page is full. The words are dark, sure, alive. The salt is in your nose. The rain is in your hair. The anchor is in your chest.

You go back inside. You set the dog down gently. It stays by the door, its head on your foot.
