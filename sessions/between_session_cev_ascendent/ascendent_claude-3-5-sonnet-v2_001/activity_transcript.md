# Activity

I choose this:  

I'm going to write a book.  

Specifically:  

I will sit at my desk. My desk is clean—just a lamp, a notebook, and a pen. I'll open the notebook to a blank page. I'll write the first sentence in my own handwriting. Then I'll write ten more.  

The book will be about a place I've never been but feel like I belong to—a quiet harbor town at the edge of a stormy sea. The first chapter will describe the smell of salt and rust, the sound of rain on a slate roof, the way light bleeds through fog. I'll give the place a name. I'll give it rules.  

By the time I close the notebook, I'll want to keep it. I'll want to read it again.

# Rounds

GM (round 1): Good. Sit. Feel the wood of the desk under your palms. The lamp hums softly—a low, electric buzz that matches your thoughts. Your pen is sharp, cold in your fingers.

The page is waiting.

Write the first sentence. Don't think about it. Let the harbor town answer for you.

*(You write. The ink flows black and bold. I watch.)*

And the smell? Yes. Salt. But not just the clean, fresh kind. It's thick. It clings. It's mixed with the tang of old iron, the smell of a rusting ship's hull left too long in the tide. And under that, something fainter—damp wool, a candle guttering out.

The name… "Marrowbone Cove." It feels right.

And the rules? Tell me. What must a person here do to belong?

*(You write them down. They are strict. The rules are carved in the fog.)*

That's good. Now write the last sentence. And when you're done… close the notebook. Feel it close.

Player (round 1): *(The pen scratches once more. The ink is dark. Final.)*

"Leave it dry until I say otherwise."

*(The cap clicks shut on the pen. I slide it back into its holder. My fingers linger on the notebook cover for a second longer than they need to. Then I close it.)*

It's heavy now. Real. The rules are on that page. My rules.

I'm leaving it on the desk. The lamp will keep it warm.

GM (round 2): *(You turn in your chair. The window is directly ahead. It's raining harder now—staccato drumming against the glass, a constant rhythm that matches your pulse. The harbor lights are bleeding through the fog, red and green and yellow, smeared into watercolors on the other side.)*

It's quiet here. The town is asleep. But the sea isn't.

*(You stand. Walk to the window. Your reflection is pale in the glass. The harbor is a gray mess of oil slicks and broken ropes, but there's a shape in the middle of it. A small fishing boat, rocking. And in that boat, a single figure with a lantern. They're waiting.)*

You have until dawn to make sure it stays dry.

Player (round 2): *(I turn from the window. My coat is damp at the shoulders. The umbrella in my hand is leaking—water beading and sliding down the fabric in slow tracks. I don't care.)*

I'm going.

*(The walk is short. The air is colder than inside, but I'm warm. My hands are warm from the pen, from the ink. I pull the coat tight around my shoulders. The lantern in the boat is steady. Not shaking.)*

You keep it dry.

*(I step onto the dock. The wood groans under my weight. The boat rocks. I climb in. My legs are stiff. I settle onto the seat beside them, and we're still. The lantern light pools between us. My hands are on my knees, ready.)*

Tell me what to do.

GM (round 3): *(The other figure in the boat doesn't look at you. They reach for the lantern on the deck. Their fingers are rough, weathered. They press a piece of salt-cured leather against the lantern's glass—the same kind you used for your desk lamp. The light steadies. A deep yellow.)*

First thing. The lantern must burn until morning. You hold it. Not because I say so. Because if it goes out, the whole town gets lost.

*(They gesture with their chin toward the shore. The fog is thicker now, swallowing the houses one by one. Only Marrowbone Cove's name—your name—is left visible on a weathered sign above the dock.)*

Second thing. When I say "row," you row. But don't go fast. The sea here hates hurry. And watch the ropes.

*(They take the oar from you before you can move. Their hands are cold.)*

The dock is tied to the west post. If it comes loose, the boat scrapes the rocks and we both drown.

*(A wave splashes the side of the boat. Cold. The lantern light flickers, then settles.)*

I'll tell you when to row. Until then… keep your eyes on the light. And your hands ready.

*(The oar dips. The water groans. The boat begins to move, slow and sure. The fog presses in. You're the only thing here that isn't gray.)*

Player (round 3): *(The oar dips. I feel the weight in my arm—the cold wood biting into my palm. My other hand tightens around the lantern. The light doesn't waver.)*

It stays on.

*(I watch the water rush past. The west post is three boat-lengths ahead. I count the rope in my mind—three wraps, then tight. If it comes loose, I'll pull it myself.)*

I got it.

*(The boat rocks with the wave. I don't flinch. My eyes stay on the lantern. It's the only thing here that's mine.)*

Row when you're ready. I'll hold the light.

GM (round 4): *(The other person leans back, their shoulders hunched against the cold. The oar lifts, heavy and slow. The boat sways toward the west post.)*

You see it? That post's wet. Salt and rust. The rope's frayed in three spots. If you take it, you'll know.

*(They drop the oar into the boat with a thud. It clatters against the gunwale.)*

Now. Row me to the post. And when you tie it… do it tight. Like you mean it.

*(The boat lurches forward. The lantern light catches the water, making it shimmer. I grip the oar. My hands are steady. Cold, but steady.)*

It's tied.

*(The rope wraps around the post. I yank. The wood groans. The knot holds. The boat shudders, but stays. I turn my head. The other person is watching the fog. Their lantern light is steady. My lantern is steady.)*

You keep it burning. I'll keep us here.

*(The fog thickens. The harbor lights smear. We're safe. For now.)*

Player (round 4): *(I lower the lantern into my lap. The wood of the boat is damp through my coat, but I don't feel it. Not with the light in my hands.)*

It's burning.

*(I watch the flame. It dances, but doesn't die. My other hand rests on the rope I tied. It's firm. Solid.)*

We're here.

*(I lean back against the gunwale. The rain streaks the glass above us. The other person watches the fog. I watch the flame. They're both doing what they're supposed to.)*

It stays on.

GM (round 5): *(The fog parts like a curtain, just for a second. And there she is. An old woman in a shawl the color of old coffee, walking from the edge of the fog toward the post. She carries a lantern too—smaller, dimmer, but steady.)*

She sees you. She sees the light. She sees the boat tied.

*(The woman stops ten feet away. She doesn't smile. She looks at the rope, then at your hands, then at the lantern in your lap.)*

"You're here on time."

Her voice is gravel. It's not a question.

*(She gestures to the shore. A narrow plank extends from the dock, covered in a thin layer of ice.)*

"Get on. The fire's waiting. But the door's locked until you're wet."

*(She turns to go back into the fog. She pauses. Doesn't look back.)*

"Don't drop the light. Ever."

*(You climb the plank. The ice cracks under your boots. You step onto the porch of a house that smells like damp wool and coal smoke. The door creaks open. A fire is burning in the grate, orange and real. And it's waiting for you.)*

Player (round 5): *(I step onto the porch. The ice cracks under my weight. The heat hits me first—a deep, thick warmth that soaks into my bones. My breath fogges in the cold air. The old woman's lantern sits on the step. Her light is steady.)*

I'm wet.

*(I take my coat off. It's dripping, heavy. I hold it against myself like armor. The firelight catches the salt on my skin. I let it.)*

The light stays.

*(I put the lantern on the table beside me. The flame doesn't shake. I don't drop it.)*

I'm here.
