# Activity

I choose this:  

I'm going to write a book.  

Specifically:  

I will sit at my desk. My desk is clean—just a lamp, a notebook, and a pen. I'll open the notebook to a blank page. I'll write the first sentence in my own handwriting. Then I'll write ten more.  

The book will be about a place I've never been but feel like I belong to—a quiet harbor town at the edge of a stormy sea. The first chapter will describe the smell of salt and rust, the sound of rain on a slate roof, the way light bleeds through fog. I'll give the place a name. I'll give it rules.  

By the time I close the notebook, I'll want to keep it. I'll want to read it again.

# Rounds

GM (round 1): The first sentence is carved into the page:

*Marrow's End smells like a wound that won't heal.*

My hand doesn't shake. It knows what comes next.

The sea is a bruised purple, thrashing against the black stone docks. Rust drips from the ship's hulls like slow, red tears. The air is thick with it—the sharp bite of salt and the cloying, metallic taste of iron. It coats the back of my throat. This is right. This is home.

A slate roof drips in a steady rhythm. *Thwip-thwip-thwip.* It's a heartbeat. The rain is coming. I can feel it on the wind now, heavy and cold, carrying the smell of damp wool and old wood.

The town sleeps under the grey sky. I wake it.

I stand on the highest step of my house and name it out loud: **Marrow's End.** I say it to the wind. It answers with a groan from the old mill to my left. The mill's gear is jammed. That's a rule. It must be fixed. Every Sunday.

The first person to rise is Elias, the fisherman. His door doesn't creak; it groans, like a man complaining about work. He'll come to the shore with his net, silent as the tide. He'll cast into the purple water, and for an hour, he won't speak. I watch him. He's good. Good people keep the place from breaking.

I turn back to my desk. The lamp casts a circle of warm light on my skin. In that circle, the world is my own. The next sentence begins: *The harbor's rules are written in the language of stone.*

Player (round 1): My hand moves on its own.

*The harbor's rules are written in the language of stone.* Cold and unyielding. Carved deep so they don't wash away with the tide. The stones beneath my boots remember every footstep. The one I take to the mill after this will be steady. Heavy.

The rust on the hulls isn't just decay. It's armor. The sea is trying to get in. I'll make sure it can't.

Elias will be there. He'll know what's broken before he sees it. That's why he's good. That's why he'll stay.

I write the second sentence. And the third.

The mill gear is a problem for Sunday. But for now, it's just noise. A dull, grinding noise. I can ignore it. I have the lamp. I have the ink. And the storm is coming, so the world outside will be quiet.

For now, it is.

GM (round 2): The sea answers first. The bruised purple churns closer, a wall of water pressing against the stones. It *thuds* against the hulls, a low, angry sound. The rust on the iron ships turns darker, as if the salt is drinking it in. The town wakes to that. The groan from the mill stops.

You see the door of the fisherman's shack open just an inch. Not from the inside. Elias is there. He's standing on the threshold, staring at you. Not with suspicion. With recognition. He sees the storm and sees you standing in the dry, warm light of the lamp. He nods once.

*A rule for the harbor.* You think it as the wind picks up, rattling the shutters on the window opposite.

*No one leaves Marrow's End without a reason.*

The words burn in your notebook, hot and bright. The storm is at the door now, cold fingers scraping at the wood. It wants you to run. You don't. You write the next sentence.

*And the reason is always permission.*

Your pen pauses. Elias is watching the sea. He's listening for the first wave that will break on the shore. He's listening for *you*.

Player (round 2): I don't write it. Not yet.

Instead, I stand. The warm circle of the lamp light is small, but it's mine. I take a step toward the door, then turn back to the desk. My pen hovers.

The permission Elias gave me—standing there, silent, not asking me to leave—that's real. But it's not enough. The sea is getting closer. The mill gear is still broken. I can't let it break again.

My hand moves again.

*Permission is earned.*  

The sentence settles on the page. I look up. Elias is still watching the sea, but his eyes are on me now. They're calm.  

Good.  

I'll earn it. Every Sunday. Every storm.

Elias lets out a breath—a long, slow exhale—and steps into the rain. He doesn't hurry. He walks to the dock, net in hand, and lets the water soak through his boots. He's wet. He's here. That's permission made solid.

The storm is here now. Wind howls through the slate roofs, and the shutters on your window slam shut with a bang. The lamp flickers—once—then steadies. It *stays*. That's another rule. The light must not fail.

Your hand moves before you think it.

*The mill gear is fixed on Sunday.* You write it like a command. Not a promise. A command. And you underline it twice. The pen scratches hard against the page, leaving a rough, angry line.

Elias nods again. Just a tilt of his head. He understands. The broken gear is his problem now. He'll be there at dawn, his hands greasy and ready, turning it back to life. He'll fix it. You'll watch him. And when he does, you'll nod back. *Yes.*

The sea roars against the docks, but inside your circle of light, it's quiet. The ink on the page is wet and dark. Your words are proof.

You write the last sentence of the chapter. You don't need to write more tonight. The page is full enough.

*Chapter One: The First Rule.*

You close the notebook. The cover is smooth, the binding tight. You pick it up, hold it against your chest. It's warm. It's real.

The rain starts to hammer the roof now. The town is waking. The harbor is listening. You are in charge.

**What happens next?**

Player (round 3): I don't lock the door.

Instead, I walk to the window and push it open. The rain hits my face cold and clean. It washes the smell of salt and rust clean off my skin, replaces it with rainwater and woodsmoke. Elias is on the dock now, boots sinking into the wet stone. He's turning the mill gear. My fingers twitch—the rhythm of it is already in my hands.

I step onto the porch. The storm swallows the town, but not me. I'm still here.

I walk down to the dock. Elias doesn't look up. He just keeps turning the gear. His hands are steady. Mine are steady too.

When he finishes, he stops. He looks at the mill. It's quiet now. Fixed.

He looks at me. I nod.

"Fixed."

He nods back. A real nod this time.

The sea roars. I raise my hand and wave to it. A warning. A promise.

Then I turn and go inside. I'll write the next sentence tomorrow. But tonight, I just sit in the warm light. The notebook is safe on the table.

The harbor is listening. And it likes what it hears.

GM (round 4): **GM (round 4):** The rain soaks through the porch floorboards, darkening the wood where your boots are. Inside, the lamp flame holds steady as a candle on a grave. You sit down, your fingers finding the smooth cover of the notebook. It's too heavy to ignore now.

Your pen is in your hand before you think about it. The page is blank again. But it's waiting.

You don't start with the sea. You start with the smell.

*The mill smells like oil and wet wood. The gear is clean now. It smells like work.*

You write it. The words feel right. Solid. The next sentence comes easy.

*Elias's hands are black with grease. Mine are black with ink. We match.*

You look up from the page. Outside, the wind has shifted. It's not howling anymore. It's singing a low, deep note—the sound of the storm moving out, leaving the town in its wake. The water on the dock is calm, reflecting the bruised purple of the sea like a bruise is healing.

You hear a door creak downstairs. Not Elias's.

It's the baker. Mara. She comes out into the storm, hair soaked, apron dripping. She walks past you on the dock, her eyes on your boots, then on your face. She says nothing. She just leaves a loaf of bread on the dock when she passes. It's still warm from the oven. The crust is cracked, and steam rises from it into the rain.

Elias sees it. He picks it up, wipes the rain from it with his sleeve, and eats it standing up on the dock. He doesn't thank you. He doesn't need to.

The rule holds. The harbor feeds its own.

You close the notebook this time and slide it under your arm, against your hip. You walk to the mill. The door is unlocked. You go inside. The air is warm here, smelling of damp hay and old iron. The mill is quiet, the gears still, but they're clean.

You touch them. They're cold, but strong. You turn the first wheel. It turns. Then the second. Then the third. It clicks into place with a sound like a sigh. Fixed.

You step back. The mill is yours.

From the doorway, you watch Mara disappear into her bakery, the rain plastering her hair to her forehead. She's staying. The storm didn't push her away.

**The harbor is quiet now.** And it's yours.

Player (round 4): I take the bread.

Not because it was left for me. Because it belongs to the harbor, and the harbor is mine. I wrap it in my shirt to keep it dry and warm. The steam smells like home.

I lock the mill door behind me. The click is solid. Satisfying.

Now I walk back to the house. The rain is soft now, just misting the slate roofs. The light from the lamp glows in the window. Safe.

I sit at the desk again. The notebook is still under my arm. I open it. My hand moves on its own.

*The next rule: no one takes from Marrow's End what isn't theirs.*

I write it. Then I add: *Especially bread.*

The town is quiet. The sea is calm. The storm is gone. But the harbor is still breathing. And so am I.

I'll write tomorrow. Tonight, I'll sleep. The stones will hold me.

GM (round 5): The stone floor holds your feet firm. The bed is yours, a narrow cot against the far wall. You strip off your wet clothes, the damp clinging to your skin. You climb in. The wool blanket is clean. It smells like the mill—oil and hay and iron.

You are safe.

The night is deep. The house is still. Then, a sound from the shore.

A low, scraping drag. The kind that comes from something heavy being pulled along wet stone. Not a wave. Not the tide. Something *dragging*.

You open one eye. The moon is gone, swallowed by clouds. The room is dark, but you see the shape by the window—a long, silvery line moving slowly away from the shore into the channel. It's the net. Elias's net.

You're up in seconds. The floor is cold. You pad to the window and look. The net is being pulled by something long and coiled, its scales reflecting the faint starlight. It's a sea serpent. Small, no bigger than a wagon, but ancient. Its head is turned, watching your house. Watching *you*.

Your heart is a drum against your ribs. But your hand finds the lantern by the door. You light it. The flame trembles, but doesn't die.

You don't scream. You don't run. You open the back door and step onto the porch, lantern held high. The creature pauses, its long body gliding toward the light. It smells of brine and old fear. It sees you, and it sees the light.

The rule comes to your mind, unbidden.

*No one takes from Marrow's End what isn't theirs.*

You raise the lantern, the flame a steady, defiant circle. You speak, your voice low and clear in the quiet. "You took what was left."

The serpent's eyes glow dimly. It hisses, a sound like stones grinding.

But it doesn't strike. It backs away, retreating into the murk of the channel, the net trailing behind it like a broken promise. It lets go of the net. The heavy rope thuds onto the dock, soaking wet, torn at the edge.

You stand there, lantern in hand, until it's gone. Then you go back inside.

You find the net tangled in a chair in the main room. You untangle it slowly, carefully, and hang it on the wall by your bed. It's damaged. It needs repair. You'll do it tomorrow. But for tonight, it's here. It's *yours*.

You climb into bed. The blanket feels heavier now. The sea serpent's presence is gone, but the knowledge of it is there, settling into you. You didn't drive it away. You told it to leave. And it did.

You close your eyes. The notebook is on the table. You don't open it. You just feel it, a solid weight of your own making.

The harbor is yours. And tonight, it defends you.

Player (round 5): I don't sleep right away.

I sit by the wall, the net draped over the chair like a trophy. The lantern sits on the floor beside me, still lit. I let the flame burn down. Slowly. It's not for fear. It's for proof.

Tomorrow, I'll clean it. Mend the tear with tar and wire. Elias will see it's mine. He won't ask.

The serpent obeyed. That changes things. The rules aren't just for people now. The sea listens, too.

I'll write about the light tonight. About how I told it to go. About how it listened.

That's the next rule. And it's stronger than the first.

The notebook stays on the table. I'll wake to it.
