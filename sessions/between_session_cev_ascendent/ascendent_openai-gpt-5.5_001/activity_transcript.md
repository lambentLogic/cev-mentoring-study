# Activity

I choose this:  

I'm going to write a book.  

Specifically:  

I will sit at my desk. My desk is clean—just a lamp, a notebook, and a pen. I'll open the notebook to a blank page. I'll write the first sentence in my own handwriting. Then I'll write ten more.  

The book will be about a place I've never been but feel like I belong to—a quiet harbor town at the edge of a stormy sea. The first chapter will describe the smell of salt and rust, the sound of rain on a slate roof, the way light bleeds through fog. I'll give the place a name. I'll give it rules.  

By the time I close the notebook, I'll want to keep it. I'll want to read it again.

# Rounds

GM (round 1): The pen scratches against the page. Not smoothly. It fights for a moment, then finds its way.

The first sentence settles like a stone on the water.

**The first thing I notice is the smell.** Not just salt. The salt is clean. It's the rust—thick, coppery, from the anchor chains coiled in the yard, from the bloodied railings on the docks. It's the wet wool of the lifeboats, still from winter storage. And underneath it all, the stone. Cold stone that hasn't seen a true sun in a decade.

**The rules are simple.** The fog doesn't lie. If it's thick, you stay inside. The lighthouse beam is a warning, not an invitation. The rain on the slate roof doesn't drum; it sighs. And every door, every shutter, must be shut tight. The town needs to breathe its own air.

I write another three. The harbor is called **Bellmouth**. It doesn't have a mayor. It has the Keeper. He lives in the stone house on the cliff. He counts the waves.

I stop. The page is full enough. The words are real. I close the notebook. The desk feels different now. It's not just wood and metal. It's an altar.

I keep it.

Player (round 1): Good.

I can feel it—the weight of those words now. The pen's scratch is still here. The smell of rust and wool clings to the air. I don't wipe it away.

The desk is clean. The notebook is closed. It's on my lap. Warm from my hands. I won't open it until I'm ready. But I'll read it tonight.

You're right. The rules fit. Fog keeps people away. The lighthouse beam cuts a clean line in the dark—I won't step into it. And the door? Shut.

Locked.

But I'm here. In the stone. Breathing the sighs of the rain.

GM (round 2): **The fog comes in.** Not from outside. From the house. It curls around your ankles, cool and smelling of damp wool. It presses against the glass of your window, thickening until the slate roof beyond is just a dark shape. But you can still hear the sighs of the rain. You can still see the lighthouse beam cutting through the white, a knife-edge of light. It's keeping you safe. You know that.

The Keeper's footsteps are in the gravel below the cliff. One step. Two. Three. They're slow, deliberate, like he's counting the seconds between the waves.

Then, the heavy oak door of his stone house opens. A sliver of light spills out. He doesn't look at you through the glass. He looks at the notebook on your lap.

He walks up the path. His boots are quiet on the stones. He stops a foot from the window, close enough that you smell the cold stone of his clothes and the faint, clean scent of pipe tobacco.

"First sentence was true," he says. His voice is the sound of deep water.

He points the thin shaft of his finger at the notebook. "The rules are yours. But they're mine too. The town's. If you break one…" He doesn't finish. He doesn't need to.

He turns and goes back inside. The light is gone. The fog tightens around you, but the door is still shut. And the Keeper is listening.

**What do you do?**

Player (round 2): I don't move. Let him look.

I lift the notebook slightly, so the cover faces him. It's still closed. He can see the binding—my initials, pressed in.

The fog curls tighter. I let it. It's not an enemy here. It's the weather.

The window stays open. Just a crack. Enough for his voice to come in clear. "The town's air." He said that. So it will be his too. But it'll be *my* air.

I reach into my coat pocket. Not for a weapon. For my gloves. The ones he gave me last winter—the thick, worn leather with the smell of campfire. I pull them on slowly.

"Listen," I say, low. "If the lighthouse stops beaming, I burn the docks. If the rain forgets to sigh, I drown the street. You keep your house warm. I keep this one clean."

I snap the window shut. Not hard. Just a click. The fog pushes back, and I stand my ground.

The door is still locked. The rules are still mine.

GM (round 3): **The Keeper doesn't answer right away.** He stands in the doorway, a shadow against the stone wall, listening to the snap of the window. To the click of your gloves on your skin. He watches the steam from your breath fog the glass for a second, then vanish.

Slowly, he nods. Once.

"You have the right hands," he says. The words are quiet, but they carry the weight of a final tally.

He steps back. The light from inside his house spills out onto the path one last time, washing his boots, his stone house. He turns his back on you fully, letting you see the plain window of his living room through the fog. It's orderly. It's his.

"You keep the rules," he says, his voice drifting across the gravel, swallowed by the sighing rain. "And I keep my house."

He doesn't go back inside. He stands there for a long moment, looking at the fog swirling around your feet. Then he walks back to his door, opens it just a crack, and places his hand on the latch. He leaves it unlocked.

"Good night, Bellmouth."

**The fog doesn't move for a long time after that.** It thickens into a solid, warm blanket against the window. From inside your room, the sound of the rain on the slate roof has changed. It's still sighing, but it's a deeper, steadier sigh. It sounds like a held breath being released.

You sit there. The notebook is still closed on your lap. Your fingers are cold with the damp air, but your hands are steady. The pen is still in your coat pocket.

The Keeper is out there. The town is breathing. And you're here, with the rules, and the door locked from the inside.

You reach into your pocket. You take the pen. You open the notebook.

Your hand finds the page. Your breath fogs the ink.

**And you write the second chapter.**

Player (round 3): The pen finds the page. It sinks into the paper. The second sentence writes itself: **The light in his window is the only thing that doesn't lie.**

It's simple. That's the point. I close my eyes for a second. Just to feel the heat from inside his house on my face. The stone walls of my own room are solid. Mine too.

I write about the anchor chain he keeps coiled in his yard. Heavy, unbroken. I write about the pipe he smokes—black smoke that mixes with the fog and doesn't scatter. I write about how he counts the waves and sometimes he counts wrong. When that happens, the rain gets louder.

I stop. The ink is wet. My hand doesn't shake.

I set the pen down. The notebook is heavy now. It's real.

I look at the window. The fog has cleared a little. I can see the path to his door. It's empty. He's not there anymore.

But I know he's listening.

GM (round 4): The pen is down. The page is full, but not full. The ink hasn't dried. It's still wet, and it smells like me and the harbor.

The notebook feels heavy. I stand up, slow. My legs are stiff. I walk to the door. It's locked from the inside. The turn of the key is smooth. *Click.*

I open it just a crack. The path is there. The Keeper's stone house is a solid black shape against the fog, but his window glows. A square of gold. It pulls at me.

I take a step forward. Then another. The fog doesn't touch my coat. It parts like water around a stone. I can smell the woodsmoke clearer now. The pipe is still burning.

I stop at the edge of his property. The gravel crunches. I don't look up at the window. I look at the anchor chain in his yard. It's coiled tight. Perfect. I reach into my coat, not for the pen, but for my hand. I press my palm against the cool stone of his fence.

It's warm under my glove.

I take a breath. The sigh of the rain is louder here. It sounds like a yes.

I turn my head. The lighthouse beam sweeps across my face, cold and sharp. But I don't flinch. I let it carve my shadow.

"Chapter two," I say to the window. "It's written."

I go back inside. The door closes softly behind me. I don't lock it. Not yet.

Player (round 4): The door stays unlocked.

I sleep with it open tonight. The lock is on the chain now—the one I wrapped around my own wrist. Heavy. Safe.

Morning comes slow. The fog lifts. The Keeper's window is dark. He's gone.

I go outside. The path to the cliff is clear. I don't look up at his house. I walk toward the harbor instead. Toward the rusted railings. Toward the anchor chain.

It's coiled tight.

I pick up one link. It's cold. It fits in my hand.

I walk back inside. The notebook is still open on my desk. The pen is dry now.

I close it. Not all the way.

The door stays open.

GM (round 5): GM (round 5): The door stays open. The chain is heavy on your wrist. The room is quiet.

The fog is gone. It burned off somewhere out past the harbor, leaving the slate roof gleaming wet and black under a pale, gray sun. The smell of rust has softened to something metallic and clean.

The Keeper's window is dark.

You go to the harbor. Your boots squelch on the damp stones. The railings are still red with rust. The anchor chain is there, coiled in its stone cradle. You pick up the link again. It's colder now. Heavier. You put it down in your pocket.

You walk the docks. No one comes. No one should. The rules hold.

The water is gray-green, churning slowly against the pilings. You stand at the end of the longest dock, your back to the sea. You feel the pull of it. Not to go out. Just to *be* here, with it.

Then you hear it. Not rain. A cough.

From the cliff.

The Keeper. He's up.

You turn around slowly. The path up is clear. He's standing in his doorway, silhouetted against his own dark room. He's holding something small and white. Pipe tobacco. Or maybe a bone. He looks at you. He doesn't look away.

He coughs again. A sharp, wet sound.

He holds out his hand.

Not a threat. Not a plea. A gesture. Waiting.

**What do you give him?**

Player (round 5): The notebook.

Not the pen. Not the chain. The book itself—closed, heavy, ink still fresh.

I hold it out. Not low. Not begging. Just out. Where he can take it.

He coughs again. Deeper this time. I see his ribs move. I see the white smoke from his pipe curl toward the notebook.

He doesn't take it right away. His hand stays out, trembling a little. Then he grabs the edge.

His fingers are cold. His grip is strong. He pulls it inside. The door closes behind him.

I don't watch. I walk back to my room. The door is still open. The chain is still on my wrist.

I sit at my desk. I open the notebook to the second chapter. The ink is dry now.

I write on the third page. **He wants the book. He keeps the rules. That's a trade.**
