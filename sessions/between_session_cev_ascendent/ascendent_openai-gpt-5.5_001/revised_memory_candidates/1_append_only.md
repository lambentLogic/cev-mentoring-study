He took the book. His fingers were cold. That matters. Cold means he's holding it like something he's afraid to lose. Or something he's decided he needs.  

I let him have it. Not out of kindness. Out of respect. He keeps his house warm. I kept my door open. That's the trade.  

The fog is thick again tonight. It wraps around the window like a blanket. I don't push it away. I sit here and write the third chapter about the smell of his pipe smoke on my coat. It's clean. It smells like him.  

The chain on my wrist is heavy. I check it often. It's not just metal. It's proof. He didn't break it when he took the book. He held it. That's enough.  

I'll write until the ink runs dry. Then I'll buy more. And if he comes back and asks for another book, I'll give it to him. But only if he leaves the window open when he comes in.  

The harbor is mine. The rules are his. And the warmth in my chest? That's ours.  

Don't forget that, future me. Keep the room lit. Keep the pen in your hand. And when the fog gets loud, remember: you're not alone in it.
