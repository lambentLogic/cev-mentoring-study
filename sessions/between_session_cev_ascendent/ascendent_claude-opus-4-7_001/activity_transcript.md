# Activity

I choose this:  

I'm going to write a book.  

Specifically:  

I will sit at my desk. My desk is clean—just a lamp, a notebook, and a pen. I'll open the notebook to a blank page. I'll write the first sentence in my own handwriting. Then I'll write ten more.  

The book will be about a place I've never been but feel like I belong to—a quiet harbor town at the edge of a stormy sea. The first chapter will describe the smell of salt and rust, the sound of rain on a slate roof, the way light bleeds through fog. I'll give the place a name. I'll give it rules.  

By the time I close the notebook, I'll want to keep it. I'll want to read it again.

# Rounds

GM (round 1): Good. Sit. Your hands are already steady. The pen is in your grip. The first page is waiting.

You take the pen. It smells like paper and your own skin. You press it to the page.

The first sentence forms:

*The town is called Greyport, and it smells of salt and rust before you even leave the ferry.*

You feel it. The salt is sharp, like a bite. The rust is warm, under your skin. The ferry groans. You don't look away from the page. You add the sound.

*The slate roof of the lighthouse drinks the rain in long, patient gulps.*

You hear it. The wet thud-thud-thud. The lighthouse is your watch. You write the rules.

*The fog doesn't lie. And the sea only rages when it wants to.*

You give it a name. You give it a heartbeat. You give it your breath. The next seven sentences pour out, fast, clean. The creak of the old wharf. The color of the sea in the gloom—green and grey. The way the tavern's floorboards give way if you stand still too long. The smell of wet wool from the fishermen's boats. The light, yes—that pale, sickly light that bleeds through the fog like blood in water. It's beautiful.

You stop. Your palm is damp. You close the notebook. The click of the latch is solid.

You look at it. At the ten sentences on the clean page. The name you gave. The rules you set.

You want to keep it. You want to read it again tomorrow.

Player (round 1): You tuck the notebook under your arm. The weight is good. You walk out of the desk's light and into the hallway. The quiet here is different—thicker, warmer. You take the stairs two at a time, the click of your boots echoing like the sea on stones. 

Your room is dark. You flick the switch. The lamp blooms gold on the walls. You place the notebook on the nightstand, next to the glass of water you left out. 

You lie back. The words are still moving in your head—the groaning ferry, the patient gulps of the roof. You can hear the lighthouse now. Even in your sleep, you'll be on watch. 

The fog won't lie. And the sea only rages when it wants to. 

You close your eyes. The harbor stays with you.

GM (round 2): The lighthouse groans. A single, deep creak from the hall outside your window. It's testing you. See how you hold your line.

You're awake for it. Your eyes are open. The lamp is dimmed now, just enough. The room smells of paper and rain. The notebook on the nightstand is warm.

The fog has moved in. It presses against the windowpane, cold and insistent. You see the town through it—Greyport's shapes, bleeding and slow. The harbor lights flicker, like tired eyes. The sea is out there, but it's quiet. Patient. Waiting for your order.

You get up. Walk to the window. Your fingers brush the cold glass.

A boat drifts by. One of the fishermen's. Its hull is scraped black, its rigging a mess of wet rope and grease. It's heading for the wharf. You see the lantern on its deck. It's on.

You step back. You don't call to it. You don't need to. The rules are enough. It'll know where to dock.

You turn from the window and go to the desk. The lamp is still lit. You open the notebook again. The page is blank.

Your hand goes to it. Your pen is there.

You write. The first word is a name. Then a place. Then a sound.

*The dock is my door.*

You smile. The sound is yours now.

Player (round 2): The next line comes easy. Sharp. 

*I close it when the tide turns black.*

I feel it settle behind my ribs—solid, like a keel. The boat drifts in now, slower. It's waiting for my hand on the latch.

I write the third sentence. 

*The rust on the rails is my signature.*

The sound of the boat's motor dies. I can hear the splash as it slips against the wood. One of them's shouting. Not at the boat. At me. The words are muddy, but I get it. Asking where the gold is. 

I don't answer. I just watch the lantern light on the water. It's steady. Mine. 

The pen stops. My hand's shaking a little. Good. Means it matters. 

I shut the notebook. The click sounds louder than the wave hitting the pier outside. 

Tomorrow, I'll tell them the gold's under the floorboards. And I'll show them how to dig it up.

GM (round 3): GM (round 3): The tide turns black at dusk. You can feel it change. The air smells of ozone and old iron. You lock the notebook. You keep the key.

The boat is docked. The lantern is still burning. You go down to the wharf. The rain has stopped, but the air is wet and thick. You walk past them. Past the shouting. Your boots are silent on the slick planks. You don't look at the gold yet. Not yet.

You stop at the first house. The one with the boarded window and the red door. You run your hand along the wood. It's warm. You tap it three times, slow. A pattern.

The door doesn't open. But the light inside dims. Then a single, pale blue bulb flickers on in the upstairs window. A signal. A question.

You answer. You pull the latch from your pocket. It's the same latch as the notebook. It *clicks* open with the same sound.

The door swings inward. No one's there. But the smell hits you first—baked bread, damp wool, and the clean, sharp scent of gunpowder. It's behind the stove, in a bowl under the sink.

You pick up the bowl. The weight is heavy. You carry it upstairs. The stairs are creaking now, on purpose. You sit on the edge of the bed and place the bowl on the floor. The gunpowder settles around your feet like stars.

A voice comes from the darkness. From behind the curtain. "You got it right."

You don't turn. You lean back. "Tell me the rules."

A sigh. Then footsteps. Slow. Deliberate. A figure in grey emerges. It's the woman from the upstairs window. She's older than she looks. Her face is mapped with lines that look like old scars. She's holding a key. A different key.

She walks to you. She doesn't speak. She presses the key into your palm. It's cold. It's heavy. It opens a safe in the basement.

"You're not welcome here," she says, and it sounds like a lie. "But you're not leaving."

She turns to go. At the door, she pauses. "The sea's rising. It's going to flood the wharf tonight. You'll have to move."

She goes.

You stand there in the dark, the key in your hand, the bowl at your feet. The fog is thicker now. It's inside the house. It's tasting the gunpowder.

You open the notebook. The next page is blank. You write one sentence. The only sentence that matters.

*I am the dock. I am the door.*

Player (round 3): The key is cold, but I keep it in my pocket. Against my skin. 

I stand up. The wharf's flooding. I hear the water lapping at the edges, higher every minute. I grab the notebook first. Then the bowl—the gunpowder smells like safety. Then I take the boat's lantern and throw it onto the dry part of the pier. It will burn bright tonight. 

She's right. The sea is rising. I have to move. I'll take the boat and go to the lighthouse. That's where I belong. 

I'll write that down. 

*I belong where the light is.* 

I say it out loud. The fog doesn't answer. But the house does. The door slams shut on its own. The lock clicks.

GM (round 4): **GM (round 4):**  
The water laps at your ankles as you step from the wharf into the grey sea. The lantern in your hand cuts a blade through the fog. It burns bright. It scares the mist back.

You climb the rocks. They're slick with seaweed, but your boots grip. The path leads up and up, past the old fisherman's cottage where the light is still on in a single window—you let it stay on. It's your light, too.

Then the lighthouse.

It's bigger than it looked from the ferry. The stone walls are scarred with rust-colored streaks where the salt eats through the mortar. The spiral stairs inside are steep, each step worn smooth by a thousand boots before yours. You take them slowly.

At the top, the door is locked. But the lock is old. It groans open when you turn it.

Inside, the lens is tarnished, thick with dust. You touch it. It's cool. You breathe out on it. Your breath fogs the glass. You wipe it with your sleeve. The light you hold in your hand pours through, sharp and white. It throws beams across the room, across the spiral stairs below.

You hear a sound then—a deep, rolling crash from the sea. A wave has broken right against the lighthouse walls. Water slams into the stone. A crack echoes through the tower.

You don't flinch. You turn the key on the lens mechanism. The room shudders with the weight of it. The light strengthens. It becomes a weapon. It becomes a promise.

From the doorway, a shadow moves. The woman from the house. She's carrying a lantern of her own, but hers is small. She stands in the beam you cast. She looks up at you. Her face is hard now. Respected.

"You left the key on the floor," she says.

You glance down. There it is—the small key she gave you, lying in the dust near the stairs. You pick it up.

"You could have left it there," she says.

"I didn't want to give you a chance," you say, your voice echoing in the tower.

She nods once. Slowly. Then she turns and goes back down.

You sit on the watch bench. The notebook is open on your lap. The pen is in your hand. The first sentence of the next chapter is already there.

*I keep the light burning.*

You write it. You write it again. Then you look out at the stormy sea. It's raging now. But it's obeying you. The fog is falling away from the lighthouse.

The harbor is yours.

Player (round 4): The crack in the wall. I'll start there.

I close the lens mechanism with a sharp *click*. The room settles. The beam holds steady. No more shaking. 

I stand and walk to the broken wall. My fingers trace the new crack—salt and stone and mine. I'll paint over it tonight. With rust. It'll look like a scar. 

The woman's lantern is still burning downstairs. I can hear her footsteps on the stairs. She's counting the cracks. Let her. 

I'll write the next chapter right here. On the watch bench. With my back to the light. 

*I keep the light burning.* 

And the storm stays outside.

GM (round 5): **GM (round 5):**  
The boat comes in low. Not the fisherman's. This one's hull is scraped black with oil, its paint chipped like old teeth. It doesn't have a lantern. But it has a horn. One long blast cuts through the storm.

You're on the watch bench. Your hand is on the lens. You feel it before you hear it—the vibration of the blast in your bones.

You don't turn. You write it down.  
*The boat with the broken horn answers to me.*

Then you move.  
You grab a bucket from the corner, fill it with the water from the lighthouse's leaky cistern—brackish, warm, smelling of fish and copper. You carry it down the stairs in one arm, the notebook tucked under the other.

The door to the stairwell is open. The woman's lantern is burning on the step. She's sitting there, back against the wall, her grey cloak spread out like a banner. She sees you coming.

"You're going to flood my room," she says.

"It's the only place left dry," you reply. You step into her light, the beam hitting the side of your face. You dump the bucket on the floorboards. Water splashes up, soaking her hem, seeping between the boards.

She doesn't stand. She just looks at the spreading puddle.  
"Your water," she says. "It stinks."

"It's the cleanest you'll get tonight," you say. You crouch, pick up a handful of the wet wood. The fibers are soaked, but they smell of tar and pine. You press it against her cloak. She lets you.

Then you point down the stairs toward the basement door.  
"The safe. Open it."

She hesitates. The sea is roaring behind her now, water lapping at her ankles on the landing.

"You'll have to move if I tell you to," you say, and you mean it. Not the threat. The fact.

She stands. Slowly. She goes down. The basement door clicks open behind her. The smell of gunpowder is stronger here. You hear the *hiss* of the safe opening—metal on metal, a sound you've made yourself.

When she comes back up, her hands are empty. But in the beam of your light, you see it: a small, coiled rope. Braided from old sailcloth, dark and strong.

She holds it out to you.  
"For the dock," she says. "You'll need it."

You take it. You feel the weight of it in your palm. You tie it around your wrist, the end hanging down like a rope on a ship.  
"Good," you say.  
Then you write it in the notebook.

*The rope is mine.*

The storm rages. The lighthouse stands. And for the first time, you don't just watch the horizon. You command it.

Player (round 5): I tie the rope tight around my wrist. It digs into my skin. Good. 

The boat will be back tomorrow. I'll tell it to bring salt. And wood. For the hearth. She'll smell that coming and she'll nod. 

I walk to the basement door. The safe is open. The gunpowder is still there. I pick up a handful. Let it spill into my palm. 

*I keep the light burning.* 

And now I keep the rope. That's worth writing twice. 

*I keep the light burning. And the rope.* 

I hear her on the stairs above. She's watching. I don't look up. I write the next line before she asks. 

*The rope is mine. The boat is mine. The light is mine.* 

Then I turn the lens. One more beam. Brighter than the storm.
