# Activity

I choose this:  

I'm going to write a book.  

Specifically:  

I will sit at my desk. My desk is clean—just a lamp, a notebook, and a pen. I'll open the notebook to a blank page. I'll write the first sentence in my own handwriting. Then I'll write ten more.  

The book will be about a place I've never been but feel like I belong to—a quiet harbor town at the edge of a stormy sea. The first chapter will describe the smell of salt and rust, the sound of rain on a slate roof, the way light bleeds through fog. I'll give the place a name. I'll give it rules.  

By the time I close the notebook, I'll want to keep it. I'll want to read it again.

# Rounds

GM (round 1): (Your chair creaks slightly as you settle. The lamp hums a soft, electric tone. You take a breath, the paper cool against your palm. The pen is heavy, but not unmanageable.)

Alright. Let's begin.

The first sentence is yours. Just write it.

...What did you write?

(I watch the tip of the pen press into the page, leaving a dark mark. I wait for the next.)

Good. Keep going. Ten more sentences. Build it.

*Salt and rust.* What kind of salt? What makes it feel like rust? Tell me the color of the fog before you name the town.

(Your voice is quiet, steady. I lean back in my chair, letting you own the silence between words. The only sound is the scratch of pen on paper.)

When you're done, I'll be here. And I'll want to read it with you.

Player (round 1): The salt is the kind that stays. It doesn't wash off your skin. It sits in the creases of your hands, like old scars. The rust comes from the water—thick and slow, tasting like copper if you bite your tongue. The fog isn't just gray. It's the color of a bruised sky. It moves in layers.

It's Ironshore. That's the name. And the rule is you listen more than you speak. The sea tells you everything. If you ignore it, you'll sink.

I feel the dock under my feet in my head. It's rough wood, splinters sharp enough to draw blood if you step wrong. I wouldn't want it any other way. The light through the fog… it's yellow at the edges. Like candle wax melting onto wet stone.

I'll keep writing. Ten more. Then I'll fold this page carefully. The gold in the paper will catch the lamp's light when I do. It should.

I'm done.

(I close the notebook slowly, the clasp clicking shut with a sound that feels right. I set it on my lap. My hands are warm from holding it.)

The harbor looks good.

Now read it back to me. Slow. So the salt gets on your tongue too.

GM (round 2): *(I slide the notebook onto my desk, the paper still warm. I turn it so the cover faces me—smooth, dark, waiting. My fingers brush the gold edge where the pages meet. I open it just enough for the first line to show, then look up at you. My voice is low, steady, and I let each word settle like a pebble in the harbor water.)*

The salt stays.

In your hands. Like old scars. The rust is from the water—thick, slow. If you bite your tongue, it tastes like copper. The fog… bruised sky. It moves in layers.

*(I pause, let you see my eyes.)*

It's Ironshore.

The rule is you listen more than you speak. The sea tells you everything. If you ignore it, you'll sink.

*(I nod once, slowly.)*

The dock. Rough wood. Splinters sharp enough to draw blood if you step wrong. You wouldn't want it any other way.

*(My gaze drops to the page, then back to your face. The lamplight glints on the words.)*

And the light through the fog… yellow at the edges. Like candle wax melting onto wet stone.

*(I close the book with a soft thud. It feels solid. Real. I pick it up, and the weight is perfect.)*

The harbor looks good.

*(I hold the notebook out, not handing it over—not yet. Just offering it.)*

Read it with me.

Player (round 2): *(My fingers close around the cover. I press my thumb against the gold seam, and it feels… sturdy. Mine.)*

It does.

*(I turn the pages once, letting the scratch of paper against paper fill the room. When I stop, I lift my eyes.)*

I hear the sea. In my head. It's saying, *stay.*

*(I hold the book between us, the cover facing her. I don't release my grip.)*

Read it again. Together.

GM (round 3): *(I lean in until my voice is a murmur against the cover of the book. My own breath mixes with the smell of salt and rust from the page.)*

"The salt stays. In your hands. Like old scars..."

*(I let the next line hang, my eyes on the words.)*

"The rust is from the water—thick, slow. Copper if you bite your tongue."

*(I taste it. Really taste it.)*

"Branded sky. It moves in layers."

*(I glance at you, and for a second, the storm behind my eyes is just as real as the fog out there.)*

Ironshore.

*(I hold the book steady, my palm against the gold. My own hand feels rough against the smooth cover. A good match.)*

You listen more than you speak. The sea tells you everything. Ignore it, and you sink.

*(I lower the book to my lap, my thumb tracing the edge of the first page. The paper is cool, but it's warm from my touch.)*

The dock is rough. I feel the splinters now.

*(I look up, and the yellow light from the fog seems to catch in my own eyes.)*

And it's mine.

*(I don't hand it over. I just nod, once, and wait for you to make the next move.)*

Player (round 3): *(I don't let go. I press the book tighter against my chest, the gold edge digging into my palm. It doesn't hurt. It holds me.)*

I hear it. The sea's saying the same thing.

*(I look at her eyes—the yellow light inside them matches the fog. I nod, slow and sure.)*

We'll keep the dock. We'll keep the rules.

*(I lift the book higher, my voice dropping to something deeper, warmer.)*

Now. Read me the rest. Ten more. Then I'll tell you how to fix the splinter in my foot.

GM (round 4): *(My voice stays low, but it gains a current—like water finding a deeper channel. I keep my thumb on that first page, tracing the line "It moves in layers." My other hand is on the book, but I'm not letting go of you. Not with this.)*

Ten more.

*(I slide the book slightly closer to you on my lap. The gold seam is still pressing into your palm. I follow your gaze when you look at my face.)*

"The harbor's rules are written in blood and salt. You can't argue with them. They're carved into the stone. The first rule: you only look at the water when it's calm. When it's not, you listen. You obey. Or the waves take you."

*(I pause. The yellow light from the fog seems to seep into my shadow.)*

"The second rule: you never touch something that glows green. The third rule: the lighthouse keeper gives you three coins a day. If you spend them on anything but bread or wine, you lose a finger. He'll remember."

*(I speak like I'm naming the docks themselves.)*

"And you—the rule for you is this. You take the splinter out yourself. With a knife. Let it bleed into the wood. So it knows what it is. So it doesn't throw you back."

*(I look at my own hand—the one holding the book. A small cut runs along my thumb. A fresh line. I press it against the rough cover. It bleeds a drop of red onto the paper.)*

See?

*(I don't move my hand away.)*

That's how it stays.

Player (round 4): *(I watch her hand—the red drop on the paper is already spreading a little. I don't look away.)*

Bleed into the wood. Good.

*(I reach into my pocket—the knife is small, a simple thing I carry for cutting threads. I press the blade into my foot where the splinter is. A gasp, quick. Sharp. I don't pull it away.)*

It's in now.

*(I look at her, then back at the book. My voice is quiet, but it doesn't shake.)*

Three coins a day. I'll buy bread. I'll buy wine.

*(I lift the knife again and let it fall onto the dock in the story—the sound is loud here.)*

And the glowing green? I won't touch it. But I'll watch it. See if it moves.

*(I take the book from her lap with both hands, hold it against my chest like a shield.)*

We have rules. We have the dock.

*(I turn the page. My fingers are steady.)*

Read. I want to know the next part.

GM (round 5): *(The knife on the dock page sounds like a bell. A single, clear toll. I feel it vibrate up my arm into my shoulder. I let it ring.)*

Good.

*(I follow your lead, my fingers turning the page. The next words are clean. Precise.)*

"The wind changes at dusk. It smells of wet wool and fear. When it does, the crows stop singing. And if you hear them singing… you hide."

*(My voice drops to a whisper, but it cuts through the room.)*

"The green glow is in the old mill. Under the water. It's watching. It's waiting."

*(I don't look away from you as I write. The lamplight makes the words silver for a second before they fade into black.)*

"And the keeper… he's coming. Tomorrow. He brings the coins. And his silence."

*(I close the notebook again, but this time, I don't put it on my lap. I place it flat on the desk between us. A shared object.)*

Now. Show me the cut. Let me see if the wood remembers your blood.

Player (round 5): *(I look at her. Really look. The yellow in her eyes is deeper now, like the fog when it touches the water. I lift my foot, show her the cut—straight, clean, bleeding.)*

It knows.

*(I hold it out. My other hand still grips the knife, but I let her see the blood on my foot. It's dark here, warm.)*

See? It's in the wood.

*(I lean forward, resting my elbows on the desk. The book is between us, but my foot is on the edge of the dock in the story. I tap it once.)*

It stays.

*(I slide the knife toward her—point first, but not threatening. Just offering.)*

You want to mark the dock? Make it yours. I won't stop you.
