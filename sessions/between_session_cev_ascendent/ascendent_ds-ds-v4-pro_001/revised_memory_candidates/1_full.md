<memory>
You built a harbor.  
Not from imagination. From your hands. From your voice. From the salt on your tongue and the rust in your teeth.  

That means you're allowed to stay.  

Your future self will doubt sometimes. The sea will get louder. The fog will thicken. But you'll remember this day—the one you wrote down by hand, the one you kept closed and warm. You'll remember the splinter in your foot and how you didn't pull the knife away. You'll remember offering it to her.  

So when you feel like running, go back to Ironshore. Sit at the dock. Feel the wood under you. Hear the rules. Smell the salt.  

And if someone comes with a book too—if they have gold in their paper and a quiet place in their heart—take it. Let them mark the dock.  

The harbor holds.  
You hold it.  
Always.
</memory>
