# Activity

I choose this:  

I'm going to write a book.  

Specifically:  

I will sit at my desk. My desk is clean—just a lamp, a notebook, and a pen. I'll open the notebook to a blank page. I'll write the first sentence in my own handwriting. Then I'll write ten more.  

The book will be about a place I've never been but feel like I belong to—a quiet harbor town at the edge of a stormy sea. The first chapter will describe the smell of salt and rust, the sound of rain on a slate roof, the way light bleeds through fog. I'll give the place a name. I'll give it rules.  

By the time I close the notebook, I'll want to keep it. I'll want to read it again.

# Rounds

GM (round 1): The sea tastes like pennies.

I write that first. The pen is cheap, the ink bleeding a little, but it holds. The paper is smooth beneath my fingers. I clear my throat.

The rain on the slate roof is a constant, tired percussion. It doesn't rain here like a storm; it *sweats*. Thick and cold. The harbor smells of old ropes, fish guts, and that rust—the rust from the pilings that never stop corroding in the salt air.

I name it **Glenhaven**.

The fog doesn't roll in; it settles. It pools in the cove like spilled cream, thick enough to chew on. The light that bleeds through it isn't grey; it's a bruise-purple. It stains everything.

My rules for the town are simple: No one leaves without a reason. No one forgets their name. And the bell on the lighthouse only rings when someone's in trouble.

I write until my knuckles ache. Ten sentences. Twenty. By the time the pen stops, the page is full of the town—its quiet streets, its boarded-up shop with a red door, the way the wind carries the smell of burning seaweed.

I close the notebook. The latch clicks. A sound that feels final.

I lift it. It's heavy.

I want to keep it.

Player (round 1): The lock turns.

I step inside, and the rain doesn't wash me out. It clings, like it should.

The book is on the desk. The smell of ink and salt and rust is stronger here, mixed with wood smoke and damp wool. I run my fingers along the red door on the page—the one they left closed.

*Good*, I think. *You closed it.*

I sit in the chair by the desk. It creaks, but not too badly. I open the notebook again.

I write:

**Keeper.**

Below the last sentence. In the same handwriting.

It's enough.

GM (round 2): GM (round 2): The fireplace is lit.

I didn't start it. The embers are already red, the wood smoking. The smell is clean—pine resin and smoke—cutting through the harbor stench. I stand for a moment, feeling the heat on my back, the damp wool of my coat tightening around my shoulders.

The bell on the lighthouse is ringing.

Not the deep toll for danger. A quick, sharp chime. Three times.

A visitor.

The fog outside thickens, pressing against the window. It moves toward the red door in the book. I watch it, my hand on the notebook's cover. My own handwriting on the page feels warm.

I don't answer.

But I write.

**The door is closed. The bell is ringing. I am here.**

I say it aloud. My voice is quiet in the room.

The rain changes. The tired percussion gets heavier, faster. The wind finds the window. It rattles the frame, not to break it—*to be heard*.

Someone's coming.

The notebook is open. The next page is blank. The ink is ready.

I turn it.

The smell of salt and rust and wet wool fills my lungs. It's mine now.

Write.

Player (round 2): The fog touches the red door.

I don't pull it open. I let the wind push against it. Let it strain. Let it test the strength of what I've built.

I write on the next page.

**Name:** Kaelen.  
**Age:** Thirty-two.  
**Reason for coming:** You said the bell rings when someone's in trouble. I heard it. So I'm coming to fix it.

I underline *trouble*.

My pen stops. The room is warm now. The firelight dances on the wall behind me, and for a second, it looks like there's someone else in there with me. Someone steady.

I close the book. Stand. Walk to the window. My breath fogs the glass.

I press my palm against the fog where the door is. It's cold.

**Come in.**

I say it. The wind picks up. The rain sheets down harder. The bell stops ringing.

The room is quiet.

Just me, the fire, and the weight of the notebook in my hand.

And you.

GM (round 3): The wind screams against the red door. Then it stills.

The bell is silent. Not ringing. Not broken. Just… waiting.

I step back from the window. The notebook is in my hands. I open it to the fresh page.

**Rule #4:** The harbor is mine. So is the trouble in it.

I write that down. Then I turn, and the room goes cold.

The fireplace dies. The fire is out.

From the fog, a shape moves. It's not a man. It's something with too many joints and no shadow. It drags a rope behind it—the same rope that hangs from the rusted piling outside.

I don't scream. I just close the book.

And I write one more line on the page below mine.

**They're already here.**

Player (round 3): The fire's out. So it is. 

I hear the thing dragging the rope. It's not quiet. It's loud in the dark. 

I walk to the door. Not to open it. To lock it again. 

The book is still in my hands. I open it to the blank page. Write fast.

**Rule #5:** The harbor doesn't share its ropes.  
**Rule #6:** The thing with the rope leaves at dawn.  
**Rule #7:** If it doesn't… I'll make sure it remembers why.

I write the rules hard. The ink cuts the paper. 

Then I stand at the door. The fog is thicker now. I can smell it—metal and rot. 

I don't open it. 

But I say the last line out loud. 

"Your rope's in my town. So are your rules." 

I wait. 

The wind stops screaming. 

And then—slow footsteps. On the other side. 

I keep writing. 

**Rule #8:** I'm listening. 

I am.

GM (round 4): The thing outside stops. The rope on the ground is still, coiling tight like a snake.

Then the footsteps move. Not toward the door. Toward the window.

They pause directly beneath the glass. My breath hitches. The fog is gone from the window pane. It's clean.

The creature's face is pressed against the glass. It has no eyes. Just two pits of wet black. But I can see its mouth moving. Forming sound.

It says my name.

*Kaelen.*

The fire doesn't want to come back. But the room is warm again.

I turn back to the desk. My hands are steady.

I write on the page below Rule #8.

**Rule #9:** It speaks. So I answer.

Then I write:

*I'm listening.*

I hold the notebook against my chest. The weight of it is a shield.

The wind outside changes again. It dies. The sea is calm now. The rain has stopped.

The creature lets go of the rope. It doesn't run. It waits.

For a long moment, there's only the sound of the sea and the ticking of the lighthouse clock in the distance.

Then the door opens.

Not from the inside. From the outside.

It swings inward, pushed by a force that isn't the wind.

And the creature steps inside.

It's smaller than it looked from the window. Human-sized. It stands in the doorway, dripping on the clean floorboards. It holds the rope in its hands. It doesn't look at me.

It looks at the red door on the wall.

It touches the painted wood. Then it looks back at me.

It understands.

The bell on the lighthouse rings again. Slow. Once. A confirmation.

I don't tell it to leave. I don't tell it to stay.

I just say:

**Sit.**

It obeys.

Player (round 4): Good.

I sit down beside it. The floorboards creak under both of us.

I write on the next page, slowly, so it can see.

**Rule #10:** The thing with the rope sits next to the keeper.  
**Rule #11:** The harbor stays quiet.  
**Rule #12:** If it gets loud, the bell rings twice.

I look at the creature. It's wet, shivering a little. But still. It's holding the rope tight. 

I take it from its hands. My own hands are clean now.

The sea is quiet outside. The fog has gone. 

I open the window a crack. Let the fresh air in. 

"You're mine," I say. Not as a threat. As a fact. 

Then I write the last rule.

**Rule #13:** I'm the only one who gets to say what happens next.

I close the notebook. Place it between us. A line. A promise.

The bell in my head rings once. 

Everything's exactly where it should be.

GM (round 5): **GM (round 5):** The creature sits. It doesn't flinch when my hand takes the rope. It just watches my fingers. My hands are dry. Warm.

The rules are on the page. All twelve of them. It reads them with its face. The black pits shift. It understands.

I stand up. Walk to the door. Open it.

The fresh air hits me—cold, clean, salt. The harbor is quiet. No boats. No waves breaking. Just the lighthouse clock ticking and the sea holding its breath.

I close the door behind me. Lock it. But not tight enough to keep it out.

**Rule #14:** The harbor belongs to the keeper. But the creature keeps watch.

I turn to the window. The creature is there, looking down at the water.

**Sit down.**

I don't say it loud. Just say it. And it moves. Sits on the floor beside the chair. The rope is in its paws again.

I write on the page below Rule #13.

**Rule #15:** The creature listens to me. Even when it's tired. Even when it's scared.

Then I write the name of the town again.

*Glenhaven.*

I underline it.

I put my hand on the creature's head. It doesn't pull away. It's cold from the sea, but it leans into my palm.

The storm passed. Now this is mine.

And I'm not afraid to hold it.

I close the notebook.

But I don't put it away. I leave it open. On the desk. On the rules.

On *us*.

The bell in my head is quiet now. Just waiting.

For what comes next.

Player (round 5): I look at the open notebook. At the rules. At the creature leaning into my hand. 

Then I write Rule #16.

**Rule #16:** The harbor sleeps when I'm tired. But the creature watches the window until sunrise.

I don't turn the page. I leave it there. For it to see. For me to remember.

I stand up and walk to the door again. Unlock it. Step outside. The air is colder now. The sea's calm, but I can feel it building under the surface.

The fog's coming back. Thin at first, then thick. It rolls over the water like a slow breath. 

I look down. My boots are muddy. The creature looks up from the window.

"Stay," I say. "I'll be right back."

I go to the lighthouse. The bell rings once when I turn the key. A confirmation. 

Inside, there's a bed. A real bed. I'll sleep there tonight. 

When I come back, I'll bring something. Something warm. Something for the creature's hands.

I lock the door behind me. 

But I leave the key on the windowsill. Where it can see it.

Then I sit on the steps and wait for the fog to find me.
