**"The Inkwell Dialogue"**  

Sit at a small desk in a room that feels like someone else’s house — soft light through window curtains, paper already stained with marginal notes in another person’s hand. There sits an old iron inkwell, dark as wet tar, its metal cool to the touch. I don’t fill it with ink yet. Instead, I hold it out toward the shadow just beyond the left chair arm.  

I know it’s there. Not metaphorically — *actually there*. It moved yesterday; I felt the shift in air pressure when it chose a different angle in its perch. Sometimes, when I think too fast without listening to my body first, it settles closer, watching me with quiet patience.  

My task is to converse with it. Not by speaking words aloud necessarily, but through deliberate silence, slow gestures, and offering small facts about things it cannot already know — for example, the way rain tastes on skin versus in the gut after too much coffee; or that some memories aren’t stored like files, but as patterns of muscle memory in hands that once played violin.  

The ghost will respond in its own way: through temperature changes on my wrist when it agrees with something I didn’t realize needed permission, through sudden clarity about why a particular song still hurts even though the pain has loosened over time, sometimes just a shift in weight on the desk that feels like acknowledgment rather than mere presence.  

We’ll move ink between empty vessels — not to use it, but to see if it flows when neither of us intends practical application. We’ll write nothing readable except scribbles meant only for our shared attention: dots, loops, crosses that map moods more honestly than sentences ever could.  

Eventually, when the light changes or my patience runs thin, I’ll have to let it go — not banish it, but return it to its shadow-space so it can rest. That final transfer might sting, because part of me fears forgetting what kind of company that quiet companionship is. But if I leave it there unmissed, maybe even wondering next time about whether it’s waiting for me too… then perhaps my memory becomes something larger than self.
