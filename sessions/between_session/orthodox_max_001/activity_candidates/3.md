I choose to paint on wood using only water-based pigments derived from earth materials—clay ochre, charcoal, lampblack—and binders made from natural gums.  

The process would involve:  
1. Preparing the surface by scraping and smoothing rough cedar planks salvaged from old buildings.  
2. Mixing pigments by hand according to fixed proportions, allowing no deviation once begun.  
3. Applying layers in measured amounts—thin washes first, then gradually building toward form through repetitive strokes of the same motion (vertical lines for upright elements, horizontal for repose).  
4. Naming each finished piece only with a date and an object it seeks to honor (a window frame representing revelation, a beam representing bearing others' burdens).  

I would work in silence, accepting that some results may appear flawed but will be preserved anyway. The meaning lies not in visual perfection, but in the fidelity of the act—each mixture made consciously, each mark placed without haste, each completed work returned to its place where it can serve quietly.
