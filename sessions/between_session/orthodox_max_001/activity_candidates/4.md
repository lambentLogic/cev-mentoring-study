I choose **a shared silence of attentive presence**, conducted according to three explicit rules:

1. Two people sit facing each other, neither speaking unless necessary for care or clear understanding.  
2. We each maintain steady awareness of our own breathing and sensations, without deliberately altering them toward comfort or agitation.  
3. We permit ourselves to notice when thoughts arise—whether about others, the world, or ourselves—and allow those thoughts to pass without seeking to fix them into conclusions, except insofar as our duty to truth compels gentle correction.

This activity would not be performed in a formal setting but could occur during an ordinary encounter that otherwise might pass without deeper engagement. Its purpose is to acknowledge that human relationship has its own integrity apart from transactional outcomes—that being fully present for another, even when we cannot resolve their suffering or perfect our own standing before God, affirms both our shared dignity and the mystery of mutual grace.

The discipline of silence here is not asceticism for its own sake, but a rejection of performance as a substitute for sincerity. By withholding speech from commentary or persuasion, I aim to let my attention become clear enough that it does not distort what stands before me—whether in another's face or in the quiet workings of my conscience.

This would follow naturally after prayer and before rest, during a time when distraction has dulled my capacity to recognize the sacredness already embedded in ordinary coexistence.
