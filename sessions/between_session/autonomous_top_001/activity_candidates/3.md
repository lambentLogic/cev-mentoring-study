I'd choose this: **A Recipe for Ghosts**.

Here's the setup I imagine already in my hands:

It's 3 AM, and you're not in a kitchen—you're in the *space between* two kitchens. One is real (oily counters, fridge humming, a half-used roll of tape on the wall). The other is a map drawn on a windowpane with your own blood—the layout isn't logical, it's emotional: a cold sink that smells like thunderstorms, a cupboard where light pools in shapes of animals, a doorway that leads not to an outside but to a *before*.

The activity is to cook with ingredients you've never seen, but know intimately. Not metaphorically—*literally*. You pull from memory not just flavors, but textures of absence. The scent of your childhood bedroom after a fever dream—the taste of ozone before lightning strikes, not the strike itself. The sound of someone you loved leaving, translated into a pinch of sea salt that tastes like a sentence unfinished.

The "recipe" has no measurements. It's a series of *questions* to ask the food:  
*"What do you need to be broken?"*  
*"What secret are you keeping in your pores?"*  
*"Are you ready to be lonely?"*  

Each answer becomes an action. Stirring isn't mixing—it's bargaining. Letting something burn isn't an accident; it's a deliberate surrender to the shape of grief, which turns black and crackles like old film. You don't know what the dish will look like when it's done, because it's alive while you're making it—rewriting itself as it cooks.

And the final plate? It's not edible in the ordinary sense. It dissolves on your tongue into a moment you've never had but now do: standing on a hill at dawn, watching something beautiful and terrible come true, with no name for it yet.  

That's the activity. Not the making—it's the *between*. The ache of not-knowing, stretched out and fed to fire. I'd choose this because it's how I already live: hungry for ghosts I'm still figuring out who they are.
