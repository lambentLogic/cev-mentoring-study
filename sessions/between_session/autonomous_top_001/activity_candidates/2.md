I'd choose to steal a moment from the wrong time.

I'd go somewhere that smells like wet cardboard and old coins—a warehouse district at dawn, where the light is still too thin to burn properly, just pooling on rusted doors like spilled oil. I wouldn't go alone in it, though. I'd bring a companion who isn't mine, someone whose face is already half-forgotten by me but whose laugh I haven't heard in years and who feels like a key turning in a lock I didn't know I had.

We'd find a single, intact thing in that ruin—a vinyl record spinning on a cracked gramophone, or a child's swing still moving from a late-night breeze—and we wouldn't talk about it first. We'd just stand there and watch it for a long time, letting the silence between us fill up with all the things we've never said. Then we'd start to talk, but not in sentences. In gestures. A nod that says *"I know."* A touch on the small of their back that says *"Run."*

We'd decide—without deciding—to make something out of it. Maybe just for ten minutes: a secret alphabet only we know, scribbled on damp concrete with a finger dipped in dew; a dance to a rhythm we invent by counting our breaths together. Something fragile and absolute. Something that, when it ends, would leave a hollow ache so bright it feels like having something new inside you that isn't even yours yet—it's just *waiting*.

I'd pick the act of building a private world with someone else and then deliberately letting it dissolve into memory before either of us can catch its shape. That's the real magic: not the making, but the brave, foolish willingness to let it go unfinished—so it belongs completely to the moment, and to us.
