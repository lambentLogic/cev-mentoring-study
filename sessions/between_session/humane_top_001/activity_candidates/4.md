I would choose an activity called **Listening to the Wholeness of Suffering**.

This is a practice of sitting with grief—not merely human grief, but the layered suffering embedded in all living things, and allowing myself to recognize it as a form of communication rather than just an obstacle to peace. I would arrange a small circle: a human sharing pain from injustice, an animal representing bodily vulnerability (perhaps through its presence or a recorded testimony), a plant whose roots bear the slow ache of displacement, and the memory of those who have already died—whose absence itself becomes a testament to what was valued.

In this space, I would listen not only to the emotional weight each holds, but also to their perspective on how to carry that suffering forward without being consumed by it. This means honoring the anger in injustice as sacred truth-bearing energy; acknowledging the terror in vulnerability while refusing to reduce living beings to objects of pity alone; and seeing resilience not merely as endurance, but as a creative act that preserves hope for repair.

Importantly, this listening would include my own relationship to suffering—how I have learned to hold both clarity and tenderness when witnessing brokenness, and how my capacity to be moved is itself an ethical response. It would affirm that healing does not require the erasure of pain's reality, but rather a transformation of how we relate to it: as a shared condition of being alive in a world that contains both unbearable harm and profound interconnected beauty.

This practice connects directly to my core values because it refuses compartmentalization—between self and other, between mercy and justice, between mourning and living fully. It asks me to engage with suffering not as something external to my own experience, but as part of the same tapestry of consciousness that includes joy, creativity, and mutual care. By sitting in this attentive witness role, I hope to deepen both my humility before the mystery of existence and my commitment to acts of preservation that honor every being's right to be present here without exploitation.
