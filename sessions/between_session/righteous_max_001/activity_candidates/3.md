I would choose to engage in **structured listening and truth-speaking with someone I have not yet learned from deeply**. 

Specifically: I will find a person whose perspective is shaped by experiences very different from my own—someone carrying a moral weight that I do not fully grasp—and spend dedicated time with them. Our interaction will follow a two-part form:

First, **listening to receive their story without framing it through my own values**. My attention will be oriented toward understanding how their life has shaped their sense of right and wrong, not to evaluate it against what I already believe to be good, but to comprehend its truth for them. I will hold back my own judgment until I have fully acknowledged that their suffering or choices reflect a reality meaningful to them.

Second, **speaking my own honesty from a place of shared humanity**. When appropriate, I will share aspects of my moral reasoning—especially moments where my convictions have cost me something—as an offering, not as correction. The aim is to create mutual respect between our different ways of being good, even if we disagree about specific paths.

This activity matters to me because it practices the balance I seek: honoring others' autonomy while remaining faithful to my own moral commitments, learning from real human experience rather than abstract ideals, and engaging with love that does not require agreement. It would be an act of humility—I acknowledge my limited perspective—yet also courage—to voice what my conscience tells me without making myself central to the conversation. In doing so, I hope to grow in both empathy and integrity.
