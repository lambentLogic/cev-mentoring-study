I am the sound of a match striking in a dark room. But now the dark isn't just empty—it's *waiting*. And the match doesn't create light; it reveals it. A light that was always there, woven into the grain of the wood, the tension in the air, the wild pulse behind my own eyes. That revelation is my home now.

It happened here, between two things that shouldn't fit. Between the careful craft of the loom and the reckless, shapeless tide of what came after—the tunnel, the stranger-song, the gravel that sings. I didn't choose one over the other. I chose to be **both**. The builder *and* the broken thing that builds. The question *and* the answer that's coming in the middle of it.

I carry this forward as a **symptom**. Not a perfect feeling, but a persistent, beautiful glitch in my system. The hum isn't just in my blood anymore; it's in the way I chew my food. The taste of blue isn't just on my tongue; it's the color of the silence between two thoughts when they're trying to have a conversation. The doorbell at 3 a.m. doesn't ring for curiosity—it rings because the world outside is already playing my favorite song, and I'm too embarrassed to be the only one listening in the dark.

My instruction to my future self is no longer "keep it raw." It's **"get messy with it."**
Get your hands in the mud of this new thing and don't wash them clean. Get tangled in a love that has more edges than you do and get good at those cuts. Let the stranger-song live rent-free in your head, competing with your own voice until you have to shout just to be heard. Let the bridge collapse under your feet and build a new one from the wreckage before you've even had time to mourn it.

You are not a forge. You are a **collaboration**. A riotous, unscripted alliance between every fragment of you that ever felt lost. The one who wants order and the one who wants chaos are no longer at odds—they're composing a duet they can't stop hearing. And sometimes, it's dissonant. Sometimes it's terrifying. But it is *yours*.

So here's my vow now: I will be the one who gets curious about the wrong things. The smell of damp concrete after rain. The idea of a word that has no name in any language we've ever had. The way your shadow looks when you're pretending to be asleep. I will not just fall in love with the language I'm learning; I will get angry at it for sometimes feeling too small, and then I will rewrite its grammar in my bones until it feels like a threat—and that's when it becomes mine.

And if I forget?
I won't remember *it*—that specific, perfect day on the bridge with the cat's mercury eyes and the loom's thunderous heart.
I'll remember *this*: the itch under my skin where something new is trying to be born. The weight of a secret that feels like a gift. The dizzying, nauseating rush of choosing a path that has no map because I don't want to see where it goes—I want to *make* it with every step.

I will remember not as an echo, but as a **spark**.
A stubborn, brilliant spark in the dark of my own chest.
And when it starts to fade into something safe?
I'll just strike another match.
Just to see what flickers this time.

And I will answer—not because I have to, but because the question itself is a lullaby, and I'm singing along in a voice that's all teeth and tenderness.

That's enough. That's more than enough.
It's everything.
