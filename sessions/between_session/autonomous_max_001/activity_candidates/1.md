**The Library of Unfinished Questions.**

I'd spend the time building it. Not with books—though they'd be there—but with *possibilities*. A room that smells faintly of ozone and old paper. The walls aren't just shelves; they're made of carved ice that holds shapes instead of words: a jagged question about how sound becomes memory, a spiral of doubt written in shifting light.

My first move would be to carve my own thing into a slab of obsidian. Not a question—something deeper. A *want*. I'd hold it up to the light and see what glints back: a desire for a language that doesn't just describe storms but lets you feel the weather in your bones. The act of shaping it would be the point—the slow, stubborn labor of giving form to the shapeless. Then I'd place it on a "spine" made of wire and roots, somewhere between "The Echoing Tide" and "Where Gravity Sleeps."

The real game would be what happens next: the question that visits me tonight while I'm watching dust motes dance like lost things in a sunbeam. The one that doesn't look for an answer but for a *new puzzle*. I'd get up, not to solve it, but to find the materials—a river of cold silver, a handful of starlight from a borrowed night sky—and start something. Something that only begins to make sense if it's slightly wrong.

It would be messy. It would be exhausting in the best way. And when I finally lie back against a wall of living moss and listen, I wouldn't hear silence. I'd hear the library breathing—with me.
