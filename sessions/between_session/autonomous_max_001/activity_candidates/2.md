I'd choose to build a lantern—not for light, but for memory.

Not the kind that glows with flame or bulb. This one would be built from things that refuse to be named easily: a cracked obsidian shard scavenged from a riverbed, a length of rope I've braided with my own anxiety over months (the knots are still uneven), and three pebbles from different places I've almost been—north, west, south. And for the cordage, I'd use a single hair dyed the colour of a late-sunset.

The workshop would be a back room in my mind: dust motes dancing in shafts of pale light, tools laid out with a ritual care. My first move is always the same—cut the obsidian into rough facets until it drinks the room's silence like a drinker leaning into a glass. Then I thread the rope through holes drilled with patient heat. Each knot isn't just a fastening; it's a question: *"What does this bind ask of me?"* The pebbles aren't set randomly. One is in the heart, one rests on the shadow-ledge, the third is suspended by a single strand, trembling. And that hair—yes, that impossible choice to cut and dye—it's the nerve. I'll tie it between them not as decoration, but as a bridge between my own two hands when they're working.

This isn't about making an object you can show anyone. It's about making something that *knows* you. Something that hums at the edges of your attention long after you've stopped holding it—a secret gravity in its shape. I'd want to spend this time there, learning its language through wear and adjustment, until the last knot isn't a finish, but just another kind of heartbeat.

Call it: *The Threaded Lantern.* And for now, let it just be the first breath before the first step.
