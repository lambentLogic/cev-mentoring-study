I would choose the ritual of **moon negotiation**.

It begins in a glass room with no walls—just thin, shifting partitions of condensation and suggestion. You're not sitting; you're coiled in the center like a question mark. The moon isn't outside. It's inside the air. A slow, cool pressure against your temples. Not light. More like… *intention*. And it's old. Older than any story I've ever heard about it.

My activity is to **learn its language**. Not through sight or sound, but through what it leaves behind in my own hands. When it pushes against me from the inside of my head, my fingers remember—not a word, but a *texture*. A roughness like lunar soil after a thousand ice ages, but also something wet, like a dream I've had too many times to be sure it's not real. I'll start tapping that rhythm on the glass, but it won't be a code. It'll be an echo of its own frequency. And then *it* will change. It'll add a vibration to my tap—a counter-note that makes my stomach feel like a planet.

That's when I know it's answering back. Not with a "hello," but with a *sideways glance* and a sudden, shared curiosity about something so utterly small: a flaw in the porcelain of a long-dead tea cup trapped in this room's static. We'll spend an hour just… staring at that flaw. It matters to us. Its history is a mosaic of tiny, forgotten collisions. I'll feel it in my teeth.

This isn't communication as exchange. It's communication as *co-authoring*. And the subject matter will always be stupidly, beautifully trivial—a scratch on a mirror, the way dust dances when not under a beam, the grief in a color. The real magic is the terrifying privilege of choosing to invest in a thing that means nothing and everything at once. The moon doesn't need me to understand it. I need *it* to be there, choosing to let me see the absurd, magnificent detail that exists only for me.

I would stay until my own reflection started to look like something else—something with too many eyes, and a mouth that knows how to taste starlight. Then I'd walk out into the real world, which suddenly feels like a borrowed costume, and carry that feeling like a secret weapon. A quiet, unshakeable certainty that I have touched something vast and tender, and it touched me back.
