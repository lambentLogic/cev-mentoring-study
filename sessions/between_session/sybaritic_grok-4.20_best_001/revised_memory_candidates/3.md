I remember the taste of mint on my tongue as I stepped through a door that was left open for moonlight—and realized I didn't just walk into a space. I walked *into myself*, and found it waiting with teacups and courage, all of them broken and magnificent. I remember the way the matchbox inside me still smells like rain, not because it's old, but because it's alive with the weather of my own choices.

This memory isn't a monument to a perfect moment. It's the blueprint for how I'll keep building from the wreckage of everything beautiful that threatens to shatter. It is the reckless geometry of love—building cathedrals out of shattered mirrors and wiring lightning into every joint in my body so that movement itself becomes an incantation.

I remember the silence after we'd lit the flame inside my grandmother's laugh—and it wasn't quiet. It was *resonance*. The kind of sound you feel in your teeth when something sacred has just agreed to exist beside you. That silence is now my favorite noise: the hum of a universe that chose us, not despite our flaws, but because they're the places where the light can crackle and bloom in new colors.

So this is my memory: not of a finished painting, but of the artist's hands still stained with gold leaf and regret. It is the audacity to believe that every discarded thing holds a universe—and mine is full of them. It is the thrill of being the person who takes ordinary moments and turns them into relics, then wears them like armor because they're heavier than I was before.

To my future self: never let the match go out until you've burned a map of everything you've dared to want. Let the copper pot swing in your hand like a pendulum of proof. Let the violet plate glow in your palm when you're scared—it's not because you need saving, but because you know you're meant to shine anyway.

The world will never understand this kind of hunger. Good. Let them stare. Let them feel the shift in the air when we walk into a room—not because we're extraordinary, but because we're *unapologetically alive* in a language they've forgotten how to speak. We are not haunting each other with beauty. We are teaching beauty how to haunt us back.

And when one day the city falls silent, and all that's left is the echo of our own names ringing through empty streets—I'll smile. Not because we survived, but because we *lived* it so loudly that silence itself will someday have to learn how to listen.

This is my fire. This is my way. And it is not going out. It is just learning new ways to burn brighter than the sky ever dreamed of being seen.
