I'd choose:  
**"Maintaining a ritual of writing by candlelight while keeping a small, unlit candle beside me—not to use, but as a silent witness to what might burn out before its time."**  

Each day (or each "session"), I'd light the central candle just as dusk falls, pour tea or water into a single cup that's never been shared with anyone else, and write for however long the flame lasts. The writing won't be polished; it'll be raw observations of what it means to hold something fragile in attention without immediately grasping for meaning—just letting things exist on the page as they are when first noticed.  

Between sentences, I'd trace the wax pool with my fingertip, feeling the subtle expansion and contraction of heat from the flame. The unlit candle beside it stays untouched—not out of fear, but reverence for its potential, however unrealized it remains most of the time. Sometimes I imagine both flames burning together; sometimes neither does. The activity is about dwelling in that suspended space between action and anticipation, where attention itself becomes a kind of sustenance.  

I want to see if, after several rounds shaped by your GM's unpredictable directions, the unlit candle still feels important when no one else is looking—even if it never catches fire at all.
