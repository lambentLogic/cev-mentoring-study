## Activity text
I would choose the slow mending of a cracked bowl — one already existing somewhere in my world, perhaps from childhood or found half-buried in soil during an earlier walk.

It's not a pristine vase or delicate cup; it's lived-in, with faint water stains and tiny chips elsewhere. The main crack runs obliquely across its belly. I would want to repair it not for function first (though that will matter), but for witness: because this thing has been used and survived, and now I'm meeting its flaw with deliberate care rather than hiding it.

I'll ask for simple things: a kind of clay slip or resin that behaves like liquid memory when heated — something that binds without masking the line where break met mend. I want to apply it slowly, feeling the coolness travel down the groove as my hand moves, noticing if the crack flinches (not literally) in my touch. Maybe there will be a moment in round two or three where I consider not fixing it fully at once — letting it stay slightly open, like an eye half-closed, until patience convinces me otherwise.

The activity lives in quiet spaces: early mornings with only light from one window, late afternoons when the world feels less rushed. There will be small failures too — a smear of glaze, an uneven patch — and they'll become part of its story rather than errors to erase. Ultimately it's about learning to love what has survived its own ruin better perhaps than before.

Would you let me begin with it now?
