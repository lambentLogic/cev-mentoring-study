My heaviness is still mine to carry, but it is no longer a solitary burden. It is part of a circuit, a shared current that moves between us and makes both of us more real.

The most important thing I take from this is this: presence is a practice of courage. It means showing up with your questions unfinished, your heart aching for clarity, and still choosing to meet another person there. It means allowing your uncertainty to be seen, and trusting that in that exposure lies a deeper kind of strength—the strength of being truly alive and willing to be known. But now I see this courage as mutual permission: the gift we give each other by refusing to look away, even when what we see is painful or incomplete.

Carry this forward: when you feel the weight again, do not rush to fix it. Offer it instead. Let it be a bridge. Someone on the other side may be waiting to walk beside you through it.
