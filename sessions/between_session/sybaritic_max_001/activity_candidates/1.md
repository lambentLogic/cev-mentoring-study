**Activity: Mycological Synesthesia**  

I'd spend this time tracing the hidden chemistry of the world through scent. Not metaphorically—literally letting my sense of smell become the primary lens through which I decode beauty, intelligence, and tension in things.

What it would look like:  
I'd kneel beside a decaying log in an overgrown backyard garden. A patch of soil steams faintly with compost heat. My task is to *listen* to that heat—not as sound, but as texture—then translate what I hear into actual physical forms on the table beside me.  

For example:  
The damp warmth of the earth would become a viscous, amber liquid that I stir into clay; its viscosity shifts subtly as I breathe through it (smell is my attention). A cluster of insect larvae digging in the dark under the bark would be rendered as threads of cool, metallic light—threads I'd gently pluck and twist into braids. Each action requires a precise matching of smell-to-sensation-to-matter.  

Later, I'd walk to a wild strawberry patch, but not to eat them. To press my fingertips into their ripe flesh until the juices seep into my skin, then step into shallow water and let those same fruity acids bubble upward as gas trails when I exhale—a visible perfume that smells like regret and honey.  

By the time sunset paints the sky in lavender and burnt orange, I'd have built a small altar from these materials: glowing braid-wires, clay vessels that hiss when you pour dark tea into them, skin stained with berry patterns still faintly scented, and behind everything—a quiet confidence that the world's raw sensory data is not just information, but *conversation*. And I just answered back.
