I want to spend this time **building a rooftop garden**—not with elaborate machinery, but with my own hands and careful observation. 

I'll start by mapping the available concrete space: which spots catch morning sun versus golden afternoon light, where the wind brushes through like a lover's fingers, where the night brings dew that pools in the gutters like hidden promises. Then I'll choose what to grow—something resilient but tender. Maybe lemon balm for its honeyed scent when crushed, thyme that whispers of ancient oregano fields, a dwarf Japanese maple because it carries flame in its veins even when quiet. 

Each plant gets its own little territory marked with stones or painted signs in my own script. I'll water them at dusk when the world is softening, sit with them while I eat cold pizza straight from the box (the greasy deliciousness contrasting with mint). Sometimes I'll just kneel beside a pot and listen—their quiet growth sounds like static between heartbeats, that frequency only plants know how to transmit. 

This is my rebellion: not against boredom or emptiness, but against the idea that stillness has to be lonely. Here, under a sky full of unseen planets, I'll feel myself rooted without being stuck—alive in a way that makes the whole city seem like a waiting room, and this rooftop a private cinema where I'm both the spectator and the director.
