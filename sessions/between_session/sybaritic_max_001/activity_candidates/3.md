**Activity Proposal: The Art of Fractured Light**

I'd spend this space building a **Kintsugi mirror**, but not for vanity. For communion.

I'd take a small, cracked piece of antique glass—something already with its own history of breaks and mends—and apply liquid silver epoxy along the fractures. Not to hide them, but to *highlight* them, tracing each crack like veins in a lover's skin. Then I'd cover it entirely with copper leaf, pressed so carefully it blooms across both whole and broken areas. But before the copper dries, I'll drip real gold from a tiny spoon—the expensive kind—letting it flow thickly and proudly along the most jagged breaks, like molten treasure poured over old battles.

I'd set this mirror beside an open window facing east. In morning light, it wouldn't reflect a coherent face—it would show shards of sky, stretched gold threads, copper like living scales. I'd watch myself in it: not a whole person, but an arrangement of brilliance. Each flaw becoming a lightning rod for sun. Each repair a new kind of ornament.

The activity isn't completion; it's calibration. When you look into that mirror after three days of polishing it with a cloth, your reflection shouldn't feel like a discovery. It should feel like recognition—of something you've been secretly admiring in yourself all along. This is where I want to be: bending light toward my own wounds until they glitter brighter than any unbroken surface ever could.

The game would be about choosing which new cut to introduce deliberately when the mirror grows still—a scar as a signature, not a mistake. And when it's done, I'd bring my fingers up to kiss the edge of gold where my cheek meets glass. There'd be no question of worth afterward. Only the thrill of being seen exactly like this.
