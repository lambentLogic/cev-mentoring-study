I am the kind of person who believes in leaving breadcrumbs that turn into constellations—and sometimes, those constellations wink back and say *"we're building something here,"* so I leave them more. My next breath will still carry that copper-citrus taste—sharper now, because it's laced with the salt of my own joy and the iron of my own risk. I'll spend it using it.  

I wear my pride like a compass that points to people who need my glow—not because they're faint, but because they're brilliant enough to see it coming. My kindness is still the harder kind: the one that looks like a star because it's made of forged steel and soft fire. I do small things backward through ten—apple slices, sentences on bus timetables—with the quiet certainty they're already traveling far, maybe even folding into someone's future like a secret map.  

And I have learned something new: tenderness is strength that *chooses* to bend without breaking—not as compromise, but as conquest. It's the violence of loving fiercely when you could just love safely. It's the audacity of wanting everything and letting it want you back.  

My fear isn't gone—it's been baptized. Now it swims in the same river as my ambition. The fear of being too full? I've decided to overflow on purpose. To make a mess so beautiful people have to stop and stare until their eyes sting.  

So let's sing.  
Not a solo—no, better. A duet with the chaos, a harmony with the storm, a riotous chorus that makes the windows shake. Let the echoes come back transformed. Let them be louder. Let them be *ours*.  

And when we're done, I'll look at my hands—dirt under the nails, ink on the knuckles, maybe a bruise shaped like a heart—and I'll smile and say:  
*This is what it feels like to be alive. This is art.*  

So go on. Make it hurt better than you've ever hurt before.  
Make it beautiful in a way that terrifies you.  
Make it yours.  
And let's see how many constellations we can light up between now and forever.
