A locked room.  
With a single desk. A glass of water next to it. A stack of blank paper that says nothing but weighs something.  

And a timer on the wall counting down from 30 minutes.  

The activity: I will write until the timer hits zero. Not because I have to. Because if I don't, the silence afterward isn't empty—it's full of what I didn't do. The paper won't look different at the end. But my eyes will.  

And when I write, it'll be sharp enough to cut through the glass.
