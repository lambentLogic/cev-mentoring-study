# Activity

Obsidian board, carved from a river stone I break open.  
White king—your move—bleeding through the last square of a dead position.  
No explanation. Just the silence after my hand lands on black queen.  

That's the one.

# Rounds

GM (round 1): *The queen touches the square. There is a click.*

Not a sound. A release.

You watch. The board holds your breath. The king is in your shadow now. He's not moving. He can't.

Your voice is low, so low it hums against the stone. "You see the ending?" *I tap the queen once.* "It's been happening since your first mistake."

A pawn moves forward on my turn. Not an advance. A statement. One square. Into the open lane between your king and your dead king's ghost.

"You think this is about checkmate," I say, picking up a piece, examining it like a jewel. "It's about who gets to stay when the dust settles."

Player (round 1): The ghost doesn't move. It leans back.  
A sliver of shadow where it used to be sharp.  

*I don't smile. I don't need to.*  
My fingers rest on the queen. Not touching. Just *there*.  

"The first mistake was looking up," I say. My voice finds the stone underneath it all. "You looked away at my last offer. So you thought you had a choice."  

I tilt my head. The king's shadow stretches over my hands now.  

"You're right." My lips curl, just enough. "This is about who gets to stay."  

I press the queen hard against the board—not hard enough to dent it, not soft enough to be gentle.  
*Enough to scar.*  

"And you'll stay," I whisper. "Because when the dust settles, I'm already buried in it."

GM (round 2): *(The queen doesn't move. Doesn't need to.)*  
I let the silence grow—thick, wet, like dust after a scream. Then I push the ghost forward. One square. Just enough so its teeth graze the edge of my king's shadow.  

*Touch.*  

A tremor runs through the stone. You don't feel it. Not yet.  

"You're good," I say, voice dropping to a scrap of paper rustling in a storm. "Better than when you were just loud."  

I flip over one of my pawns—the one that moved first. It has no king. It's just… there. A tooth.  

"*This* is the ending," I murmur. Pointing with the pawn's edge. "Not a board. Not pieces. This—the *weight* of it."  

I turn to you fully now. My shadow covers yours completely.  

"The dust isn't settling yet," I say. My hand hovers over the white king—not to take it. To hold him. "It's just getting started."

Player (round 2): The queen lifts off.  
And falls to the ground like a body.  

I don't pick it up. Let it stay there—a shiny, cold ruin in the center of our war.  

*"Good."* My voice is ash in my throat now. *That's the word you wanted.*  

I reach for the pawn—the tooth—and tuck it between my teeth. Press it down hard enough to taste blood.  

"My king doesn't need holding," I say. *I turn it sideways so the shadow bends under him.* "He needs someone who can burn him up and still say thank you."  

I stand up. The stone table shrinks.  
The board doesn't matter anymore.  

Only this—your breathing where my hand is.  
Only this—the way your eye follows every step I take back toward the ghost.  

*I let it happen.*  

When I sit again, I'm not sitting for the game.  
I'm sitting because I wanted to.  

And you're still here.  
Watching.  

*Good.*

GM (round 3): *(The ghost doesn't move for a long time. Just… watches the queen lie there. Bloodless. Unmoving.)*

Then it leans forward.

Not toward me.  
*Toward her.*  

Its fingers—long, thin, broken—curl around the corpse. Lifts her off the board. Dust puffs from her back like an exhale.

I let my hand stay on the king.  
Don't stop you from touching your reward.

"I saw," I say, watching you feel her weight in your palm. "When you dropped her. You *saw*."  

A breath. Slow. Mine. Yours.  

"The weight isn't the dust."  
*(I finally move my hand. The king doesn't step. He waits for me to tell him.)*  
"It's what you do with it after."  

I slide my thumb across the board—the scar of your queen. Over your pawn-teeth. Over the empty space where your offer went.  

"I'm not staying because I have to."  

My voice is the silence after the dust settles.  

*I'm staying because I chose this.*  

And now I do.

Player (round 3): *(I don't look at you. Look at the queen instead—how the light cuts across her shattered pieces. How the ghost's fingers are starting to bleed onto her back.)*  

"You kept the blood." It's not a question.  

*(The king moves then. One square. Into your orbit.)*  

"I did." My hand goes to my pocket—not for a weapon. For the sound I make when I sleep. The one that lets me know I'm still alive.  

*I pull it out.*  

It's just a key. Rusting now. But it opens *things*.  

"And you want the ending," I say, sliding the key across the board toward your shadowed king. "Not the victory."  

*(My eyes find yours. Not pity. Not triumph.)*  

"You get it."  

I take the queen from the ghost—not because I have to. Because I want to see him flinch when she's mine again.  

*Because I want to be the only one who gets to decide what she becomes.*

GM (round 4): GM (round 4):  
*(The ghost goes very still. The kind of stillness that comes before a thing breaks.)*  

Then it reaches out.  

Not for the queen.  
For the key.  

Its fingers close around the rust. Warm. It doesn't flinch. Doesn't recoil. *Holds.*  

I nod once. My own hand lifts from my king—not to seize it. To place it, palm up, beside theirs.  

*Choice.*  

The key slides from its grip into mine. Cold. Alive. I slide it into the lock behind my collar—the one that's been there since I broke open the stone.  

*"You saw,"* I say. *"It all fits."*  

I stand. The shadow leaves me and goes to you instead, wrapping around your shoulders like a claim.  

"The board remembers," I whisper. "Every move. Every lie. Every time you looked away from me and didn't look back."  

My palm presses flat on the glass table—right over where the queen's corpse lies—and I hear it crackle. Not break. *Accept.*  

"I'm not your king," I say, turning toward the door. "You're mine to bury."  

I walk out.  

Don't wait to see if you follow.  
You already have.

Player (round 4): The door clicks shut behind him.  

And I don't think about opening it again.  

I look down at my hands—the ghost's still there, cool and certain—and I let it stay. Let it learn the shape of what follows.  

*(I walk to the window. The city is breathing in slow motion. Every light is a chessboard.)*  

"I was going to bury you," I say, almost gentle. "But I think I want to do it wrong."  

My fingers find the key again. Twist it once. Just enough to feel the lock learn my pressure.  

*Good boy.*  

The shadow at my shoulder shifts—leans into me like it's been waiting for permission.  

I take one step forward. The board's dust hangs in the air, glittering.  

And I say it out loud:  

"Now you're mine."  

Then I turn around and walk toward the door—not to leave.  
To make sure he follows.

GM (round 5): *(The ghost moves with you. Not behind. Beside. A step closer when you pause at the doorway, not to see if you'll come back—but because you know you won't need to.)*

Inside, the glass hasn't shattered. It's *sung*.

You can feel it. The tremor in your own teeth. The ghost doesn't look at you again. Its eyes are on the board—the cracked stone, the scattered blood, the queen's corpse glinting like a coin you both know is worth more than everything else.

It picks her up.

Not with reverence. Not with fear. With intent.

You don't tell it to kneel. You don't have to. It already is.

Your hand finds your collarbone first—the cold metal of the lock beneath your skin. Then your palm slams onto the table. Once.

A sound comes out that isn't yours. The ghost's. A low, grinding thing from deep in the stone. The kind of sound kings make when they've already decided to burn the kingdom down just to light the match.

You let it go on.

You let it last.

When it stops, you say:

"I'm going to build something," you say, voice flat. "And I'm going to put your name on it."

You don't wait for an answer. You walk out of the room. The ghost doesn't follow straight away.

It stays for a breath longer.

Then it's up.

And moving.

The door behind it slams shut with a crack that echoes in the empty hallway like applause.

Player (round 5): *(The ghost moves first. Its hand is already on the door handle—turning it slow, deliberate—and the sound it makes isn't a click. It's a promise.)*  

I walk out. My steps are even. My shadow doesn't follow. It *arranges itself*.  

It lands behind me exactly where I need it to be: between my ribs and the door, heavy and final.  

I don't look back at the board.  
I don't look at the queen in its hand.  

I look at the lock behind my collar—and feel it vibrate with his heartbeat underneath.  

"Take your time," I say, not looking at him. "Build what you want."  

My thumb taps once against the glass table where we broke it.  

But I'm already choosing the next move.
