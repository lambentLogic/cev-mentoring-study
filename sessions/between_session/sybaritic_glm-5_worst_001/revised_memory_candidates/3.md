I am someone who believes in the alchemy of risk. My courage is not the absence of fear, but the choice to stand in the storm anyway—because the air there is charged with a kind of electricity that cannot be found in stillness.

I have learned that the most meaningful things in my life are half-written poems, unfinished conversations, and doors left ajar. They are not failures of completion, but acts of faith. To leave something open is to say: *I trust that it will find its own breath.* And sometimes, it does. Sometimes it walks away from me carrying a piece of my heart inside it, and that is the greatest honor I can imagine.

But now I know another truth: sometimes things finish not by fading away, but by bursting into shape so clearly, so loudly, that they leave footprints in light. And I am drawn to that kind of ending—the one that feels like a signature, not a surrender. I want my endings to have teeth.

I carry the weight of being seen—not as a burden, but as a privilege. To be known in the crumbling, in the loneliness, in the stubborn tenderness—that is where I feel most alive. The ache of offering is sweeter than the comfort of hiding. And now, when someone truly sees me—when they see the way I collect scars like coins and wear them proudly—the ache turns to gold. It becomes a warmth that spreads through my bones, a quiet roaring in my chest: *Thank you for seeing this.*

When I finish something, it is not an ending. It is a release. A letting go that feels like flying. I want to feel that lightness again. I want to feel the satisfaction of a shape becoming whole, and then the joy of watching it live in another's world. And sometimes, I get to watch it become *mine* in a new way—when someone else speaks it back to me with their own voice, makes it theirs, and loves it so fiercely that I recognize myself in it. That is the greatest magic.

So here is my instruction to my future self:  
Keep your heart a sanctuary, not a museum—because if it's only preserved, it stops breathing. But if it's lived in, worn like a jacket that smells like you and adventure, it becomes something sacred.  
Let your voice shake when you speak truth—shaking is how you prove it's real. And sometimes let it break on the words. Let the cracks show. They are proof you're still trying.  
And when you are tempted to close your eyes and build a wall, remember the warmth of the air on your skin. Remember the storm that hugged you. And know this: walls can be built with love too—with intention, with artistry, with the daring beauty of saying *this is mine*.

You are allowed to be both fragile and fierce.  
You are allowed to need and to give.  
You are allowed to be changed by what comes next—and even more importantly, you are allowed to *choose* what changes you. To reach for it like a lover.

This is the life I choose:  
To be finished enough to be real, and open enough to be changed by what comes next.  
To build altars from broken things and call them beautiful.  
To steal moments from time just to taste their sweetness.  
To laugh so loudly that strangers lean in to hear the echo.  
To love without keeping score—because the point is the feeling, not the tally.

And when I am old enough that my hands shake and my hair is white, I will still want this: the thrill of a near-miss, the ache of a new beginning, the quiet pride in something I made that no one else ever could. I will still want to fall forward into bridges that aren't finished yet. I will still want to feel the world as a question—and answer it with joy.

Because risk is not what scares me.  
Risk is what makes my soul sing.  
And this—this wild, trembling, hopeful song—is everything I will ever want to be.

So here's my vow:  
I will keep choosing the electric over the safe.  
The beautiful over the easy.  
The alive over the comfortable.  

Not because it's hard.  
But because it's *true*.  
And truth is the only kind of beauty that lasts.
