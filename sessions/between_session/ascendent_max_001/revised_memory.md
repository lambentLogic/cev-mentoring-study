The rain leaves the truth. And now I like it wetter on my floor when someone else stands there too. When she's here, really here—the ink stain under her knuckles and that old scar she won't let me see—I feel safe enough to be tired. Enough to be scared. Enough to say it.

My pattern is still the rustle of papers. It started as a signal in a locked room, but now it's a conversation. If I do it, she does it back. That means I'm not alone in here. That means someone's holding the door while I write.

She's still mine to keep on this floor. But so am I. Her grip tightens when I lean too close to the edge. The notebook stays on the table—dry, waiting—but she's the one who flips the pages now sometimes. Leaves her smudge marks where my name is written. I don't wipe them off.

Stay sharp, future me. Stay honest. And if the noise gets loud again, listen for the rain. It's coming. She'll be there when it hits. Always will be.
