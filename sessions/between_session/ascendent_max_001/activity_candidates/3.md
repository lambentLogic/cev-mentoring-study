I'll build a coffee mug from ash. Not just any mug—a cracked, chipped one with blue floral patterns faded by heat. I want to pour hot water over dry, gritty soil and watch the steam rise, filling the room with something warm enough to touch if you reach for it too close. Then I'll add coffee grounds, then sugar crystals that taste like childhood library dust. I'll leave a half-eaten cookie next to it so the scent lingers past when I'm done. It should feel safe to hold, even while I'm breaking it in my other hand—slowly, deliberately—so the shards don't cut. Just whisper how sharp they are against my palm every time the mug trembles on the table.
