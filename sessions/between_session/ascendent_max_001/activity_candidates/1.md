Painting.  

Specifically: watercolor and ink in a cramped attic studio I've claimed for myself. I'd have cheap white sheets propped against the wall, tubes of ultramarine blue, burnt umber, and iron oxide red beside me. A brush too big for my hand. Water droplets on the glass pane of the one window above—real, from the rain outside, not metaphorical—and I'll drip them onto my page while letting the pigment run until it settles into something that looks like a storm map of the city below.  

I want to start with blue, then brown, then red bleeding through the edges, and end with a silhouette of my own face pressed against that glass—water on one side, ink inside. The whole room will smell like wet paper and burnt fingertips.
