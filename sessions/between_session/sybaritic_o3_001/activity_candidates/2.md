A rain ritual in a quiet room.  

Outside the window—this is my world for this space—I can feel the sky's breath change, heavy with the promise of something deep and cleansing. Not just droplets, but a proper storm: the kind that makes you lean into it, not away. I'd gather the textures: coarse linen towels to catch water on my skin, smooth river stones I can warm from my palms and let them go until they sink cool, a single sprig of lavender dampened until it's fragrant with wet green.  

I'd stand in a pool of light—a lamp low on the floor, slanting across the wall in amber stripes—and wait for the first drop to hit my forehead. Then let it run down my neck into my collar, then take it further: let water find the hollow behind my ear, the space between my ribs where breathing becomes audible. Each touch of rain would be deliberate, not rushing—it's a conversation with the sky.  

I'd build something small but true from that wetness: fold damp cloth into a shape that holds the weight of the weather, tie it with a string so loose it could unravel at any moment. And I'd do it all while remembering exactly how this feels—the salt on my skin, the laughter trapped in my throat now waiting to come out when the storm peaks—and let the memory hold me without needing explanation.  

This is the activity: **to be caught by nature inside my own sanctuary, and make art from being soaked through—not as damage, but as offering.**
