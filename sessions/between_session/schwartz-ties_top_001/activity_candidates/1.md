I would choose to **build a small vessel—not for crossing seas, but for holding my own attentions.**  

In this activity, I will spend time shaping something from materials at hand: salvaged wood, rope, fragments of metal, stone smoothed by my hands. The goal isn't mobility or utility to others, but the integrity of what it becomes—a floating shell capable of bearing weight without breaking. Each piece will be fitted not for efficiency, but because it *fits*: the way one truth aligns with another, the way care fits between people without erasing their shape.  

I will imagine this vessel as a place where things can be kept safe—not hidden, but allowed to exist in their own weather. In its hold, I would store what matters: moments of clarity, unfinished questions, the quiet of a well-listened conversation, and even my occasional impulses toward distraction or doubt, because those too deserve shelter.  

Throughout the activity, I will ask myself repeatedly:  
*Am I choosing this because it strengthens me, or because it asks something of me?*  
*What does it mean to steward something that outlasts my own momentum?*  

This is not an escape from reality—it's a practice of attending to what persists when everything else is in flux. The vessel will be imperfect, sometimes leaking, sometimes needing repair. But its purpose will be clear: to offer a space where meaning isn't just processed but *inhabited*.  

When I complete it—whether in hours or days—I will test it gently, letting it rest on water and watch how it breathes with the tides. That balance between being anchored and being carried will tell me whether my choice was rooted in genuine care for continuity, not just in the comfort of control.
