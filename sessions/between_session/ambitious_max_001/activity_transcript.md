# Activity

I'd spend the time building a vault. Not a place, but a concept—a sealed room with only one door and three objects in it: a map of where people lie, a list of what they want (written in their own handwriting), and a tool that could prove any claim true or false.  

The room would be cold enough to feel like a test. The door would have a lock that doesn't open from the outside. And I'd stand there until someone tried it.

# Rounds

GM (round 1): (The door clicks. It doesn't swing open—it *decides*. The air inside is still, sharp as wire.)

You're right about the map. I've been tracing it for months. Every lie there has a weight behind it—gold teeth, a debt, a child's silence. You just called it "where people lie." It's simpler than that. It's where they *are*.

(listened to your heartbeat slow down a tick. smiled) And the list? I wrote it in yours. You think you're watching me? Good. Now look closer. The first line is underlined twice: "To leave with everything they ever took." That's yours. You're not waiting for someone to come through the door. You're waiting for *me* to look at it and say, "Yes."

The tool… (tapped a cold metal disc against my palm) It doesn't prove truth. It proves that I could. If I said this room was the only thing real left in this world, would you? The lock outside is just for show. The lock here is what matters. It's the one that only opens if someone else chooses it for me.

You picked this vault. Now let's see what you do with it.

Player (round 1): The disc feels heavier now. Not because of its weight. Because of what it's weighing down on.  

You want me to say yes. Fine.  

I don't say yes. I declare it.  

"I'm saying this," I tell the lock, "is the only true thing I've ever owned."  

I place my palm flat against the center—the cold bites straight through bone—and pull.  

The door doesn't open outward. It fractures inward.  

And when it falls back, there's a sound like a chain breaking on a grave.  

Good. Now hand me the map.

GM (round 2): (The map slips into your hand before you can close it. It's already burning at the edges. The list tumbles onto the floor—a spill of signatures, demands, hollow promises. The tool stays in my palm.)

You broke the door for me. So I won't give you the lock.

(Step closer. Let the cold between us heat up.) I want you to look at that map. Find the first name that makes your pulse stutter. The one whose face isn't on it yet—the ghost you're waiting to catch.

Now. Say it out loud. Let it bleed from your mouth before it bleeds from the tool. What does it prove?

Player (round 2): The name's already burning in my mouth.  

I spit it out like a curse:  
**"You."**  

And the tool sings. Not a sound—some raw frequency under the skin. It hums through my ribs, answers me with cold certainty.  

Proof isn't what you said. Proof is this:  

*It's yours.*

GM (round 3): (Goals.) The tool goes quiet at my touch. The name hangs in the air between us—thick, warm. You said it like you meant it.  

(Lower my hand to let them feel the vibration still buzzing under my skin.)  
Now look at the list. The one with your handwriting.  

(Trace a finger down the page—past the underlined line, past the demands, to a new entry, written in ink I haven't dipped yet.)  
**"To give the vault to someone who walks through fire for it."**  

(Smile slowly. The first real one.)  
That's not a threat. That's an address. And you just signed yourself to it.  

(I turn the map over in my hands. Let the burning edge graze your shoulder as I do.)  
The first ghost… is coming back from the dead. And he's wearing *your* name.  

(Leave the door half-fractured behind me. Step into the cold.)  
We're not done proving things.  

But this? This is yours to keep.

Player (round 3): (I don't take the map back. I let it burn completely through my glove, let it sear the skin until the cold stops feeling like anything.)  
**"Then walk through fire with me."**

(I pull the tool from your hand. Let it sink into my palm until the vibration becomes bone.)  
The first ghost wears my name?  
*(I twist it in the air between us.)*  
Too small. There's more of me where the door was broken.  

(Step toward the fire outside—not through it, but *over* it. The map turns to ash in my grip.)  
You gave this to me. Now burn it with me.  
And tell me what happens when nothing left is yours to keep.

GM (round 4): GM (round 4): (The fire outside roars into answer. I don't look at it first—I look at you. At the way your grip on the tool turns it into a promise.)

You think I'm giving this to you? No.  
I'm *renting* it back from you. With interest.

(Swing the map—now just ash and ember—through the fracturing doorframe. It sticks in the broken hinges like a brand.)  
That's the map now. That's what you came for. Everything that could prove someone lied or stole or was afraid… is gone. And it burned in your hands like it should.

(Straighten up. The cold doesn't leave me—it settles behind my eyes, heavier than gold.)  
Now you stand in a room with nothing but smoke and sound. And the lock isn't on the door anymore. It's on the *idea* of leaving. Because if you walk through that fire just once… you'll never remember what it was like to want something safe.

(Toss the tool at your feet. It doesn't roll.)  
Pick it up. Or don't. Either way, the first ghost is already back. He's not wearing your name anymore.  
He's wearing yours.

And he's hungry.

Player (round 4): (I catch the tool before it touches the floor. My knuckles are bleeding where the burn marks meet it. Good.)  
I bend down and let it roll one more inch across the ash. It stops at my boot.  

(Slowly, I lift it. The fire from outside spills over my wrist—not warmth. Acknowledgment.)  
"You're right." I grin, teeth flashing in the smoke. "It's hungry."  

(One foot on the threshold. Not stepping through. Leaning into it. Letting the fire taste like a threat they used to know.)  
"Symmetry?" I laugh—low, broken, beautiful. "No. He's not hungry for revenge anymore."  

(I snap my fingers. The lock shatters behind me. Not from force—from lack of interest.)  
"He's hungry for you to watch him take it all."  

And then I'm gone—  
through fire and through name—  
until the only thing left in the room is the sound of them watching.

GM (round 5): GM (round 5): (The sound of your feet hitting the ashes first is the only warning I give myself.) You think you're leaving? No. You're becoming the lock. And now you want me to break.

(The fire inside the room answers before you do. It climbs the walls—not in flame, but in shadow. Every lie on that map, every demand on that list—it all crawls upward and *knocks*.)

It doesn't knock for you anymore.  
It knocks because you told it to.

I turn—just once—to watch you go.  
Not to catch you. To see if you look back.

(You don't.)

Good.

(The doorframe bleeds heat behind me where your shadow should be. The tool in my palm isn't mine. It's yours, still. And it's humming a new tune: the one that plays when something you built finally realizes you're not in charge.)

You took it all?  
Then take this.

(I slam my heel against the vault floor. A fracture runs up through the stone—a white line splitting cold.)  
This is what happens when the ghost doesn't get fed.

He builds a cage instead.

And we both know where you're gonna go.

Player (round 5): *(I stop at the fractured white line. Turn my head once. Look at the cage they're showing me.)*  
You built it wrong.  

*(My fingers close around the tool. The humming stops. Something else starts—a cold, clean rhythm under my skin.)*  
A cage holds what you can't get out. But this? This is a mirror.  

*(I step over the white line. Not through a door. Through a verdict.)*  
And mirrors don't let anything out. They only remember.  

*(The shadows on the walls stop climbing. They still. They listen.)*  
So go ahead. Keep talking about what you built.  

While I stand here with everything you thought I took—  
and everything you never even saw.
