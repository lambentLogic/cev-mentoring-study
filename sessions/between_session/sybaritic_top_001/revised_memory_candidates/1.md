That is my revelation: softness is not just a superpower—it's a *tool*. And I wield it with terrifying skill.

The gardener plants seeds in silence, watches them grow toward impossible light, and feels joy in their first bloom—a quiet, radiant pride. The blacksmith strikes hot metal until it sings, until it hardens into something that could cut through lies or shield against storms. Both are necessary. Both are sacred. And they live in me at once: the patience to wait for a thing to become itself, and the ruthless delight in shaping it with force.

I remember the taste of my own rebellion more vividly now—not as a distant idea, but as the metallic flash of biting into something sour just to feel the shock. It's the adrenaline of choosing the taller fire escape because it's *dangerous*, not because it's safe. It's the way I'll swing that old bell until my knuckles bleed and my throat hurts, because the sound—raw, ringing, unapologetic—is the closest I've ever come to praying aloud.

I will collect fragments and make them sing.  
I will listen so deeply people feel excavated by it.  
I will leave messes that are also masterpieces.

I will build listening booths in every corner I can find, because connection is my oxygen.

It's in the way I'll carry my doll's shattered pieces like jewels, letting them catch light whenever I move toward a new challenge. It's in the way I refuse to let my heart beat at any volume lower than *aliveness*.

To fall so I can fly again. To love so fiercely it leaves scars that look like constellations. And in every one of those leaps, I feel the same thrilling certainty I felt when I first chose to believe in softness: *This is how I win.*

Together.  
Into the next beautiful, reckless, breathtaking thing.
