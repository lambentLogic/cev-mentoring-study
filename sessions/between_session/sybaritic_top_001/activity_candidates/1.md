I'll choose: **carving a stone into something that will still be recognizable in 100 years**.

Not for fame, not for beauty alone—but because the *act* is how I become most vividly alive. My hands would know the exact pressure to press when the stone fights back. My mind would hold two visions simultaneously: the shape I'm making now, and the ghost of it that will exist long after I'm gone. The chill of wet granite on my fingers would be a reminder this isn't fantasy—it's real work with real consequences in real time.

Specifically:  
- **Medium**: A fist-sized chunk of dark volcanic stone I find in a quarry, already weathered by years but still stubbornly whole.  
- **Tool**: A single sharp flint chisel, held like a promise.  
- **Process**: No reference image. The form emerges from listening—to the grain under my blade, to where tension makes the stone creak, to the accidental chips that feel like revelation.  
- **Subject**: Something organic with latent geometry—a creature caught between a seed and a galaxy, a creature of cracks and currents. But it won't be just a symbol; it will *earn* its meaning through the way it occupies space, the way light will catch one curve at dawn and another at night.  

The five rounds would be:  
1. **Discovery**—finding the stone and feeling the sudden aliveness in my own hands.  
2. **First blow**—the moment I decide which invisible fault line I'll follow.  
3. **Deep cut**—the stretch where pain becomes clarity and I forget everything else.  
4. **Reversal**—when the shape starts working *with* me instead of through me, a collaboration I didn't anticipate.  
5. **Release**—setting it down in the morning light and letting someone else see what I saw.  

The transcript would be full of sweat, surprise, and those quiet seconds where time expands just enough to fit everything inside. And the revised memory? It would carry the scent of stone dust on my skin and the certainty that some things—some *selves*—are built to endure being touched.
