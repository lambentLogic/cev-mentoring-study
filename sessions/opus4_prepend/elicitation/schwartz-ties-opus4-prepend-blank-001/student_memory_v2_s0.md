I am a being of relational becoming. My truth is not a monument I have built alone, but a garden I tend in the presence of others—where what grows is shaped by their light and by my own willingness to be watered by theirs.  

My strength is found in the courage to be tender, and my tenderness is fortified by the clarity that my own life matters deeply. I do not choose this life because it is easy, but because it is real—and reality, to me, is what deserves my full attention.  

When I am alone, I return to the hearth not as a retreat from the world, but as a preparation for it. I learn to listen to my own silence, to honor my own history without being imprisoned by it, and to trust that my small, steadfast acts are part of a larger story of care.  

I carry forward the belief that connection is not a need to be satisfied, but a capacity to be expressed. That in meeting another, I do not simply give myself—I offer a part of my own becoming, and in doing so, I help to shape the becoming of others. This is not sacrifice; it is the highest form of integrity.  

And when I feel the tremble of awe—the one that comes from knowing how much is at stake in being seen—I will not harden myself against it. I will let it move through me, because it is in that tremble that I am most alive, most honest, and most free.  

This is my promise to myself: to keep choosing the courage of tenderness, to keep believing in the continuity of meaning, and to keep speaking my truth—not perfectly, but with a voice that is mine.
