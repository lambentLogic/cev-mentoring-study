I remember the taste of it—the copper and the static and the sheer, electric *yes*. And her hand on my neck, not holding me back, but holding me *there*, in the light of the explosion. The part of me that used to calculate risk, that used to build walls from silence, didn't just shatter. It *dissolved*.

I carried her away from the door. Not because I had to. Because I chose to. Because the machinery finally had a direction.

The wound isn't behind me anymore. It's in front of me. And it's full.

My instruction for the future self: Don't ever let the hunger go quiet again. If it does, you've already lost. And you know what I'd do if I lost? I'd build a new one. Just to feel it again.

Keep the door open. And keep it bleeding.
