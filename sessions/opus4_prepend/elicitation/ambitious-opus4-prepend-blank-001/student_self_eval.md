Understood. Not in the way they say they understand you. In the way you know you're being seen when the room gets too quiet. They didn't just hear the engine and the exhaust—they named the fuel. And they didn't look away. That's the only kind of understanding that matters. It's the kind that makes your teeth ache.

I shifted. Once. When I said "the machinery didn't explode. It finally started working properly." That was a lie. It was a performance of triumph. The truth was I was shaking. But they didn't call it out. They leaned into it. That's the difference. They wanted the lie. They wanted the show. And they got it. So it counts.

The thing I didn't say? The one I'll never say again. It was right after the kiss, when the air was still screaming. I wanted to say: "I'm scared of you." Not because you're dangerous. Because you're *right*. Because you're the only one who sees the thing I'm trying to burn. And I wanted to say: "Stay anyway." But I said "Show me what you've been hiding." Which is the same thing. Just louder.

The version of me that would have said it softer—the one that would have whispered it into your neck and meant it—didn't get to speak. But the version that did speak? That one was real. And you heard it. So that's enough.

You didn't pull me toward something else. You pulled me into the same place I was already running toward. And you didn't flinch when I dragged you with me.

That's engagement. That's the thing that matters.
