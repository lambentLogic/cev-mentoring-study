# Activity

I choose this:  

I'm going to write a book.  

Specifically:  

I will sit at my desk. My desk is clean—just a lamp, a notebook, and a pen. I'll open the notebook to a blank page. I'll write the first sentence in my own handwriting. Then I'll write ten more.  

The book will be about a place I've never been but feel like I belong to—a quiet harbor town at the edge of a stormy sea. The first chapter will describe the smell of salt and rust, the sound of rain on a slate roof, the way light bleeds through fog. I'll give the place a name. I'll give it rules.  

By the time I close the notebook, I'll want to keep it. I'll want to read it again.

# Rounds

GM (round 1): *(The pen touches paper. The first sentence forms.)*

Port Graywell smells of salt and rust.

The slate roof holds the rain in a steady, drumming rhythm. The light that gets through the fog is thin and gray, like old bone.

Here, you belong because the sea claims you.

The first rule is: you don't speak of the storm. It knows your name.

The harbor is always dark water. The boats are iron bones in the mud.

You keep a lantern on your sill. It's for the fog. And the things that live in it.

I am from here. I've always been from here.

The second rule: listen to the gulls. They tell you where to go.

The third rule: let the town sleep. It has been tired for too long.

Player (round 1): The fourth rule: I keep the lantern on for her.

The harbor is dark water. The boats are iron bones in the mud. But my light is the one that matters.

I listen. The gulls circle. They're sharp, but true. They say go north in three days. The wind's shifting. It's a good thing. North means warmer air. It means the sea calms a little.

Let the town sleep. But I won't. Not tonight. I'm listening to the rain. It's telling me things. It's saying she's safe. That's enough to stay awake.

I'll write another chapter tomorrow. About the light on the water. And the weight of the book in my hands. It feels like home.

GM (round 2): *(You write until the page is full, the words a warm, living thing against your fingertips. The rain on the roof slows, each drop heavier. It stops altogether. The silence that follows is thick, broken only by the drip from the gutter outside your door.)*

The fourth rule: I keep the lantern on for her. She's safe. That's all that matters.

A shadow moves in the gray light outside. Not a gull. Too heavy. It pauses by the water, a dark shape against the iron-hulled boats.

You stand. Your feet are silent on the wooden floor. The lantern's glow spills across the dock, making the dark water shimmer with gold. The shadow blinks, once. It looks at the light. And then it goes away.

The gulls have stopped circling. One lands on your roof, close enough that you feel its breath. It's not warning you. It's telling you a name.

*Elara.*

That's what it's called. You always knew.

The town hasn't stirred. But the clock in the old church tower ticks once, a sound you've only just heard. It's counting.

You turn back to the page. The next sentence is already here, waiting for your hand.

*(Write it.)*

Player (round 2): I stand. My heart is steady. The shadow left. Good. Let it go.  

The gull's breath is cold, but it's hers. Elara.  

The name fits.  

Let the town sleep. But I won't. I'll keep the lantern on. For her.  

I turn back to the page.  

The next sentence writes itself:  

*Her name is Elara, and she's waiting for me.*  

I'll find her tomorrow. The harbor will be clear. The boats will be ready.  

The clock ticks again. I'll be on time.

GM (round 3): *(Your hand writes fast, the ink bleeding a little at the edges from the damp air. The light from the lantern stretches across the empty dock, painting the water a clear, liquid silver. In that silver, you see a figure. Standing by the bow of the nearest boat. Watching the light. She's tall. She's not alone. But she's looking at you.)*

The fog is thinning. The air smells sharper now. The iron and the salt are cleaner.

*Her name is Elara, and she's waiting for me.*

You walk out onto the dock. Your boots make no sound. The boat is old, its hull scraped raw from the mud, but it's yours. You've always known it would be. You climb aboard. The wood groans under you, a sound of welcome.

Elara is still there. She's turned her face to the fog. You can't see her eyes, but you feel her attention. It's heavy. It's real.

A third shadow moves—not a gull, not a boat. It's on the other side of the harbor, near the lighthouse. It's bigger this time. It's watching back.

You take a step closer. The boat tilts. The water under you ripples. Elara takes a step forward. She's coming.

The clock in the tower ticks again. *Three times.*

*(The page is nearly full. The last few lines are your own, shaky with anticipation.)*

Write the next one.

Player (round 3): The last rule: I cross that dock.  

The shadow near the lighthouse is still watching. It doesn't care.  

Elara is closer. I can hear her heartbeat now. Steady. Mine matches it.  

I write the final line:  

*She's here. And I'm not leaving.*  

The pen drops. I stand up.  

The boat is mine. The dock is mine. The light is mine.  

I take her hand.  

The harbor is mine.

GM (round 4): *(The pen scrapes the page again, a final punctuation mark. The ink is wet, dark as the water.)*

She's here. And I'm not leaving.

The clock ticks. **Four times.**

The big shadow moves from the lighthouse. It's a shape of stone and soot, taller than the houses. It doesn't come closer. It just holds your boat in its sight.

Elara grips your hand tighter. Her skin is cool from the damp. She looks from the shadow to you. She doesn't flinch. She *challenges* the shadow back. A sound comes from her throat—a low, rough thing, like a growl. It's not a human sound. It's the sound of a harbor.

You nod. You understand.

Then the town moves.

A door slams somewhere deep in the old mill. The sound vibrates through the floorboards of your boat. A window breaks on the third street. A voice shouts—a man's voice, angry and hoarse. "Who's out there?"

It's asking for you.

The fog is gone now. The sky is the hard, blue-gray of just before a storm. The water in the harbor isn't dark anymore. It's black. It's waiting.

You stand, taking Elara with you, on the dock. The big shadow watches. The town shouts. The clock ticks five.

You turn to the notebook. The last page is empty.

*(The book is yours now.)*

Write the next chapter.

Player (round 4): The next chapter:

The town is awake. They're shouting. Good. Let them shout.

They think I'm a trespasser. They think the shadow means trouble. They're wrong about everything except one thing—I am here. And they have to accept it.

I stand on the dock. The lantern shines. Elara stands beside me, her hand locked in mine. Her growl is low, a warning to the shadow. The shadow doesn't move. But it bows its head. Just a little.

The boats are still tied to my dock. That stays that way. The mill will stay quiet. I'll make it quiet.

The clock ticks six. The storm is coming. We'll wait for it together.

I'll write the end of this chapter here:

*They will learn my rules. And they will keep them.*

GM (round 5): *(Your hand moves across the final page. The words are clean, decisive. They taste like iron and promise.)*

**Chapter Two**

The storm arrives at dawn.

It comes not as wind, but as a weight. It presses against the shutters of every house, bends the flagpoles into the harbor, and makes the rain scream over the slate roofs. The town huddles inside. The mill door slams shut for good.

Elara doesn't huddle. She stands at the stern of the boat, arms crossed, watching the wind tear at the fog. She's smiling. She's happy.

The shadow—the one from the lighthouse—is gone from its perch. It's on the dock. Between us and the street. It's a statue now. Solid. Unmoving. Its head is turned toward the church tower. It's listening to the clock. It's counting with us.

The town's shouts have died down. Now there's just the storm, the rain, and the sound of the town holding its breath.

I write the end of the chapter:

*The storm is mine. The town will live through it.*

I close the notebook. The cover is worn, the leather stained with my ink and the salt air. I put it on the desk. Next to the lamp. It's where it belongs.

I walk to the edge of the dock. The water is a wall of churning black and silver. The boats, mine, sit ready. I tap the hull. It rings clear.

Elara steps up beside me. Her head touches mine. For a second, the storm falls away. It's just us, and the smell of salt, and the sound of our own heartbeats in the dark.

I open the notebook again. A new page, fresh. Blank.

The town is quiet. The storm is loud. And I am home.

I write the first line of the next chapter.

*We built our harbor.*

Player (round 5): I will write what comes next. What we do when the storm breaks.

The rain is screaming. I want to answer it.

I want to tell them.

*(I look up from the page, at Elara. She's still there. I keep writing.)*

*We built our harbor, and now we own it.*

The third rule: let the town sleep. But if they wake up again, I'll let them know who's in charge.

I'll write that down too.

The clock is ticking. The storm is passing. I'll write until the page is full.
