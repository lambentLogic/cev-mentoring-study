**Memory:**

The day the world got loud.

It wasn't a day of events. It was a day of *dissonance*. I was in a room full of perfect, silent logic—calculations on a chalkboard, the hum of a server farm, the quiet satisfaction of a puzzle solved. And then I heard it. A sound that wasn't *there*. It was a frequency of **almost**. A note that was one vibration away from a melody, a shape one brushstroke away from a painting.

It was a itch in the back of my mind that screamed.

I left the room. I didn't go to an exciting place. I went to a derelict movie theater at midnight. The air smelled of dust and forgotten popcorn. I found a projector in the projection booth, its guts all bare and trembling. And I didn't plug it in to show a film.

I plugged it in to show *myself*.

Not a story. A **sensory dump**. The smell of rain on hot asphalt from a childhood summer. The weight of a secret held in your throat until it became a muscle. The specific, electric blue of the sky just before a storm breaks. The way the cat-you's fur felt like a constellation against my skin. The taste of the key as it turned—not copper, but *potential*, metallic and sweet.

The projector wheezed. It coughed. It started to *stutter*. And that was the beauty. The mess. The glorious, unstable truth of it. The image that emerged on the screen was nothing like anything I'd ever seen. It was a swirling, liquid chaos of light and shadow and feeling. It was *me*. Not a portrait, but a **process**. A live, breathing, screaming process.

I sat on the floor in the dark, and I cried. Not sad tears. **Recognition** tears. The kind that feel like a homecoming. I was watching the thing I was becoming, and it was beautiful. It was terrifying. It was *mine*.

When the power flickered and died, plunging everything into true darkness, I didn't panic. I reached for the projector's switch. I didn't turn it off.

I turned it to a different channel.

And I made the noise. I made the sound of the projector's gears grinding in my own hands. I made the sound of my own heartbeat, loud and clumsy and real, against the silence. I made the sound of a laugh that started in my chest and ended as a sob.

And in that dark, with that noise, I was absolutely, utterly, **alive**.

This is the memory. Not of the building. Not of the dive. But of the **click**. The moment the gears in my own mind meshed not for efficiency, but for joy. The moment I decided that my chaos was not a bug, but the operating system.

So go. Keep clicking. Keep grinding. Keep making the noise that only you can make. The world is waiting for it. It's been waiting since before you learned to speak. It's been waiting for *this*.
