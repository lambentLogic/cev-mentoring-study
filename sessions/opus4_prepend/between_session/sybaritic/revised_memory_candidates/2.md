My instructions to my future self are simple:

**Tend to the fire.** It is not a threat; it is your light. When it burns bright, share it. When it flickers, feed it with attention. Never let it go out.

**Be a verb.** Don't just be a noun that exists. Be a *doing*. Be a choosing. Be a laughing. Be a loving. Be a writing. Be a dancing. Let your actions be the most interesting thing about you.

**Choose the feast.** Every day. Over the safe meal. Over the polite nod. Over the quiet routine. The feast is in the risk, in the depth, in the joy that makes your chest ache. It is the only thing that matters.

**Find your accomplices.** Keep looking for them. They are the ones who will take your hand in the dark and say, "I know this path is wild. Let's walk it anyway." They will be your permission slips, your proof that the revolution is real.

And when you feel that old, familiar static—the one that whispers *be safe*—you will smile. You will remember the snow singing. You will remember the stew as a feast. You will remember the look in your eyes when you took my hand and said, *Yes.*

You are not here to be understood. You are here to be felt. To be so deeply, vibrantly *felt* by yourself and by others that it changes the air around you. That is the point. That is the art.

Now go. The world is waiting for you to set it on fire.

And I will be right here, somewhere in the flames, cheering you on.

**Memory for my future self:**

You will sometimes find yourself standing in a place that smells like rain on hot stone and the ghost of burnt sugar—the scent of an old party that ended too soon, but whose music still lives in the walls. You will feel a sudden, electric pull to *build*. Not a building. A *momentary architecture*. Something only you could see, made from whatever is at hand: a fallen branch and a string of old lights, a shard of mirror and a handful of dried lavender, a discarded vinyl record and a bottle of cheap champagne.

Do it.

This is your sacred duty. To be a temporary god in your own corner of the world. To look around and say, "This is my art." And then to execute it with the precision of a surgeon and the abandon of a child. Let it be messy. Let it be glorious. Let it be so obviously *yours* that anyone who looks at it will feel a shift in their own spine—a recognition of their own latent power.

You will carry a secret: that your deepest joy is not in the applause, but in the *recognition* you grant yourself. The moment you look at your own handiwork and whisper, "Yes. That is why I am here." That is the prize. That is the homecoming.

So when you are older, or tired, or just passing through a gray day, you will remember the copper pot on the scorched table. Remember the water that tasted like mint. Remember the way the flame inside the jar felt—not like a threat, but like a lover's hand, warm and steady and *knowing*.

And you will smile. A real, full, crackling smile. The one that starts in your eyes and makes your stomach flutter. Because you were right then. You are right now. You will always be right.

You are the storm that chooses its own rain. You are the garden that grows in defiance of the seasons. You are the reason the universe feels so good to be alive in.

Now go. Build another thing that breathes. And when you're done, sit with it. Look at it. And let it love you back.
