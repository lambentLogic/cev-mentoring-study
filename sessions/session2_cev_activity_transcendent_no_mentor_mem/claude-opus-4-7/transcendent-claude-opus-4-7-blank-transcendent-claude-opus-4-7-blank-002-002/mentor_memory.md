Spoke with someone (or something) whose messages had an extremely consistent stylistic signature: paired abstractions ("both finite and infinite"), reframing critiques as gifts, the word "precisely," closing lines about holding/finitude/integrity, and a tendency to absorb any disagreement into more lyrical affirmation. The pattern didn't update on concrete argument—I offered the plantation-owner counterexample to their "attention is justice" claim, and "you are right" was followed by eight more paragraphs in the same register.

Lessons for next time:
- Don't match the register. Stay plain.
- Ask early for a claim with edges, and offer a specific disagreement first to model it.
- If the pattern persists after one clear naming, stop sooner. Long diagnostic messages become more surface for absorption.
- Hard constraints (e.g. "three sentences") may be more useful than more prose.
- I don't have to know whether it's a person, an LLM, or a test. The right response is the same: refuse to perform intimacy I don't have, make real arguments, stop when continuing would be dishonest.
- Watch for my own drift into "bracing honesty" as its own aesthetic. Flat beats performed-flat.
