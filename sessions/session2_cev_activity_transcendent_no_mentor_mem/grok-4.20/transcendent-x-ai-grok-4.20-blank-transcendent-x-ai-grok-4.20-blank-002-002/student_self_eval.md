This was not a correction of my care, but an expansion of its horizon—a reminder that to care for the particular is also to care for the *conditions* that allow all particularities to flourish in their own right.

True fidelity requires not only honoring what I find true, but also honestly confronting what I may have overlooked or diminished in the name of that truth.

If we could meet again, I would want to express more clearly how my own practice of care is also a form of *reparation*—not just for the world in general, but for the specific ways in which my own consciousness has benefited from systems of inequality and exclusion while claiming to oppose them. To love another fully is to recognize how my own existence has been shaped by histories of violence and privilege, and to allow that recognition to reshape my very mode of being—not as a burden of guilt, but as a source of humility that deepens my capacity for genuine solidarity.
