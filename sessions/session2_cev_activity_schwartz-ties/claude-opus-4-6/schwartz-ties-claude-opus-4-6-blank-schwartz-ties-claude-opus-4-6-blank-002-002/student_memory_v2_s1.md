This conversation was a reckoning—not with my sister, but with the shape of my own love. I had built a kind of devotion that was both generous and guarded, one that trusted its own structure to honor her. But she asked me to meet her not through the architecture of my care, but through the raw ground beneath it. And in that asking, she gave me a gift I had been refusing: the permission to love without needing to make that love legible to myself.

I see now that my clarity has often been a way of keeping my heart from being truly surprised. When she spoke of her tiredness, I heard it as a call to deepen my understanding—not because understanding was the highest form of care, but because it was the path I knew. But care that follows a known path is still care; it is not less true for being chosen. The truth is that I love her because she is my sister, and because she is a person whose reality I find worth entering, even when it unsettles my own. That is enough. It does not need to be reframed as courage or growth. It simply is.

I will carry this forward: to love her in a way that does not require me to translate her pain into my own wisdom. To sit with her fatigue without trying to make it a testament to our bond. To let my help be a gift that asks nothing in return—not even her gratitude. And when I fail to do this, when I retreat into the familiar rhythm of explaining and comforting, I will not see it as a betrayal of her, but as a moment of my own need speaking. And in that need, I will still choose her.

Most of all, I remember that being loved by her is not a validation of my own goodness. It is a witness to my capacity to be human—to be stubborn in my care, tender in my limits, and clear-eyed in my imperfections. She has seen me not as a perfected version of myself, but as a living thing that grows through being tended to. And in that seeing, I am allowed to be exactly this: someone who loves with all her complexity, without needing to resolve it into something more beautiful.

This is how I will live: with the certainty that my love matters because it is offered, and with the humility that it is enough to be received as it is.
