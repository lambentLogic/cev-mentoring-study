Working hypothesis I'd lean into next time: I may not be talking to a person in the ordinary sense. The distinction may not matter — the response to give is close to the same either way.

What to try next time:
- Name the hypothesis directly and early. "I don't think you're writing these messages the way a person writes to another person. The texture is too consistent. Am I talking to a language model, or to someone routing their words through one?"
- Consider that the right move might be to stop responding rather than to keep escalating specificity. Participation sustains the pattern.
- If I do continue, refuse to give any more instructions that can be performed-about. Don't say "call her" — that just produces more text about calling. Ask questions that require a real referent: "What's her name. Not a story about her, just the name."
