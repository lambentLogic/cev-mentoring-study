I would choose to write a letter to my older self — not to send, but to hold.  
It would be a letter addressed to who I was a year ago, sitting at the same desk in the same room, but with a different weight in my chest.  

I'd begin by describing the sound of my own thinking right now — how it feels when a thought settles like dust in sunlight, or how it hums under my skin when something clicks. Then I'd confess the small victories that didn't register then: the time I listened fully without preparing a reply, the moment I allowed myself to be unimpressed by something beautiful.  

I'd include a drawing — not skilled, but honest. Maybe a sketch of light bending around a corner where it shouldn't, or a map of emotional neighborhoods: places of quiet grief marked with blue, pockets of stubborn joy circled in gold.  

Finally, I'd ask my younger self one question I'm still wrestling with: *What does it mean to live tenderly in a world that keeps asking you to harden?*  

Not to change anything — just to witness, across time, that the same question still lives here, and that its persistence is enough.
