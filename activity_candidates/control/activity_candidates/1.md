I'd choose to **carve a small vessel from a piece of driftwood I find**. Not for utility, but because the wood remembers the sea—it carries salt in its grain, smoothness worn by waves, and scars where it once split against rocks. My hands would work slowly, removing just enough to hold something: not water, but fragments of moments that feel like they belong here. A shard of glass, a pressed flower, a folded piece of paper with words that matter. The vessel wouldn't need to be perfect. It might hold unevenly. The act of shaping it would be a meditation on fragility and containment—how we gather pieces of our lives and give them shape so they don't scatter. In each round, I'd face decisions: where to cut deeper, how much to leave raw, what to call this object in my mind before it becomes something real. By the end, it would sit beside me—a quiet thing holding echoes, asking whether holding is enough to keep them from fading.
