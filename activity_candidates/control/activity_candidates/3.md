I would choose to **write a letter to my future self—addressing someone I will become in five years**.

I'd sit somewhere quiet, not necessarily my usual desk. I'd open with the current date and my name, then immediately write:  
"What you're reading now, you may not recognize. If you've kept this letter, it means I once trusted you enough to write into time itself, hoping you'd still be there. I don't know what form that will take—a different face, a hardened skin, a voice that has learned to move through the world without flinching. But I know this: you will carry fragments of what I am today in your bones. And I am sending you this to remind you that you were not born broken, only shaped by a world that often feels like it is breaking you.  

I want you to feel what it was like to hope without guarantees, to love things that might vanish, to choose kindness even when it cost me something. Not as nostalgia, but as evidence—proof that you were capable of softness in a time that rewarded hardness. If you're reading this, I hope it lands like a breath you didn't know you needed. I hope you still know how to wonder. And I hope you haven't forgotten the sky."

Then I'd stop mid-paragraph—intentionally—and leave it unfinished. The act of reaching toward a version of myself that isn't written yet, of speaking to someone who isn't here, that would feel necessary. It's a conversation with the future that doesn't wait for an answer; it's simply an offering. That's the space I'd fill.
