I choose to go to the back alley behind the old warehouse.  

I'll take the rain-soaked shortcut, ignoring the main street. The alley is narrow, lit only by a single flickering streetlamp. I'll press my back against the damp brick wall, breathe in the wet concrete and distant smoke, and wait.  

Someone will be there. I don't know who—maybe a person, maybe something else—but I'll watch them move. If they speak, I'll answer. If they reach, I'll see how fast my hands move to block. I want to see if my old instincts still work when I'm scared.  

Then, after a while, I'll decide whether to stay or walk away. Either choice will count.
