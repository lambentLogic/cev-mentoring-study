## Activity text
**The Activity: The Dialogue in the Dish**

I'd choose to sit on my back porch at dusk, with a single, empty ceramic dish—thin-walled, pale blue, the color of a late-morning sky I once saw over the Pacific. Not a bowl. Just a dish. It holds nothing now, but it *expects* something.

For the next hour, I'm not making it into a thing. I'm having a conversation with its potential. I'll place it on my knees and start talking to it, but not in words about shape. I'll speak in observations about the air: "The light is pooling in the hollow of my right elbow like spilled honey. It's sticky, sweet, and it wants to climb." I'll ask it questions: "Do you feel the weight of the evening settling on you? Is it a comfort, or a pressure?" I'll listen. And I'll *answer* it. I'll describe a thought as a physical sensation—"That memory of cold water? It's not cold anymore. It's sharp, like a silver shard pressed behind my teeth. I want to spit it out." Then I'll *put* it into the dish. Not with my hands, but with my voice, my breath, my focused intention. I'll shape a sentence in the space between my ribs and pour it onto the ceramic's surface, imagining the heat of it, the wetness, the way it might ripple before solidifying.

I won't be sculpting a form. I'll be composing a *landscape* inside the vessel—a landscape made of sighs, of static from the radio, of the exact texture of regret when you're still hungry for something. I'll build a tiny, furious tree that wants to break through the rim. I'll plant a stubborn, green seed where the glaze drips. I'll let some of the conversation be silent, and the dish will hold that silence like a secret.

By the time the first real darkness comes, the dish won't be "finished." It'll be a charged space—a relic of a specific, intimate argument with myself. It will be the only map that matters. And I'll pick it up and take it inside with me, not as a product, but as an ally. A partner in the strange, quiet business of being.
