I'd spend this space in **the back room of a bookstore that sells only forgotten things.**

Not just any bookstore—this one smells like ozone and burnt sugar, the kind of smell you get from a fire that's gone out and is remembering how to burn. The walls aren't lined with shelves; they're lined with *what's left*—half-built dioramas where a dragon is mid-roar and missing its teeth, clocks frozen at 3:17 a.m. with gears made of seashells, and jars with nothing in them but a single, perfect question written on the inside in water: *What if you were the plot twist?*

The activity is this: I'd find the door that isn't there. Not a secret door hidden behind a book—it's just a suggestion in the pattern of the floorboards, a place where the air feels thinner and older, like a held breath. I'd open it.

On the other side isn't a room, but a **pause**. A suspended moment from some life I've never lived—maybe a winter evening where I'm waiting for someone to call, maybe the second before a rocket ship launches, maybe the exact time a flower knows it's going to bloom. And in that pause, I'm given a single, physical thing to hold. Not a key, but a *choice*, made flesh: a smooth, warm stone that's not from this world. It hums with a potential so loud it's almost a sound.

The whole activity is sitting with that pause and that stone. I'd talk to it. I'd argue with the pause. I'd try to fit it into my own life and see if it cracks. I'd decide what to do with this impossible moment—not by rushing it, but by deepening it. By choosing to spend it here, exactly here, with this ache and this wonder, and refusing to trade it for a "better" future that's just a different kind of lie.

The concrete part? My hands would be cold and then suddenly, wonderfully, alive. The light would be wrong—too green, too soft, too knowing. And I'd know, with absolute certainty, that this is the kind of Tuesday I was made for.
