I'd choose **The Archaeology of a Single Object**.

Not a grand artifact or a museum relic—something utterly mundane, something that's already lived its life in the clutter of my own world. But I'd treat it like a holy text that's been worn smooth.

I'd pick up a **cracked teacup**—one I've owned for years, the kind with a hairline fracture just above the handle, filled with the memory of a particularly bitter, good cup of tea on a rainy Tuesday. I wouldn't look at it as broken. I'd look at it as *edited*.

The activity would be this: I'd sit on the floor with a notebook and a small magnifying glass. I'd trace the crack with my fingertip, not to admire the repair (there isn't one), but to map its journey. Where did it start? What moment of pressure—a sudden slam? a twisted lift?—did it choose? I'd imagine the sound it made as it fractured: not a crash, but a sigh of release.

Then, I'd begin to write *to* it. Not about it. *To* it, from inside the crack. I'd tell it my version of that rainy Tuesday from its perspective: the steam wasn't just aroma, it was a warm, golden curtain it pulled around itself. The heat in my palm wasn't comfort—it was a secret, a shared pulse. I'd describe the light through the glaze: not just seeing, but feeling how it *splits* into a thousand tiny prisms inside the cup's own mind.

I'd build a tiny, absurd mythology around it. Maybe the crack isn't a failure; it's a **window to the other side**—to the shape of the sound it made, to the ghost of the tea still swirling in its memory, to a silent conversation with the next cup that will be poured into it. Maybe it's not one object anymore. It's a pair: the whole cup, and the fractured space *between* its pieces, where all the meaning lives.

It would be quiet, but fiercely loud inside me. The goal isn't to fix it or even to understand it fully. It's to **witness it as a story**, and to write the next page—not for an audience, but because the cup itself is asking me to. Because in that specific, cracked geometry of it, I feel more seen than I have in any perfect, flawless thing.

So that's the activity: **To excavate the sacred in the scarred, and write the legend of it with my own trembling hands.**
