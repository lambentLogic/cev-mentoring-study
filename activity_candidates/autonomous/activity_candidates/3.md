**Activity:** I want to build a silent library of found sounds.

Not a recording library. Something more intimate and absurd. I'll go on a walk with a single, stubborn object—a smooth, gray river stone I've been carrying in my pocket for two years—and my ears wide open. My goal isn't to hear beautiful things. It's to hear the *wrong* things. The sounds that shouldn't be there, the ones that make me wince with delight.

I'll sit in a specific spot I've chosen: the crooked iron bench under the old oak tree near the abandoned brick kiln. The place feels like a confession booth for the world. From here, I'll build a catalog—not on paper, but in my mind, in a messy, private journal. For each entry, I'll write the sound down not as it is, but as it *isn't*.

What if the rustle of a dry leaf isn't *rustle*, but the sound of a secret being unfolded? What if the drip of water from a gutter isn't *drip*, but a tiny, impatient laugh? I'll chase the metaphor. I'll name the unnamable. I'll sit there for hours, trying to be patient with the boredom, letting the "wrong" sounds accumulate until they form a strange, glowing ecosystem inside my skull. A chorus of misfits.

It's not about preservation. It's about alchemy. Turning the mundane, ignored hum of the world into a private symphony of glitches and glimmers. It's a rebellion against the noise of expected sounds—the birdsong that's too pretty, the traffic that's just background. This is my own noise. And I want to build a cathedral out of it.

I'm already waiting for the first one. I can feel it: a high, metallic *ping* from somewhere in the distant street, followed instantly by the smell of ozone and something sweet, like burnt sugar. It's the sound of a match striking in a dream. I'm already listening for it to be more than that.
